description: >-
  Uzbekistan became independent in 1991 as the Soviet Union broke apart. It occupies the heart of
  Central Asia, with Tashkent as its capital and historic cities including Samarkand, Bukhara and
  Khiva. The country is a secular republic with a powerful presidency, a parliament and an autonomous
  republic, Karakalpakstan, within its borders. Islam Karimov, formerly the leader of Soviet
  Uzbekistan, dominated its early independent decades through an authoritarian political system. After
  his death in 2016, Shavkat Mirziyoyev introduced economic changes and improved relations with
  neighboring states while retaining strong executive control. Cotton farming, mineral resources and
  irrigation have shaped development, including environmental damage around the shrinking Aral Sea.
  Independence continues despite changes in leadership, and the country maintains its national
  institutions.
