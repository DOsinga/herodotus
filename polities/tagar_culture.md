description: >-
  The Tagar culture flourished in southern Siberia, especially the Minusinsk basin, approximately between 800 and
  100 BCE. Named after an island in the Yenisei River, it is an archaeological culture
  rather than a documented kingdom. Its communities raised cattle, horses, sheep and goats, cultivated crops
  and lived in timber houses with substantial hearths. Bronze working was particularly important, producing weapons,
  tools and ornaments decorated with animal forms comparable to those of other steppe societies. Large
  burial mounds bounded by stone slabs, sometimes with tall corner stones, show striking differences in
  wealth and burial practice. Collective graves became more common over time. Changes associated with Xiongnu
  influence helped reshape the region, followed by the Tesinsky and Tashtyk archaeological traditions in turn.
