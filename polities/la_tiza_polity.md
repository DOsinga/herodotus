description: >-
  La Tiza was a governing community in the Nasca drainage of southern Peru, represented here from approximately 400 to 750 CE. Its history is reconstructed through excavations rather than written records or named rulers. The settlement belonged to the wider Nasca world, whose communities cultivated desert valleys and shared pottery, textiles and religious practices without forming one unified state. Archaeological work at La Tiza examines households, burials and differences in social standing to understand leadership. Its occupation continued through later periods, so the dates mark a political phase rather than the entire settlement's life. During the Middle Horizon, Wari expansion altered local relationships. New elite tombs and evidence of nonlocal individuals show connections with Wari, replacing the earlier political order.
