description: >-
  The Taifa of Zaragoza was a Muslim monarchy in the Ebro valley of northeastern Iberia,
  emerging as the Caliphate of Córdoba fragmented in the early eleventh century. This
  record follows the conventional 1013 starting date. Zaragoza was first ruled by the
  Tujibid family and later by the Hudids, whose court became an important regional centre
  of learning and artistic patronage. Its rulers balanced warfare, tribute, and alliances
  with neighbouring Christian kingdoms and rival Muslim states. El Cid served the Hudid
  rulers, illustrating how military partnerships crossed religious boundaries. Pressure
  from Aragon and the Almoravids narrowed the taifa's options. Almoravid government
  replaced its royal court in 1110. Hudid resistance continued elsewhere, but no longer
  represented an independent government in Zaragoza itself.
