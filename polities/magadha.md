description: >-
  Magadha was a kingdom in the eastern Ganges plain, centred on present Bihar in India.
  This record begins with Bimbisara around 544 BCE and follows the Haryanka,
  Shishunaga, and Nanda rulers until 322 BCE. Its early capital was Rajagriha,
  protected by surrounding hills; government later moved to Pataliputra near major
  river routes. Military conquest and political alliances brought neighbouring
  territories under royal control, making Magadha one of the strongest northern Indian
  states. The region was central to the lives and teachings of the Buddha and Mahavira,
  and its kings supported religious communities. Ancient accounts differ over the exact
  early chronology and royal succession. Chandragupta Maurya overthrew the Nandas,
  turning Magadha's territorial and administrative base into the expanding Mauryan
  imperial state.
