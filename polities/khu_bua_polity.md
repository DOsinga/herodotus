description: >-
  Khu Bua was a fortified urban community near present day Ratchaburi in western Thailand, flourishing
  roughly between 550 and 1050. Its oval ramparts and moats enclosed one of the larger
  centres associated with Dvaravati culture. Buddhist monuments, sculpture and religious objects reveal both Theravada and
  Mahayana traditions, alongside some Brahmanical material. Imported glass beads connect the town with trade reaching
  South Asia and the Middle East through coastal and river routes. Stucco figures depict musicians,
  merchants and people of rank, offering glimpses of its social life. Its rulers and precise
  political structure remain poorly known, and shared Dvaravati styles do not establish a united kingdom.
  The estimated period marks its main urban phase rather than a securely dated royal history.
