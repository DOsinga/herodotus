description: >-
  The Twipra Kingdom was a hereditary monarchy of the Tripuri people in northeastern India and
  adjoining parts of modern Bangladesh. This record begins around 1400, when the historically documented Manikya
  dynasty emerged, rather than with the much earlier ancestry claimed in royal legend. Its kings
  ruled from successive capitals, including Rangamati, later called Udaipur, and supported Hindu worship alongside local
  religious traditions. Expansion brought them into repeated conflict with Bengal's rulers, the Mughals and neighbouring
  eastern powers. The hills remained a royal stronghold as control of the plains weakened. Britain
  acquired the plains in 1761 and increasingly constrained the hill kingdom. Its independent period ends
  here around 1871, when British supervision became decisive, although the princely monarchy survived until 1949.
