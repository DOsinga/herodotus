description: >-
  The Abbey of Saint Gall was a Benedictine monastery in northeastern Switzerland, founded in the
  eighth century on the site of the hermitage of the Irish monk Gallus. Its abbots became princes
  of the Empire in 1207 and built a territory in the land south of Lake Constance, with their
  residence at Wil as well as at the abbey. The Appenzell peasants rebelled successfully in 1405,
  and the town of St. Gallen bought its freedom in the fifteenth century. In 1451 the abbey allied
  with four Swiss cantons, and Abbot Ulrich Rösch rebuilt its power, buying the county of
  Toggenburg in 1468. The prince-abbots ruled their lands as an associate of the Confederacy until
  the Helvetic Republic dissolved their state in 1798.
