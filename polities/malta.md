  Malta is an island state in the central Mediterranean, south of Sicily, independent from Britain since 1964. Its main inhabited islands are Malta and Gozo, and its capital is Valletta. Independence initially left the British monarch as head of state; Malta became a republic in 1974 without ending its parliamentary system. An elected House of Representatives supports a government led by the prime minister, while the president performs chiefly ceremonial duties. The last British military facilities closed in 1979. Malta's position between Europe and North Africa has shaped its ports, migration and foreign relations. Maltese and English are official languages, reflecting different strands of its history. It joined the European Union in 2004 and adopted the euro currency in 2008.
