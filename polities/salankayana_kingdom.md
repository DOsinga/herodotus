description: >-
  The Salankayana kingdom ruled part of coastal Andhra in southeastern India, approximately
  from 300 to 440 CE. Its centre was Vengi, identified with modern Pedavegi near Eluru,
  between the Krishna and Godavari rivers. Royal inscriptions, mostly copper plates
  recording land grants, provide the main evidence for its rulers and territory. Kings
  supported Brahmins and temples, while one surviving stone inscription records a grant to a
  Buddhist community. Their documents shifted from an earlier Middle Indo-Aryan language
  toward Sanskrit, reflecting changes in royal culture. The Gupta emperor Samudragupta's
  inscription names Hastivarman of Vengi among southern kings captured and released during
  his campaign. Salankayana chronology remains uncertain, and neighbouring dynasties
  sometimes overlapped. Vishnukundina expansion eventually displaced the kingdom in the
  fifth century.
