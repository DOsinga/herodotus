description: >-
  The County of Luxembourg was a hereditary territory around Luxembourg City in the
  western Holy Roman Empire. Its origins lay in the castle acquired by Siegfried in 963;
  this record follows its separate government from 1250. Counts expanded their lands
  through inheritance, marriage, and conflict, ruling a region larger than today's
  country. The Luxembourg family gained exceptional influence after Henry VII became
  German king in 1308 and later emperor. Other relatives acquired Bohemia and the imperial
  crown, linking this small territory to wider dynastic ambitions. Luxembourg became a
  duchy in 1354. Absentee rulers often entrusted it to relatives or pledged it to
  creditors. Philip the Good of Burgundy seized Luxembourg in 1443, ending its separate
  government within the Burgundian lands.
