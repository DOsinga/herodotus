description: >-
  The Novgorod Republic governed a trading city and vast northern territories in medieval Russia from
  1136 to 1478. Its republican period conventionally begins with the dismissal of Prince Vsevolod,
  though its institutions developed gradually. The city assembly, or veche, selected officials and
  could invite or remove princes, while wealthy boyar families dominated political life. An archbishop
  also exercised important civic authority. Novgorod's merchants linked Baltic markets with inland
  routes, exporting furs and other northern products through contacts with Hanseatic traders. Its
  reach extended towards the White Sea and Urals, drawing tribute from diverse communities. Princes
  provided military leadership against neighbouring powers. Moscow's growing strength eventually
  overwhelmed the republic, and Ivan III abolished its assembly and imposed direct government in 1478.
