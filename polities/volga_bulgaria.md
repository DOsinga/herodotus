description: >-
  Volga Bulgaria was a kingdom around the meeting of the Volga and Kama rivers in today's
  European Russia, recorded from about 900 to 1236. Bulgar groups established a growing state
  centred on Bolghar, with native rulers governing even while acknowledging Khazar overlordship.
  Its inhabitants included several peoples, and river routes connected northern traders with
  Islamic markets farther south. In 922 ruler Almish received an embassy from the Abbasid caliph;
  Ibn Fadlan's account provides important evidence for the kingdom and its adoption of Islam.
  Farming, crafts, and commerce sustained substantial towns, including Bilyar and Suar. Relations
  with neighbouring Rus' principalities included trade and war. Mongol conquest in 1236 ended the
  kingdom's own government, although Bulgar communities and urban traditions survived afterward.
