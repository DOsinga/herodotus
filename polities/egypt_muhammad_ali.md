description: >-
  Egypt under Muhammad Ali and his successors was a hereditary state governed from Cairo between 1805 and the British occupation
  of 1882. Although formally subordinate to the Ottoman sultan, its rulers controlled domestic affairs and pursued military campaigns in Arabia,
  Sudan, Greece and Syria. Muhammad Ali strengthened central authority through land seizures, state monopolies, conscription and a reorganized army. New
  factories, schools and irrigation works supported his ambitions, while cotton became a source of revenue. European intervention limited expansion but
  secured hereditary rule for his family. Later rulers encouraged railway construction and the Suez Canal, accompanied by mounting debt and
  foreign financial supervision. Following the Urabi movement, British forces invaded in 1882 and effectively took control despite retaining Egyptian rulers.
