  Tuvalu is a Pacific island country formed from nine small islands and atolls, with its
  government on Funafuti. It became independent from Britain in 1978 after separating from
  the Gilbert and Ellice Islands colony. Its constitution combines parliamentary
  government with a monarchy: elected representatives choose the prime minister, while a
  governor general represents the sovereign. The islands have predominantly Polynesian
  traditions, with community life closely tied to councils, churches and extended
  families. Limited land and resources make fishing rights, overseas aid and remittances
  important sources of income. Tuvalu joined the United Nations in 2000. Its low elevation
  leaves it especially exposed to rising seas and extreme weather, giving the country a
  prominent voice in international discussions about climate and sovereignty.
