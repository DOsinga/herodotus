description: >-
  The Botai culture represents communities living in northern Kazakhstan approximately
  between 3700 and 3100 BCE. It takes its name from a settlement beside the Imanburlyq
  River, where excavations uncovered numerous sunken houses, pottery, stone tools, and
  abundant horse bones. Botai, Krasny Yar, and related sites indicate relatively permanent
  villages whose inhabitants depended heavily on horses for food. Enclosures, bone wear, and
  residues on pottery have prompted arguments for early horse keeping and milking, although
  interpretations remain disputed. Genetic research links Botai horses more closely with
  Przewalski's horses than with the main ancestry of modern domestic breeds. These findings
  illuminate a distinctive relationship between people and horses, not a documented kingdom.
  No written records identify its rulers, institutions, or political boundaries.
