description: >-
  The Principality of Kakheti governed part of eastern Georgia from 787 to 1010, emerging as Arab
  authority weakened. Its rulers used the title chorepiscopus, or chorbishop, combining a
  religious designation with princely political power. Grigol established the separate
  principality, whose centres included Ujarma and Tianeti. Mountain communities and fertile
  eastern valleys supported a state that competed with other Georgian rulers for influence over
  Kartli. Kakheti also confronted Hereti to the east and negotiated tributary arrangements with
  the caliphate without simply losing its own government. Its princes resisted the efforts to
  unite the Georgian lands under the Bagratids. Bagrat III conquered Kakheti in 1010,
  interrupting this separate rule. Renewed independence after 1014 belonged to the later kingdom
  of Kakheti and Hereti.
