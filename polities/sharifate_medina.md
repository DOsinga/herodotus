description: >-
  The Sharifate of Medina was an emirate centred on the holy city of Medina in the Hejaz, ruled
  by Husaynid sharifs, descendants of the Prophet through Ali and Husayn. Around 940, as
  Abbasid authority collapsed, Ubayd Allah ibn Tahir took power in the city, and his
  descendants, the Banu Muhanna, held it on and off for nearly six centuries. The emirate
  controlled little beyond the city, depended on grain from Egypt, and usually recognised the
  Fatimids, Ayyubids or Mamluks as overlords, while the sharifs themselves were Twelver Shia.
  Meccan sharifs seized Medina several times in the eleventh century. From the fourteenth
  century the Mamluk sultans chose the emirs and later sent a garrison, but the emirs still
  governed until the Ottoman conquest of 1517.
