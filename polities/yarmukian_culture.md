description: >-
  The Yarmukian culture belongs to the Pottery Neolithic of the southern Levant, represented here
  approximately between 6400 and 6000 BCE. Its name comes from the Yarmuk River near Sha'ar
  HaGolan, the principal excavation site in northern Israel. Distinctive pottery and clay
  figurines connect settlements across northern Israel and Jordan, with related finds reaching
  Lebanon and more southerly places. These objects define an archaeological culture, not a
  documented kingdom: no surviving texts identify rulers, political boundaries, or a common
  government. Female figurines are especially prominent among its remains, although their meaning
  is debated. Yarmukian communities existed alongside other regional pottery traditions. Their
  eventual replacement by later Neolithic assemblages records changing material practices, rather
  than a known conquest or single political collapse.
