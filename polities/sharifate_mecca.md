description: >-
  The Sharifate of Mecca governed the Islamic holy city and surrounding parts of the Hejaz
  from 967 to 1916. Its emirs were sharifs, claiming descent from Muhammad, whose religious
  standing helped sustain local authority over pilgrims, townspeople and nearby tribes.
  Different ruling families held the office before Qatadah's descendants established a
  lasting line around 1200. The sharifs acknowledged successive Fatimid, Ayyubid, Mamluk and
  Ottoman overlords, with their freedom changing as outside governors and troops intervened.
  Pilgrimage and Red Sea trade gave Mecca importance far beyond its territory. Ottoman rule
  eventually produced two overlapping sharifian and provincial administrations. In 1916,
  Sharif Hussein launched the Arab Revolt and proclaimed the Kingdom of Hejaz, changing the
  old emirate into a kingdom during wartime.
