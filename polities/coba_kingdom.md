description: >-
  Coba was a Maya kingdom in the eastern Yucatan Peninsula, in present Quintana Roo,
  Mexico. This record estimates its regional government from about 200 to 1100 CE,
  distinguishing political power from the site's longer settlement history.
  Inscriptions identify rulers, including women, while pyramids and ceremonial groups
  show the resources available to its court. A remarkable network of raised stone roads
  linked the city with surrounding settlements and Yaxuna. Coba maintained alliances
  and elite marriage connections with powerful Maya centers farther south, including
  Calakmul and Tikal. Competition from Chichen Itza gradually reduced its influence and
  control of important routes. Later buildings and religious activity demonstrate
  continued occupation, but do not establish that an independent royal court survived
  beyond the estimated end.
