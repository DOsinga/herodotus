  Ghana is a West African country on the Gulf of Guinea, independent since 1957, with
  Accra as its capital. It united the British Gold Coast, Ashanti, the Northern
  Territories, and British Togoland at independence. Kwame Nkrumah became its first prime
  minister and then president when Ghana adopted a republican constitution in 1960. His
  government promoted industrial development, African unity, and support for liberation
  movements, but increasingly concentrated political power. A military coup removed him in
  1966, beginning decades of alternating military and civilian governments. The
  constitution introduced in 1992 established the present multiparty presidential
  republic. Cocoa, gold, and later petroleum have remained important to its economy.
  Ghana's name recalls the medieval Ghana Empire, which lay farther northwest, not inside
  it.
