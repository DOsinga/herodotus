description: >-
  The Shulaveri–Shomu culture describes early farming communities in the southern
  Caucasus, especially the middle Kura basin and neighbouring valleys of present-day
  Georgia, Azerbaijan, and Armenia. Its dates here, approximately 6000 to 4000 BCE, are
  estimates rather than the lifespan of a single government. Excavated settlements contain
  circular or oval mudbrick houses, storage buildings, hearths, pottery, and stone tools.
  People cultivated cereals and legumes and kept sheep, goats, cattle, and pigs; hunting
  and fishing supplemented these foods. Evidence of canals shows early efforts to manage
  water for fields. Repeated rebuilding created settlement mounds, while differences
  between sites indicate local variation. Archaeology does not establish named rulers or a
  unified state. Later regional developments preceded the emergence of the Kura–Araxes
  cultural tradition.
