description: >-
  After the Ilkhanate collapsed in the 1330s, the town of Shaki in the southern foothills of the
  Greater Caucasus and its district became an independent principality. Its first rulers came
  from the Orlat, a Mongol tribe settled in the area, beginning with Sidi Ahmed Orlat. Timur
  captured Shaki in 1392 and killed its ruler Seyid Ali, but Seyid Ali's son Seyid Ahmed
  recovered the principality and joined Timur's later campaign together with the Shirvanshah
  Ibrahim I. From the middle of the fifteenth century Shaki was ruled by a Muslim family of
  Armenian origin, which kept its government when the Safavid Shah Ismail I imposed his
  suzerainty early in the sixteenth century. Shah Tahmasp ended the principality in 1551 and
  appointed a Qizilbash governor, making Shaki a Safavid province.
