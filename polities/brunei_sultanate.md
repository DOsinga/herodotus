description: >-
  The Bruneian sultanate was a Malay monarchy centred on Brunei Bay in northern Borneo,
  represented here from 1368 until British protection began in 1888. Islam became central
  to its royal identity, while the sultan and powerful nobles controlled river districts
  through crown, official, and traditional hereditary estates. Maritime trade connected
  its sheltered port with China and other Southeast Asian centres. Under rulers such as
  Bolkiah, Brunei extended influence along Borneo's coasts and maintained dynastic ties
  across the Sulu Sea. The extent and character of those relationships remain debated.
  Succession disputes and European expansion weakened the state, while Sarawak and North
  Borneo passed from its control. The 1888 protectorate preserved the monarchy but
  transferred its external relations to British authority thereafter.
