description: >-
  Barqa, the Arabic name for Cyrenaica in eastern Libya, lost much of its settled life in the
  mid-eleventh century when Bedouin tribes from Upper Egypt moved west in the Hilalian
  migrations. The Banu Hilal went on to Ifriqiya, while the Banu Sulaym stayed and took over
  the grazing lands of the Jebel Akhdar and the coast. This regional record covers the tribes
  that held the region afterwards. The Sa'adi tribes of Cyrenaica, split into the Harabi and
  Jabarina groups, descend from them, and each controlled its own section of the country; the
  Barghathi held the land around Benghazi. The Ayyubid commander Qaraqush and later the Mamluks
  claimed the region, but at most drew tribute through alliances with the tribes. No state
  governed Barqa directly until the Ottomans in the 1630s.
