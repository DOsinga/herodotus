  The Maldives is an island state spread across coral atolls in the Indian Ocean southwest of India
  and Sri Lanka. It became independent in 1965, ending a British protectorate that had controlled its
  external relations. The sultanate continued briefly before a republic was established in 1968. Malé
  is the capital and the centre of national administration, with a president heading government and an
  elected legislature. Fishing long supported island livelihoods, while tourism became a major source
  of income after independence. Political life has included prolonged presidential rule,
  constitutional reform, competitive elections, and disputes over power. Its low islands face serious
  threats from rising seas and environmental change. This record follows the continuing independent
  country across changes of regime and leadership.
