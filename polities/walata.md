description: >-
  Walata was a small Saharan town-state at the oasis of Oualata in southeastern Mauritania,
  recorded here as a chiefdom from 1433 to 1480 and from 1591 to 1912. Once a terminus of the
  trans-Saharan trade under Ghana and Mali, it declined as Timbuktu took over the caravan
  traffic. After Mali lost control in 1433, Walata was governed by its own prince; Leo
  Africanus described it as a small kingdom of a few villages whose prince paid tribute to the
  Songhai ruler. After Morocco destroyed the Songhai Empire in 1591, local Soninke families
  known as the Lemhajib dominated the town for a century and a half, before Hassaniya-speaking
  families took the lead. France occupied it in 1912.
