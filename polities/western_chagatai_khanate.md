description: >-
  The Western Chagatai Khanate was the Transoxianan successor to the divided Chagatai realm
  in Central Asia. From 1347, its khans supplied dynastic legitimacy while military chiefs
  exercised much of the real power. Amir Qazaghan dominated the western lands and installed
  figurehead rulers, but his assassination helped unleash competition among rival local
  military commanders. The region combined Mongol political traditions with the towns and
  farming districts around Samarkand, Bukhara and Qarshi. Tughlugh Timur of eastern
  Moghulistan conquered Transoxiana in 1360, temporarily reuniting the divided realm. After
  his death in 1363, Timur and Amir Husayn recovered control. Their alliance then collapsed,
  and Timur defeated Husayn in 1370. The resulting Timurid state retained nominal khans but
  rested on Timur's own authority and armies.
