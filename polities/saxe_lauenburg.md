description: >-
  Saxe-Lauenburg was a duchy on the lower Elbe, east of Hamburg, ruled by the northern branch of
  the Ascanian dukes of Saxony. It came into being when the Ascanian brothers divided the Duchy of
  Saxony in 1296, and the Lauenburg line lost the electoral dignity to Saxe-Wittenberg in 1356.
  The duchy was small, lying between Holstein, Mecklenburg and the Lüneburg lands, with its
  capital first at Lauenburg on the Elbe and from 1619 at Ratzeburg. Its dukes often pledged
  parts of their land to Lübeck and their neighbours, and the estates of nobles and towns gained
  a strong say in government. The duchy turned Lutheran in the sixteenth century. When the last
  Ascanian duke died in 1689, the Guelph dukes of Lüneburg-Celle seized it.
