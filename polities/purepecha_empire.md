description: >-
  The Purépecha Empire developed around Lake Pátzcuaro in western Mexico from about 1300 and lasted until 1530. Its rulers brought neighbouring communities under a central government whose principal seat became Tzintzuntzan. The king, often called the cazonci, governed through nobles and officials who collected tribute and managed conquered territories. Traditions preserved after the conquest credit Tariácuri and his successors with establishing its power. Expansion reached beyond present Michoacán, and fortified frontiers helped the empire resist repeated Aztec attacks. Its craftspeople were noted for metalworking, while religion supported royal authority. Spanish arrival initially left its ruling house in place under increasing pressure. In 1530, Nuño de Guzmán had the last king, Tangáxuan II, executed, bringing the territory under direct colonial control.
