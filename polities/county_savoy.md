description: >-
  The County of Savoy grew across the western Alps between the eleventh century and 1416. Its
  conventional beginning in 1003 follows Humbert I's comital rule, not a documented founding of
  all its later territories. His descendants expanded through marriages, inheritance, and
  warfare, holding lands on both sides of the mountains. Control of Alpine routes helped make the
  counts important intermediaries between France, Italy, and the empire. They governed their own
  domains despite their place within the Holy Roman Empire. Chambéry became a later
  administrative centre rather than the county's capital throughout its history. Acquisitions
  such as Nice and Geneva enlarged the state. In 1416 Emperor Sigismund raised Amadeus VIII to
  duke, continuing Savoy's government under a new rank and identity.
