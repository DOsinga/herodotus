  Angola is a republic on the Atlantic coast of southern Africa, independent from Portugal since
  1975, with its capital at Luanda. Independence followed an anticolonial war and Portugal's
  revolution, but competing liberation movements soon fought for power. The MPLA established the
  government, while UNITA and other opponents continued armed resistance, drawing in Cuban,
  Soviet, South African, and American support. Civil war lasted until 2002 and displaced millions
  of people. The state moved from a socialist one party system toward multiparty institutions,
  while retaining a strong presidency. Oil and diamonds have financed reconstruction but also
  deep inequalities. Portuguese remains the official language alongside many African languages.
  Cabinda, separated from the rest of Angola, forms an economically and politically important
  coastal exclave.
