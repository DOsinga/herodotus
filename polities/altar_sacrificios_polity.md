description: >-
  Altar de Sacrificios was a Maya centre near the meeting of the Pasión and Salinas rivers in
  present Guatemala. Its governing institutions are represented here from about 400 BCE to 950
  CE, not from the site's earliest settlement. River access made it a trading location, with
  imported jade, obsidian and marine objects found in burials. Pyramids, terraces, carved
  monuments and a ballcourt record its development through the Preclassic and Classic periods.
  An emblem glyph indicates a political identity, although its reading remains unknown and the
  city was not a dominant regional power in the Late Classic. Monument building and population
  declined before abandonment. Changing pottery and trading connections suggest new influences
  during its final occupation, without proving outside provincial rule.
