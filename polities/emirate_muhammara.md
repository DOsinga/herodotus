description: >-
  The Emirate of Muhammara was an autonomous Arab sheikhdom at the head of the Persian Gulf,
  centred on the port of Muhammara, today Khorramshahr in Iran. Its sheikhs came from the
  Muhaisin, a branch of the Banu Ka'b, who had dominated the plain of Khuzestan, then called
  Arabistan, since the eighteenth century. Sheikh Jabir built up Muhammara as a trading port in
  the middle of the nineteenth century, nominally under Qajar authority. His son Khaz'al, who
  seized power in 1897, governed his tribes, ports and customs without Persian interference and
  dealt directly with the British, who valued him as the protector of the oilfields and the
  Abadan refinery of the Anglo-Persian Oil Company. Reza Khan's army ended this autonomy in 1925,
  when Khaz'al was arrested and taken to Tehran.
