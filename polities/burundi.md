  Burundi is a landlocked country in Africa's Great Lakes region, independent from Belgian administration since 1962. It first restored national
  government as a constitutional monarchy, but a coup abolished the crown in 1966. Subsequent military and civilian presidents governed a
  republic deeply affected by conflict between Hutu and Tutsi communities. Mass killings and the civil war that began in 1993
  caused extensive deaths and displacement. Peace negotiations eventually produced a power sharing framework and elections, although political tensions and repression
  continued. Most people live in rural areas and depend on agriculture, with coffee an important export. Kirundi gives the population
  a shared language. Gitega became the political capital in 2019; Bujumbura, on Lake Tanganyika, remains the country's main economic centre.
