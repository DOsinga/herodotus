description: >-
  Johor emerged as a separate mainland state after the Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824 divided the old
  Johor-Riau realm, leaving the peninsula and Singapore in the British sphere. Real power lay with
  the temenggongs rather than Sultan Hussein's line. Temenggong Ibrahim opened the interior to
  Chinese gambier and pepper planters under the kangchu system, and a treaty of 1855 transferred
  authority over most of Johor to him. His son Abu Bakar founded Johor Bahru as the capital and
  modernised the administration, building a government that impressed British visitors. In 1885 a
  treaty with Britain recognised him as sultan but gave Britain control of Johor's foreign
  relations; a British general adviser followed in 1914.
