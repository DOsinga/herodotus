description: >-
  Katsina was a Hausa kingdom in what is now northern Nigeria, represented
  here from 1348 until its incorporation into the Sokoto Caliphate in 1807.
  Its ruler, the sarki, governed a walled trading centre and surrounding communities
  through the Hausa system of royal offices. Early origins are preserved in
  competing traditions, including stories of Kumayo and the rise of Muhammad Korau.
  Under Muslim rulers, Katsina became a centre of Islamic scholarship, attracting visitors
  from across the western Sudan. Caravans, leatherworking, and regional commerce supported its
  prosperity. Its kings fought Kano and dealt with the powers of Songhai
  and Bornu, sometimes paying tribute. The jihad associated with Usman dan Fodio
  displaced the Hausa dynasty and established a Fulani emirate under Sokoto's authority.
