description: >-
  The Taifa of Ceuta was a small Muslim state on the African side of the Strait of Gibraltar,
  centred on Ceuta and including Tangier. After the breakup of the caliphate of Córdoba both
  ports belonged to the Hammudid rulers of Málaga, who from 1036 governed them through
  officers drawn from the Barghawata, a Berber tribe known for its unorthodox religion. As
  Hammudid power collapsed in the 1050s, the Barghawata commander Saqqut, a former slave who
  had risen to lead the garrison, took power in his own name shortly before 1061. He was said
  to field 12,000 horsemen and allied with the taifa kings across the strait. The Almoravids
  took Tangier in 1077 and killed Saqqut, and his son lost Ceuta to them in 1084.
