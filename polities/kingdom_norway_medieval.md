description: >-
  The medieval Kingdom of Norway developed along Scandinavia's western coast,
  traditionally dated from Harald Fairhair's unification in 872. That beginning is
  uncertain, and royal control expanded gradually rather than through a single decisive
  event. Kings governed alongside regional assemblies, while Christianization
  strengthened links with the wider European church. Rival claimants fought prolonged
  civil wars before the monarchy consolidated in the thirteenth century. Bergen became a
  major royal and trading centre, and the crown extended its authority to North Atlantic
  possessions, including Iceland and Greenland. Magnus the Lawmender introduced a common
  national law code. The Black Death later reduced population and resources. Dynastic
  inheritance linked Norway with neighbouring monarchies, and in 1397 it entered the
  Kalmar Union with Denmark and Sweden.
