description: >-
  Sa Huỳnh was an archaeological culture of central and southern Vietnam, represented here
  from approximately 1000 BCE to 192 CE. Its sites include coastal settlements and
  cemeteries named after the discovery at Sa Huỳnh. Lidded burial jars, iron tools and
  weapons, glass beads and distinctive animal headed ear ornaments help identify its
  material remains. Imported stones and shared ornament styles connect these communities
  with Taiwan, the Philippines and other shores of Southeast Asia. Such evidence shows
  skilled craft production and maritime exchange, but does not reveal a unified state or
  named rulers. Scholars often connect Sa Huỳnh populations with the ancestors of the Cham.
  The estimated end marks the emergence of Champa, without implying every local community
  changed at once.
