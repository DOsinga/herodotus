name: "Sipan Polity"
description: >-
  Sipan was a royal centre in the Lambayeque valley of northern Peru, represented here from about 100
  to 650 CE. Its adobe platforms and rich burials reveal a local Moche court rather than a government
  covering every Moche community. The Lord of Sipan's tomb contained elaborate gold, silver, and
  copper ornaments, ceremonial equipment, and sacrificed companions. These finds connect rulers with
  religious ceremonies depicted in Moche art and show the skilled work supporting elite display.
  Marked adobe bricks suggest organized labour contributions to monumental construction. Archaeology
  provides much stronger evidence for these rulers than for ordinary residents or administrative
  boundaries. The ending date estimates regional political change around Pampa Grande, not a
  documented conquest or a precisely dated royal succession.
