description: >-
  The Herodian Kingdom ruled Judea and neighbouring lands from Jerusalem between 37 and 4
  BCE. Herod the Great secured the throne with Roman support after defeating the last
  Hasmonean king. Although dependent on Roman approval, he maintained a royal government,
  army and considerable freedom within his territory. His lands included Samaria, Galilee,
  Idumea and districts east of the Jordan. Herod reshaped the region through ambitious
  building projects, including the enlarged Jerusalem Temple, the port of Caesarea and
  fortified palaces such as Masada. His court combined Jewish religious responsibilities
  with ties to the wider Greek and Roman world. Family disputes and ruthless succession
  politics marked his final years. After his death, Augustus divided the kingdom among
  Archelaus, Antipas and Philip instead.
