description: >-
  The Kidarite Kingdom ruled Bactria and neighbouring parts of Central Asia during the fourth
  and fifth centuries, with Balkh representing its centre here. Its beginning around 320 and
  ending around 467 are estimates drawn from difficult written and coin evidence. Named after
  the ruler Kidara, the dynasty belonged to the groups often called Huns or Chionites in
  ancient sources. Its kings adopted Kushan royal titles and issued coins drawing on Kushan
  and Sasanian models, reflecting the political traditions they inherited. Their territories
  connected Iranian lands with routes toward India, where related rulers also held power.
  Competition with the Sasanians and advancing Hephthalites progressively weakened the
  kingdom. Sasanian victory over its remaining Bactrian stronghold around 467 marks this
  record's political end.
