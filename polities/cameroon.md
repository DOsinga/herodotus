description: >-
  Cameroon became independent in 1960 when French administration ended, with Yaoundé as its capital. Southern Cameroons, formerly administered by Britain, joined in 1961, creating a federation of French and English speaking territories. President Ahmadou Ahidjo centralized power and established a single party system; a 1972 referendum replaced the federation with a unitary republic. Paul Biya succeeded him in 1982. Multiparty politics later returned, but the presidency retained extensive authority. The country spans coastal forests, highlands, savanna, and the Lake Chad region, bringing many languages and communities within one state. Disputes over the loss of anglophone autonomy have produced prolonged conflict. Oil, agriculture, and the port of Douala support its economy. Cameroon continues as a presidential republic with a centralized government.
