  Fiji became independent from Britain in 1970, forming a Pacific island state with its
  capital at Suva. Most people live on Viti Levu and Vanua Levu within a larger
  archipelago. Independence established a parliamentary government under a
  constitutional monarchy, but military coups in 1987 replaced the monarchy with a
  republic. Further coups in 2000 and 2006 interrupted civilian government and reshaped
  constitutional arrangements. Relations between Indigenous Fijians and descendants of
  Indian indentured workers have been central to these political disputes, alongside
  questions of land, chiefly authority, and equal citizenship. Sugar production and
  tourism have played major economic roles. Despite repeated changes of regime, Fiji has
  continued as one independent state, participating in regional diplomacy and
  contributing troops to international peacekeeping.
