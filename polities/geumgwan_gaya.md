description: >-
  Geumgwan Gaya was a kingdom around modern Gimhae, near the mouth of Korea's Nakdong River. Tradition dates its foundation by
  King Suro to 42 CE, but this is a legendary account rather than a securely established historical beginning. Archaeology shows
  a later developing society with rich burials, iron production, and connections across the sea to Japan. Geumgwan was an important
  individual Gaya state, not a government ruling every community grouped under that name. Its royal house drew wealth from river
  access and trade, while competing with neighbouring Korean powers. Pressure from Silla gradually reduced its position. In 532, the last
  king surrendered to Silla, and his family entered the Sillan aristocracy. Their descendants included the celebrated general Kim Yu sin.
