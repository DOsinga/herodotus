  Cuba is an island republic in the Caribbean, with its capital at Havana. It became
  independent in 1902 after Spanish rule and an American occupation. The United States
  retained extensive powers of intervention, and a second occupation interrupted Cuban
  government between 1906 and 1909. Sugar exports, foreign investment and struggles over
  political power shaped the early republic. Governments alternated between electoral rule
  and dictatorship, culminating in Fulgencio Batista's regime. The revolution of 1959
  brought Fidel Castro to power and transformed Cuba into a socialist state led by the
  Communist Party. Alignment with the Soviet Union and confrontation with the United
  States made it central to Cold War politics. Despite major changes of regime, this
  record follows the continuing Cuban state.
