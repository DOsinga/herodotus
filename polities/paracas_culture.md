description: >-
  Paracas culture developed on Peru's southern coast, especially in the Pisco, Ica, and
  Chincha valleys, approximately between 800 and 100 BCE. It is known through settlements,
  ceremonial buildings, pottery, and well preserved burial textiles rather than a documented
  kingdom. Communities farmed irrigated land and fished coastal waters, while ritual
  gatherings connected people from different valleys and the highlands. Elaborate
  embroidered cloth wrapped the dead in funerary bundles, revealing skilled production and
  differences in status. Burials also provide evidence of skull shaping and surgery. Painted
  ceramics and shared religious imagery help archaeologists trace relationships across the
  region. No single government can be securely assigned to the entire culture. Its later
  transformations contributed to Nazca traditions, with substantial continuity amid regional
  changes.
