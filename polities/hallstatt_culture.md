description: >-
  The Hallstatt culture names an early Iron Age archaeological horizon across central
  Europe, approximately from 800 to 450 BCE. Its name comes from the Austrian settlement and
  cemetery associated with extensive salt mining. Most communities depended on farming, but
  metalworking and trade linked centres with Mediterranean societies. Rich burials
  containing weapons, wagons and imported vessels reveal powerful elites, while fortified
  settlements such as Heuneburg show centres of production and exchange. These finds do not
  establish one state or a common government across the whole region. Eastern and western
  groups differed in burial practices and material styles. Some centres declined or were
  abandoned near the end of the period. The later La Tène culture followed, developing new
  artistic and social traditions.
