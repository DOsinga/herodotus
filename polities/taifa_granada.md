description: >-
  The Taifa of Granada was a Muslim kingdom in southern Iberia, ruled by a branch of the Zirid family from 1013 to 1090. It emerged during the breakup of the Cordoban caliphate, when Zawi ibn Ziri and his Berber followers established themselves around Granada. The monarchy strengthened the city as a capital and at times controlled Málaga. Its rulers balanced warfare, alliances and payments to neighbouring powers in a fragmented Andalusian political landscape. The Jewish scholar Samuel ha-Nagid became a minister and military leader, while the massacre of 1066 exposed the precarious position of the Jewish community. Almoravid conquest removed the Zirid ruler in 1090. This kingdom was distinct from both the Zirids of Ifriqiya and the later Nasrid emirate.
