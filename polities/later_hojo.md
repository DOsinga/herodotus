description: >-
  The Later Hōjō were the Sengoku daimyo house that ruled the Kantō plain of eastern Japan
  from their castle at Odawara, from 1493 to 1590. Their founder Ise Shinkurō, later called
  Hōjō Sōun, was a minor shogunal official who came east to help his nephew in the Imagawa
  succession. He seized Izu in 1493 and Odawara around 1495, and conquered Sagami by 1516. His
  son Ujitsuna took the Hōjō name, captured Edo in 1524 and pushed into Musashi, and
  Ujiyasu defeated the Uesugi at Kawagoe in 1546. The house ruled through land surveys,
  regular taxes and a large body of retainers, and fought the Takeda and Uesugi for decades.
  Toyotomi Hideyoshi besieged Odawara in 1590 and ended their rule.
