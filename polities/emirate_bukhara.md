description: >-
  The Emirate of Bukhara was a Central Asian monarchy ruled by the Manghit family from 1785 until 1920. Its capital was Bukhara, a major center of Islamic scholarship and commerce, and its territory included fertile river valleys and mountain districts across present Uzbekistan and Tajikistan. Emirs governed through officials and provincial governors, with Sunni Islamic institutions central to public life. Persian served administration and literary culture, alongside Turkic languages. Rivalry with Khiva and Kokand shaped regional politics before Russian expansion changed the balance. Defeat in 1868 cost Bukhara Samarkand and other lands; a Russian protectorate followed in 1873, though the emir retained domestic rule. Red Army intervention overthrew the last emir in 1920, establishing the Bukharan People's Soviet Republic afterward.
