description: >-
  The Ubaid culture describes farming communities in Mesopotamia, represented here from
  about 6500 to 4000 BCE. Its name comes from Tell al Ubaid in southern Iraq, while
  Eridu provides an sequence of buildings and settlement remains. Painted pottery, large
  houses, clay figurines and stamp seals connect sites across a wide area, including
  northern Mesopotamia and the Persian Gulf coast. People cultivated crops, kept
  livestock and used river and marsh resources. Some buildings interpreted as temples
  suggest growing communal institutions, and differences between settlements indicate
  increasing social complexity. These remains do not establish a single kingdom, named
  rulers or uniform government. Regional developments varied considerably. The later
  Uruk period followed, bringing larger urban centres and further changes in production
  and organisation.
