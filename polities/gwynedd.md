description: >-
  Gwynedd was a Welsh kingdom based in the northwest, including Anglesey and the mountains of
  Snowdonia. It emerged around 500, with Maelgwn securely known in the sixth century, while traditions
  traced its founders to Cunedda and his descendants. Kings and later princes governed through courts
  and local officials; Aberffraw became a principal royal seat rather than a permanent capital
  throughout its history. Its rulers repeatedly competed for supremacy in Wales and resisted English
  and Norman expansion. Llywelyn the Great strengthened the principality in the thirteenth century,
  and his grandson Llywelyn ap Gruffudd obtained recognition as Prince of Wales. Edward I's campaigns
  reduced their lands and power. After Llywelyn's death, Dafydd's capture in 1283 ended the princely
  government and English conquest.
