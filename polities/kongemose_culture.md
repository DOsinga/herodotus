description: >-
  The Kongemose culture describes hunter gatherer communities in southern Scandinavia, especially
  Denmark and southern Sweden, between approximately 6000 and 5200 BCE. It takes its name from a
  settlement in western Zealand, uncovered during drainage work in the twentieth century.
  Archaeologists identify it through distinctive flint blades and rhombic arrowheads, together
  with scrapers, drills and tools made from bone or antler. Small stone blades were fitted into
  bone daggers, sometimes decorated with geometric designs. Communities hunted deer and wild boar
  and fished from many small coastal settlements. The evidence reveals ways of living and making
  tools, not a documented kingdom or shared government. Kongemose followed the Maglemosian
  tradition and preceded Ertebølle, marking changes within the longer Mesolithic settlement
  history of Scandinavia.
