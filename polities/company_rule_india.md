description: >-
  Company rule in India was the government of expanding territories by the British East
  India Company between 1757 and 1858. Victory at Plassey began its political ascendancy
  in Bengal, followed by revenue rights granted after further military success. A
  commercial corporation developed armies, courts and a civil administration, with
  Calcutta as its governmental centre. Governors general directed policy under
  increasing oversight from Britain. Wars against Mysore, the Marathas and the Sikhs
  extended its territory, while treaties subordinated many surviving Indian rulers.
  Revenue demands and changes in landholding reshaped rural life. The Company also
  introduced educational reforms, railways and telegraphs. The rebellion of 1857 exposed
  deep opposition to its rule. In 1858, Britain's government assumed direct
  administration, ending the Company's authority.
