description: >-
  Western Xia was a Tangut-led state in northwestern China from 1038 to 1227, centred
  on Xingqing, present Yinchuan. Its emperors governed the Yellow River region and
  important stretches of the Hexi Corridor, placing the kingdom across routes
  connecting China with Central Asia. The court developed a distinctive Tangut script
  while drawing on Chinese institutions and Buddhist traditions. Monasteries,
  translated scriptures, surviving manuscripts, and monumental royal tombs reveal a
  rich culture whose records were badly damaged by conquest. Western Xia alternately
  fought and negotiated with the Song, Liao, and Jin states, using its military
  strength and frontier position to survive between larger neighbours. Mongol campaigns
  first forced submission and later destroyed the kingdom, ending its separate
  government in the year 1227.
