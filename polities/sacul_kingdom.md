description: >-
  The Kingdom of Sacul occupied a mountainous part of southeastern Peten in modern Guatemala,
  near routes linking Maya lowland cities with the upper Mopan valley. Its royal government is
  estimated here from around 700 to 900, rather than inferred from every phase of settlement.
  Sculpted monuments establish the presence of King Ch'iyel, whose investiture in 760 involved
  a visit by a ruler of Ucanal. Sacul used its own emblem glyph, identifying a distinct royal
  court. After defeat by Ixkun in 779, relations changed and the kings later appear as allies.
  Plazas, pyramids and a ballcourt reveal a ceremonial centre. Its last dated stela belongs
  to 800; later occupation and construction do not establish how long the royal government
  continued there.
