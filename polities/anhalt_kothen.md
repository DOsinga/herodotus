description: >-
  Anhalt-Köthen was a small German state centred on Köthen, governed separately from 1603 to
  1847. This period began when the sons of Joachim Ernest divided the reunited Anhalt lands,
  giving Köthen to Prince Louis. Members of the Ascanian house ruled it as a hereditary
  principality within the Holy Roman Empire. Court culture became especially notable under Prince
  Leopold, who employed Johann Sebastian Bach as his music director. The state became a duchy in
  1806 and passed through the Napoleonic Confederation of the Rhine before joining the German
  Confederation. Changes of ruling branch and minority regencies shaped its succession without
  ending its identity. When Duke Henry died without an heir in 1847, the territory passed to
  Anhalt-Dessau under the Ascanian line.
