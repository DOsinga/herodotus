description: >-
  The Lusatian culture describes communities across much of Poland and neighboring parts of central Europe during the later Bronze Age and early Iron Age, approximately 1300 to 500 BCE. Archaeologists identify it through settlements, pottery, metalwork, and burial practices rather than records of a single government. Cremated remains were commonly placed in urns within extensive cemeteries. Farming and livestock supported villages, while fortified settlements such as Biskupin show coordinated construction using timber ramparts and closely arranged houses. Bronze tools, weapons, ornaments, and imported objects reveal craft production and wider exchange networks. Neither a shared language nor one ruling authority can be securely assigned to these remains. Regional changes, settlement abandonment, and contacts with steppe groups accompanied the culture's eventual transformation.
