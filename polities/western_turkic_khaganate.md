description: >-
  The Western Turkic Khaganate ruled much of Central Asia from the division of the first
  Turkic empire in 603 until 657. Its Ashina rulers led a confederation of steppe peoples
  and exercised influence over the trading cities of Sogdia. Suyab, in the Chu valley of
  present Kyrgyzstan, became a principal royal seat. The confederation was known as the
  Ten Arrows, with divisions grouped into Dulu and Nushibi factions. Tong Yabgu brought
  the state to its greatest strength, but his murder in 630 opened repeated struggles for
  the throne. Tang China exploited these divisions while conquering neighbouring oasis
  states. In 657, Su Dingfang defeated Ashina Helu, ending the independent government;
  later khagans appointed by the Tang did not restore its power.
