  Kuwait is a Gulf state whose independent government dates from the end of British
  protection in 1961. The ruling Al Sabah family presides over an emirate centred on Kuwait
  City. A constitution adopted in 1962 established a national assembly alongside substantial
  powers retained by the emir. Oil transformed a trading and pearling port into a wealthy
  state, supporting public services and attracting a migrant workforce. Iraq invaded in
  August 1990, displaced the government, and claimed the territory. A United States led
  coalition expelled Iraqi forces in 1991, restoring Kuwaiti rule after the occupation
  represented by the gap here. Reconstruction followed extensive destruction, including
  burning oil wells. Relations between the ruling family and elected politicians have
  repeatedly shaped disputes over power.
