description: >-
  The Yangshao culture describes Neolithic farming communities along China's middle Yellow
  River and nearby valleys, approximately 5000 to 3000 BCE. It is an archaeological culture,
  not a documented kingdom with named rulers. Villages such as Banpo and Jiangzhai reveal
  houses, storage pits, kilns, cemeteries, and sometimes surrounding ditches. People grew
  millet, raised pigs and dogs, and supplemented farming with hunting and fishing. Painted
  pottery, often decorated with geometric designs or animal motifs, is among its most
  recognizable remains. Settlement sizes and burial differences changed over time,
  suggesting developing social distinctions without proving one shared government. Its name
  comes from the Yangshao site in Henan, excavated in 1921. Later regional traditions,
  including Longshan, followed as settlement patterns and material culture changed.
