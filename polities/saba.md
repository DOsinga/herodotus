description: >-
  Saba was an ancient South Arabian kingdom centered on Marib in present day Yemen. Its
  beginning around 800 BCE is approximate, while inscriptions provide unusually rich
  evidence for its rulers, temples, warfare, and public works. Early leaders used the
  title mukarrib, associated with joining communities together; later rulers adopted
  royal titles. Irrigation, including the great Marib dam, supported agriculture beside
  the desert. Sabaean merchants participated in the valuable frankincense and myrrh
  trade linking Arabia with distant markets. The kingdom competed with neighboring
  South Arabian states and experienced changing fortunes over many centuries. Biblical
  and later religious traditions about the Queen of Sheba should not be treated as
  straightforward contemporary records. Himyar's final conquest around 275 ended Saba's
  separate royal government.
