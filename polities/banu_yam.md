description: >-
  The Banu Yam are a tribe of the Hamdan confederation whose homeland is the oasis of Najran in
  southwestern Arabia, on the edge of the Empty Quarter. They combined farming villages in the
  Najran valley with a large Bedouin section. Najran is well documented in the late ninth and
  tenth centuries, when the first Zaydi imams of Sa'da tried to control it, but after the early
  eleventh century it almost vanishes from the written record for six hundred years. This
  record treats the Yam, who had become Isma'ili Shia in the time of the Sulayhid dynasty, as
  the holders of the oasis in those years. In the seventeenth century they backed the Makrami
  da'is, Isma'ili religious leaders who settled among them and founded the Principality of
  Najran in 1633.
