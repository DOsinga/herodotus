description: >-
  Si Thep was an urban political centre in the Pa Sak valley of present Thailand,
  active here from approximately 500 to 1300. Its moats, large enclosed settlement,
  religious buildings, and royal inscriptions indicate a court rather than merely an
  isolated village. A seventh-century inscription records a king ascending the throne,
  although the full sequence of rulers remains unknown. The city developed through
  Dvaravati and later Khmer cultural phases, drawing on connections with India and
  neighbouring Southeast Asian centres. Hindu images, Buddhist monuments, and
  substantial brick structures show changing religious patronage. These artistic
  influences do not by themselves prove direct foreign rule. Si Thep eventually
  declined and was abandoned around the thirteenth century, as other centres gained
  importance across central Thailand.
