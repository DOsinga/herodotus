description: >-
  The Bahmani Sultanate ruled much of India's Deccan plateau after a rebellion against Delhi in
  1347. Its founder, Alauddin Bahman Shah, established a Muslim monarchy at Gulbarga; later
  sultans moved their court to Bidar. Persian court culture mixed with local languages and
  traditions, while immigrants and established Deccani nobles competed for office. The kingdom
  repeatedly fought Vijayanagara over frontier lands and resources, and its ministers organized
  provinces governed by powerful commanders. Mahmud Gawan expanded the realm and supported
  learning, but his execution in 1481 deepened political divisions. Provincial rulers asserted
  independence in 1490, the endpoint used here for effective unified government. Bahmani titles
  survived longer under increasingly powerless sultans, while successor states continued many of
  its cultural and administrative traditions.
