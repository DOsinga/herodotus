description: >-
  Strasbourg governed itself as an imperial city in Alsace from 1262, when its citizens defeated their
  bishop at Hausbergen, until French annexation in 1681. Its position near the Rhine supported trade
  and made it strategically important between powers. Municipal government developed beyond the
  leading families, with craft guilds gaining a role after the political upheaval of 1332. The city
  became a center of printing, humanist learning and Protestant reform, associated with Martin Bucer
  and a period of residence by John Calvin. This prosperity existed alongside persecution, including
  the massacre of Jewish residents in 1349. Strasbourg remained neutral during the Thirty Years' War
  and retained its autonomy afterward. Louis XIV's forces surrounded the city in 1681, compelling
  surrender and French control.
