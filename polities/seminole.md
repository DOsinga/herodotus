description: >-
  The Seminole were a Native American people who formed in Spanish Florida during the eighteenth
  century from Creek and other refugees who moved into lands emptied of their earlier inhabitants.
  Lower Creeks from Georgia, Hitchiti-speaking Mikasuki, Yuchi, Yamasee and others settled the old
  Apalachee fields around Tallahassee, whose name means old town, and spread down the peninsula.
  They lived in independent towns led by their own chiefs and councils, held the Green Corn
  ceremony and herded cattle, and gave refuge to people escaping slavery in the southern colonies.
  Red Stick refugees from the Creek War of 1813 to 1814 enlarged their numbers. In 1817 and 1818
  Andrew Jackson burned their towns around Tallahassee, and Spain ceded Florida to the United
  States in 1821.
