name: "Nala Kingdom of Pushkari"
description: >-
  The Nala Kingdom of Pushkari ruled parts of present day Chhattisgarh and Odisha,
  especially Bastar and Koraput, around the fifth and sixth centuries CE. The dates 450
  to 550 are estimates based on coins and inscriptions whose chronology remains
  disputed. Its capital Pushkari is tentatively identified with Garhdhanora. Royal
  inscriptions name Arthapati, Bhavadatta, and Skandavarman, while gold coins suggest
  additional rulers. These kings issued land grants and supported Hindu religious
  traditions. Bhavadatta apparently expanded into Vidarbha, perhaps occupying the
  Vakataka capital Nandivardhana, before suffering reverses. Pushkari was damaged during
  struggles with neighbouring powers, and Skandavarman claimed to restore it. Vakataka
  and Chalukya attacks contributed to decline, but no single securely dated conquest
  explains the final disappearance of this royal state.
