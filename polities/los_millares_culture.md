description: >-
  The Los Millares culture belonged to Copper Age southeastern Iberia, represented here between approximately 3200 and 2300 BCE. Its type site near modern Almería contains houses, substantial stone defences, outlying forts, and a large cemetery of collective tombs. Farming remained central, but copper smelting and metalworking distinguish the settlement's economy. Decorated pottery, stone idols, and burial goods show shared cultural practices and connections with broader prehistoric exchange networks. Differences between tombs suggest social inequality, while the scale of construction implies organised communal labour. These remains do not reveal named rulers or establish a known state, so the record represents an archaeological culture. Dates and the relationship between settlement decline and wider cultural change remain approximate; the Argaric culture followed regionally.
