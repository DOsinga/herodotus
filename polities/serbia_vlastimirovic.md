description: >-
  The early Principality of Serbia was a Balkan state ruled by the Vlastimirović family from
  approximately 780, interrupted by Bulgarian occupation between 924 and 933. Much of its
  history survives through the Byzantine work De Administrando Imperio, leaving its earliest
  rulers and territorial limits uncertain. Princes governed inland communities and navigated
  pressure from Bulgaria and Byzantium through alliances, wars and dynastic contests.
  Vlastimir resisted Bulgarian attacks, while Christianity became established under his
  successors during the ninth century. Bulgarian intervention repeatedly changed rulers
  before Simeon imposed direct control in 924. Časlav restored the principality and expanded
  its influence after returning from Bulgaria. Around 970, Byzantine administration replaced
  native government. Ras represents a later regional center, not a proven permanent early
  capital.
