  Panama is a presidential republic on the narrow land bridge between Central and South America,
  with its capital at Panama City. It separated from Colombia in 1903 with United States support.
  A treaty then gave the United States control of a canal zone across the country, and the Panama
  Canal opened in 1914. The waterway made international shipping central to Panama's economy and
  politics. Military leaders dominated government after the 1968 coup, including Omar Torrijos
  and Manuel Noriega. The United States invasion of 1989 removed Noriega, after which elected
  civilian government resumed. Treaties negotiated in 1977 led to full Panamanian control of the
  canal at the end of 1999. The republic continues with an elected president and a National
  Assembly.
