description: >-
  The Bana kingdom was a royal community in southern India, spanning parts of the northern
  Tamil and Andhra borderlands from approximately 350 to 921. Its rulers claimed descent
  from the legendary Mahabali and his son Bana, using that ancestry to express their status.
  Inscriptions attest the family from the fourth century, though the earliest chronology and
  changing territorial boundaries remain uncertain. Gudimallam represents one court; Kolar
  and other centres also served different rulers. Bana kings navigated relationships with
  Pallavas, Gangas, Nolambas, and other neighbours, sometimes acknowledging powers while
  maintaining local government. Chola expansion under Parantaka destroyed their royal
  governments around 921. Later Bana families appear as chiefs and governors elsewhere, but
  those careers were not a continuation of this kingdom.
