description: >-
  Corinth was a Greek city-state on the narrow isthmus linking the Peloponnese with
  mainland Greece, represented here from 700 to 338 BCE. Access to ports on both sides
  supported trade, shipbuilding, and a wide network of overseas settlements, including
  Syracuse and Corcyra. Government passed from the aristocratic Bacchiad clan to the
  tyrants Cypselus and Periander, then back to oligarchic rule. Corinthian pottery
  circulated widely, reflecting the city's commercial and artistic importance. The
  fortified height of Acrocorinth protected the city and commanded movement across the
  isthmus. Corinth often allied with Sparta, but its rivalries also contributed to wider
  Greek wars. After Philip II's victory at Chaeronea in 338 BCE, a Macedonian garrison
  occupied Acrocorinth and ended the city's independent political control.
