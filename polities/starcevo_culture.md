description: >-
  The Starčevo culture represents early farming communities across the inland Balkans, with an
  estimated span from 6200 to 4500 BCE. It takes its name from a site on the Danube opposite
  Belgrade in present Serbia. Settlements and material remains trace the northward spread of
  agriculture and pottery from communities connected with Anatolia and Greece. Its pottery
  includes coarse vessels as well as painted and fluted forms; bone spatulas are another
  distinctive find. Related traditions in neighbouring regions are often grouped as
  Starčevo–Criş. Archaeological and genetic evidence illuminates movement, local contacts and
  changing settlement patterns, but does not establish a single state or named rulers.
  Regional developments gradually gave way to cultures such as Vinča and contributed to
  farming's European spread.
