name: "Western Ganga Kingdom"
description: >-
  The Western Ganga Kingdom governed southern Karnataka in India from approximately 350 to
  1004. Its first rulers were based around Kolar, later making Talakad on the Kaveri River
  their seat. Hereditary kings administered a region commonly called Gangavadi, supported by
  local officers, landholders, and military retainers. The kingdom retained its native
  government while acknowledging stronger Chalukya and Rashtrakuta rulers, often supplying
  troops for their wars. Ganga patronage helped Kannada and Sanskrit literature flourish and
  supported Jain institutions alongside Hindu traditions. The minister and commander
  Chavundaraya commissioned the great Bahubali statue at Shravanabelagola, one of its best
  known legacies. As Rashtrakuta power collapsed, renewed Chola expansion threatened the
  kingdom. The Chola conquest of Gangavadi in 1004 ended its separate royal government.
