description: >-
  The Ilkhanate was a Mongol-ruled state centred on Iran, governing much of Iraq and neighbouring regions from the later thirteenth century until 1335. It grew from Hulegu's western conquests and became a distinct realm during the fragmentation of the Mongol Empire. Its khans combined Mongol military leadership with Persian administrators and adopted Iranian traditions of kingship. Capitals included Maragha, Tabriz and Soltaniyeh. Campaigns against the Mamluks sought control of Syria but failed to secure lasting conquest. Ghazan's conversion in 1295 made Islam the state religion and accompanied reforms in taxation and administration. Court patronage supported historical writing, architecture and exchange across Eurasia. Abu Said's death without a heir in 1335 broke central authority, leaving competing commanders and regional successor states.
