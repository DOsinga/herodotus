description: >-
  The Kingdom of Kartli ruled eastern Georgia around Tbilisi from 1478 to 1762,
  emerging as the united Georgian monarchy broke apart. Kings of the Bagrationi house
  shared power with nobles and governed a Orthodox Christian population. Its position
  between larger empires made warfare, tribute, and negotiated submission recurring
  features of political life. Iranian rulers usually claimed overlordship, while
  Ottoman armies occupied the region at several points. Some kings adopted Islam and
  Persian court practices without ending support for Georgian churches. Rostom's
  seventeenth century reign encouraged trade and recovery after devastation. Following
  Nader Shah's death in 1747, Teimuraz II asserted greater independence. When he died
  in 1762, his son Heraclius II, already king of Kakheti, united both kingdoms through
  dynastic succession.
