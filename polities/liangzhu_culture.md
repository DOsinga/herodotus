description: >-
  The Liangzhu culture flourished in China's lower Yangtze region approximately between
  3300 and 2300 BCE. Its principal centre lay near modern Hangzhou, within a landscape of
  wetlands, waterways and rice fields. Archaeologists have uncovered city walls, raised
  platforms, dams and elaborate cemeteries, showing substantial coordination of labour and
  pronounced social differences. Finely worked jade objects, especially cong tubes and bi
  discs, distinguish elite burials and suggest shared ritual traditions across a wider
  region. These remains support arguments for an early state society, but they do not
  reveal named kings or the exact structure of government. Liangzhu is therefore
  represented here as an archaeological culture. Flooding and changing environmental
  conditions may have contributed to its disappearance around the estimated end date.
