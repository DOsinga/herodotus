name: "Chasséen culture"
description: >-
  The Chasséen culture was an archaeological tradition of Neolithic France, dated approximately
  4500 to 3500 BCE. Named after finds near Chassey-le-Camp in Burgundy, it spread across southern
  regions and into northern river basins and plateaux. These were farming and herding
  communities, not a known kingdom with rulers. Excavated villages, pottery, stone tools, and
  animal remains reveal settled life in small groups. Their ceramics were lightly decorated, and
  flint working was well developed before metal tools became common. At Bercy beside the Seine,
  preserved wooden canoes, bows, and other objects show activities usually lost from
  archaeological records. Regional differences and uneven dating make its boundaries approximate.
  Later Neolithic traditions replaced it in different areas rather than through one documented
  political collapse.
