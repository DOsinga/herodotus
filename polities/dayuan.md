description: >-
  Dayuan, the Great Yuan, is the name Chinese historians gave to the kingdom of the Ferghana
  valley in Central Asia, whose chief city was probably Alexandria Eschate, today's Khujand. Its
  townspeople descended partly from Greek settlers planted by the Persians and by Alexander the
  Great, and it seems to have belonged to the Greco-Bactrian kingdom until the migrations of the
  Yuezhi and Saka cut it off around 160 BCE. Zhang Qian described its walled cities and famous
  horses around 130 BCE. When Dayuan refused to trade its horses, the Han dynasty sent armies
  that besieged its capital and forced it to accept a new king in 101 BCE. Later Chinese
  histories describe the Ferghana kingdom under other names, ruled by its own kings and often
  subject to steppe powers.
