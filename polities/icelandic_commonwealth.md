description: >-
  The Icelandic Commonwealth was the self-governing society of Iceland from the founding of the
  Althing in 930 until submission to the Norwegian crown in 1262. Settlers from Norway and the
  British Isles had occupied the island from the late ninth century. Iceland had no king:
  chieftains called goðar held their offices as property, led local assemblies and sat in the
  Althing, which met each summer at Þingvellir to make law and settle disputes. A lawspeaker
  recited the law. Iceland accepted Christianity at the Althing around 1000. The sagas and much
  Old Norse literature were written down in this society. In the thirteenth century great families
  fought for power, and in 1262–1264 the chieftains swore allegiance to King Haakon IV of Norway.
