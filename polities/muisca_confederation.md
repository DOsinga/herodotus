description: >-
  The Muisca Confederation record represents late preconquest political life in the
  highlands of present Colombia, from about 1450 to 1537. Muisca communities occupied the
  Bogotá plateau and neighboring valleys, farming maize and other crops and trading salt,
  textiles, and precious materials. Leadership rested with chiefs and alliances among
  local communities, not a single emperor ruling a unified state. Bacatá's zipa and
  Hunza's zaque were prominent rulers, while Duitama and Sogamoso formed other independent
  centres. Gold offerings and ceremonies associated with lakes helped inspire European
  stories of El Dorado. Spanish forces under Gonzalo Jiménez de Quesada invaded in 1537
  and took major centres. The singular name simplifies several regional confederations;
  conquest and the destruction of their leadership continued beyond that year.
