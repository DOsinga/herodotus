description: >-
  Cardial Ware culture describes early farming communities around the Mediterranean, identified especially by pottery decorated with impressions from cockle shells.
  Its dates are estimated here from about 6400 to 5000 BCE, covering the wider spread of related impressed ceramics rather
  than one political entity. Early examples occur near Greece and the Adriatic; the tradition later reached Italy, southern France, Iberia,
  and parts of North Africa. Coastal movement helped carry farming practices westwards, alongside contact with existing hunter gatherers. Settlements, cave
  deposits, pottery, and human remains provide the evidence, but do not reveal a single government or common set of rulers.
  Regional ceramic styles overlapped and changed over time. The representative western Mediterranean point marks this archaeological horizon, not a capital.
