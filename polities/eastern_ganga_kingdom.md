description: >-
  The Eastern Ganga Kingdom ruled Kalinga on India's eastern coast, beginning conventionally
  in 493 and ending in 1434. Its earliest chronology is uncertain, and the kingdom expanded
  greatly over its long history. Early rulers used Dantapura and Kalinganagara; later kings
  governed from Kataka, now Cuttack, across Odisha and neighbouring regions. This was a
  hereditary monarchy whose rulers supported Hindu temples, linked royal authority with
  religious patronage and maintained armies against rival regional powers. Anantavarman
  Chodaganga enlarged the kingdom, while later rulers defended it against northern
  invasions. The dynasty is especially remembered for the Jagannath temple at Puri and the
  Sun Temple at Konark. Its principal kingdom ended when Kapilendra established the Gajapati
  state, although related Ganga ruling lines survived elsewhere.
