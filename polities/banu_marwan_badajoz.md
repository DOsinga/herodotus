description: >-
  The Banu Marwan of Badajoz were a Muwallad family, local converts to Islam from the
  northwest of the peninsula, who ruled the lower Guadiana valley from Badajoz between 875 and
  930. Their founder, Abd al-Rahman ibn Marwan al-Jilliqi, rebelled against the emir Muhammad
  I in 868. After a long resistance he was allowed to settle at Badajoz, which he began to
  fortify around 875. With help from Alfonso III of Asturias he defeated the emir's army, and
  he went on to dominate much of the western part of al-Andalus with his ally Sadun al-
  Surunbaqi. His descendants kept Badajoz independent of Córdoba for decades. Abd al-Rahman
  III ended their rule when he took the city in 930, as part of his campaign against the rebel
  lordships.
