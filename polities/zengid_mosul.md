description: >-
  The Zengid Emirate of Mosul ruled northern Mesopotamian lands from Mosul after Zengi's
  death in 1146 divided his dominions. His son Sayf al-Din Ghazi inherited this eastern
  branch, while Nur al-Din governed the Syrian territories separately. The ruling family
  had risen through military service under the Seljuks and maintained a Sunni Muslim
  court supported by soldiers and officials. Mosul remained a commercial and artistic
  centre, especially known for metal vessels. Its rulers resisted Saladin's attempts to
  take the city. Their treaty of 1186 required military support for the Ayyubids but
  preserved Zengid government. During the final reigns, the former slave and regent Badr
  al-Din Lu'lu' exercised increasing power. He replaced the last Zengid ruler in 1234,
  establishing his own regime.
