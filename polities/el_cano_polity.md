description: >-
  El Caño was a chiefly centre in Panama's Coclé region, represented here by its elite
  funerary complex between approximately 750 and 1000. Excavations near the Río Grande reveal furnished graves,
  stone monuments, and evidence of a ranked society within the wider Gran Coclé culture. Gold
  breastplates, bracelets, pendants, and other ornaments distinguished high-status people, including individuals interpreted as warrior chiefs.
  Some burials contained many accompanying bodies, suggesting funerary sacrifice or the burial of attendants. These
  finds indicate concentrated wealth and authority without revealing the exact workings of government. Nearby Sitio
  Conte also housed chiefly families, but their relationship remains uncertain. Earlier settlement and later habitation
  at El Caño cannot alone show that the same political community persisted throughout those centuries.
