description: >-
  The Ayyubid Emirate of Homs was the smallest of the principalities into which Saladin's
  family divided Syria. In 1179 Saladin gave Homs to the heirs of his uncle Shirkuh, and
  their line kept it for most of the next century, facing the Crusader County of Tripoli
  and the Hospitallers of Krak des Chevaliers. The emirs owed loyalty to the Ayyubid sultans
  but ran their own affairs and often changed sides in family wars. Al-Mansur Ibrahim
  briefly made Homs important, winning victories over the Khwarazmians in the 1240s. His son
  al-Ashraf Musa lost the city to an-Nasir Yusuf of Aleppo in 1248, regained it under the
  Mongols in 1260 and kept it as a Mamluk vassal. When he died without heirs in 1262, Homs
  passed to the Mamluk Sultanate.
