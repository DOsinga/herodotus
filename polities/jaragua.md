description: >-
  Jaragua was the largest of the Taíno chiefdoms of Hispaniola, covering the southwest of
  the island in present-day Haiti and the Dominican Republic. It had grown from the union of
  two earlier chiefdoms, and its cacique Behechío ruled from a town near present Léogâne,
  with twenty-six lesser districts under him. Jaragua avoided the early fighting with the
  Spanish and agreed in 1497 to pay tribute in cotton and cassava bread. After Behechío's
  death his sister Anacaona, the widow of Caonabo, led the chiefdom. In 1503 the governor
  Nicolás de Ovando visited her court, seized the gathered caciques at a feast and burned
  them in the house where they met. Anacaona was hanged, and the Spanish founded a town
  there the next year.
