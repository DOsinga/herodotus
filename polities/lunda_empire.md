description: >-
  The Lunda Empire was a central African monarchy and network of related states, centred
  on Musumba in the southern Congo region. Its beginnings are conventionally placed around
  1665, although Nawej, ruling from about 1695, is the first securely attested ruler. The
  paramount monarch held the title Mwaant Yaav, supported by court offices and succession
  traditions that linked each ruler to predecessors. Expansion and migration spread Lunda
  political practices into present-day Angola and Zambia, while provincial leaders
  retained considerable local authority. Agriculture, tribute, and long-distance trade
  sustained the royal centre. Nineteenth-century upheaval, especially Chokwe expansion,
  weakened the empire. Chokwe victories broke the government by 1887, while colonial
  partition later divided its territory; Lunda royal titles and cultural institutions
  survived these changes.
