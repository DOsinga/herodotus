description: >-
  Yayoi culture describes agricultural communities across much of the Japanese
  archipelago, represented here approximately from 300 BCE to 300 CE. It is an
  archaeological category, not one kingdom or a single ethnic population, and
  its starting date remains debated. Wet rice cultivation, simpler pottery,
  metalworking, weaving, and new settlement forms spread from northern Kyushu
  through contacts with the Asian mainland. Migrants mixed with existing Jōmon
  communities, while farming supported population growth and increasing social
  differences. Yoshinogari's enclosed settlements and burial remains provide a
  representative center. Fortifications and weapons suggest conflict among
  communities. Chinese accounts describe local rulers and, later, Himiko's
  Yamatai, rather than a unified Japan. The following Kofun period saw larger
  political formations associated with Yamato and monumental tombs.
