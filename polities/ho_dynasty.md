description: >-
  The Hồ dynasty ruled northern Vietnam as Đại Ngu after Hồ Quý Ly displaced the Trần
  monarchy in 1400. Its court occupied the fortified capital of Tây Đô, in present Thanh
  Hóa province. Quý Ly soon passed the imperial throne to his son Hồ Hán Thương, while
  retaining influence as retired emperor. Their government promoted written Vietnamese,
  introduced paper money and pursued reforms of landholding, education and administration.
  Military campaigns gained territory from Champa, but resistance from Trần supporters
  weakened the new regime. Relations with Ming China deteriorated over the dynasty's
  legitimacy and a claimant to the former throne. The Ming invasion began in 1406 and
  brought the short monarchy to an end, replacing Vietnamese imperial government with
  Chinese rule.
