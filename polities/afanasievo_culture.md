description: >-
  The Afanasievo culture represents early pastoral communities in the Altai and
  southern Siberia, dated here approximately from 3300 to 2500 BCE. Related sites
  also extend into Mongolia and Dzungaria. Archaeologists identify it through
  pottery, stone and bone tools, metal ornaments, and burials enclosed by stone
  arrangements. People raised cattle, sheep, and goats, helping establish
  livestock husbandry in regions previously occupied mainly by hunters and
  gatherers. Graves contain individuals or small groups rather than evidence for a
  known royal dynasty. Genetic studies connect these communities with Yamnaya
  populations far to the west, supporting an eastward migration across the steppe.
  Proposed links with later Tocharian languages remain interpretations, not
  written testimony. Its archaeological remains reveal no identifiable state or
  documented governing institutions.
