description: >-
  The Gour kingdom was the largest of the small Hindu kingdoms of the Sylhet region, in what is
  now northeastern Bangladesh, before the Muslim conquest. Its traditions trace it to an
  offshoot of the Jaintia royal house in the seventh century, but its early kings are
  legendary, and firm history starts with the copper plates of the eleventh and twelfth
  centuries, after the Kamarupa kingdom lost its hold on the region. Its kings patronised
  Brahmins and Hindu temples in a land with many Buddhists and animists, and kept war boats on
  the wide marshes of the Surma plain. It split around 1170 and reunited.
  Its last king, Gour Govinda, lost Sylhet in 1303 to an army of Shamsuddin Firuz Shah of
  Lakhnauti, remembered alongside the Sufi saint Shah Jalal.
