description: >-
  Cholula was a urban and religious center in the Puebla Valley of central Mexico. This
  record follows its local government from an estimated 100 CE to the Spanish intervention of
  1519, rather than treating every archaeological phase as a different state. Its Great Pyramid grew
  through repeated construction and became one of the largest monuments in the Americas. Fertile farmland,
  pottery, textiles, and routes linking the Gulf coast with inland and Pacific regions supported the
  city. Ruling elites and alliances changed over its long history, while pilgrimage and worship, especially
  associated with Quetzalcoatl, sustained its wider importance. Hernán Cortés and his allies massacred inhabitants in
  1519 and replaced the leadership, bringing Cholula's independent government to an end under Spanish power.
