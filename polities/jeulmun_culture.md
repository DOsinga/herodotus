description: >-
  The Jeulmun pottery period describes communities on the Korean Peninsula from roughly 8000
  to 1500 BCE. It is an archaeological period, not a known kingdom or unified government.
  Its name refers to pottery decorated with comb-like patterns, although regional styles
  varied. Coastal settlements such as Dongsam-dong preserve shell middens, fishing
  equipment, stone tools, and evidence of exchange with neighbouring regions. People
  combined fishing, hunting, and gathering with changing forms of plant cultivation. Millet
  farming became more important in some places, but subsistence remained varied across the
  peninsula. Settlements and pottery trace these changes without revealing rulers or clear
  state institutions. Late Jeulmun communities increasingly occupied inland areas. The
  period gradually gave way to the Mumun tradition and expanding agricultural settlements.
