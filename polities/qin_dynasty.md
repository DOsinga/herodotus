description: >-
  The Qin dynasty ruled China's first unified imperial state from 221 to 206 BCE, with its capital at Xianyang. King Zheng of Qin conquered the rival kingdoms and took the title First Emperor. His government replaced hereditary regional rulers with officials responsible to the court. It standardised writing, currency, weights and measures, and expanded roads and frontier defences. Large labour demands supported palaces, canals and the emperor's monumental tomb, guarded by the terracotta army. Strict laws and organisation helped the state impose its authority but also generated resentment. After the emperor died in 210 BCE, court struggles weakened his successors. Rebellions spread, and Ziying surrendered to Liu Bang in 206. Qin's short rule nevertheless established institutions inherited by later emperors.
