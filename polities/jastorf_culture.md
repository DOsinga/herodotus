description: >-
  The Jastorf culture describes Iron Age communities in northern Germany, southern
  Scandinavia and adjoining areas of Poland, approximately from 600 BCE to the end of the
  first century BCE. Its name comes from a cemetery near Jastorf in Lower Saxony.
  Archaeologists recognise it chiefly through cremation graves, urnfields, pottery and
  ornaments rather than written accounts of government. Burial goods are often modest, and
  weapons are uncommon in its cemeteries. Earlier northern Bronze Age traditions continued
  alongside contacts with Hallstatt and later La Tène communities farther south. Regional
  groups differed, showing that shared objects did not necessarily mean political unity. The
  culture is often associated with Germanic populations, although archaeological patterns
  alone cannot establish their languages or a single ethnic identity.
