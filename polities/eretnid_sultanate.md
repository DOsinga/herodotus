description: >-
  The Eretnid Sultanate governed much of central and eastern Anatolia from 1343 to 1381,
  with major centres at Sivas and Kayseri. Its founder Eretna was a commander of Uyghur
  origin who rose through the administration of the Mongol Ilkhanate. As Ilkhanid power
  collapsed, he balanced relations with competing Mongol leaders and the Mamluks before
  asserting his own authority. His victory over a Chobanid army in 1343 secured his
  position as an independent sultan. The state combined Mongol political traditions with
  Muslim kingship and local administrative networks. Eretna's successors faced disputed
  accessions, powerful officials and neighbouring rivals. The judge and minister Burhan
  al-Din increasingly controlled government, then took the throne in 1381, replacing the
  Eretnid dynasty with his own ruling house.
