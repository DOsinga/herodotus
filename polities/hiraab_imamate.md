description: >-
  The Hiraab Imamate was a Somali state of the Hiraab clans of the Hawiye, governed by imams of
  the Yacquub line, that succeeded the Ajuran Sultanate in central and southern Somalia. Tradition
  tells how the Darandoolle clan rose against Ajuran rulers who had neglected Islamic law and
  imposed heavy taxes, killing the Ajuran imam at a well. By 1700 the Hiraab held a wide territory
  from the Shabelle valley to Mudug and along the coast from Mogadishu towards Hobyo, and the imam
  resided in Mogadishu. The imamate repelled a French landing in 1701 and kept Europeans away from
  the coast. Its government rested on clan elders and the religious standing of the imams.
  Zanzibar's garrisoning of Mogadishu in 1871 ended its hold on the city.
