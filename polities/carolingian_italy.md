description: >-
  The early medieval Kingdom of Italy governed much of northern and central Italy from Pavia
  after the Carolingian partition of 855. Louis II inherited the Italian realm and imperial title,
  continuing institutions shaped by earlier Lombard and Frankish rule. Kings relied on bishops, counts, and
  powerful regional families, whose competing ambitions weakened royal authority. After the Carolingian line faltered, rival
  claimants including Berengar, Guy, Hugh, and their successors struggled for the crown. The kingdom also
  faced Magyar raids and intervention from rulers beyond the Alps. Otto I's campaigns broke Berengar
  II's power: Pavia fell in 961, while Berengar continued resistance at San Leo until capture
  in 963. Later imperial claims to Italy are distinct from this kingdom's recorded governing period.
