description: >-
  The Rinpungpa were a ruling family whose regime dominated Tsang in Tibet from 1435 to 1565. Rising from the estate of Rinpung, they used kinship with the Phagmodrupa dynasty, military victories and local alliances to build their own power. Norzang established control around Shigatse, and Donyo Dorje later extended their influence across much of central Tibet. Their leaders were secular lords closely tied to Buddhist institutions, especially the Karma Kagyu school, whose monasteries and religious art received patronage. Around 1500 they approached wider Tibetan dominance without creating lasting unity. Rival regional rulers and religious networks limited their authority as Phagmodrupa influence revived. In 1565 their former subordinate Karma Tseten defeated them, founding the Tsangpa regime that replaced their regional dominance.
