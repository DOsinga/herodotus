description: >-
  Villanovan culture describes the early Iron Age phase of Etruscan society in Italy, rather than
  a unified kingdom. This record covers approximately 900 to 750 BCE before the separately
  recorded Etruscan city states. Its name comes from a cemetery discovered at Villanova near
  Bologna, though related remains extend through Tuscany, Lazio and other regions. Cremated
  remains were often placed in distinctive pottery urns shaped like two joined cones. Weapons,
  ornaments and weaving tools reveal skilled metalworking and household activities. Settlement
  remains include huts supported by wooden posts. Increasing differences between graves suggest
  developing social ranks, while imported goods show Mediterranean connections. The culture
  developed into later Etruscan urban societies; its archaeological label does not identify a
  government shared across Italy.
