description: >-
  Poverty Point culture developed in the lower Mississippi region approximately between 1700 and
  1100 BCE. Its best-known centre, in northeastern Louisiana, contains earth mounds and curved
  ridges surrounding an open plaza. The builders were hunter-gatherers whose food came from
  rivers, wetlands, and surrounding forests, rather than a farming state. Stone objects and other
  materials brought from distant regions reveal exchange networks. Large earthworks show
  organized labour, but archaeology does not identify kings, officials, or a single government
  ruling every associated settlement. The site's use as a residential, trading, or ceremonial
  centre remains debated. Clay cooking objects, tools, and ornaments provide a richer picture
  than monuments alone. Its abandonment ended this principal centre, while later communities
  reused parts of the landscape.
