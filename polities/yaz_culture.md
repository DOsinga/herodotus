description: >-
  The Yaz culture represents early Iron Age communities in Margiana, Bactria, and neighbouring parts of Central Asia, here dated approximately 1500 to 550 BCE. Named after Yaz Depe in present-day Turkmenistan, it followed the Bronze Age settlements of the Bactria–Margiana complex. Rural communities clustered around fortified buildings and maintained irrigation systems that supported farming in dry landscapes. Early painted pottery was commonly handmade; later phases increasingly used the potter's wheel and iron tools. Archaeologists have found little burial evidence, prompting suggestions of practices involving exposure of the dead, though religious identification remains uncertain. Fortifications and buildings imply organised communities, not proof of one state or known kings. This record ends with Achaemenid expansion, while material traditions continued under Persian rule.
