description: >-
  Okjeo was a society of village chiefdoms on the coastal plains of northeastern Korea,
  centred on the Hamhung plain in what is now South Hamgyong. Chinese sources describe about
  five thousand households with no supreme ruler, each village led by its own hereditary
  chief, speaking a language close to that of Goguryeo and farming the fertile strip between
  mountains and sea. Okjeo was dominated in turn by Gojoseon, by the Han commanderies
  of Xuantu and Lelang, and by Goguryeo. This record covers the years after 30 CE, when the
  Han abolished the eastern division of Lelang and left the Okjeo chiefs to govern themselves
  under Han titles. King Taejo of Goguryeo made Okjeo a tributary in 56, after which Goguryeo
  officials supervised its chiefs and collected its produce.
