description: >-
  The early Deva kingdom ruled Samatata in southeastern Bengal, chiefly within present Bangladesh, during an estimated period from 720 to
  860. Its Buddhist kings governed from Devaparvata, usually identified with the Mainamati area near Comilla. They succeeded the Khadga rulers
  and belonged to a regional court distinct from the much later Hindu Deva dynasty. Inscriptions and coins provide the main
  evidence for their sequence, beginning with Santideva; some chronologies place its rise closer to 740. Royal support helped make Mainamati
  a religious and administrative centre. Monasteries, temples and associated ruins, including Shalban Vihara, show the scale of Buddhist patronage. The
  court formed part of Bengal's riverine and coastal world. Later Buddhist Chandra rulers continued Samatata's importance under a different dynasty.
