description: >-
  The Panduvamshi kingdom governed Dakshina Kosala in central India, around present
  Chhattisgarh, with its royal centre probably at Shripura, now Sirpur. This record places
  the Sirpur line approximately between 600 and 790, although its chronology remains debated
  because inscriptions usually give regnal years rather than fixed calendar dates.
  Hereditary kings claimed descent from the legendary Pandavas and the lunar lineage, using
  these traditions to support royal standing. Their grants and inscriptions document land
  donations, religious patronage and relations with neighbouring powers. Tivaradeva and
  Mahashivagupta Balarjuna are rulers in the surviving evidence. Sirpur's temples and
  Buddhist remains show varied religious life under royal protection. Connections with the
  earlier Mekala family and later Somavamshi rulers are possible but have not been
  established.
