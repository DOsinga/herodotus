  Jordan is a Hashemite monarchy in western Asia, centred on Amman and the lands east of
  the Jordan River. It became independent in 1946, when British-protected Transjordan
  was raised to a kingdom under Abdullah I. The royal family retained a leading role
  under the constitution adopted in 1952, alongside a cabinet and parliament. Jordan
  gained the West Bank during the 1948 war and annexed it, but lost control to Israel in
  1967 and relinquished its claim in 1988. Palestinian displacement, regional wars, and
  later refugee arrivals have strongly shaped its population and politics. King Hussein
  ruled through decades of regional upheaval. A peace treaty with Israel followed in
  1994. The kingdom has continued under his son despite limited natural resources.
