description: >-
  San Lorenzo was an early Olmec centre in the Gulf lowlands of present-day Veracruz, Mexico,
  flourishing approximately 1400 to 1000 BCE in this record. Its leaders organised earthworks and the
  movement of large basalt blocks from distant sources. Sculptors turned this stone into colossal heads,
  thrones, and other monuments associated with elite authority. Farming communities in the surrounding river landscape
  supported the centre, while exchanged materials linked it with places beyond the lowlands. Archaeology suggests
  a strongly ranked society, although the names of its rulers and its precise political arrangements
  are unknown. San Lorenzo lost its leading position as other centres, especially La Venta, became
  more prominent. Its monuments and settlement remains preserve evidence of early Olmec power and craftsmanship.
