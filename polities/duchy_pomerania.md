description: >-
  The Duchy of Pomerania lay along the southern Baltic coast in what are now Germany and Poland.
  Its history here begins with Wartislaw I in 1121 and ends with Swedish occupation in 1630.
  Dukes of the Griffin family governed the territory, initially under Polish overlordship and
  later within the Holy Roman Empire. Christian missions, German settlement, and growing towns
  changed the region during the medieval centuries. Stettin and Wolgast became important centres
  of branches that repeatedly divided and reunited the duchy. Coastal towns participated in
  Baltic commerce, while the dukes struggled with Brandenburg and neighbours. The Reformation
  brought Lutheran institutions. Sweden intervened during the Thirty Years' War; after the last
  duke died, the lands were divided between Sweden and Brandenburg.
