description: >-
  The Beylik of Hamid was a Turkish principality around Eğirdir and Isparta in southwestern Anatolia,
  lasting from about 1300 until 1391. It emerged as the Seljuk Sultanate of Rum weakened
  and frontier leaders established their own hereditary governments. Dündar Bey built the dynasty's position from
  a family of earlier Seljuk frontier rulers. Its rulers, called beys, controlled towns and surrounding
  agricultural lands in a region increasingly contested by neighboring Turkish principalities. A related southern branch
  formed the Beylik of Teke around Antalya, rather than remaining the same government. Ottoman expansion
  reduced Hamid's room for action; some districts, including Akşehir and Beyşehir, were sold to Murad
  I. Bayezid I annexed the remaining principality in 1391, incorporating its lands into Ottoman administration.
