description: >-
  Nim Li Punit was a Maya royal centre in the foothills of the Maya Mountains in
  southern Belize, represented here from approximately 500 to 850 CE. Its name means Big
  Hat and refers to a ruler's elaborate headdress carved on a monument. Royal imagery
  and titles indicate a local court, although relationships with nearby kingdoms remain
  debated. Plazas, terraces, pyramids, a ballcourt, and tombs formed the ceremonial
  core. Fertile soils and mountain streams supported settlement, while imported obsidian
  demonstrates connections with sources in present day Guatemala. Dated stelae
  especially illuminate the eighth century, and a carved jade pendant adds evidence of
  royal ritual. Occupation ended during the wider ninth century Maya upheavals; the
  precise reasons and abandonment date are uncertain.
