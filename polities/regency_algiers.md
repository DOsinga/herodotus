description: >-
  The Regency of Algiers governed much of present day northern Algeria from its
  Mediterranean capital. This record begins in 1659, when locally chosen military leaders
  displaced the power of Ottoman appointed pashas. Aghas initially ruled, followed by deys
  from 1671, supported by a military establishment, councils and provincial beys. The
  regency retained ties with the Ottoman sultan while directing its own diplomacy and wars.
  Corsair activity, maritime trade, taxation and agricultural production sustained its
  economy. Captives and demands for tribute brought repeated conflict with European powers
  and the United States. Its inland authority varied across communities and regions rather
  than covering the modern country uniformly. A French invasion captured Algiers in 1830,
  ending the regency and beginning French colonial conquest.
