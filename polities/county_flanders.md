description: >-
  The County of Flanders developed on the North Sea coast from Baldwin I's grant in 862.
  Its counts governed their own lands while holding much of the county as a French fief,
  with additional possessions inside the empire. Bruges, Ghent, and Ypres grew rich
  through commerce and cloth production, drawing English wool into an increasingly urban
  economy. Town governments and merchant interests often challenged the counts, while
  French kings sought tighter control. Philip IV annexed Flanders in 1300, but the Flemish
  victory at Courtrai in 1302 restored local government. Later treaties reduced its
  territory without ending the county. In 1384 its inheritance passed to Philip the Bold,
  connecting Flanders to Burgundy and forming a foundation for the wider Burgundian state.
