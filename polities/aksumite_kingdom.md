description: >-
  Aksum was a monarchy centred in the northern Ethiopian and Eritrean highlands, emerging
  around the first century and surviving into the tenth. Its kings linked inland farming
  communities with Red Sea commerce through ports such as Adulis. Coins, inscriptions,
  monumental stone stelae and imported goods reveal a wealthy kingdom connected with
  Mediterranean and Indian Ocean trade. King Ezana adopted Christianity in the fourth
  century, establishing a lasting religious tradition. Aksum also intervened across the Red
  Sea and conquered Himyar in the sixth century. Later changes in trade and regional power
  weakened its coastal reach, but inland government continued after the royal residence moved
  away from Aksum. Its poorly documented collapse around 960 is conventionally associated
  with the attacks of Gudit.
