description: >-
  Uaxactun was a Maya kingdom in the forests of present northern Guatemala, close to the
  larger centre of Tikal. Its royal government is estimated here from 300 to 900 CE, with
  the first surviving dated stela erected in 328. Temples, palaces, carved monuments and
  elite burials show a court embedded in the political and ritual life of the Classic Maya
  lowlands. The intervention associated with Siyaj K'ak' in 378 linked Uaxactun closely to
  Tikal and Teotihuacan, but local elites continued building temples, commissioning
  monuments and maintaining privileged burials. A later pause in monument production did not
  necessarily end its government. Construction and population recovered during the Late
  Classic period, before decline and abandonment followed its last dated monument in 889.
