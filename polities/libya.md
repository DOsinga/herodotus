  Libya is a North African state extending from the Mediterranean coast into the
  Sahara, independent since 1951. It united Tripolitania, Cyrenaica, and Fezzan after
  Italian colonial rule and wartime Allied administration. Idris I initially ruled a
  constitutional monarchy, supported by the Senussi religious movement. Oil
  discoveries transformed the economy and increased the state's resources. Muammar
  Gaddafi overthrew the monarchy in 1969 and built an authoritarian government, later
  presented as rule through popular committees. His long rule ended during the 2011
  uprising and civil war, supported by international military intervention. Subsequent
  rival governments, armed groups, and foreign involvement have prevented a settled
  political order. This record follows the country's continuity across those regime
  changes, with Tripoli marking its principal administrative centre.
