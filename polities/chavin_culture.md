description: >-
  Chavín culture developed in Peru's northern Andean highlands and spread religious and
  artistic influences toward the coast during the first millennium BCE. Its approximate
  dates here are 900 to 200 BCE. The main ceremonial center, Chavín de Huántar, stood where
  mountain routes connected different ecological zones. Stone temples contained galleries,
  ventilation shafts and drainage channels, while plazas provided spaces for ceremonies.
  Sculptures combined human, feline, bird and serpent features in a distinctive religious
  imagery also found on pottery and other objects. Farming, irrigation, camelid herding,
  textiles and metalworking supported these communities. Archaeology suggests organized
  labor and powerful religious specialists, but does not establish one documented state or
  royal dynasty. The culture's influence declined as other regional traditions developed
  across Peru.
