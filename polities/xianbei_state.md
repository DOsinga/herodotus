description: >-
  The Xianbei state represents successive loose unions of pastoral peoples north of Han China during
  the second and early third centuries. Around 156, Tanshihuai brought many Xianbei groups under his
  leadership from a base near Mount Danhan. He divided their lands into three sections governed by
  subordinate chiefs and led repeated raids against China's northern frontier. A major Han
  expedition failed in 177, confirming the strength of his mounted forces. This was a loose
  confederation, however, rather than a tightly administered territorial kingdom. After Tanshihuai
  died around 181, succession disputes broke its unity. Kebineng later gathered another powerful
  following and interacted with the Chinese state of Wei. His murder in 235 ended that revival; the
  precise political boundaries remain uncertain.
