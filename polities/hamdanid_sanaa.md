description: >-
  The Hamdanid Sultanate of Sanaa was a state in the northern highlands of Yemen, ruled from
  1099 to 1174 by three successive lines of the Hamdan tribe. When the Sulayhid regent Saba'
  ibn Ahmad died in 1098, the tribal chief Hatim ibn al-Ghashim took Sanaa and the title of
  sultan. His sons followed him, then the al-Qubaib line from 1116, and from 1139 a second
  Hatimid line under Hamid al-Dawla Hatim, who entered the city with Hamdani horsemen. The
  sultans were Ismailis of the Hafizi branch, loyal to the Fatimids of Egypt, and fought the
  revived Zaydi imams of Saada. In 1173 Ali ibn Hatim joined the Zurayids of Aden to defeat the
  Mahdids, but the Ayyubid army of Turan Shah took Sanaa in August 1174.
