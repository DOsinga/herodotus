description: >-
  The Nordic Late Neolithic describes farming societies in southern Scandinavia, approximately 2350 to
  1700 BCE, not a single kingdom or documented government. Jutland is a representative point for a
  wider archaeological horizon. Elaborate flint daggers, often shaped after imported metal weapons,
  give this period its description as the Dagger Period. Their manufacture required skilled
  craftworkers, and burial contexts show that served as objects of status rather than ordinary tools.
  Farming intensified and communities maintained exchange connections across the Baltic and with
  continental Europe. Bell Beaker influences were especially visible in Jutland, while regional
  traditions continued elsewhere. Archaeologists reconstruct these changes from settlements, graves,
  and artifacts. The gradual spread of bronze technology led into the Nordic Bronze Age around 1700
  BCE.
