description: >-
  The Prince-Bishopric of Utrecht was an ecclesiastical principality in the medieval Low Countries, centered on Utrecht in today's Netherlands. This record covers its separate government from 1250 until 1528. Its bishop exercised both spiritual authority and temporal rule, although the diocese extended beyond the lands he actually governed. The territory included the Nedersticht around Utrecht and the more distant Oversticht to the northeast. Cathedral chapters, towns, and nobles influenced politics and resisted episcopal ambitions. Conflicts with Holland and Guelders, together with disputed successions, weakened the bishop's position. Burgundian and Habsburg rulers increasingly intervened in appointments and regional struggles. In 1528 Bishop Henry surrendered his temporal authority to Charles V. The lands became Habsburg lordships, while the bishop's religious office continued.
