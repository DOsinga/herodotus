description: >-
  The Maghsharen were a Tuareg group of the Niger bend, credited by tradition with founding
  Timbuktu as a seasonal camp, who dominated the city in the middle of the fifteenth century. As
  the Mali Empire weakened, the Tuareg under their chief Akil took control of Timbuktu around
  1433. Akil left the running of the city to its own governor, the Timbuktu-koi, and to its
  scholars, while his nomads took a share of the revenues and raided the surrounding country.
  Their rule was resented by the townspeople, and a quarrel between Akil and the Timbuktu-koi led
  the governor to invite Sunni Ali of Songhai to intervene. Sunni Ali took the city in 1468, and
  the Tuareg withdrew into the desert.
