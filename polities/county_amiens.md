description: >-
  The County of Amiens was a feudal county centred on the city of Amiens in Picardy, recorded
  here from 945 to 1077 and again from 1085 to 1118. Walter I, who also held the Vexin and
  later Valois, became count in 945, and his descendants ruled Amiens together with those
  lands for more than a century. When Count Simon of Valois became a monk in 1077, his lands
  were divided and Amiens reverted to King Philip I. The lords of Boves, led by Enguerrand I,
  took the county around 1085. Their violent rule drove the citizens to form a commune, which
  King Louis VI supported against Enguerrand's son Thomas of Marle. Ralph I of Vermandois then
  took Amiens, around 1118.
