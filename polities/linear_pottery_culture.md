description: >-
  The Linear Pottery culture was an archaeological tradition of early farming communities across
  central Europe, approximately from 5500 to 4500 BCE. Its name comes from the bands of incised
  decoration on pottery vessels. Communities spread from the middle Danube into the Rhine and
  Elbe valleys and adjoining regions, bringing cultivated cereals and domestic livestock. Timber
  longhouses housed households within villages, often on fertile soils suited to cultivation.
  Tools, burials, and exchanged materials reveal varied connections between settlements. Some
  sites show enclosures and violent deaths, but these do not establish a single state or shared
  government. Regional differences developed over time, and several later cultures replaced its
  pottery traditions. The central German marker represents this broad archaeological
  distribution, not a capital.
