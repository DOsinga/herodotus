description: >-
  Nakhon Lamphun was a small tributary state of Siam in northern Thailand, centred on the old
  Haripunchai capital of Lamphun on the Kuang River south of Chiang Mai. The town had been
  emptied in the wars between Burma and Siam and was resettled under Chiang Mai. In 1814
  Bangkok made it a separate state under Khamfan, one of the seven princes of the Chet Ton
  dynasty, and his descendants ruled it from then on. Like its neighbours Chiang Mai and
  Lampang, Lamphun had its own ruler, viceroy, courts and levies, sent tribute to Bangkok
  and contributed men to campaigns against the Burmese. The Siamese reforms of the late
  nineteenth century brought commissioners from Bangkok, and in 1899 Lamphun was absorbed into Monthon Phayap.
