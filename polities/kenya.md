description: >-
  Kenya gained independence from Britain in 1963 and became a republic the following year. Centered on Nairobi, the East African state includes Indian Ocean coastal communities, highland farming regions, and dry northern lands. Independence followed nationalist organizing and the violent colonial response to the Mau Mau uprising. Jomo Kenyatta led the first government, followed by Daniel arap Moi, under whom one-party rule became entrenched. Multiparty politics returned in the early 1990s, but contested elections and ethnic rivalries strained institutions. The 2010 constitution introduced devolved county governments and revised national checks on power. Kenya's presidential republic has become an important regional economic and diplomatic center. Agriculture, trade, tourism, and services coexist with persistent disputes over land, inequality, corruption, and political representation.
