description: >-
  The Lawik principality was the small state of the Lawik family at Ghazni in present day
  Afghanistan, which governed the town in the tenth century before the Ghaznavids made it the
  capital of an empire. The family was originally Buddhist or Hindu and closely tied to the
  Shahi kings of Kabul, and a branch held nearby Gardez. Its rulers converted to Islam over
  time, and medieval historians such as Juzjani remember them as the native lords of Ghazna.
  After the Saffarid hold on the east weakened about 900, the Lawiks governed Ghazni under
  loose Hindu Shahi suzerainty. In 962 the Samanid commander Alp-Tegin besieged the citadel
  for four months and took it from Abu Bakr Lawik. A brief recapture around 965 did not last.
