description: >-
  Coles Creek culture was a Late Woodland culture of the lower Mississippi valley in present
  Louisiana, Mississippi and Arkansas, which followed the Troyville culture. Its people built
  large platform mounds arranged around open plazas, a new kind of ceremonial centre that later
  spread across the Southeast in the Mississippian period. Sites such as Greenhouse, Mazique and
  Lake George show mound building on a growing scale over several centuries. Communities lived
  mainly by hunting, fishing and gathering wild plants, with some cultivation, and maize became
  important only late in the period. Pottery with incised decoration marks the culture's spread.
  Around 1200 Coles Creek developed into the Plaquemine culture under growing Mississippian
  influence.
