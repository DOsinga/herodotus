description: >-
  Ammon was an Iron Age kingdom east of the Jordan River, centred on Rabbah, the
  site of modern Amman. Its independent period is estimated here at about 1000 to 582
  BCE. Kings ruled a population whose language was closely related to neighbouring Hebrew and Moabite.
  Inscriptions, seals, sculpture, and excavated settlements supplement biblical accounts of conflict and dealings with Israel
  and Judah. The deity Milkom held an important place in Ammonite religious life. Agriculture and
  routes linking the plateau with surrounding regions supported the kingdom. Ammon later paid tribute to
  Assyria, retaining local kings despite imperial pressure. Babylonian expansion ended that royal independence; this record
  follows Nebuchadnezzar's campaign around 582 BCE. Ammonite communities and their capital continued under subsequent rulers.
