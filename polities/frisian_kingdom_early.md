description: >-
  The early Frisian kingdom was a western coastal realm in the present day Netherlands, emerging approximately around 600 and ending in 734. Its rulers controlled changing stretches of river mouths, marshlands and North Sea trading routes rather than every Frisian community. Dorestad was an important commercial and political centre, though both it and Utrecht repeatedly changed hands. Aldgisl is securely attested in the later seventh century, followed by Redbad, who resisted Frankish expansion and Christian missions. Frankish writers sometimes called these rulers dukes, while other sources described them as kings. Defeats reduced the realm without immediately eliminating its government. In 734 Charles Martel defeated Poppo at the Battle of the Boarn, bringing the remaining western royal territory under Frankish control.
