description: >-
  The Prince-Bishopric of Sion was the state of the bishops of Sion in the upper Rhône
  valley, the Valais. Rudolph III of Burgundy granted the bishops the county of Valais
  in 999, and they ruled it as princes of the Holy Roman Empire. Their power rested on
  the castles of Sion and on alliances with the communities of the upper valley, known
  as tithes or Zenden. The bishops fought the counts of Savoy for the lower valley,
  which the upper Valais conquered in 1475. Over the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries
  the tithes took over more of the government, allying with the Swiss Confederacy and
  limiting the bishop's rights. In 1628 they forced the bishop to give up his temporal
  sovereignty, and the Valais became a republic of the tithes.
