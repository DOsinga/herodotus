description: >-
  The Uyunid Emirate ruled eastern Arabia from 1076 to 1238, centred at Al Ahsa. Abdullah ibn Ali,
  from the Banu Abd al Qays tribe, founded its government during the final collapse of Qarmatian
  power. Assistance from the Abbasid caliphate and Seljuk armies helped him defeat the Qarmatians
  in 1077, without removing his family's local government. Uyunid emirs subsequently controlled Al
  Ahsa, Qatif and Bahrain at times, but family disputes and divided rule repeatedly weakened their
  position. The court belonged to an Arabic speaking monarchy; its rulers' religious affiliation
  remains debated. Salghurid intervention from southern Iran undermined the dynasty in the
  thirteenth century. This record ends with the conventional loss of Uyunid government in 1238,
  before the Usfurids' later regional takeover.
