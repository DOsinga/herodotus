description: >-
  The Indo-Scythian Kingdom represented here was the realm of Saka rulers in Gandhara and
  neighbouring northwestern South Asia, roughly from 90 BCE to 19 CE. Maues established
  royal power around Taxila, followed by rulers including Azes who displaced surviving
  Indo-Greek kings. The kingdom drew on earlier Greek and local traditions rather than
  replacing every institution with a new system. Coins used Greek and Kharoshthi
  inscriptions, royal titles, mounted rulers, and varied religious imagery. Buddhist
  monuments and inscriptions also connect Saka patrons with the region's religious life.
  Chronology and the relationships between kings remain debated. Indo-Parthian expansion
  ended this Gandharan kingdom around 19 CE. Other Saka governments continued elsewhere, but
  they are distinct from the northwestern realm described in this record.
