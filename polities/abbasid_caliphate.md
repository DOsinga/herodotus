description: >-
  The Abbasid Caliphate replaced the Umayyads in 750 and built its principal capital at
  Baghdad in 762. Hereditary caliphs claimed leadership of the Muslim community and governed
  through ministers, provincial officials, and armies. Early rule stretched across much of
  the Islamic world, supporting trade, scholarship, translation, and urban growth.
  Provincial dynasties and military commanders gradually reduced central authority. In 945
  the Buyids took Baghdad, leaving the caliphs without independent government, although
  their religious position continued. This record resumes in 1152 with the recovery of
  political control in Iraq under al-Muqtafi. Later caliphs governed a smaller territorial
  state rather than the earlier empire. Mongol forces captured Baghdad in 1258 and killed
  al-Musta'sim, ending the independent Abbasid state represented in this record.
