description: >-
  Denmark and Norway remained joined under one crown after Sweden left the Kalmar Union in
  1523. Governed increasingly from Copenhagen, the monarchy included Norway's North Atlantic
  possessions as well as territories associated with the Danish crown. The Reformation
  established Lutheran churches, and Norway's council was abolished in 1537. Royal power
  became hereditary and absolute in the seventeenth century. Trade, shipping, agriculture,
  timber production and overseas colonies linked the state with the Caribbean, India and West
  Africa, while repeated wars with Sweden cost it important Baltic territories. Its
  involvement in the Napoleonic Wars ended disastrously. The Treaty of Kiel in 1814 required
  the king to surrender Norway to Sweden, breaking the union, although Denmark retained
  Iceland, Greenland and the Faroe Islands.
