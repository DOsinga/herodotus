name: "Bell Beaker culture"
description: >-
  Bell Beaker culture describes an archaeological phenomenon spread across much
  of western and central Europe approximately between 2800 and 1800 BCE. Its
  name comes from drinking vessels shaped like inverted bells, often found with
  copper daggers, stone wristguards, ornaments, and archery equipment. These
  objects linked widely separated communities without demonstrating a single
  people, government, or state. Local settlements and burial customs differed,
  and older traditions often continued alongside Beaker practices. Exchange and
  migration both contributed to its spread, with genetic evidence showing
  substantial population movement in some regions. Richly furnished graves
  suggest differences in social standing, but political institutions remain
  unknown. Its regional forms gradually developed into later Bronze Age
  cultures, leaving important changes in metalworking and connections between
  communities.
