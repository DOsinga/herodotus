  Russia emerged as the Russian Federation in 1991 after the Soviet Union dissolved. It
  spans northern Eurasia from eastern Europe to the Pacific, with Moscow as its capital.
  The constitution adopted in 1993 established a federal republic with a powerful
  president, a prime minister and a bicameral legislature. Early market reforms brought
  severe economic disruption, inequality and the rise of wealthy oligarchs. Wars in
  Chechnya challenged federal control. Under Vladimir Putin, political authority became
  increasingly centralised, with growing restrictions on opposition and independent
  media. Russia retained the Soviet Union's permanent Security Council seat and much of
  its nuclear arsenal. Conflicts with Georgia and Ukraine reshaped relations with
  neighbouring countries. Its invasion of Ukraine in 2022 brought international
  sanctions and isolation.
