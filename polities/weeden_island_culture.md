description: >-
  The Weeden Island culture names related archaeological traditions in Florida, southern
  Georgia, and southeastern Alabama, represented here from 200 to 900 CE. Kolomoki, a mound
  centre in Georgia, provides the map's representative location. These communities shared
  elaborate ceremonial pottery and burial practices, but everyday ceramics differed across
  regions. Decorated vessels found in mounds reveal a religious and artistic world rather
  than proving that one government ruled the whole area. Settlement remains and middens show
  varied local ways of living, while farming became increasingly important in some inland
  districts. The tradition developed through earlier Woodland cultures and took different
  regional forms. Its conventional end marks cultural change, not a documented conquest, and
  related practices continued in later communities across the Southeast.
