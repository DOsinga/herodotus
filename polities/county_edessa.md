description: >-
  The County of Edessa was the first crusader state, founded in 1098 in northern Mesopotamia and
  lasting through its remaining western territories until 1150. Baldwin of Boulogne became ruler
  after entering an Armenian governed city during the First Crusade. Frankish counts and their
  nobles held castles among largely Armenian and Syriac Christian populations, with Muslim
  communities also present. Unlike the other crusader states, Edessa lacked a Mediterranean
  coastline and remained vulnerable to surrounding powers. Zengi captured its capital in 1144, a
  defeat that helped prompt the Second Crusade. The county continued west of the Euphrates around
  Turbessel. After Count Joscelin II was captured, his wife Beatrice sold the surviving
  fortresses to Byzantium in 1150, ending the county's separate territorial government.
