description: >-
  The Tulama were the Oromo confederation that took over most of Shewa, in central Ethiopia,
  during the great Oromo migrations of the late sixteenth century. Their clans, among them the
  Gulele, Ekka, Galaan and Selale, settled the highland pastures around the present site of Addis
  Ababa and north to the Blue Nile gorge, where the old Christian towns of Debre Berhan and
  Tegulet had stood. Like other Oromo they governed themselves through the gadaa system, in which
  elected officials of successive age grades led the clans for eight-year terms, with assemblies
  that made law and settled disputes. The Amhara kings of Shewa pushed against them from the
  north: Sahle Selassie defeated them in the 1820s, and Menelik conquered the remaining Tulama
  lands around Entoto by 1881.
