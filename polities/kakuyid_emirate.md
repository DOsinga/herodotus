description: >-
  The Kakuyids were a Daylamite dynasty, related by marriage to the Buyids, that ruled Isfahan
  and much of the Jibal in western Iran and later the desert city of Yazd. Ala al-Dawla Muhammad,
  son of Rustam Dushmanziyar, was governor of Isfahan for the Buyids by 1008 and soon governed it
  as his own state, adding Hamadan and other towns as Buyid power weakened. The philosopher
  Avicenna served him as physician and adviser for his last years. The Ghaznavids held Isfahan
  for a short time in the 1030s, and the Seljuk Toghrul Beg besieged and took it in 1051. Toghrul
  then gave the family Yazd and Abarkuh, where Kakuyid princes ruled as Seljuk vassals and in-
  laws until the line ended around 1141 and the Atabegs of Yazd followed.
