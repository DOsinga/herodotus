description: >-
  Izapa was a regional center near the Pacific coast of Chiapas, Mexico, close to the
  Guatemalan border and Tacaná volcano. Its monumental development and carved stone
  sculptures are especially associated with the Late Formative period. This record begins
  around 700 BCE as an estimate for regional political prominence, not the first settlement.
  Large plazas, pyramids, and stelae indicate organized ceremonial activity, though rulers
  and governing institutions are not securely known. The sculptures connect Izapa with wider
  Mesoamerican traditions and have prompted comparisons with early Maya narratives. Fertile
  volcanic soils supported agriculture in the Soconusco region. Later activity shifted
  toward Group F, including construction through the Early Postclassic. Occupation ended
  around 1200; continuous political identity across this sequence remains an estimate.
