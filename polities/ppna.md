description: >-
  Pre-Pottery Neolithic A describes an early farming horizon in the Levant, Anatolia, and upper Mesopotamia, dated approximately 10000 to 8800 BCE. It is an archaeological category rather than evidence for a single state. Communities built round houses with stone foundations and mudbrick walls, cultivated plants, stored food, and continued hunting wild animals. Stone blades, arrowheads, sickle tools, and grinding equipment reveal changing ways of obtaining and preparing food before pottery became common. Jericho provides the type site, including communal structures whose purposes remain debated. Göbekli Tepe belongs to the same horizon and preserves monumental ritual buildings. Burials beneath house floors connect domestic life with treatment of the dead. The following Pre-Pottery Neolithic B brought further changes in settlement and farming.
