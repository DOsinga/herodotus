description: >-
  The Sunda Kingdom governed western Java, with royal authority attested by the Kebon Kopi II inscription in 932 and ending in 1579. Kings ruled from several seats, including Kawali and Pakuan Pajajaran near present Bogor, rather than from one permanent capital. Hindu traditions coexisted with Buddhist and local religious practices. Farming supported the inland court, while coastal ports exported pepper and connected Sunda with Asian trade. Sri Baduga Maharaja's reign is remembered for prosperity and royal works. As Muslim coastal powers expanded, Sunda sought Portuguese support through a treaty in 1522, but lost ports soon afterward. Banten's pressure steadily reduced the kingdom. Its capture of Pakuan in 1579 ended the western royal government, although Sundanese communities and traditions continued afterward.
