description: >-
  The Slab-grave culture occupied parts of Mongolia and neighbouring southern Siberia during the later Bronze
  Age and early Iron Age, approximately 1100 to 300 BCE in this record. Its name
  comes from graves enclosed by upright stone slabs, often arranged as rectangular fences. Burials, pottery,
  bronze ornaments, and occasional weapons provide the main evidence for communities that lived across the
  eastern steppe. The archaeological label does not identify a single kingdom, known dynasty, or shared
  government. Similar burial practices spread over a area while local traditions varied. Connections with earlier
  Ulaanzuukh communities and neighbouring steppe cultures help explain its development. Later Xiongnu populations incorporated descendants
  of these communities, but cultural and biological continuity does not imply an unchanged political system.
