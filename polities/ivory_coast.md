  Ivory Coast, officially Côte d'Ivoire, is a republic on the Gulf of Guinea in West Africa,
  independent from France since 1960. Its territory includes southern forests, coastal lagoons and northern savannas, with
  communities speaking many languages alongside official French. Félix Houphouët-Boigny became its first president and maintained close
  ties with France. Cocoa and coffee exports supported early growth, attracting workers from neighbouring countries. Abidjan
  was the original capital and remains a major economic centre; Yamoussoukro became the political capital in
  1983. One party rule gave way to multiparty politics in 1990. Later struggles over citizenship, elections
  and succession contributed to a coup and civil wars. The state continued through these regime changes,
  retaining its borders and international identity despite conflict.
