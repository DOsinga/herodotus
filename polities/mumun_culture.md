description: >-
  The Mumun pottery period describes farming communities across the Korean Peninsula,
  represented here from approximately 1500 to 400 BCE. It takes its name from the plain,
  undecorated vessels used for cooking and storage. Excavated villages contain pit houses,
  fields and stone tools, showing the growth of intensive agriculture alongside hunting
  and fishing. Millet, rice and other crops supported large settlements, including centres
  associated with Songguk-ri. Megalithic tombs, bronze daggers and jade ornaments indicate
  emerging differences in wealth and status. Some communities probably developed chiefly
  leadership, but Mumun was not one documented kingdom with a single government. Later
  fortified settlements suggest greater conflict. Its estimated endpoint marks a changing
  archaeological record, while Gojoseon provides a separate political successor in the
  north.
