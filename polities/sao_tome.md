description: >-
  São Tomé and Príncipe became independent from Portugal in 1975. The republic consists
  principally of two volcanic islands in the Gulf of Guinea, with its capital on São Tomé.
  Portuguese settlement had created plantation economies based first on sugar and later on
  coffee and cocoa, using enslaved and coerced African labour. Independence brought a
  government led by the liberation movement under Manuel Pinto da Costa. A single party
  initially controlled political life, and the state took over the plantations.
  Constitutional reform in 1990 opened the way to multiparty elections. The country
  developed a system with a president, prime minister and elected National Assembly. Its
  economy continues to reflect the plantation legacy, while tourism and outside assistance
  also play important roles.
