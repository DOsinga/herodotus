description: >-
  The Gahadavala dynasty ruled a kingdom in the middle Ganges plain of northern India, in present
  day Uttar Pradesh and western Bihar, in the late eleventh and twelfth centuries. Chandradeva
  founded it shortly before 1090, filling the gap left by the decline of the Kalachuris of Tripuri,
  and his family governed from Varanasi and for a time from Kannauj. Govindachandra, the greatest
  king, annexed Kalachuri lands, fought the Palas of Bengal and kept the Ghaznavid raiders of the
  Punjab away. The kings issued many copper plate land grants, which are the main record of their
  rule, and protected the holy city of Varanasi. In 1194 a Ghurid army under Qutb ud-Din Aibak
  defeated and killed Jayachandra at Chandawar, ending the kingdom as a major power.
