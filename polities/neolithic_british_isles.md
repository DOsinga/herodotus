description: >-
  The Neolithic British Isles record describes farming communities across Britain and Ireland between approximately 4000 and 2500 BCE, not a single kingdom or government. Migrants brought crops, domestic animals and ways of living to landscapes previously occupied by hunter-gatherers. People cleared woodland, made pottery and polished stone tools, and built settlements whose forms varied by region. Their monuments include chambered tombs, long barrows, henges and stone circles, with Avebury serving here as a representative site. These structures show organised labour and changing ritual practices, but they do not identify named rulers or a common political authority. Archaeology provides the evidence because no written accounts survive. Around 2500 BCE, Bell Beaker arrivals and metalworking introduced changes associated with the Bronze Age.
