  Saint Vincent and the Grenadines is an eastern Caribbean state comprising Saint Vincent and the northern part of the Grenadine island chain. It became independent from Britain in 1979, following a decade as an associated state with responsibility for internal affairs. Kingstown is its capital and main administrative centre. Government follows a parliamentary constitutional monarchy, with the monarch represented locally by a governor general and an elected House of Assembly sustaining the prime minister's administration. Its population reflects Indigenous Caribbean roots, African enslavement, and later migration under colonial rule. Agriculture, especially bananas, long supported the economy, while tourism gained importance. Major volcanic eruptions, hurricanes, and changing export markets have repeatedly challenged the islands, which continue as an independent Commonwealth country.
