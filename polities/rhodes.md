description: >-
  Rhodes was a Greek island state in the southeastern Aegean, formed when Lindos,
  Ialysos, and Camirus united in 408 BCE. Their new capital, also called Rhodes,
  occupied the island's northern tip. Its civic government and wealthy merchants
  developed a powerful fleet and extensive commercial connections. Although outside
  powers intervened during the fourth century, Rhodes became an independent force
  after Alexander's death. It resisted Demetrius's famous siege in 305 and 304 BCE,
  celebrating its survival with the Colossus, a bronze statue of Helios. Rhodian ships
  guarded trading routes and challenged piracy while maintaining relations with larger
  kingdoms. Roman pressure later reduced its influence. The treaty of 164 BCE tied its
  foreign policy to Rome, ending separate action under this record's definition.
