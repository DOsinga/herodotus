description: >-
  The Oyo Empire was a powerful Yoruba state in what is now southwestern Nigeria and neighbouring
  Benin, with origins conventionally placed around 1300. Its ruler, the Alaafin, governed from
  Oyo-Ile alongside the Oyo Mesi council and religious authorities who could constrain royal
  power. Cavalry helped Oyo dominate inland routes and impose tribute on neighbouring states,
  including Dahomey. Commerce linked agricultural production, regional markets and the Atlantic
  slave trade. Rivalries between kings, councillors and military commanders weakened the empire
  during the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. Ilorin's breakaway and warfare forced the
  abandonment of the old capital. A successor royal centre survived at New Oyo, but imperial
  influence contracted. A treaty of 1888 placed its foreign relations under British control, while
  the kingship continued.
