description: >-
  The Phùng Nguyên culture is an archaeological grouping in northern Vietnam's Red
  River region, conventionally dated to about 2000 to 1500 BCE. Its name comes from
  the Phùng Nguyên site near Viet Tri, discovered in 1958. Settlements commonly
  occupied raised ground beside rivers and streams, linking farming communities to
  fertile lowlands and waterways. Rice cultivation formed an important part of the
  region's changing economy during this period. Excavated objects include distinctive
  polished stone adzes, stone weapons, and carefully shaped pottery, including vessels
  supported on three legs. These remains document shared craft traditions rather than
  a securely identified kingdom. No written evidence establishes named rulers, a
  capital, or one central government. The dates represent estimated archaeological
  boundaries, not recorded political events.
