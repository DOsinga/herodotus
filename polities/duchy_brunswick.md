description: >-
  The Duchy of Brunswick was a German monarchy centred on Braunschweig, restored when
  Frederick William returned in December 1813 after Napoleonic rule. The Congress of Vienna
  confirmed the settlement in 1815, and Brunswick joined the German Confederation. Its Welf
  rulers inherited the earlier Brunswick-Wolfenbüttel lands, which included territories
  rather than one compact area. Frederick William died fighting Napoleon in 1815, leaving a
  young heir. Duke Charles II's government provoked unrest in 1830, after which his brother
  William took power. A constitution introduced representative institutions and limits on
  ducal authority. Brunswick later aligned with Prussia during German unification. Its
  separate control of war and foreign affairs ended on joining the North German
  Confederation in 1867, although the monarchy survived until 1918.
