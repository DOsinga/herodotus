description: >-
  Majiayao culture describes Neolithic farming communities in the upper Yellow River region of northwestern China, especially Gansu, Qinghai and adjoining Sichuan. This record uses approximate dates of 3300 to 2300 BCE, while broader archaeological definitions can include later material. Settlements on river terraces reflect the spread of agriculture into this region. Painted pottery is its most distinctive evidence, with sweeping lines, geometric patterns and human figures decorating vessels used in daily life and burial. Large workshops suggest specialised craftspeople and increasingly complex social organisation, but no named rulers or unified state are established. Early metal objects also show participation in wider exchanges. Pottery styles changed across successive phases, and Qijia communities occupied many of the same regions along these rivers.
