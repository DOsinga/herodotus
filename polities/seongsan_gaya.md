description: >-
  Seongsan Gaya was a separate community around modern Seongju in southeastern Korea,
  traditionally counted among the kingdoms of Gaya. Its existence is represented here from
  about 300 to 562, but neither its foundation nor its conquest has a known date. Later
  Korean histories include Seongsan among the Gaya states and connect their beginnings with
  legendary founders. Archaeology offers firmer evidence for the region's communities than
  these origin stories provide. Gaya consisted of several local governments rather than a
  single kingdom with one ruler. Its wider setting included farming settlements, iron
  production and exchange across southern Korea and the sea towards Japan. Seongsan
  eventually came under Silla as that kingdom absorbed the Gaya region during the sixth
  century, ending local independence.
