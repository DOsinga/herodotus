description: >-
  The Turdetani were the people of the lower Guadalquivir valley in southwestern Iberia, heirs
  of the Tartessian world, recorded here as a chiefdom from about 549 to 238 BCE. Greek and
  Roman writers describe them as the most civilised of the Iberian peoples, living in many
  walled towns with written laws and a script of their own. Archaeology shows fortified
  hilltop towns such as Carmo, rich metalworking and farming, and close trade with the
  Phoenician city of Gadir. Towns were led by local rulers, and some kings, such as Culchas in
  the late third century, controlled groups of towns, but no single state united them.
  Carthage under Hamilcar Barca conquered the region from 237 BCE.
