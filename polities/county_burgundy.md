description: >-
  The Free County of Burgundy, later called Franche-Comté, lay between the Saône and the Jura in
  eastern France, east of the Duchy of Burgundy. Count Otto-William formed it in 982 from lands
  of the Kingdom of Burgundy, and it passed to the Holy Roman Empire with that kingdom in 1032.
  This record follows its separate government from 1250, when imperial authority faded, with
  Dole, Gray and Poligny among the counts' seats; Besançon, the largest town, was an imperial
  city outside their control. Count Otto IV placed the county under Philip IV of France in 1295,
  and royal officials governed it for twenty years. Countess Joan II and her heirs then ruled it
  again, until Philip the Bold of Burgundy inherited it in 1384.
