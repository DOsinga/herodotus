description: >-
  The Gambia became independent from Britain in 1965, governing a narrow territory along the
  Gambia River in West Africa. Senegal surrounds it on land, while its Atlantic coast provides
  access to the sea. The capital, Banjul, developed from the colonial settlement of Bathurst.
  Independence initially brought a constitutional monarchy, replaced by a presidential
  republic in 1970 under Dawda Jawara. A brief confederation with Senegal followed an
  attempted coup in 1981 but dissolved in 1989. Yahya Jammeh seized power in 1994 and ruled
  until an election defeat led to his departure in 2017. Farming, especially groundnuts,
  tourism, and remittances support the economy. The country's river geography and colonial
  boundaries have shaped its trading connections, diverse communities, and relationship with
  neighbouring Senegal.
