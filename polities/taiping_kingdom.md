description: >-
  The Taiping Heavenly Kingdom governed large areas of southern and central China between 1851 and 1864 during a rebellion against Qing rule. Hong Xiuquan, who believed himself the younger brother of Jesus, headed a religious monarchy with subordinate kings commanding armies and administration. The rebels captured Nanjing in 1853 and renamed it Tianjing, their heavenly capital. Their program attacked established religious practices and promised major changes in landholding and social relations, though warfare limited implementation. Strict rules separated men and women and banned several familiar customs. Rivalries among leaders produced a bloody internal crisis in 1856. Qing regional armies gradually recovered territory, assisted by foreign forces. Nanjing fell in 1864 after Hong's death, destroying the kingdom's central government and court.
