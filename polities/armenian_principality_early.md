description: >-
  The early Armenian principality represents periods when native princes governed
  Armenia between Byzantine and caliphal powers. Its first phase begins around 639 with
  Theodore Rshtuni, an approximate starting point for separate government. Great noble
  houses controlled their own estates while a presiding prince coordinated leadership,
  military forces, and relations with larger neighbors. Dvin served as a major
  political center. Arab tribute and recognition did not always remove these princes'
  authority, but direct administration ended the first period in 705. Autonomy returned
  under Ashot Bagratuni in 732 and ended after rebellion in 748. Another Bagratuni,
  Ashot, gained effective royal power from 862 through alliances and careful diplomacy.
  Caliphal recognition of his kingship in 885 established the succeeding Bagratid
  Kingdom of Armenia.
