description: >-
  The Dervish State grew from a Somali religious resistance movement in the late nineteenth
  century and survived until 1920. Its followers occupied shifting parts of the northern Somali
  interior and the Ogaden, eventually building a fortified headquarters at Taleh. Mohammed
  Abdullah Hassan, a Sufi teacher and poet, exercised final authority with a council of advisers
  and commanders; Islamic judges settled disputes. The movement opposed British, Italian, and
  Ethiopian expansion, drawing supporters from several clans while also fighting Somali rivals.
  Its mounted forces combined raids with retreats into difficult country. British aircraft and
  ground troops destroyed the Taleh stronghold in 1920. The defeat ended its territorial
  government, although Hassan escaped and the movement remained important in later memories of
  Somali resistance.
