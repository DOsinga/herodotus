  The Lao People's Democratic Republic was established in 1975 after the communist Pathet Lao displaced the monarchy. It governs landlocked Laos in mainland Southeast Asia from Vientiane, beside the Mekong River. The Lao People's Revolutionary Party controls the state through a socialist one-party system, with a president, prime minister, and national assembly. Its early government maintained close ties with Vietnam and imposed economic controls that encouraged many people to flee. Reforms introduced from 1986 allowed more private enterprise and foreign investment without ending party rule. Hydroelectric dams, mining, and improved transport have reshaped the economy, while rural agriculture remains important. Laos also carries the legacy of extensive wartime bombing, including unexploded ordnance that still affects farms, villages, and daily life.
