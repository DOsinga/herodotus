  Mauritius is an island state in the southwestern Indian Ocean that became independent
  from Britain in 1968. Its capital is Port Louis, and its territory includes the main
  island and Rodrigues. Independence first brought a constitutional monarchy with a
  prime minister and elected parliament; a republic replaced the monarchy in 1992 while
  keeping parliamentary government. Earlier Dutch, French, and British rule left a
  plantation economy and a population shaped by slavery, indentured labour, and
  migration from several continents. Since independence, manufacturing, tourism, and
  financial services have broadened an economy once heavily dependent on sugar. The
  country's varied languages and religious communities reflect that history. Mauritius
  has maintained its statehood through these economic and constitutional changes,
  alongside historical disputes over territory.
