description: >-
  The Duchy of Alamannia governed lands around Lake Constance, the upper Rhine, Neckar, and Danube during its period of practical independence from Frankish rule, beginning in 638. Its dukes drew power from local noble families rather than a capital; Cannstatt marks an important political centre. Christian missions, monasteries, and written law helped shape the region, though older religious practices persisted. Duke Lantfrid was associated with the Lex Alamannorum, a law code defining social ranks and obligations. Frankish mayors of the palace repeatedly campaigned against the duchy as their power grew. In 746 Carloman summoned its nobles to Cannstatt and had many executed. Their destruction ended the native ducal government and brought Alamannia under direct Frankish administration for the following generations.
