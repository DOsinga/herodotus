description: >-
  The Taifa of Seville was an Andalusian Muslim principality ruled by the Abbadid family
  from 1023 to 1091. Abu al-Qasim, the city's judge, established local government while
  the Caliphate of Córdoba was disintegrating. His successors expanded into neighbouring
  territories, making their state one of the region's strongest taifas. Al-Mu'tadid conquered
  several smaller principalities, while his son al-Mu'tamid, himself a poet, gathered
  writers and artists at court. Seville also gained Córdoba and influence over Murcia.
  Payments to Christian rulers bought protection without ending its own government.
  After Alfonso VI captured Toledo, al-Mu'tamid sought Almoravid help from North Africa.
  Their armies defeated the Christians at Sagrajas in 1086, but subsequently turned
  against the taifa rulers, conquering Seville and exiling al-Mu'tamid in 1091.
