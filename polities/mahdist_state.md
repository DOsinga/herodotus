description: >-
  The Mahdist State governed much of Sudan from 1885 to 1899, with its capital at Omdurman.
  It grew from Muhammad Ahmad's religious uprising against Egyptian rule, which he denounced
  as oppressive and un-Islamic. His followers captured Khartoum in January 1885, killing its
  governor Charles Gordon. Ahmad died shortly afterward, and his successor Abdallahi
  consolidated a centralized government grounded in the Mahdi's religious teachings.
  Officials collected taxes, courts enforced its version of Islamic law, and armies
  campaigned against Ethiopia and Egypt. Warfare, forced movements, repression, and famine
  placed heavy burdens on the population. An Anglo-Egyptian invasion defeated the main
  Mahdist army at Omdurman in 1898. Abdallahi continued resistance until his death in battle
  in 1899, completing the destruction of the state.
