description: >-
  Western Wei ruled the western part of northern China from 535 to 557, with its capital
  at Chang'an. It emerged from the division of Northern Wei, after Emperor Xiaowu fled
  west and died. The general Yuwen Tai installed Yuan Baoju as emperor, preserving the
  imperial titles and court of the Tuoba ruling house while exercising the main
  political and military authority himself. Although smaller than Eastern Wei, its
  eastern rival, Western Wei survived repeated attacks and won a victory at Shayuan in
  537. It also expanded southward into former Liang territories, including Sichuan.
  After Yuwen Tai's death, his family consolidated control. In 557, Yuwen Hu deposed the
  last Wei emperor and established Yuwen Jue as ruler of Northern Zhou instead.
