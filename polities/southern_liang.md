description: >-
  Southern Liang was a kingdom in northeastern Qinghai and parts of Gansu, in northwestern China,
  from 397 to 414. Its founders belonged to the Tufa branch of the Xianbei and rose as Later Liang
  weakened. Tufa Wugu proclaimed a separate government, followed by his brothers Tufa Lilugu and
  Tufa Rutan. Their courts moved among Lianchuan, Xiping, Ledu, and briefly Guzang as military
  fortunes changed. The rulers used Chinese princely and royal titles while commanding a population
  that included Xianbei, Han Chinese, and Qiang communities. They acknowledged Later Qin at times
  but continued governing and campaigning themselves. Rivalry with Northern Liang drained their
  strength. In 414, Western Qin captured Ledu while Rutan was campaigning elsewhere, bringing the
  kingdom to an end.
