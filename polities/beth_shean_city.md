description: >-
  Beth Shean was a Canaanite town at the meeting of the Jezreel and Jordan valleys in the early
  Iron Age. For about three centuries it had been an Egyptian garrison seat, but in the later
  twentieth dynasty Egypt lost its hold on Canaan, and the garrison town was destroyed. Around
  1130 BCE a new Canaanite town was built over the ruins, without Egyptian officials. Its
  rulers are unnamed. The Book of Judges says the Israelite tribe of Manasseh failed to take
  it, and the Book of Samuel says the Philistines hung the body of Saul on its walls after the
  battle of Gilboa, though no Philistine presence has been found. Around 1000 BCE it passed
  into the Israelite kingdom, and Solomon's district lists name it.
