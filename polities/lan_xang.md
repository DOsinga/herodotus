description: >-
  Lan Xang was a Lao kingdom in the Mekong valleys of mainland Southeast Asia
  from 1353 to 1707. Fa Ngum united regional towns through conquest and
  alliances, establishing his court at present-day Luang Prabang. Kings governed
  through a network of local rulers rather than uniform territorial
  administration, while Theravada Buddhism became central to court life and
  royal authority. Setthathirath moved the capital to Vientiane in the sixteenth
  century and resisted Burmese expansion. The kingdom also faced wars with
  Vietnam and rival Tai states. Sourigna Vongsa's seventeenth-century reign
  brought relative stability, religious patronage, and wider commercial
  contacts. After his death, succession struggles weakened the monarchy, and Lan
  Xang divided into the separately governed kingdoms of Luang Prabang and
  Vientiane in 1707.
