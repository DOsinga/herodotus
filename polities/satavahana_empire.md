description: >-
  The Satavahana Empire ruled much of India's Deccan between approximately 120 BCE and 224 CE. Its royal family gave the state its conventional name, although its origins and early chronology remain disputed. Pratishthana, modern Paithan, was an important capital; other centres served rulers as their territory shifted. Kings governed through officials and local elites, linking inland farming regions with towns and commercial routes. They issued coins, used Prakrit in inscriptions and supported both Brahmanical traditions and Buddhist establishments. Long struggles with the Western Satraps repeatedly changed control of western India. Gautamiputra Satakarni restored power after major losses, followed by renewed expansion under successors. By the early third century, the empire fragmented into smaller kingdoms, including Andhra Ikshvaku, Abhira and Chutu.
