description: >-
  The Bhauma-Kara kingdom governed much of present Odisha in eastern India between an
  estimated 736 and about 950. Its court was based at Guhadevapataka, generally placed
  in the Jajpur region. Royal land grants reveal a monarchy working through local
  chiefs, revenue divisions and districts. Early rulers supported Buddhism, while later
  kings and queens favoured forms of Hindu worship without abandoning religious
  patronage across traditions. Several women ruled in their own right, especially
  Tribhuvana Mahadevi, who restored unity after invasions weakened the kingdom. Disputed
  successions and marriages with neighbouring ruling families marked its later years.
  The final queen, Dharma Mahadevi, lost power to the Somavamshis. Both the starting
  chronology and precise location of the capital remain subjects of continuing historical
  debate.
