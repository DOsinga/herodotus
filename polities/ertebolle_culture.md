description: >-
  The Ertebølle culture belongs to the late Mesolithic of Denmark, southern Sweden and
  nearby Baltic coasts, approximately between 5300 and 4000 BCE. Named after a Danish shell
  midden, it is known through settlements, burials, tools and food remains. Communities
  fished, collected shellfish and hunted land animals, seals and other marine resources,
  using dugout boats on coastal and inland waters. Flint, bone and antler supplied tools,
  while distinctive pottery included pointed vessels and small lamps. Some sites now lie
  underwater because coastlines changed after occupation. Cemeteries suggest lasting
  attachments to particular places, but they do not reveal a unified government. Contact
  with farming neighbours preceded the spread of Funnelbeaker communities, which brought a
  different economy and new settlement patterns to Scandinavia.
