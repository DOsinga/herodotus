description: >-
  Colchis occupied the eastern Black Sea coast and river valleys of western Georgia. This
  record follows its separate political existence from about 600 BCE until Pontic control
  around 101 BCE. Its population included communities ancestral to later Georgian
  peoples, while Greek settlements connected the coast with Mediterranean trade.
  Political authority was divided among local rulers, and the surviving evidence does not
  establish an organised monarchy throughout the region. Fertile lowlands, timber, metals
  and access to mountain resources supported exchange. Excavated goldwork and coins
  reveal both local traditions and contact with Greek culture. Greek mythology made
  Colchis the home of Medea and the Golden Fleece. Mithridates VI of Pontus eventually
  took control, although the precise chronology of that incorporation is uncertain.
