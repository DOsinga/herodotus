description: >-
  The Khmer Empire governed much of mainland Southeast Asia from centres in present
  Cambodia between 802 and 1431. Its beginning is conventionally tied to Jayavarman II's
  consecration as a universal ruler. Hereditary kings supported temples, officials and
  large networks of agricultural settlements, though succession and regional control were
  often contested. Angkor became the best known royal centre, sustained by reservoirs,
  canals and rice cultivation. Suryavarman II built Angkor Wat, while Jayavarman VII
  sponsored Angkor Thom and Buddhist monuments after warfare with Champa. Hindu and
  Buddhist traditions both shaped kingship and public building. Later religious change,
  political pressure and damage to water systems contributed to decline. The move away
  from Angkor around 1431 marks the transition to a later Cambodian kingdom.
