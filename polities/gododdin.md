description: >-
  Gododdin was a Brittonic kingdom in what is now southeastern Scotland and northeastern England,
  associated with the earlier Votadini people. Its post Roman kingship is placed here around 450,
  although its origins and borders remain uncertain. Din Eidyn, the fortified centre on
  Edinburgh's Castle Rock, was an important hilltop royal seat. The kingdom belonged to the
  northern British world later remembered in Welsh tradition. The poem Y Gododdin commemorates
  warriors who gathered under elite patronage and suffered a disastrous defeat at Catraeth,
  usually identified with Catterick. Both the poem's dating and its relationship to historical
  events are disputed. Anglian expansion steadily reduced remaining British power in the region.
  The conventional fall of Eidyn to Northumbria in 638 ends this record.
