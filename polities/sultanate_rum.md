description: >-
  The Sultanate of Rûm was a Seljuk monarchy in Anatolia, founded in 1077 on lands taken from the
  Byzantine Empire. Its name referred to the Romans whose former territory and Christian
  population it governed. Initially based at Nicaea, the court moved to Konya after the First
  Crusade. Turkish rulers employed Persian administrative and literary traditions while promoting
  Sunni Islam. Victories over Byzantine armies and rival Turkish principalities strengthened the
  state, and access to Mediterranean and Black Sea ports supported trade. Caravanserais, mosques,
  and schools marked its prosperity. Defeat by the Mongols at Köse Dağ in 1243 brought tribute
  and growing outside intervention. Its separate government ended by 1262 under Ilkhanid control,
  although Seljuk royal titles and influential coinage survived longer.
