description: >-
  Pashash was an elite centre near Cabana in Peru's northern Ancash highlands, represented here as a local chiefdom from approximately 300 to 1000 CE. Its rich burials, stone sculpture, and fine pottery connect it with the wider Recuay cultural tradition, but do not establish a single government across all Recuay settlements. Elaborate jewellery, including gilt arsenic bronze objects dated between 300 and 600, indicates skilled metalworking and concentrated wealth. Regional art depicts warriors, animals, and ceremonial figures, while fortified centres elsewhere illuminate a landscape of competing communities. The later Pashash cultural phase continued into the end of the first millennium. Precise political boundaries, rulers, and the precise end of the local governing community are unknown, so both dates remain estimates.
