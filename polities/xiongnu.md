description: >-
  The Xiongnu formed a powerful steppe confederation centred in Mongolia, represented here from Modu Chanyu's rise in 209 BCE until its division in 48 CE. A supreme ruler called the chanyu headed a hierarchy of regional leaders and military commanders. Modu united competing groups and expanded against neighbouring peoples, including the Donghu and Yuezhi. Mobility, horse breeding, and mounted warfare supported a far-reaching political network rather than a government based solely in cities. Relations with Han China alternated between raids, major campaigns, trade, and marriage agreements accompanied by gifts. Succession disputes and Chinese pressure weakened unity over time. In 48 CE, the confederation separated into northern and southern branches, which followed different paths in their relations with the Han empire.
