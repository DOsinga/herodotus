  Myanmar, also known as Burma, has been an independent state in mainland Southeast Asia since 1948. It gained freedom from British rule as a parliamentary republic, with Rangoon as its capital. Its population includes the Bamar majority and other communities whose demands for autonomy have shaped prolonged internal conflict. A military coup in 1962 replaced parliamentary government with an authoritarian socialist system. Later military governments suppressed opposition and changed the country's English name to Myanmar in 1989. Partial political opening brought elected civilian leaders into government during the following decades, without removing the armed forces' influence. The 2021 coup triggered renewed resistance and civil war. The capital moved to Naypyidaw, while Yangon, formerly Rangoon, remained the country's largest commercial city.
