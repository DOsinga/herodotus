  Mali is a landlocked West African republic, independent since 1960, with its
  capital at Bamako on the Niger River. Formerly French Sudan, it briefly shared
  independence with Senegal in the Mali Federation before becoming a separate
  republic. Modibo Keïta established a socialist one-party government, replaced by
  military rule after the coup of 1968. Popular protest and another coup opened
  the way to multiparty elections in the early 1990s. Its economy relies heavily
  on farming, livestock, cotton, and gold, while northern deserts contrast with
  the more densely settled southern riverlands. Tuareg rebellions and Islamist
  insurgencies have challenged government authority, especially since 2012.
  Further military takeovers in 2020 and 2021 changed the regime without ending
  the state or its national political identity.
