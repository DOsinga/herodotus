description: >-
  The Kingdom of Altava was a small Berber monarchy centred on an inland Roman
  town in present day western Algeria. This record follows its reduced government
  after the defeat of Garmul in 578, rather than the larger Mauro-Roman kingdom
  that preceded it. Its people maintained elements of Roman urban life and Latin
  culture alongside Berber traditions. Christianity remained important, and a
  church built at Altava belongs to this later phase. Written evidence is thin, so
  its rulers, boundaries, and political relations cannot be reconstructed
  securely. Connections with Koceila's later coalition resisting Arab expansion
  are especially uncertain. The kingdom's conventional endpoint around 708
  reflects the Umayyad conquest of the region, not a precisely documented final
  act by its poorly known government.
