description: >-
  The Malacca Sultanate grew around a port on the Malay Peninsula near the narrow passage between
  the Indian Ocean and South China Sea. Founded around 1400, it became a major meeting place for
  merchants trading spices, textiles, and other goods across Asia. Its rulers adopted Islam, and
  the royal court helped spread Muslim learning and Malay literary culture. The sultan governed
  through officials, including the bendahara, while harbour administration accommodated traders
  from different communities. Diplomacy with Ming China helped strengthen Malacca's position
  against regional rivals. Its influence extended beyond the port into parts of the peninsula and
  Sumatra. Portuguese forces captured Malacca in 1511, ending government at its capital. The
  displaced royal family continued resistance and helped establish successor sultanates.
