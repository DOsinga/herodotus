description: >-
  The Qijia culture occupied the upper Yellow River region of Gansu and eastern Qinghai in
  northwestern China during the early Bronze Age. Its span here, approximately 2300 to
  1500 BCE, represents an archaeological estimate rather than the life of a known state.
  Named after Qijiaping, it followed the Majiayao culture and is identified through
  settlements, pottery, burials and early metalworking. Communities farmed and raised
  livestock, while copper objects and some bronze tools reveal connections with wider
  Eurasian exchange networks. Knives, axes and mirrors are among its distinctive finds.
  Differences between graves suggest social distinctions, but no named rulers or secure
  system of government is known. Later regional cultures replaced Qijia traditions as
  settlements and material practices changed across the region.
