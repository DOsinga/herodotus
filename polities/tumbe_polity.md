description: >-
  Tumbe was an early trading settlement on northern Pemba Island, off the coast of
  present Tanzania. This record covers its excavated early phase, approximately 600 to
  950 CE, not the later settlement at the place. Archaeology shows a community
  connected with smaller sites and Indian Ocean exchange. Locally made pottery belongs
  to the Early Tana Tradition, whose decorated jars link Tumbe with settlements along
  the wider East African coast. Imported ceramics, including vessels associated with
  Persian Gulf trade, demonstrate contacts beyond the island. Evidence concerns houses,
  household production, pottery, and trade rather than named rulers or a constitution.
  Nothing establishes how the community was governed. Tumbe therefore represents an
  archaeological culture and settlement, not a securely identified kingdom or
  city-state.
