description: >-
  Nabhani Oman was the state of the Al Nabhan, an Arab family of the Azd who ruled the
  interior of Oman as hereditary kings from 1154 until 1624. Their rule pushed the elected
  Ibadi imams aside into a mostly religious role for several centuries. The kings governed
  from fortified towns in the interior, chiefly Bahla, Nizwa and Maqniyat, and drew their
  wealth from the overland trade in frankincense and from the oases. The great mud-brick
  fort of Bahla, built between the twelfth and fifteenth centuries, is their best-known
  monument. Their power was often divided among rival members of the family and
  challenged by restored imams, while the coast fell under the Kingdom of Hormuz and later
  the Portuguese. The Ya'ruba imam Nasir ibn Murshid ended Nabhani rule in 1624.
