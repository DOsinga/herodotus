description: >-
  Mapungubwe was a kingdom near the meeting of the Shashe and Limpopo rivers, in the borderlands of
  present South Africa, Zimbabwe, and Botswana. Its royal centre occupied Mapungubwe Hill from about
  1220 to 1300, following the nearby settlement of K2. Archaeology reveals a sharply divided society:
  rulers and elite families lived above the population on the hill, and rich burials contained gold
  ornaments, including the rhinoceros figure. Farming and cattle supported its inhabitants, while gold
  and ivory travelled through coastal trade networks in exchange for imported glass beads and other
  goods. Its original name and royal history remain uncertain. Around 1300 the hill was abandoned, as
  regional settlement and power shifted and Great Zimbabwe became the leading centre farther north.
