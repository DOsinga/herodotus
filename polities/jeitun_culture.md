description: >-
  The Jeitun culture represents early farming villages around the Kopet Dag foothills in
  southern Turkmenistan and northeastern Iran, here dated approximately 6200–4500 BCE.
  It takes its name from Jeitun, a settlement north of Ashgabat excavated from the
  1950s. Villagers built rectangular houses from clay blocks, with plastered floors,
  fireplaces, and adjoining yards. They cultivated wheat and barley, kept sheep and
  goats, and supplemented farming with hunting. Stone sickle blades and grinding tools
  show how crops were harvested and processed. Decorated pottery and clay figurines
  connect these communities with other early farmers farther west. No named rulers or
  clear state government are known. The later Anau culture followed this regional
  tradition, although the dates and boundaries remain estimates drawn from archaeology.
