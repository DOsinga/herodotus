description: >-
  Van Xuan was a short-lived independent monarchy in what is now northern Vietnam,
  governing from Ly Bi's revolt in 542 until Sui conquest in 602. Ly Bi proclaimed
  himself emperor in 544, giving his state a name expressing hopes of lasting
  prosperity. Long Bien was its capital, although the ancient site's exact location
  remains uncertain. Liang armies forced the founder away from the lowlands, and
  resistance continued under Trieu Quang Phuc, who used marshy terrain to sustain his
  campaigns. Rival claims by members of Ly Bi's family divided the leadership. Ly Phat
  Tu defeated Trieu Quang Phuc and ruled the reunited territory. He eventually
  surrendered to a Sui invasion, returning the region to Chinese administration after
  decades of contested independence.
