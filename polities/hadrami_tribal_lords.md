description: >-
  Wadi Hadhramaut is the long valley of southern Arabia whose mud-brick towns, chief among them
  Tarim and Shibam, lived from dates and trade. This regional record covers the valley from the
  end of Abbasid control to the rise of the Kathiri sultanate, a period in which no single
  state held it. Power lay with local tribal dynasties based in Tarim and Shibam. In the tenth
  century Ibadi imams still had a following in the valley. The Ba Alawi sayyids, descendants of
  Ahmad al-Muhajir who came from Iraq in the tenth century, settled in Tarim and made it a
  centre of Sunni learning. The Rasulids of Yemen claimed the valley in the thirteenth century,
  but their rule was brief.
