description: >-
  The County of Soissons was a feudal county centred on the city of Soissons in the Aisne
  valley, recorded here from 969 to 1185. Guy I, of the Herbertian house of Vermandois, held
  it from 969, and it passed through his daughter Adelise to the lords of Bar-sur-Aube,
  through Adelaide to William Busac, count of Eu, and in 1141 to the lords of Nesle. The
  counts shared power in the city with its bishops, and the citizens obtained a commune
  charter from King Louis VI. The county lay close to the royal lands around Laon and Senlis,
  and its counts were loyal vassals of the Capetian kings. From the 1180s royal officials
  governed the region, while the counts kept their title until the fourteenth century.
