description: >-
  The Bijapur Sultanate was an Adil Shahi monarchy in the southwestern Deccan, established by Yusuf Adil Shah in 1490 as Bahmani authority weakened. Its capital, Bijapur, became a major centre of architecture, painting, poetry, and music. Rulers and nobles drew on Persian and local Indian traditions, with both Shia and Sunni Islam receiving official favour at different times. Bijapur joined the alliance that defeated Vijayanagara at Talikota in 1565 and expanded into southern India. It also absorbed Bidar in 1619. Maratha resistance under Shivaji and growing Mughal pressure weakened the state, despite periods of accommodation with Delhi. Aurangzeb besieged and conquered Bijapur in 1686, ending its dynasty's rule. The city's great monuments, including Gol Gumbaz, preserve its distinctive artistic legacy.
