description: >-
  The Later Lê dynasty governed Đại Việt, in present day Vietnam, from 1428 to 1527 and
  again from 1533 to 1789. Lê Lợi founded the monarchy after leading resistance against Ming
  occupation. Early emperors built a centralized administration, promoted Confucian learning
  and examinations, and expanded southward at Champa's expense. Lê Thánh Tông strengthened
  laws and provincial government during the fifteenth century. Mạc Đăng Dung seized the
  throne in 1527, but supporters restored a Lê claimant six years later. During the restored
  period, the Trịnh lords exercised effective government in the north while the Nguyễn
  governed the south, both invoking imperial legitimacy. Civil wars and the Tây Sơn
  rebellion destroyed this political order. The final Lê emperor lost power in 1789.
