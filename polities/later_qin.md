description: >-
  Later Qin ruled parts of northwestern China from 384 to 417 during the fractured Sixteen Kingdoms
  period. Yao Chang, a Qiang military leader formerly serving Former Qin, founded the state as that
  empire collapsed after its defeat at the Fei River. He occupied Chang'an in 386 and assumed the
  imperial title. His son Yao Xing defeated remaining Former Qin forces and expanded westward,
  bringing several neighbouring states under his influence. Chang'an became an important Buddhist
  centre, where the translator Kumarajiva worked with royal support to produce Chinese scriptures.
  Military setbacks, rebellious allies, and succession struggles weakened the kingdom after its peak.
  In 417 the Eastern Jin general Liu Yu captured Chang'an, ending the government of Yao Xing's
  successor, Yao Hong.
