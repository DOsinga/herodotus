description: >-
  The Maghrawa were the largest branch of the Zenata Berbers, a tribal confederation of the
  central Maghreb, recorded here as a chiefdom in the lower Chelif valley and the neighbouring
  coast from 740 to 777. Their lands lay in what is now northern Algeria at the time of the
  Arab conquest. When the Berber Revolt of 740 ended Arab rule west of Ifriqiya, the Maghrawa
  and their neighbours governed themselves under their own chiefs, and the Maghrawa took part
  in the Kharijite movements that followed. Their leaders later allied with Idris I and with
  the Umayyads of Córdoba, and at the end of the tenth century a Maghrawa line ruled Fez. Here
  the record ends when the Rustamid imamate took control of the central Maghreb.
