description: >-
  The Cucuteni–Trypillia culture developed across present-day Romania, Moldova, and Ukraine from roughly 5500 to 2750 BCE. Archaeologists link its communities through painted pottery, clay figurines, farming settlements, and related building traditions. Some settlements, including Talianki, reached remarkable sizes, with houses arranged in broad patterns around open spaces. These sites required cooperation, but their remains do not establish a unified kingdom or identifiable rulers. People cultivated crops, kept livestock, and used stone, bone, and some copper tools. Houses were repeatedly burned and rebuilt, although the reasons remain debated. Contacts connected these farming communities with neighbouring steppe groups. Its final phases saw changes in settlement and livelihood, and the culture's disappearance cannot confidently be reduced to one invasion or a sudden event.
