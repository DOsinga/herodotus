description: >-
  The Johor Sultanate emerged in 1528 as a successor to Malacca after the Portuguese captured
  that city's capital. Alauddin Riayat Shah II established a hereditary Malay Muslim monarchy
  whose lands and maritime connections reached across the southern peninsula, Singapore, the Riau
  islands and parts of Sumatra. Sultans ruled with powerful ministers and regional chiefs,
  repeatedly moving their court as warfare threatened their settlements. Johor competed with
  Portuguese Malacca and Aceh, then helped the Dutch capture Malacca in 1641. Royal succession
  disputes, Jambi's attacks and growing Bugis influence reshaped its politics. British and Dutch
  intervention eventually divided its sphere. The 1824 treaty formalised that separation, leaving
  mainland Johor and island Riau on different colonial paths rather than preserving the earlier
  sultanate.
