description: >-
  The Yaudheyas formed a military republic in eastern Punjab and neighbouring India,
  especially present Haryana. This record places their independent history approximately
  between 250 BCE and 350 CE, using coin evidence rather than a precise founding date.
  Their name evokes warriors, and their political organisation was a collective
  association rather than a hereditary monarchy. Inscriptions mention leaders with
  military and royal titles. Their coins often portray Karttikeya, the war god, and
  celebrate the victory of the Yaudheya community. They resisted larger powers, including
  the Western Satraps and Kushans, although claims of conquest show that their
  independence was contested. Samudragupta's inscription lists them among communities
  paying tribute to the Gupta ruler. Their coinage and political identity disappear during
  the fourth century.
