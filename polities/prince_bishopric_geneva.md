description: >-
  The Prince-Bishopric of Geneva was the temporal lordship of the bishops of Geneva over their
  city, recorded here from 1250 until the Reformation of 1534. Frederick Barbarossa made the
  bishop a prince of the Empire in 1154, and the bishops defended their rights against the
  counts of Geneva, who left the city for Annecy around 1219, and later against the House of
  Savoy. From the late thirteenth century Savoy held the office of vidomne in the city and
  often placed its relatives on the episcopal throne. The citizens organised a commune that
  gradually took over the city's government. An alliance with Fribourg and Bern in 1526 and
  the arrival of Protestant preachers ended episcopal rule, and the last bishop left the city
  in 1533.
