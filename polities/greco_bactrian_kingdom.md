description: >-
  The Greco-Bactrian Kingdom ruled Bactria and neighboring lands in Central Asia, centered on Bactra in present Afghanistan. It began with Diodotus's separation from the Seleucid Empire, placed here in 256 BCE, although the exact date remains debated. Greek kings governed a wealthy agricultural region whose cities combined Hellenistic institutions with local traditions. Excavations at Ai-Khanoum reveal theaters, temples, a gymnasium, and elaborate houses, while royal coins provide much of the evidence for its rulers. Euthydemus resisted a Seleucid reconquest, and subsequent expansion across the Hindu Kush helped create the Indo-Greek kingdoms. Dynastic struggles, Parthian pressure, and migrating peoples weakened Bactrian rule. Yuezhi conquest ended the kingdom around 120 BCE, though distinctive Greek cultural influences survived under later Central Asian rulers.
