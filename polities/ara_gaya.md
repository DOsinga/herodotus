description: >-
  Ara Gaya was a kingdom centred on Haman in southeastern Korea, emerging around 300 CE from the
  earlier Anya community. It belonged to the Gaya confederacy but maintained its own rulers and
  diplomatic interests. Elite tombs at Marisan provide archaeological evidence for its ruling
  class. As neighbouring Baekje and Silla expanded, Ara Gaya tried to protect its position
  through negotiation rather than direct confrontation. Its role in the Alla and Sabi conferences
  shows a functioning sixth century government dealing with neighbouring kingdoms and Yamato
  Japan. It also sought support from Goguryeo against Baekje, without securing lasting
  protection. Silla's continuing expansion ultimately overcame these efforts. Ara Gaya
  surrendered in 559, ending its separate government before the remaining Gaya kingdoms were
  absorbed.
