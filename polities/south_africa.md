  South Africa occupies the southern end of the African continent and is governed as a
  parliamentary republic. This record begins in 1939, when its parliament
  independently chose to enter the Second World War, following earlier steps toward
  sovereignty within the British Commonwealth. The Union had brought together former
  British colonies and Boer republics in 1910. White minority governments restricted
  the rights of the black majority, developing apartheid into a comprehensive system
  after 1948. The country became a republic in 1961. Resistance, repression,
  international pressure, and negotiations eventually ended apartheid. The first
  election with universal adult voting in 1994 brought Nelson Mandela to the
  presidency. Pretoria serves as the executive capital, while Cape Town and
  Bloemfontein have distinct national governmental roles.
