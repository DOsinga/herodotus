description: >-
  Guge was a kingdom in western Tibet, centred on the Sutlej valley and the royal settlement of Tsaparang. Its separate government emerged around 930 from the division of Kyide Nyimagon's western Tibetan realm. Native kings maintained their court through changing relationships with larger Tibetan and Mongol powers. Guge became a sponsor of Buddhism: rulers supported monasteries, translation and contact with scholars from India, including the invitation of Atisha. Tholing and Tsaparang preserve evidence of this religious and artistic activity. The kingdom also connected Himalayan routes toward Ladakh, Kashmir and neighbouring valleys. Jesuit visitors reached its court in the seventeenth century. In 1630, Ladakh's army conquered Guge and deported the royal family, ending its independent government while its Buddhist monuments survived.
