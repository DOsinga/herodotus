description: >-
  The Songhai Empire dominated much of the western Sahel from 1464 to 1591, with its capital at
  Gao on the Niger River. Sunni Ali expanded an older Songhai kingdom, taking Timbuktu and Djenné
  and bringing important trading centres under royal control. Askia Muhammad replaced the Sunni
  line in 1493 and strengthened administration through appointed officials and provincial
  governors. His pilgrimage to Mecca and support for Islamic scholarship linked the empire with
  wider Muslim networks, although many subjects maintained other religious traditions. River
  transport, farming, trade, and cavalry supported its power. Succession conflicts weakened the
  ruling house during the sixteenth century. A Moroccan army equipped with firearms defeated
  Songhai at Tondibi in 1591, destroying its dominance and occupying the principal cities.
