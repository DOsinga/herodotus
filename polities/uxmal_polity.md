description: >-
  Uxmal was a Maya political center in the Puuc hills of western Yucatán, Mexico, flourishing
  approximately between 700 and 1000 CE. Its rulers directed an imposing landscape of palaces,
  temples, plazas, and a ball court, built with carefully cut stone and elaborate sculptural
  decoration. Images of the rain god Chaac feature prominently in its distinctive Puuc architecture.
  Inscriptions identify Chan Chak K'ak'nal Ajaw as a ruler around the end of the ninth century,
  including a ball court dedication in 901. The city's monuments show concentrated royal and
  administrative authority, although its complete dynasty and territorial reach remain uncertain.
  Court life and major construction declined around 1000. Later occupation and legends should not be
  mistaken for an unchanged continuing Uxmal government.
