description: >-
  The kings of Egypt's Twenty-first Dynasty governed Lower Egypt from Tanis,
  represented here from 1069 to 943 BCE. Their monarchy followed the decline of
  the Ramesside state and opened the Third Intermediate Period. Although they used
  the traditional titles of pharaohs, their practical authority did not encompass
  all Egypt. High priests of Amun governed Upper Egypt from Thebes while
  acknowledging the northern kings. Family ties and religious connections helped
  link these separate centres without creating a single administration. Smendes
  founded the dynasty, and Psusennes I became one of its known rulers. Royal
  burials at Tanis preserved rich funerary objects that illuminate court culture.
  The dynasty ended with the accession of Shoshenq I and the new Libyan royal line
  in 943.
