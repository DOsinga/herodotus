description: >-
  The Catacomb culture occupied the Pontic steppe, including the Donets region, approximately between 2800 and 2200 BCE. It is an archaeological grouping with regional variants, not a documented kingdom. Its name comes from graves with chambers opening from vertical shafts beneath burial mounds. Some burials contained animal sacrifices, weapons, wheeled vehicles, or modeled clay faces, showing varied rituals and differences in status. Communities mainly kept livestock, though wheat remains and wooden ploughs indicate farming. Bronze tools and other objects suggest specialized crafts and contacts with neighboring cultures. Settlements are less well known than graves, with many interpreted as seasonal camps near water. Later steppe cultures replaced these regional traditions; the surviving evidence cannot identify a single ruling organization or dynasty.
