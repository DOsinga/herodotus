  Ecuador is a republic on South America's Pacific coast, with Quito as its capital. It
  became a separate state in 1830 when the southern districts of Gran Colombia withdrew from
  that union. Its territory includes Andean highlands, coastal plains, Amazonian forests,
  and the Galápagos Islands. Presidents and legislatures have governed under changing
  constitutions, interrupted by military rule and political crises. Nineteenth-century
  struggles between conservatives and liberals shaped the role of the Catholic Church and
  regional elites. Cocoa, bananas, and petroleum successively became important exports. Long
  border disputes with Peru brought wars and territorial losses before a final settlement in
  1998. Civilian constitutional government resumed in 1979. Ecuador remains the continuing
  state despite these changes of regime and its national history.
