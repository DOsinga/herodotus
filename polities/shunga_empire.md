description: >-
  The Shunga Empire ruled a changing part of northern and central India from 185 to 73
  BCE, with its centre at Pataliputra in Magadha. It began when the military commander
  Pushyamitra killed the last Mauryan emperor and assumed kingship. His successors also
  held court at Vidisha, linking the Gangetic heartland with central India. The monarchy
  fought regional rivals and Indo Greek forces, but its actual territorial reach is
  difficult to reconstruct from later texts and scattered inscriptions. Brahmanical
  traditions gained royal support, while Buddhist monuments at Sanchi and Bharhut
  developed during the same period. Claims of systematic Buddhist persecution remain
  disputed. Royal power weakened as local rulers became independent. Vasudeva Kanva
  overthrew the last king, Devabhuti, establishing the succeeding dynasty.
