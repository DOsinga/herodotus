description: >-
  Longshan culture describes late Neolithic communities of northern China's middle and
  lower Yellow River valleys, roughly between 3000 and 1900 BCE. It includes several
  regional traditions rather than one identifiable kingdom. Archaeologists recognise it
  particularly through polished black pottery, including exceptionally thin cups.
  Farming villages developed into larger settlements, some enclosed by rammed-earth
  walls, while rich burials and specialised buildings reveal growing social differences.
  Millet cultivation, animal raising and craft production supported these communities.
  Taosi, the representative site here, had extensive walls and sharply differentiated
  graves. These remains suggest organised leadership and competition between centres,
  but do not establish named rulers or a common government. Many large settlements
  declined around 2000 BCE, while others contributed to subsequent Bronze Age
  developments.
