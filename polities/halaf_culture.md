description: >-
  Halaf culture describes Neolithic farming communities across northern Mesopotamia,
  especially the Khabur valley, southeastern Turkey, northern Syria, and northern Iraq,
  approximately 6100 to 5100 BCE. Named after Tell Halaf, it is identified through
  shared pottery and other archaeological remains rather than a known kingdom. Its
  finely painted ceramics carried geometric patterns, plants, and other designs, and
  circulated widely between settlements. Clay figurines and stamp seals add evidence of
  craft production and social practices. Excavations at Tell Sabi Abyad show gradual
  development from earlier local pottery traditions, not a sudden arrival of an entirely
  new population. No named rulers or unified government are established. Halaf
  communities increasingly adopted Ubaid material traditions through a transitional
  period, with both continuity and abandoned settlements.
