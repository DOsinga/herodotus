description: >-
  The Jambi Sultanate was a Malay state on the Batang Hari river in east-central Sumatra. The area had
  once been the heart of the Melayu kingdom and later fell under Majapahit, from which it broke away
  in the sixteenth century. The sultanate is conventionally dated from 1615. Its rulers grew rich on
  pepper, gold and timber from the upland, which they sold to Dutch, English and Chinese traders, and
  the Dutch East India Company kept a post there. A long war with Johor in the later seventeenth
  century weakened the state, and the court often struggled to control the upriver chiefs. In 1833 an
  attack on Dutch-held Palembang gave the Dutch a pretext to force the sultan to accept their
  authority.
