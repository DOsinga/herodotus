description: >-
  The Rasulid Sultanate governed much of Yemen between 1229 and 1454, with important
  royal centres at Taiz and Zabid. Umar ibn Ali took power after the last Ayyubid ruler
  departed and later secured recognition from the Abbasid caliph. His successors built a
  Sunni monarchy supported by soldiers, officials, and revenues from agriculture and
  commerce. Aden connected the state with Indian Ocean trade, bringing customs income
  from merchants carrying goods between Asia, Africa, and the Red Sea. Rasulid rulers
  sponsored schools, religious buildings, and scholarship, and some wrote works on
  agriculture and other subjects. Their authority faced persistent resistance from Zaydi
  imams in the northern highlands. Succession struggles and military rebellion weakened
  the dynasty until the Tahirids replaced it in 1454.
