description: >-
  The Old Saxon Confederation joined communities across northwestern Germany, roughly between the Rhine and Elbe,
  from around 500 until Charlemagne completed his conquest in 804. It was not a unified
  monarchy. Local nobles and free communities retained authority, while assemblies and collective military action linked
  the Westphalian, Eastphalian, Angrian, and northern Saxon regions. Accounts place a common gathering at Marklo,
  whose location remains uncertain. Saxon religious traditions and social customs distinguished these communities from their
  Christian Frankish neighbours. Charlemagne's campaigns began in 772 and met repeated resistance, including forces associated
  with Widukind. Warfare, forced conversion, deportations, and Frankish administration gradually dismantled their independence. The conquest
  created the setting for a later Saxon duchy, rather than preserving the old governing association.
