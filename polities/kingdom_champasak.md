description: >-
  The Kingdom of Champasak was a Lao monarchy along the southern Mekong, established in 1713
  during the breakup of Lan Xang. Its founder, Nokasad, belonged to the former royal family.
  Champasak and centers such as Attapeu and Stung Treng formed a network of authority rather than
  a territory with fixed modern borders. Theravada Buddhism connected the court with other Lao
  kingdoms. Settlement spread onto the Khorat Plateau, while forest products and captives moved
  through trade routes toward Siam. Siamese conquest in 1778 brought tribute and intervention in
  royal succession, although local kings continued governing. During the nineteenth century,
  Siamese and French expansion narrowed the court's authority. Incorporation into French Laos in
  1904 ended its government, but the royal lineage survived.
