description: >-
  Cahokia was a major Mississippian centre near the Mississippi River in present Illinois,
  opposite modern Saint Louis. Its chiefdom flourished from about 1050 to 1350, drawing
  people into a large settlement surrounded by farming communities. Maize cultivation
  helped support dense populations and extensive ceremonial building. Monks Mound, broad
  plazas, timber circles and elaborate burials reveal organised labour, ritual authority
  and sharp differences in status. Archaeologists infer powerful leaders from this
  evidence, but no surviving written records identify their names or describe a formal
  constitution. Cahokia exchanged materials and ideas across distant parts of North
  America. Its population declined during the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries.
  Environmental pressures, flooding and social conflict remain debated explanations for
  the dispersal of its urban community.
