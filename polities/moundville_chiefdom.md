description: >-
  Moundville was a Mississippian chiefdom centered beside the Black Warrior River in present Alabama, represented here from about 1000 to 1450. Its main settlement became a regional political and ceremonial center supported by farming communities in the surrounding valley. Archaeologists infer hereditary rank from burials, valuable ornaments, and the arrangement of platform mounds around a plaza. Leaders and elite clans occupied prominent places within that layout, though their names and exact institutions are unknown. Maize cultivation supported craft production and exchange for copper, marine shell, and other materials. Wooden defenses protected the settlement, and elaborate pottery expressed ceremonial traditions. Population declined during the fourteenth century, while people continued using the center for burial. The reasons for its decline remain uncertain.
