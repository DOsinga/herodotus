description: >-
  Karl von Dalberg's state occupied scattered territories in Germany during the Napoleonic
  era, beginning with the 1803 settlement that left him Aschaffenburg and Regensburg.
  Frankfurt joined his lands in 1806, and further changes produced the Grand Duchy of
  Frankfurt in 1810. Dalberg governed as a prince allied with Napoleon, residing in
  Aschaffenburg rather than the city that gave the later state its name. The grand duchy
  combined Frankfurt, Hanau, Fulda, Wetzlar, and Aschaffenburg, while Regensburg passed to
  Bavaria. Its constitution designated Napoleon's stepson Eugène de Beauharnais as Dalberg's
  successor. French military influence shaped its existence and administration. Dalberg
  relinquished power in 1813 as Napoleon's position collapsed, and the state was dissolved,
  with its territories redistributed among several restored German governments.
