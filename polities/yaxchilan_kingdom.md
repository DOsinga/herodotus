description: >-
  Yaxchilan was a Maya kingdom on the Usumacinta River in present Chiapas, Mexico,
  opposite Guatemala. Its recorded dynasty begins with Yopaat B'alam's accession in 359
  and continued into the early ninth century; this record estimates royal collapse
  around 830. Kings ruled from a city sheltered within a river bend, competing with
  Piedras Negras for regional influence and river trade. Warfare, captured nobles, royal
  marriages, and ritual performances appear in inscriptions and carved monuments.
  Itzamnaaj B'alam and Bird Jaguar IV enlarged the city and left records of dynastic
  claims. Sculpted doorway lintels show royal women as important participants in
  bloodletting ceremonies. Smaller centers, including Bonampak, came under its
  influence. The disappearance of royal inscriptions marks political decline, not
  immediate settlement abandonment.
