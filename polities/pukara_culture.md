description: >-
  Pukará culture developed in the northern Lake Titicaca basin of present Peru, with its main
  phase here estimated between 500 BCE and 400 CE. The large settlement of Pukará anchored a
  landscape of smaller centers and villages. Archaeological remains reveal farming, fishing,
  camelid herding, and techniques for managing water in the demanding highland environment.
  Pottery decorated with colors and incised designs, together with carved stone figures, gives the
  culture its material identity. These objects depict people, animals, and beings associated with
  ritual practice. Their distribution suggests contacts extending beyond the lake basin toward
  other Andean valleys. No secure list of rulers or form of government survives. Pukará declined
  as Tiwanaku grew, leaving artistic and architectural traditions that influenced later
  communities.
