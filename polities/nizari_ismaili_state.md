description: >-
  The Nizari Ismaili State grew from Hasan-i Sabbah's capture of Alamut in northern Iran in 1090.
  Rather than holding one continuous territory, its Iranian government controlled fortified
  centres and surrounding communities, notably in the Alborz mountains and Quhistan. Religious
  leadership and political authority were closely connected, with successive leaders directing a
  minority Ismaili community amid hostile neighbours. Strongholds, local supplies, diplomacy, and
  targeted killings helped resist Seljuk pressure. The familiar label Assassins reflects accounts
  often written by enemies and should not reduce the state to those tactics. Alamut also
  supported scholarship and religious teaching. Mongol armies compelled its last ruler to
  surrender in 1256 and dismantled the central government; surviving Ismaili communities and
  Syrian castles had their own later histories.
