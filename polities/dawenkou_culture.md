description: >-
  The Dawenkou culture developed in eastern China, especially Shandong and neighbouring
  regions, between approximately 4100 and 2600 BCE. It is named after an excavated site near
  Tai'an rather than a documented state. Settlements, pottery, tools, and graves reveal
  farming communities that raised millet, cultivated rice in some areas, and kept animals
  including pigs. Distinctive objects include stemmed cups, tripod vessels, jade ornaments,
  and bone or ivory implements. Burials became increasingly unequal over time, with some
  containing valuable goods and others very little. This suggests growing differences in
  wealth and status, but does not establish one government for the whole culture. Local
  leadership and ritual authority remain archaeological interpretations. Dawenkou traditions
  developed into the regional Longshan culture during the later Neolithic.
