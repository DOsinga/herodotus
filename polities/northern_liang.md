description: >-
  Northern Liang ruled the Hexi Corridor of northwestern China from 397 to 439 during the
  Sixteen Kingdoms period. Founded in rebellion against Later Liang, it initially placed
  the Han official Duan Ye on the throne with Juqu military backing. Juqu Mengxun
  overthrew him in 401 and established his family's monarchy. The Juqu belonged to the
  Lushuihu, whose origins remain debated. Zhangye served as capital before the court moved
  to Guzang in 412. Campaigns against neighbouring states, including Western Liang,
  eventually brought the corridor under its authority. Buddhist patronage and
  cave-building helped make the region a religious centre. Northern Wei conquered Guzang
  in 439, ending this government. Juqu survivors later established a remnant at Gaochang,
  distinct from the polity represented here.
