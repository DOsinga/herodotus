description: >-
  The Western Kara Khanid Khanate governed Transoxiana from Samarkand after the wider Kara Khanid realm divided during the 1040s. This record begins with separate western administration in 1041. Its Muslim Turkic khans ruled an established urban region that included Bukhara, combining dynastic traditions with Islamic institutions. Ibrahim Tamghach Khan sought greater stability by limiting the grants of territory that encouraged family rivalry. Relations with religious scholars could be contentious, and the Seljuks intervened in Samarkand in 1089. Native khans continued governing under Seljuk, and later Qara Khitai, overlordship. Their period left important buildings, including Bukhara's Kalyan minaret, and contributed to Islamic culture. Khwarazmian expansion finally ended the western state in 1212, when its last khan, Uthman, was defeated and executed.
