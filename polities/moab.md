description: >-
  Moab was an Iron Age kingdom east of the Dead Sea in today's Jordan, represented from
  approximately 1000 to 582 BCE. Its kings ruled from Dibon over uplands whose northern
  boundaries shifted through conflict with neighbouring peoples. The Moabites spoke a
  language closely related to Hebrew and worshipped Chemosh as their chief deity. The
  ninth century Mesha Stele offers a royal account of victory over Israel and building
  works, providing evidence alongside biblical narratives that tell events differently.
  Agriculture and livestock supported the kingdom, while its location connected it to
  trade routes. Moab later paid tribute to Assyrian rulers but retained its own
  monarchy. Babylonian expansion ended that political independence, conventionally
  associated with Nebuchadnezzar's campaign around the estimated closing date.
