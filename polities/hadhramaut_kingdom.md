description: >-
  The Kingdom of Hadhramaut occupied eastern Yemen and adjoining parts of southern Arabia, with its
  capital at Shabwa. Its emergence is placed around 800 BCE, and it survived until Himyar's conquest
  around 300 CE. Kings governed alongside senior officials who could represent tribes, professional
  groups, or royal authority. Inscriptions in the Hadramautic language document rulers, religious
  dedications, and relations with neighbouring South Arabian kingdoms. Frankincense production and
  trade connected its inland centres and coastal ports with distant markets. Temples and ritual
  banquets reflected a polytheistic religious world shared with its neighbours but expressed through
  local traditions. Hadhramaut expanded into former Qatabanian lands before losing independence. The
  Himyarite king Shammar Yahrish incorporated it into his larger realm, ending its separate monarchy.
