description: >-
  The Samanid Emirate governed Transoxiana and, at its height, much of northeastern Iran
  from the ninth century until 999. Its autonomous rule is represented here from
  approximately 864, as Tahirid authority weakened. Hereditary Muslim emirs acknowledged
  the Abbasid caliph while directing their own administration and campaigns. Ismail
  Samani united the family's possessions and made Bukhara a major regional capital. The
  court supported Persian literature and scholarship, helping establish a lasting
  Persian Islamic cultural tradition. Trade linked its cities with steppe peoples, while
  Turkish soldiers became increasingly important in its armies. Powerful commanders and
  fiscal strains weakened later rulers. Ghaznavids took southern territories, and
  Karakhanid forces captured Bukhara in 999. Attempts by a surviving prince failed to
  rebuild the former government.
