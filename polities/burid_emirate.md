description: >-
  The Burid Emirate governed Damascus and surrounding parts of Syria from 1104 to 1154. Its
  founder, Toghtekin, had served the Seljuk ruler Duqaq and took control after Duqaq's death. Although
  its rulers acknowledged overlords, they managed the city's affairs through their own court and military
  leadership. The dynasty's name came from Toghtekin's son Buri. Damascus occupied a difficult position between
  Crusader territories and the growing power of the Zengids to the north. Its leaders alternated
  warfare with negotiated cooperation to protect their independence. The city resisted the Second Crusade's siege
  in 1148, but its diplomatic position continued to weaken. In 1154, Nur al-Din took Damascus
  and united it with his Aleppo centred state, bringing separate Burid government to an end.
