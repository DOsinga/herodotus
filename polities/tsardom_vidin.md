description: >-
  The Tsardom of Vidin was a Bulgarian monarchy on the Danube, centered on the
  fortified city of Vidin. Ivan Alexander assigned the region to his son Ivan Sratsimir
  in 1356, creating a royal government during Bulgaria's political fragmentation.
  Hungarian forces occupied it in 1365 and displaced Sratsimir; negotiations and
  military pressure restored him in 1369. His court ruled northwestern Bulgarian lands
  while balancing relations with Hungary, neighboring Balkan rulers, and the advancing
  Ottomans. After the fall of Tarnovo in 1393, Vidin remained an surviving Bulgarian
  realm. Sratsimir supported the Christian campaign that ended in defeat at Nicopolis
  in 1396. Ottoman forces then took control of Vidin. Later claims by his son
  Constantine do not extend this record's period of government.
