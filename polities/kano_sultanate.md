description: >-
  The Sultanate of Kano represents the Islamic phase of the Hausa monarchy centred on Kano
  in northern Nigeria, from about 1350 to 1807. Islam spread through visiting merchants and
  scholars, while older religious practices remained influential among the population.
  Kings, known as sarki, governed through palace officials and a council of leading title
  holders. Muhammadu Rumfa strengthened royal institutions in the fifteenth century,
  supported Islamic learning, and expanded the busy commercial life of the walled city.
  Agriculture, leatherworking, textile production, and trade across the Sahara contributed
  to Kano's wealth. Relations with Bornu and neighbouring Hausa states alternated between
  tribute, alliance, and conflict. The Fulani jihad overthrew the ruling dynasty in 1807,
  bringing Kano under the Sokoto Caliphate as an emirate.
