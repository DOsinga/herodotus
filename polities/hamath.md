description: >-
  Hamath was an Iron Age kingdom centred on the city now called Hama, on Syria's Orontes River. Its emergence around 1000 BCE is approximate. Kings ruled a territory shaped by the interaction of Luwian and Aramean traditions, leaving inscriptions that complement Assyrian accounts. King Irhuleni helped organize the coalition opposing Shalmaneser III at Qarqar in 853 BCE, preventing immediate Assyrian annexation. Later, King Zakkur ruled both Hamath and Luhuti and commemorated surviving a siege by neighbouring rulers. Assyrian expansion steadily reduced the kingdom, taking towns, imposing tribute, and deporting inhabitants. In 720 BCE, Ilu-Bi'di led another revolt. Sargon II defeated it, devastated Hamath, and incorporated its lands into Assyria, ending the city's government as an independent kingdom in the region.
