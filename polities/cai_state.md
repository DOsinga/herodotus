description: >-
  Cai was a small hereditary state in ancient China, associated with the Zhou royal Ji
  family. Its earlier origins lay in a fief granted to a brother of the Zhou founder, but
  this record begins in 770 BCE, when royal authority weakened after the court moved east.
  Cai's lords governed during the Spring and Autumn period and held the rank of marquis.
  Located near the expanding state of Chu, Cai repeatedly faced invasion and pressure from
  its larger neighbour. Its seat moved from Shangcai to Xincai and later to Xiacai as rulers
  tried to preserve their state. Chu conquered Cai in 447 BCE. The name lived on in local
  place names and became a surname adopted by descendants and inhabitants.
