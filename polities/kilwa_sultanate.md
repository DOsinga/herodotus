name: Kilwa Sultanate
description: >-
  The Kilwa Sultanate was a Muslim trading state centred on Kilwa Kisiwani, an island off present
  Tanzania, from the tenth century to 1784. Its founding tradition names Ali ibn al-Hassan, a
  prince from Shiraz, but the early chronology rests on later chronicles. Sultans governed a
  network of coastal towns whose merchants and local elites kept considerable influence. Control
  of Sofala linked Kilwa to gold from the southern African interior, and its wealth paid for
  mosques, stone houses and the palace of Husuni Kubwa. Rival ministers and disputed successions
  weakened the court before the Portuguese sacked the town in 1505 and held it until 1512. The
  restored sultanate never regained its earlier trading power, and in 1784 the Omani rulers of
  Zanzibar conquered Kilwa.
