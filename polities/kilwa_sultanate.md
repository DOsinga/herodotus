name: Kilwa Sultanate
description: >-
  The Kilwa Sultanate was a Muslim trading state centred on Kilwa Kisiwani, an island off
  present Tanzania, from the tenth century to 1513. Its founding tradition names Ali ibn
  al-Hassan, a prince from Shiraz, but the early chronology rests on later chronicles. Sultans
  governed a network of coastal towns whose merchants and local elites retained considerable
  influence. Control of Sofala linked Kilwa to gold from the southern African interior, while
  ships carried ivory and other goods across the Indian Ocean. Wealth supported mosques, stone
  houses, and the palace of Husuni Kubwa. Rival ministers and disputed successions weakened
  the court before Portuguese intervention. Their attack in 1505, followed by imposed rulers
  and commercial restrictions, broke the sultanate's earlier coastal trading power.
