description: >-
  The Sultanate of Maguindanao governed parts of Mindanao in the southern Philippines from the early sixteenth century. This record follows it from its foundation in 1515 to American occupation in 1899. Sharif Kabungsuwan helped establish Islam and a ruling family in the Cotabato valley. Sultans governed through alliances with local datus, combining religious authority, kinship, warfare, and control of trade. River routes connected inland communities with coastal markets and overseas merchants. Under Sultan Qudarat, Maguindanao became a major opponent of Spanish expansion, while maintaining relations with Dutch and British traders. Successions and rival centers gradually weakened its cohesion. Spanish pressure increased during the nineteenth century, and American occupation ended territorial rule. Sultanly titles and family traditions continued beyond that change.
