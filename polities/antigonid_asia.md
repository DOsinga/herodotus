description: >-
  The Kingdom of Antigonus grew from Alexander the Great's former possessions in Asia, beginning
  with Antigonus' appointment as general in 321 BCE. His power rested on Macedonian armies,
  provincial administrators, and control of wealthy cities across Asia Minor and Syria. Celaenae
  in Phrygia represents his early territorial base. By defeating rivals among Alexander's
  successors, he briefly became the strongest contender to reunite the empire. Antigonus and his
  son Demetrius adopted royal titles in 306 BCE after a naval victory over Ptolemy. Their
  ambitions provoked a coalition of rival rulers. Antigonus was killed at Ipsus in 301 BCE, and
  his Asian realm was divided, chiefly between Seleucus and Lysimachus. Demetrius survived, but
  his later Macedonian kingdom was a separate political development.
