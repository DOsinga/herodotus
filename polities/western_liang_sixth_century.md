description: >-
  Western Liang was a small imperial state around Jiangling in the middle Yangtze
  valley, in present day Hubei, China, from 555 to 587. Xiao Cha, a grandson of the
  Liang dynasty's founder, established its court with support from Western Wei after the
  destruction of Emperor Yuan's government. It preserved a branch of the Xiao royal
  family and claimed continuity with Liang while other southern courts ruled elsewhere.
  Three emperors governed its reduced territory: Xiao Cha, Xiao Kui, and Xiao Cong.
  Western Wei, Northern Zhou, and Sui exercised influence and maintained garrisons, but
  the local court continued administering its affairs. In 587, Sui abolished the
  monarchy and annexed its lands. Xiao Xian's later Liang restoration was a separate,
  short lived state.
