description: >-
  Sanxingdui culture is known from Bronze Age remains near Guanghan in China's Sichuan Basin, dated here approximately 1700 to 1150 BCE. Excavations reveal a large walled settlement with workshops, residential areas and spaces associated with ritual activity. Its most striking finds are bronze masks with prominent eyes, towering human figures, elaborate trees, jade objects and gold ornaments deposited in pits. These objects show skilled production and organised ceremonial life distinct from contemporary Shang traditions in the central plains. They do not identify named rulers or establish the government's form. Archaeologists often connect Sanxingdui with early Shu, but later stories about Shu kings cannot securely explain individual finds. Related discoveries at Jinsha suggest regional cultural continuity after Sanxingdui's main settlement declined.
