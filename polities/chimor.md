description: >-
  Chimor was a kingdom on the northern coast of Peru, developing around 900 and lasting until its conquest by the Incas around 1470. Its rulers governed from Chan Chan, an adobe city near modern Trujillo. A hierarchy of regional centres directed labour, irrigation, farming and the movement of goods toward the capital. Canals made cultivation possible in dry coastal valleys, while fishing and skilled craft production supported the economy. Chimú artisans worked metals, textiles and distinctive pottery, often under elite supervision. Successive rulers built separate palace compounds, leaving earlier estates to other relatives after a king's death. Expansion brought many coastal communities under Chimor's authority. Inca conquest ended the kingdom's independence and transferred some of specialists into imperial service elsewhere.
