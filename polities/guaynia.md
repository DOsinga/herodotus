description: >-
  Guaynía was the Taíno chiefdom of southern Puerto Rico, the island the Taíno called Borikén,
  whose cacique was the most powerful on the island when the Spanish arrived. Its ruler,
  Agüeybaná, lived near the bay of Guayanilla, and other caciques recognised his leadership. Taíno
  chiefdoms were ruled by hereditary caciques who organised farming villages, ballcourt ceremonies
  and the worship of zemís, and who led their people in war against Carib raiders. Agüeybaná
  welcomed Juan Ponce de León in 1508, but Spanish encomiendas soon forced the Taíno into labour
  in the gold fields. His brother and successor, also called Agüeybaná, led a rising in 1511 that
  the Spanish crushed, ending the independence of the island's chiefdoms.
