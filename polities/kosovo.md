  Kosovo is a landlocked state in the central Balkans that declared independence from Serbia in
  2008. Its capital, Pristina, houses a parliamentary republic with an elected assembly, a president, and
  a government headed by a prime minister. Most inhabitants are ethnic Albanians, alongside Serbian and
  other communities with languages and religious traditions. The declaration followed the 1998–1999 war, NATO intervention,
  and a period of United Nations administration beginning in 1999. Many countries recognize Kosovo, but
  Serbia disputes its independence and international recognition remains divided. Relations with Serbia, especially concerning the
  northern municipalities and minority rights, continue to shape its politics. The modern state carries a
  long local heritage of Byzantine, Serbian, Ottoman, and Yugoslav rule without continuing those earlier governments.
