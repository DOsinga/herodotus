description: >-
  Luodian was a Yi chiefdom on the plateau of western Guizhou, China, around present Anshun,
  ruled by chiefs of the Bole branch of the Nasu Yi. Yi genealogies trace the Bole
  settlement there to about the third century; Chinese sources first mention its spirit lord
  Apei in 836. In the 840s Tang recognised the Bole chief as king of Luodian, one of several
  Yi kingdoms it courted as a buffer against Nanzhao. Song writers placed the kingdom west
  of Yizhou, with its own script and its seat at Puli, and it sold horses from Dali to the
  Song. It rivalled the neighbouring Luoshi kingdom for leadership of the Yi tribes, whose
  chiefs ruled as spirit lords. Its chief Luo Azi submitted to the Yuan, who made Luodian a
  pacification office.
