description: >-
  The Phagmodrupa dynasty governed central Tibet from its seat at Nedong in the
  Yarlung valley between 1354 and 1618. Changchub Gyaltsen, a lord of the Lang
  family and head of the Phagmodru myriarchy, overthrew the Sakya regime that had
  ruled Tibet for the Yuan emperors, and built an autonomous kingdom as Mongol
  power declined. He divided the land into districts run by appointed officials,
  revived the memory of the old Tibetan empire and issued a law code that lasted
  into the twentieth century. Monks of the Phagmodru order held the throne, and
  family quarrels repeatedly weakened it. After 1435 the Rinpungpa ministers took
  Tsang and for a time Lhasa, leaving the dynasty mainly in Ü. The Tsangpa ruler
  conquered Ü in 1618, ending its government.
