description: >-
  The Chagatai Khanate governed much of Central Asia as a Mongol state from 1266 to 1347. It
  originated in the inheritance of Chagatai, a son of Genghis Khan, and included both settled
  Transoxiana and eastern pastoral lands. Almaliq was a royal centre. Khans ruled with
  influential military nobles, while Persian administrators helped manage towns and taxation.
  Rivalries within the Mongol imperial family shaped its emergence from imperial control. Its
  rulers balanced nomadic traditions with the needs of farming and commercial communities, and
  some attempted monetary and administrative reforms. Conversion to Islam deepened changes
  within the ruling elite but also provoked opposition. Conflict between western and eastern
  interests eventually divided the state into the Western Chagatai Khanate and Moghulistan in
  1347.
