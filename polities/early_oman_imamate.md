description: >-
  The early Ibadi Imamate of Oman united religious and political leadership in southeastern Arabia during two periods, 749 to 751 and 793 to 893. Its imams were chosen through consultation among religious scholars and tribal leaders, with public allegiance confirming their authority. This distinguished the office from an inherited royal throne, although tribal rivalries still shaped government. Al-Julanda led the first imamate, which Abbasid forces destroyed in 751. A second unified government emerged in 793, with Nizwa an inland center. Ibadi institutions connected local communities while rulers defended Oman against outside intervention. Abbasid conquest ended this territorial imamate in 893. Ibadi religious life and later imams continued, but those later developments were not an uninterrupted survival of this early state.
