description: >-
  Chorasmia, the oasis of the lower Amu Darya south of the Aral Sea, was a satrapy of the
  Achaemenid Empire until around 400 BCE, when it broke away and became an independent kingdom.
  Around 328 BCE its king Pharasmanes visited Alexander the Great in Sogdiana and offered an
  alliance, but Alexander did not invade, and Chorasmia stayed outside the Hellenistic kingdoms
  that followed. Its rulers built large fortified enclosures, palaces and irrigation canals, and
  archaeology has found hundreds of settlements and a distinctive culture with its own script and
  coinage. The names of most of its kings, and how far steppe powers such as the Kangju ruled
  over it, are uncertain. Al-Biruni dated the start of the Afrighid line of Khwarazmshahs, which
  continued the kingdom, to 305 CE.
