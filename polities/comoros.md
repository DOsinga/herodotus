  Comoros is an island republic in the western Indian Ocean, between Madagascar and the
  African mainland. It declared independence from France in 1975, with Moroni as its
  capital. Grande Comore, Anjouan, and Mohéli formed the new state, while Mayotte
  remained under French administration and became a continuing territorial dispute.
  Governments changed repeatedly through coups, authoritarian rule, and interventions
  involving foreign mercenaries. Disputes over power between the islands also produced
  separatist movements, especially in the late twentieth century. A union constitution
  introduced in 2001 sought to balance island autonomy with national government. The
  presidency and elected institutions have since undergone constitutional changes.
  Arabic, African, Malagasy, and French influences shape the islands' society, while
  agriculture and overseas migration remain important to livelihoods.
