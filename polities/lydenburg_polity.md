description: >-
  The Lydenburg Heads site represents an Early Iron Age farming community in
  present South Africa's Mpumalanga region, approximately between 500 and 800.
  It is known for seven hollow terracotta heads found near modern Lydenburg, now
  Mashishing. Six have human features and one resembles an animal. Two larger
  examples could have been worn, while smaller heads differ in size and
  decoration. Patterned neck bands and animal figures show skilled ceramic work
  and suggest symbolic meanings that remain uncertain. Researchers have proposed
  ceremonial or initiation uses, but these interpretations do not establish a
  named government or rulers. Associated pottery places the objects within a
  settled farming context. The dates and map point are estimates, marking
  archaeological evidence rather than a documented kingdom.
