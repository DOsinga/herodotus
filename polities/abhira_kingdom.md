description: >-
  The Abhira kingdom ruled the western Deccan in the third and fourth centuries, after Satavahana
  power collapsed in northern Maharashtra. The Abhiras were a pastoral people known earlier in
  Sindh, Saurashtra and Malwa, and some of their chiefs had served the Western Satraps as
  generals. Ishvarasena, the first king known by name, left a Sanskrit inscription in the Buddhist
  caves at Nashik, and the era of 248 to 249 is often linked to his accession. The Puranas count
  ten Abhira kings but disagree on how long they ruled, giving 67 or 167 years. Merchant guilds
  took endowments in the kingdom, which suggests settled government. The capital is unknown. The
  Traikutakas, who followed them in the Konkan and around Nashik, may have begun as their
  subordinates.
