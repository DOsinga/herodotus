description: >-
  The Tuʻi Vavaʻu were the Fīnau ʻUlukālala line of chiefs who ruled the northern Tongan island
  group of Vavaʻu, and for a time Haʻapai, during the Tongan civil wars. The dynasty descended
  from a junior branch of the Tuʻi Kanokupolu that had settled in Vavaʻu in the eighteenth
  century. Fīnau ʻUlukālala II extended his power to Haʻapai and helped plan the murder of the
  Tuʻi Kanokupolu Tukuʻaho in 1799, which opened half a century of war. He raided Tongatapu with
  cannon from the captured ship Port au Prince, whose survivor William Mariner described his
  court. His son cut links with Tongatapu, and Fīnau ʻUlukālala III secured Vavaʻu by 1820. On his
  death in 1833 he left Vavaʻu to Tāufaʻāhau of Haʻapai.
