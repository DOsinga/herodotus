description: >-
  Tabal was a group of Neo-Hittite kingdoms in south central Anatolia, in the plateau country
  north of the Taurus mountains in present Turkey. After the collapse of the Hittite empire, local
  rulers there kept Luwian language and Hittite royal traditions alive, leaving hieroglyphic
  inscriptions and rock reliefs. Assyrian records name a land of Tabal with several kings from the
  ninth century BCE, among them the great king Wasusarma, while the kingdom of Tuwana under
  Warpalawa lay to its south. Tabal's rulers paid tribute to Assyria when its armies came, while
  dealing with Phrygia and Urartu. Sargon II deposed King Ambaris for intriguing with Phrygia and
  Urartu and annexed Tabal as an Assyrian province in 713 BCE.
