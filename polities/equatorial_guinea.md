description: >-
  Equatorial Guinea became independent from Spain in 1968, bringing together mainland Rio
  Muni and islands including Bioko and Annobon. The mainland borders Cameroon and Gabon,
  while Malabo on Bioko served as the historic seat of government. Its constitution
  establishes a presidential republic, but power has remained tightly concentrated. The first
  president, Francisco Macias Nguema, imposed a violent dictatorship that drove many people
  into exile. Teodoro Obiang overthrew him in 1979 and built another authoritarian
  administration dominated by his family and ruling party. Offshore oil discoveries
  transformed state revenues during the 1990s, without ensuring broadly shared prosperity.
  Spanish colonial rule left a distinctive linguistic legacy, alongside local African
  languages. The country continues as one state despite changes of leadership and
  administration.
