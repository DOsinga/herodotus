description: >-
  The Pashalik of Timbuktu governed towns along the Niger bend in present Mali,
  independently from 1612 until 1833. Its origins lay in Morocco's defeat of Songhai in
  1591, when an invading army established garrisons at Timbuktu, Gao and Djenné. The
  soldiers and their descendants, known as the Arma, became a local ruling group. From
  1612 they chose their own pashas while continuing to acknowledge Moroccan sovereignty
  formally. Authority rested on the garrisons and river towns rather than firm control of
  the surrounding countryside. Rival Arma factions repeatedly replaced their leaders,
  weakening government. Tuareg groups increasingly imposed their own influence and tribute
  demands. The expanding Massina state took Timbuktu in 1833, ending the pashalik's rule
  after generations of declining regional power.
