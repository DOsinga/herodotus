description: >-
  The Tuʻi Kanokupolu was a ruling chiefly line in Tonga, originating around 1610 in western
  Tongatapu. Its founder Ngata was a son of the Tuʻi Haʻa Takalaua ruler and a Samoan mother, and his
  authority rested on kinship, marriage alliances, and supporting chiefly families. Initially
  responsible for Hihifo, the line gradually became a major source of political power alongside
  Tonga's older royal titles. Its chiefs remained junior in sacred rank to the Tuʻi Tonga, showing
  that ritual precedence and practical government did not necessarily coincide. Rivalries and warfare
  fragmented authority during the eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries. Tāufaʻāhau became Tuʻi
  Kanokupolu in 1845 and, as George Tupou I, used this position to build the unified Tongan monarchy
  that succeeded it.
