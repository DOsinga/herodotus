description: >-
  Cyrene was a Greek settlement and independent political centre in the uplands of
  northeastern Libya, traditionally founded in 631 BCE. Colonists from Thera
  established the city under Battus, whose descendants ruled as the Battiad
  dynasty. Fertile land, horse breeding, and exports of silphium, a prized plant,
  helped sustain its wealth. Its port at Apollonia connected inland farms with
  Mediterranean trade. Relations with neighbouring Libyan peoples involved both
  cooperation and conflict as settlement expanded. Royal government eventually
  gave way to a republic, while Cyrene developed religious sanctuaries and a
  philosophical school associated with Aristippus. This record covers the kingdom
  and republic before Ptolemy's intervention in 322 BCE. The city continued under
  later rulers, but no longer as the entity described here.
