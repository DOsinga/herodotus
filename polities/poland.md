description: >-
  Modern Poland regained independence in 1918 after more than a century of partition among neighbouring empires. Governed from Warsaw, the republic fought to establish its borders, including a successful defence against Soviet forces in 1920. National parliamentary institutions survived alongside increasingly authoritarian government after Józef Piłsudski's 1926 coup. German and Soviet invasions in 1939 destroyed domestic state control, beginning the occupation gap recorded here, although an exiled government and underground resistance continued. A Polish government returned in 1945 within borders shifted westward, under a communist system closely aligned with the Soviet Union. Solidarity's challenge helped bring negotiated democratic political change in 1989. Poland subsequently developed a parliamentary republic, joined NATO and the European Union, and retained its renewed independent statehood.
