  Niue is a raised coral island in the South Pacific, between Tonga, Samoa, and the Cook Islands.
  It has governed itself in free association with New Zealand since 1974, following a referendum
  and constitutional settlement. Alofi is the capital. An elected assembly selects the head of
  government, while the New Zealand monarch remains head of state. The association preserves New
  Zealand citizenship and provides assistance without making Niue simply a locally administered
  province. Niue conducts external relations and participates in international organizations,
  although it is not a United Nations member. Its resident population contrasts with a larger
  Niuean community overseas, especially in New Zealand. Migration, cyclone damage, and economic
  dependence shape public life, alongside efforts to sustain the Niuean language.
