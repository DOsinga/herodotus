description: >-
  Cheng-Han was a monarchy based at Chengdu in the Sichuan basin from 304 to 347. It emerged
  as the Western Jin dynasty struggled with rebellion, famine, and displaced populations. Li
  Xiong, whose family belonged to the Ba Di community, captured Chengdu and established
  Cheng, first claiming princely authority and then an imperial title. His rule offered
  comparative stability within a China divided among competing states. After his death,
  disputes over succession brought violence within the ruling family. Li Shou seized power
  in 338 and renamed the state Han, giving the combined polity its modern historical name.
  His successor Li Shi faced an Eastern Jin invasion led by Huan Wen. The capture of Chengdu
  in 347 ended the kingdom's separate government.
