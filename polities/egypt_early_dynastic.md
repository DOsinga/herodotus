description: >-
  Early Dynastic Egypt covers the First and Second dynasties, conventionally dated from about
  3100 to 2686 BCE. It began with the unification of Upper and Lower Egypt under a royal
  government. Narmer is closely associated with this process, although later traditions and
  archaeological evidence do not resolve every question about the first kings. Memphis became
  an important administrative centre, while Thinis and Abydos retained royal and religious
  significance. Kings presented themselves through divine authority and appointed officials to
  manage the country. Labels, sealings and early hieroglyphic inscriptions document estates,
  goods and administration. Royal tombs and changing burial practices reveal growing resources
  and hierarchy. Egyptian artistic and religious conventions developed during these centuries,
  providing the foundations for the succeeding Old Kingdom.
