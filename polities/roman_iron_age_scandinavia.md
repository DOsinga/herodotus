description: >-
  The Roman Iron Age in Scandinavia is an archaeological period conventionally dated from
  1 to 400 CE, not a single kingdom or government. Communities across Denmark, Norway, and
  Sweden lived beyond Roman rule but encountered its influence through trade, travel, and
  imported goods. Coins, bronze vessels, glass, weapons, and rich graves reveal
  long-distance contacts and differences in wealth. Farming, metalworking, and
  boatbuilding supported settlements, while bog deposits preserve equipment, clothing, and
  evidence of ritual practices. Early runic inscriptions also belong to this period. Gudme
  on Funen represents an important centre, not a capital for all Scandinavia. The
  archaeological record points to varied local societies and emerging elites; their
  political boundaries and rulers cannot be reconstructed as one unified Scandinavian
  state.
