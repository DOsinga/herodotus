  Bulgaria is a state in the eastern Balkans, bordered by the Danube and the Black Sea, with Sofia as its capital. This record starts in 1908, when the Bulgarian principality declared independence from the Ottoman Empire and became a tsardom. Ambitions to unite Bulgarian populations drew it into the Balkan Wars and both world wars, with repeated territorial losses. After Soviet intervention during the Second World War, communist control replaced the monarchy, formally abolished in 1946. The socialist government industrialised the country and aligned it closely with the Soviet Union. Communist monopoly ended in 1989, followed by a parliamentary republic and competitive elections. Bulgaria joined NATO and the European Union. These changes of government remain part of one continuing state.
