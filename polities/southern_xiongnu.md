description: >-
  The Southern Xiongnu emerged in 48 CE when eight tribes acclaimed Bi as chanyu and separated from
  the northern confederation. They settled along and inside the Han frontier, especially in what is
  now Shanxi, retaining their own ruler and military organization while accepting tribute relations
  with China. Trade, court subsidies, and service as frontier auxiliaries tied them closely to the
  Han. Their cavalry helped fight the Northern Xiongnu, but raids, internal disputes, and Chinese
  intervention weakened the chanyu's authority. Late second-century rebellions fragmented the
  confederation and drove some leaders into the Han civil wars. In 216 Cao Cao detained Huchuquan and
  reorganized the remaining people into five divisions supervised by Chinese officials, ending their
  separate government while their communities survived.
