description: >-
  The Mamluk Sultanate governed Egypt and Syria from Cairo between 1250 and 1517. It emerged when military slaves and their commanders displaced the Ayyubid rulers of Egypt. Sultans depended on powerful military households, making succession a contest of alliances and force rather than straightforward inheritance. The ruling elite initially included many Turkic soldiers and later increasingly Circassian recruits. Mamluk armies defeated the Mongols at Ain Jalut in 1260 and eliminated the remaining Crusader strongholds, including Acre in 1291. Control of pilgrimage routes and Red Sea commerce strengthened Cairo's importance. Rulers sponsored mosques, schools, and other buildings that remain prominent today. Plague, financial problems, and military rivalry troubled later rule. Ottoman victories in Syria and Egypt ended the sultanate in 1517.
