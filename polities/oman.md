  Oman is a sultanate on the southeastern Arabian Peninsula, governed from Muscat. This
  record begins in 1970, when Qaboos bin Said deposed his father and renamed the
  country, previously Muscat and Oman. The hereditary sultan retained ultimate
  authority while building ministries, armed forces, schools, hospitals, and transport
  networks with growing oil revenues. Government troops, supported by foreign allies,
  ended the Dhofar rebellion during the following decade. The country combines coastal
  trading traditions, desert regions, and an Ibadi Muslim heritage. Consultative
  institutions developed without replacing the monarchy's central power. Oman pursued a
  comparatively independent foreign policy, maintaining relations across regional
  divisions and sometimes mediating disputes. The accession of Haitham bin Tariq in
  2020 continued the same state under a new sultan.
