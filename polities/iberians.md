description: >-
  Iberian culture developed among communities of eastern and southern Iberia,
  represented here from approximately 600 to 19 BCE. Ullastret is a representative
  town, not the capital of a Iberian state. These communities spoke Iberian languages
  and shared aspects of pottery, writing, metalworking, and religious practice, while
  their political organization varied locally. Contact with Phoenician, Greek, and
  Carthaginian traders encouraged exchange and urban growth. Archaeology reveals
  fortified settlements, social differences, and stone sculptures such as figures of
  seated women. Iberian warriors served in Mediterranean armies, and their lands became
  a theater of conflict between Carthage and Rome. Roman conquest transformed local
  institutions and culture. The ending date follows Rome's completion of conquest
  across the peninsula, rather than a Iberian government's fall.
