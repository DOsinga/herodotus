description: >-
  The Pala Empire arose in Bengal around 750, when regional chiefs selected Gopala as king after disorder. His descendants governed a monarchy rooted in Bengal and Bihar, using officials and subordinate rulers to administer their lands. Dharmapala and Devapala expanded its influence across northern India and competed with the Pratiharas and Rashtrakutas for Kannauj. Pala rulers supported Buddhist monasteries and learning centres, including Nalanda, Vikramashila, and Somapura, while also patronising Hindu institutions. Sculpture, manuscripts, and religious exchange connected their courts with Tibet and Southeast Asia. Rebellions and rival kingdoms repeatedly reduced their power, despite recoveries under Mahipala and Ramapala. The remaining Bengal kingdom fell to the Senas by 1161; later rulers bearing Pala names in Bihar are not included here.
