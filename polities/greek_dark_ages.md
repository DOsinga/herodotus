description: >-
  The Greek Dark Ages describes communities of mainland Greece and the Aegean after the
  collapse of Mycenaean palace government. This cultural record uses the conventional span
  from about 1100 to 800 BCE and places its marker at Lefkandi on Euboea. It does not
  represent a single kingdom or common government. Archaeology shows smaller settlements,
  changing burial practices, ironworking, and pottery decorated with geometric patterns.
  Linear B administration disappeared, leaving few written records of political life. Rich
  burials and imported objects at Lefkandi reveal elites and overseas contacts, challenging
  older pictures of isolation. Recovery varied between regions rather than following one
  uniform course. Expanding exchange, renewed writing with the Greek alphabet, and emerging
  city states marked the transition toward Archaic Greece.
