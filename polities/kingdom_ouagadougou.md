description: >-
  The Kingdom of Ouagadougou, or Wogodogo, was the largest of the Mossi kingdoms in the basin
  of the White Volta in present-day Burkina Faso. Mossi traditions trace their ruling houses
  to horsemen from Dagbon and Mamprugu in northern Ghana, and the kingdom took shape around
  Ouagadougou late in the fifteenth century; 1495 is the estimate used here. Its king, the
  Mogho Naba, was regarded as the senior Mossi ruler and governed through ministers at court
  and provincial chiefs, while other Mossi kingdoms such as Yatenga, Tenkodogo and Fada
  N'gourma ruled themselves. The court long kept its own religion against Islam, and fought
  wars with Songhai. French troops occupied Ouagadougou in 1896, and the kingdom became part
  of the colony later called Upper Volta.
