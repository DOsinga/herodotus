description: >-
  The Pechenegs were a Turkic nomadic people who dominated the steppes north of the Black Sea
  from the end of the ninth century to the late eleventh. Driven west by the Oghuz and the
  Khazars, they crossed into the Pontic steppe around 895 and displaced the Magyars, who moved on
  into the Carpathian Basin. The Byzantine emperor Constantine VII described them as eight
  tribes, each with its own chief and grazing lands, without a single ruler over them all. They
  traded with the Byzantine towns of the Crimea, fought and allied with the Rus' and Byzantium in
  turn, and in 972 killed Prince Svyatoslav of Kiev at the Dnieper rapids. Pushed west by the
  Cumans, they were crushed by the Byzantines and Cumans at Levounion in 1091.
