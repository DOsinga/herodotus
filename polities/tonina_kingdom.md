description: >-
  Toniná was a Maya kingdom in the Ocosingo valley of present-day Chiapas, Mexico, with a recorded royal history extending from 514 to around 910. Its rulers used the title Divine Lord of Po, the ancient name associated with the city. A great terraced acropolis supported temples, palaces, and monuments overlooking the valley. Inscriptions describe royal accessions, subordinate lords, rituals, and warfare, especially rivalry with nearby Palenque. Toniná captured Palenque's king in 711 and gained regional influence. Sculptures of bound prisoners and elaborate stucco reliefs preserve the court's public image. Its final dated monument belongs to 909, shortly before political collapse. Later occupation of the site does not demonstrate that its royal government continued beyond the end of the Classic period.
