description: >-
  The Shang ruled a Bronze Age kingdom in the Yellow River region of China from about 1600 to 1046
  BCE. Its kings led warfare and religious ceremonies, drawing authority from royal ancestors and
  divination. Capitals moved over time, with Yin near modern Anyang becoming the principal centre
  around 1300 BCE. Excavated palaces, royal tombs, bronze vessels, and sacrificial remains reveal a
  wealthy court supported by farmers and specialised craftspeople. Inscriptions carved on oracle bones
  preserve questions about harvests, battles, births, and ritual, making the Shang the earliest
  Chinese dynasty securely documented by contemporary writing. Royal power extended through allied and
  dependent communities rather than uniform administration everywhere. The Zhou defeated the last
  Shang king, Di Xin, ending the ruling kingdom.
