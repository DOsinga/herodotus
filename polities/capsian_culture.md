description: >-
  The Capsian culture names forager communities of the Maghreb, especially inland Tunisia and eastern Algeria. This record uses an estimated span of 10000 to 6000 BCE, with Gafsa representing the region that gave the culture its name. Archaeologists distinguish Typical and Upper Capsian traditions through stone tool forms and manufacturing methods. People hunted animals ranging from large cattle to hares, collected snails, and used resources in landscapes then wetter than today. Ostrich eggshell beads and containers, shell ornaments, ochre, and decorated objects reveal craft and symbolic practices. Burials provide evidence of customs, but no written accounts identify rulers or governments. Later Neolithic communities retained some Capsian traditions while adopting food production, making its ending a cultural transition rather than conquest.
