description: >-
  The Okunev culture belonged to early Bronze Age communities in the Minusinsk Basin of southern
  Siberia, along the middle and upper Yenisei. This record uses approximate dates of 2500 to 1800 BCE.
  It is an archaeological culture rather than a documented kingdom, and its government and leaders are
  unknown. People raised cattle, sheep, and goats, supplementing their food with hunting, fishing, and
  some farming. Copper and bronze tools show developed metalworking, while decorated pottery
  distinguishes their material traditions. Graves enclosed by stone slabs provide much of the
  evidence. Particularly striking are carved standing stones and rock pictures showing faces, animals,
  and elaborate symbolic figures. These remains suggest shared ritual practices, but do not establish
  a unified state or hereditary monarchy.
