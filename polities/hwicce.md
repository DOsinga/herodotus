description: >-
  The kingdom of the Hwicce occupied part of western England around the Severn valley,
  including much of later Worcestershire and Gloucestershire. Its conventional beginning in
  577 follows the battle of Deorham, although its formation and exact borders remain
  uncertain. Native kings ruled a region where British and Anglo-Saxon communities
  interacted and Christianity developed early. Mercian dominance followed the battle of
  Cirencester in 628, but local rulers continued to govern rather than disappearing
  immediately. Royal families supported churches and monasteries, and the bishopric of
  Worcester, founded around 680, preserved an enduring regional identity. Charters reveal
  kings sharing or dividing authority before their titles diminished. By approximately 780,
  Mercian officials had replaced royal government, bringing the kingdom directly within
  Mercia's expanding administration.
