description: >-
  The Quba Khanate was a khanate in the northeastern Caucasus, centred on the town of Quba
  in present-day Azerbaijan, which governed itself from 1747 to 1806. Its khans came from a
  local family that had served the Safavids and Nader Shah as governors, and after Nader's
  death Husayn Ali Khan made the khanate independent in practice. His son Fath Ali Khan, who
  ruled from 1758 to 1789, built it into the strongest power on the western Caspian, taking
  Derbent and Salyan, dominating Baku and Shamakhi, and seeking Russian support against his
  rivals. After his death the khanate shrank back to Quba and Derbent, and Sheikh Ali Khan
  was caught between Russia, Qajar Iran and the Dagestani lords. Russian troops occupied
  Quba in 1806 and annexed the khanate.
