description: >-
  The Autonomous Administration of North and East Syria developed during the
  Syrian civil war, with self-governing cantons proclaimed in 2014. Centred on
  northeastern Syria, it brought Kurdish, Arab, Syriac, and other communities
  within institutions shaped by democratic confederalism. Local communes and
  councils formed its administrative framework, with paired male and female
  leadership and commitments to linguistic rights and women's participation. The
  Kurdish Democratic Union Party held influence, and critics questioned how far
  formal decentralisation matched political practice. Its territory expanded as
  the Syrian Democratic Forces fought Islamic State, supported by an international
  coalition. It sought autonomy within Syria rather than recognition as a separate
  country. Relations with Damascus, Turkish intervention, displacement, and
  changing front lines tested its institutions and ambitions.
