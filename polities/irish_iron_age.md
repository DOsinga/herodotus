description: >-
  Iron Age Ireland describes the island's archaeological societies before the written histories of its early kingdoms. This record uses conventional dates of about 500 BCE to 400 CE, although discoveries suggest ironworking began earlier. It represents no single state, and surviving evidence cannot identify a government for the island as a whole. Finds include iron weapons, decorated metalwork, wooden trackways, earthworks, and objects showing contact with Britain and continental Europe. Navan Fort and Tara contained major ceremonial buildings whose purposes must be inferred rather than read from contemporary accounts. Ptolemy recorded tribal names in the second century CE, but their boundaries remain uncertain. Roman goods reached Ireland without Roman conquest. Later Christianity and written traditions mark the transition into history.
