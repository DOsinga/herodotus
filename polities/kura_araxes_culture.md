description: >-
  The Kura–Araxes culture developed in the southern Caucasus and spread into eastern Anatolia,
  northwestern Iran, and parts of the Levant. Its approximate span here, 4000 to 2000 BCE, covers
  a varied archaeological tradition rather than a single kingdom. Communities farmed grain, kept
  livestock, and made distinctive burnished pottery with both red and black surfaces. Houses,
  hearths, burial practices, and skilled metalworking connect settlements across a large area,
  although local differences remained important. Copper and other metal objects reveal craft
  knowledge and contacts beyond the Caucasus. The Ararat plain is a representative centre, not a
  known capital. Archaeology does not establish one government or named rulers, and the spread of
  material styles need not indicate conquest by one unified migrating population.
