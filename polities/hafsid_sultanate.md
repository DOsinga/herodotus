description: >-
  The Hafsid Sultanate ruled Ifriqiya, chiefly modern Tunisia with parts of eastern Algeria
  and western Libya, from 1229 to 1574. Its founder, Abu Zakariya, broke with the Almohad
  caliphate while governing the region and established a dynasty based at Tunis. Hafsid
  monarchs combined Islamic authority with a royal administration, and some claimed the
  title of caliph. Tunis benefited from Mediterranean commerce, while immigrants from al
  Andalus contributed to its economy and culture. The state experienced succession disputes,
  regional divisions, and periods of recovery rather than uninterrupted strength. During the
  sixteenth century, Spanish and Ottoman intervention increasingly determined its fortunes.
  Ottoman forces finally captured Tunis in 1574, ending Hafsid rule and incorporating the
  region into the Ottoman imperial system of government.
