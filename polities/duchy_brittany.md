description: >-
  The Duchy of Brittany occupied the northwestern peninsula of present day France from 939
  until its union with France in 1532. It emerged after Alan II and his allies expelled
  Viking forces and restored Breton government. Dukes ruled alongside powerful local lords,
  while towns, monasteries and later representative estates shaped political life. Brittany's
  position between England and France brought both opportunities and repeated intervention.
  Rival claimants fought a long succession war in the fourteenth century, supported by those
  neighbouring kingdoms. Later dukes developed stronger institutions and defended their
  political autonomy through diplomacy. Anne of Brittany's marriages to French kings drew the
  duchy closer to the crown. Formal union ended separate ducal government, although Brittany
  retained distinctive laws and privileges afterward.
