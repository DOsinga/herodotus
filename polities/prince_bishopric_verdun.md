description: >-
  The Prince-Bishopric of Verdun was an ecclesiastical state of the Holy Roman Empire on the
  Meuse, recorded here from 1250 until France occupied the city in 1552. Emperor Otto III
  granted the bishops comital rights in 997, and they ruled a small territory around Verdun as
  princes of the Empire. Within the city the burghers won wide self-government and the status
  of a free imperial city, and quarrels between citizens and bishops recurred throughout the
  later Middle Ages. Surrounded by the duchies of Bar, Lorraine and Luxembourg and close to
  France, the bishopric depended on outside protectors. Henry II of France took Verdun
  together with Metz and Toul in 1552, the beginning of the Three Bishoprics under French
  rule.
