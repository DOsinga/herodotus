description: >-
  The Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth united the Kingdom of Poland and Grand Duchy of Lithuania through
  the Union of Lublin in 1569. It covered much of eastern Europe and contained Polish, Lithuanian,
  Ruthenian, Jewish and other communities. An elected monarch governed alongside a parliament
  dominated by the nobility, whose privileges limited royal power. The two constituent lands retained
  institutions within a shared political framework. Grain exports, religious diversity and
  aristocratic estates shaped its society, while peasants faced growing burdens of serfdom.
  Seventeenth-century wars, rebellions and invasions weakened the state, and parliamentary deadlock
  increased foreign interference. Reformers adopted a constitution in 1791, but Russia, Prussia and
  Austria partitioned the Commonwealth. Their division in 1795 extinguished its government without
  ending its peoples' political aspirations.
