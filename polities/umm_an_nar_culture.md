description: >-
  The Umm an-Nar culture flourished in southeastern Arabia, across modern Oman and the
  United Arab Emirates, approximately between 2600 and 2000 BCE. Named after an island
  near Abu Dhabi, it is known through settlements, pottery, stone vessels, towers, and
  circular communal tombs built with carefully fitted stonework. Its communities
  supplied copper and stone to Mesopotamia and handled goods moving between that region
  and the Indus Valley. This trading region is associated with ancient Magan, but the
  archaeological culture does not establish a single kingdom or a known system of
  government. Towers often protected wells, showing the importance of water. Around 2000
  BCE, changing settlement and trade patterns led gradually into the Wadi Suq cultural
  period rather than a documented conquest.
