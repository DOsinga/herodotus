description: >-
  The Vijayanagara Empire governed much of southern India from 1336 to 1646.
  Founded by Harihara and Bukka, it grew around the fortified capital of
  Vijayanagara beside the Tungabhadra River, whose ruins survive at Hampi.
  Successive royal houses ruled through provincial governors and military
  chiefs, supported by agriculture, taxation, and trade through coastal ports.
  Its courts patronized Hindu temples and literature in several languages, while
  armies employed varied religious communities. Krishnadevaraya's early
  sixteenth-century reign marked a high point of expansion and cultural
  activity. Rivalry with the Deccan sultanates culminated in defeat at Talikota
  in 1565 and the destruction of the capital. The monarchy continued from
  southern centers, but declining authority and increasingly independent
  regional rulers undermined it until its final collapse.
