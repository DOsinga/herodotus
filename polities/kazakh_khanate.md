description: >-
  The Kazakh Khanate arose in the central Eurasian steppe around 1465, when Kerei and Janibek led followers away from Abu'l-Khayr's Uzbek realm. Its khans descended from Genghis Khan and governed through alliances among mobile pastoral communities and their leaders. Control of towns along the Syr Darya, including Syghanaq, complemented the steppe economy and connected the khanate with settled trade. Kasym expanded its power in the early sixteenth century, while later rulers faced succession struggles and warfare with neighboring states. Tauke is remembered for strengthening customary law. The three zhuzes increasingly pursued separate political courses, especially under Dzungar pressure and growing Russian influence. The period ending in 1780 represents the khanate, whose unity and authority varied considerably across its constituent groups.
