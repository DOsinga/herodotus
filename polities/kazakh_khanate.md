description: >-
  The Kazakh Khanate arose in the central Eurasian steppe around 1465, when Kerei and Janibek led
  followers away from Abu'l-Khayr's Uzbek realm. Its khans descended from Genghis Khan and
  governed through alliances among mobile pastoral communities and their leaders. Control of towns
  along the Syr Darya, including Syghanaq, complemented the steppe economy and connected the
  khanate with settled trade. Kasym expanded its power in the early sixteenth century, while later
  rulers faced succession struggles and warfare with neighboring states. Tauke is remembered for
  strengthening customary law. After Tauke's death about 1718 the three zhuzes pursued separate
  courses under Dzungar and Russian pressure. Kenesary Kasymov revived the khanate in 1841, until
  Russian forces defeated him in 1847.
