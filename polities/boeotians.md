description: >-
  The Boeotians were the Aeolic-speaking people who held the plain of Boeotia in central
  Greece during the Early Iron Age, between the fall of the Mycenaean palace at Thebes
  around 1200 BCE and the rise of the Archaic city-states around 700 BCE. Greek tradition
  held that they came from Thessaly two generations after the Trojan War, and Thessalian
  cults and place names in Boeotia support a migration. After the palace on the Cadmea
  burned, much of Thebes was abandoned and a smaller settlement survived nearby, the
  Hypothebai of Homer's catalogue of ships. Local chiefs, the basileis of later tradition,
  led the scattered villages, whose people made geometric pottery and shared cults that
  later bound the Boeotian League together.
