description: >-
  The Taifa of Córdoba was a small Muslim state around the former caliphal capital in southern Iberia,
  governed separately from 1031 to 1070. After the caliphate collapsed, leading citizens entrusted
  authority to Abu al Hazm Jahwar, who ruled through consultation with ministers and judicial
  officials rather than claiming the caliphal throne. This distinctive civic government sought
  stability after years of civil war and the city's loss of imperial power. Jahwar's successors
  gradually made authority more personal and hereditary, while Córdoba faced pressure from larger
  neighbouring taifas. Rival interests drew it into struggles involving Toledo and Seville. Seville
  ultimately annexed the city, ending its separate Jahwarid government. The annexation is dated 1070
  here, although some accounts place the event in 1071.
