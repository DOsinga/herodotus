description: >-
  Kazakhstan became independent in 1991 as the Soviet Union dissolved, inheriting a territory of steppe, desert, and mountains in Central Asia. It also reaches west of the Ural River into Europe. Its government developed as a presidential republic with strong central executive power. Almaty served as capital until 1997, when government moved to the northern city now called Astana. Kazakh language and national identity coexist with a substantial Russian-speaking population shaped by imperial settlement and Soviet history. Oil, gas, uranium, and other minerals underpin much of the economy. The new state relinquished inherited nuclear weapons and pursued relations with several major powers. Political authority has remained concentrated, while economic development and the management of regional ties shape its continuing history.
