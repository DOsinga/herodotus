name: Cook Islands
description: >-
  The Cook Islands have governed themselves since 1965 as a Pacific island state in free
  association with New Zealand. Fifteen islands spread across a wide area of Polynesia, with
  Avarua on Rarotonga serving as the capital. The system is a parliamentary constitutional
  monarchy: elected representatives and a prime minister conduct government, while a
  representative performs the monarch's local functions. New Zealand citizenship and
  continuing ties allow migration, but New Zealand cannot legislate for the islands.
  Assistance with defence and foreign affairs is provided at their request, and the government
  maintains its own international relationships. Tourism is an important source of income,
  alongside fishing and other activities. Polynesian languages, chiefly traditions, and local
  institutions remain important within this modern constitutional framework.
