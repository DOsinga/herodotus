description: >-
  Waithali, also called Vesali, was an early kingdom in Arakan, on the western coast of
  present Myanmar. This record uses the traditional chronology of 370–818, not securely dated foundations and
  conquests. Its brick walled capital stood near a tidal creek and linked the Kaladan valley
  with coastal trade. Chandra kings governed a court known through coins and the Sanskrit Anandachandra
  inscription, which records royal predecessors and religious gifts. Buddhism flourished alongside Hindu traditions, showing strong
  connections with the Indian world without making this kingdom part of India. Maritime exchanges tied
  Arakan to Bengal, Sri Lanka, and other Asian regions. Its eventual decline belongs to an
  uncertain sequence of shifting Arakanese centers, rather than a precisely documented final overthrow or annexation.
