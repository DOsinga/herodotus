description: >-
  The County of Nassau was the lordship of the counts of Nassau on the lower Lahn in present Hesse
  and Rhineland-Palatinate, named after their castle above the town of Nassau. In 1255 the
  brothers Walram II and Otto I divided it: Otto took the northern lands around Siegen and
  Dillenburg, and Walram the southern lands around Wiesbaden, Idstein and Weilburg. Walram's son
  Adolf was elected King of the Romans in 1292 and was deposed and killed in battle in 1298. The
  Walramian counts gathered towns and pledges in the Taunus, including the Usingen lands in 1326,
  and gained town charters for Weilburg and Idstein. In 1344 the sons of Gerlach I divided the
  Walramian lands, and the Weilburg line became the main branch.
