description: >-
  The Indo-Parthian Kingdom emerged around 19 CE when Gondophares established independent
  rule in eastern Iranian lands and expanded into Afghanistan and northwestern South Asia.
  Its greatest extent included Sistan, Arachosia, Gandhara and parts of the Punjab, with
  Taxila serving as an important centre. Kings governed through a loose arrangement of
  subordinate rulers rather than a uniformly administered empire. Coins and inscriptions
  preserve much of the evidence for their changing territories and successions. Iranian
  royal traditions mixed with Greek and Indian forms, while Buddhism, Hinduism and
  Zoroastrianism coexisted across their domains. Kushan expansion took Gandhara during the
  later first century, greatly reducing the kingdom. Indo-Parthian rulers nevertheless
  survived farther west until Sasanian conquest around 226 ended their government in the
  region.
