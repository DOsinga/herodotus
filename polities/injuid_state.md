description: >-
  The Injuid state was a monarchy in southern Iran that emerged from the collapse of
  Ilkhanid authority in 1335 and ended in 1357. Its rulers controlled Fars and, at times,
  Isfahan, with Shiraz their main centre. The family had gained power managing royal estates
  under the Mongol Ilkhans; its name reflected those estates. Rival brothers and competing
  military leaders contested the inheritance before Abu Ishaq secured Shiraz in 1343. His
  court supported illustrated manuscripts, architecture and literary life, helping establish
  the city's reputation for book arts. Attempts to expand toward Kerman and Yazd brought
  conflict with the Muzaffarids. They captured Shiraz and then Isfahan, where Abu Ishaq was
  taken in 1357. His execution ended Injuid rule and transferred its lands.
