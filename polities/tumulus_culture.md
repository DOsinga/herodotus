description: >-
  The Tumulus culture names a Middle Bronze Age archaeological horizon across Central Europe,
  represented here approximately from 1600 to 1300 BCE. Southern Germany provides the map's
  representative point, not a known capital. Its defining evidence is burial beneath earthen
  mounds, often with bronze weapons, ornaments, and other goods. Related material appears across
  neighbouring regions, including Austria, Bohemia, and the Carpathian Basin, with local
  variations rather than a single society. Bronze working and patterns of exchange connect these
  communities to wider European networks. Rich graves and weapons suggest differences in status
  and the importance of armed elites, but no named rulers or shared government are known. Later
  Urnfield traditions brought widespread cremation practices, marking changes in burial customs
  and material culture.
