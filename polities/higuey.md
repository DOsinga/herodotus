description: >-
  Higüey was the easternmost Taíno chiefdom of Hispaniola, covering the eastern end of the
  present-day Dominican Republic and the nearby island of Saona. Its last cacique,
  Cotubanamá, was known as a strong warrior and ruled from a town near the present city of
  Higüey. It stayed largely outside Spanish control during the 1490s. In 1502 a Spanish
  soldier set a dog on a cacique at a landing, and the killing provoked a revolt that the
  Spanish answered with campaigns under Juan de Esquivel in 1503 and 1504. Cotubanamá fled
  to Saona, where he was captured, and he was hanged in Santo Domingo. The Spanish founded
  the town of Salvaleón de Higüey, which Juan Ponce de León governed before he went to
  Puerto Rico.
