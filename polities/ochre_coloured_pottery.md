description: >-
  The Ochre Coloured Pottery culture identifies Bronze Age communities across northern
  India, especially the upper Ganges and Yamuna plains, roughly between 2000 and 1500 BCE.
  Its distinctive pottery has a reddish coating that leaves an ochre stain when handled,
  sometimes with painted bands or incised decoration. Small agricultural settlements used
  houses of wood, reeds and mud, cultivated crops and kept livestock. Copper weapons, tools
  and ornaments frequently occur in the same wider region, although many copper hoards lack
  secure excavation contexts. Connections with late Harappan traditions and the meaning of
  impressive burials remain debated. The evidence does not establish one kingdom or known
  rulers. Later pottery traditions, including Painted Grey Ware, followed, with different
  sites changing at different times locally.
