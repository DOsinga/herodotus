description: >-
  The Omani Empire grew from the Yarubid imamate established in 1624 into a maritime power linking Arabia with the western Indian Ocean. Its rulers united much of Oman and expelled the Portuguese from Muscat in 1650. Omani fleets then challenged Portuguese positions along the East African coast, eventually taking Mombasa. Rustaq, Muscat, and Zanzibar served as royal centres at different times. After civil wars and Persian intervention, the Al Busaid family established a new ruling line. Under Said bin Sultan, commerce tied Arabian ports to African ivory, enslaved people, and Zanzibar's expanding clove plantations. His court increasingly focused on Zanzibar. When he died in 1856, competing inheritances divided the empire into the separate sultanates of Muscat and Oman and Zanzibar.
