description: >-
  Kinal was a Maya governing center in northeastern Petén, Guatemala, whose main
  urban phase is estimated at 600 to 900. It stood on a limestone plateau
  between seasonal swamps, with steep approaches and substantial walls
  protecting its inhabitants. A large palace complex, raised plazas, and a broad
  causeway show organized building and administrative activity, especially
  during the eighth century. Kinal probably replaced nearby Río Azul as the
  region's principal center after that city's earlier decline. Unlike many Maya
  capitals, it lacks carved stelae and altars, leaving its rulers and political
  relationships poorly documented. Its designation as a kingdom rests mainly on
  architecture and regional settlement patterns. Later occupation around Río
  Azul should not be mistaken for restored independent government there.
