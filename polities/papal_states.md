description: >-
  The Papal States were territories in central Italy governed by the pope as a temporal ruler,
  distinct from his authority over the Catholic Church. Their conventional beginning is Pepin's
  donation in 756. Rome was the chief centre, though papal residence and effective control varied over
  time. Popes administered the lands through officials while contending with local nobles, city
  governments and neighbouring powers. Renaissance rulers expanded and consolidated their territories,
  which included much of Lazio, Umbria, the Marche and Romagna. French revolutionary intervention and
  Napoleon's annexations interrupted papal government before restoration in 1814. Italian unification
  then stripped away most provinces. The Kingdom of Italy captured Rome in 1870, ending this
  territorial state; the later Vatican City arose through a separate settlement.
