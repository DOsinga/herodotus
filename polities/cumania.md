description: >-
  Cumania, which Muslim writers called the Desht-i Kipchak, was the great steppe from the Danube
  to the Irtysh held by the Cuman and Kipchak tribes from the eleventh century until the Mongol
  conquest. The Kipchaks emerged from the Kimek confederation on the Irtysh and moved west early
  in the eleventh century, reaching the Black Sea steppe by the 1050s and pushing out the
  Pechenegs. They never formed a single state: separate khans led tribal unions on the Dnieper,
  the Don, the Volga and further east, with seasonal towns such as Sharukan. They raided and
  intermarried with the Rus' princes, served Georgian and Hungarian kings as soldiers and sold
  slaves through the Crimean ports. The Mongols defeated them in campaigns between 1222 and 1241.
