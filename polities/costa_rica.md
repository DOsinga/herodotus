  Costa Rica is a Central American republic between Nicaragua and Panama, with Caribbean and
  Pacific coasts. It became a separate state in 1838 as the Federal Republic of Central
  America broke apart, following independence from Spain in 1821. San José developed as its
  capital, and coffee exports helped shape nineteenth century society and politics.
  Presidents and an elected legislature govern under a constitution established after the
  civil war of 1948. That conflict was followed by the abolition of the standing army, a
  defining feature of the country's institutions. Public education, health services, and
  protected natural areas became prominent parts of national policy. Agriculture remains
  important, while tourism and newer industries have broadened an economy once closely tied
  to plantation exports.
