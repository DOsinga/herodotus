description: >-
  The Salihid Kingdom was an Arab Christian client realm along the eastern Roman frontier in
  Syria and neighbouring parts of Jordan. Its governing period is estimated here at 400 to
  502, when the Zokomid ruling house led the Salihids before Ghassanid ascendancy. Their
  leaders acted as kings and tribal commanders, collecting dues from frontier tribes and
  helping protect Roman territory against raids. The family's Christianity formed part of
  its identity; King Dawud was remembered for founding a monastery, represented by the
  approximate map point near Deir Dawud. Surviving Greek and later Arabic accounts are
  sparse, so its seat, succession and exact chronology remain uncertain. Ghassanid victory
  ended Salihid primacy, not the continued existence of Salihid families or their
  descendants afterwards.
