description: >-
  The Republic of Ragusa governed Dubrovnik and a narrow stretch of the
  southern Adriatic coast from 1358 to 1808. Noble families controlled its councils,
  while a rector served short terms that limited personal power. Its merchants
  linked Balkan caravan routes with Mediterranean shipping, making the small republic wealthy
  without building a large territorial empire. Ragusa accepted Hungarian and later Ottoman
  overlordship, paying tribute while managing its own diplomacy and trade. Careful neutrality
  helped it survive beside stronger neighbours, especially Venice. The devastating earthquake of
  1667 damaged the city and weakened its economy, but the government endured.
  French troops entered in 1806, and Napoleon's authorities abolished the republic in
  1808, ending centuries of independent civic government on this placed Croatian coast.
