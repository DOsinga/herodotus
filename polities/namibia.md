  Namibia is a republic on southern Africa's Atlantic coast, independent from South Africa
  since 1990. Its territory had previously been German South West Africa and then came under
  South African administration, which imposed apartheid and resisted international demands
  for independence. The liberation movement SWAPO fought a lengthy struggle that became
  entangled with wars in neighbouring Angola and Cold War rivalries. A United Nations
  supervised transition led to elections and a constitution protecting rights and
  establishing an executive president, parliament, and independent courts. Windhoek became
  the capital of the independent state, with Sam Nujoma as its first president. Walvis Bay
  remained under South African control until 1994. Mining, fishing, tourism, and farming
  sustain an economy shaped by aridity and persistent inequality.
