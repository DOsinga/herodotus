description: >-
  The Tibetan Empire united much of the Tibetan Plateau and became a major Asian power
  between approximately 618 and 842. Its kings emerged from the Yarlung ruling line,
  with Songtsen Gampo extending authority beyond the Lhasa region through conquest and
  diplomacy. A court of monarchs, ministers, and noble families directed armies and
  governed the expanding realm. Tibetan writing developed during this period, while
  royal patronage helped establish Buddhism alongside older religious traditions.
  Imperial forces competed with Tang China and controlled routes through Central Asia,
  briefly occupying the Chinese capital Chang'an in 763. Later treaties recognized the
  two powers' frontiers. After Langdarma's assassination, succession struggles broke
  unified government apart. Later Tibetan states inherited imperial traditions but did
  not continue one empire.
