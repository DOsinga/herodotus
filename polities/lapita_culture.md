description: >-
  Lapita culture describes seafaring communities whose settlements spread across the western
  Pacific between approximately 1600 and 500 BCE. First appearing in the Bismarck Archipelago,
  their distinctive material culture reached islands including Vanuatu, New Caledonia, Fiji,
  Tonga and Samoa. The name comes from an archaeological site in New Caledonia. Pottery decorated
  with toothed stamps is its best known feature, alongside shell ornaments, stone adzes and
  obsidian tools. Settlers combined gardening and domestic animals with fishing and shellfish
  gathering, while exchange networks moved useful materials over long distances. These remains
  document migration and shared cultural practices, not a single Pacific state or known
  government. Regional pottery traditions changed over time, while descendants of these
  communities contributed to later Oceanic and Polynesian societies.
