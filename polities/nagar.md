description: >-
  Nagar was a Bronze Age kingdom centred on Tell Brak in the Khabur basin of northeastern
  Syria. Around 2600 BCE, the city expanded and developed large administrative buildings.
  Records from Ebla and neighbouring centres show kings, subordinate towns, diplomatic
  alliances, conflict, and dynastic marriage. Nagar dealt with Ebla and Mari as a major
  regional power, controlling routes across northern Mesopotamia. Its rulers also supplied
  prized equids to other courts. Destruction around 2300 BCE preceded Akkadian control,
  under which Naram-Sin built a palace and administrative complex. After Akkad's collapse,
  Nagar again became an independent kingdom, represented here around 2150 to 2000 BCE.
  Hurrian royal names survive on seals from this later phase. Both periods remain
  approximate reconstructions from texts and archaeology.
