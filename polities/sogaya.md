description: >-
  Sogaya was one of the separate Gaya states in southern Korea, associated with the Goseong
  region near the peninsula's southern coast. Its formation is estimated around 300 from the
  changing settlements and burial customs that accompanied the emergence of Gaya from earlier
  Byeonhan communities. Later tradition includes Sogaya among six kingdoms founded by legendary
  brothers, but it does not establish a dependable royal chronology. Archaeology and regional
  historical accounts support a distinct local political centre rather than government by one
  unified Gaya kingdom. Its neighbours belonged to networks known for ironworking, farming and
  maritime exchange. Silla gradually conquered the Gaya states; 562 marks an upper bound for
  Sogaya's absorption, not a securely documented final campaign against its particular ruling
  court.
