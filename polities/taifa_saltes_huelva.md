description: >-
  The Taifa of Saltés and Huelva was a Muslim kingdom on the Atlantic coast of al-Andalus, at the
  mouths of the Odiel and Tinto rivers in present Huelva province. It was ruled by the Arab Bakrid
  family from the island town of Saltés. It emerged from the civil war that broke up the Caliphate
  of Córdoba after 1009, when local commanders and families took power in their own districts. The
  Bakrids governed a small territory between the larger kingdoms of Seville and Badajoz. The
  geographer al-Bakri, one of the great scholars of al-Andalus, was born into the ruling family.
  From the 1040s the expanding taifa of Seville under al-Mu'tadid absorbed one neighbour after
  another. In 1051 Seville took Saltés and Huelva.
