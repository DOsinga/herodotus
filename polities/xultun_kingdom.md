description: >-
  Xultun was a Maya kingdom in northern Guatemala, northeast of Tikal, whose royal history is
  represented here from roughly 400 to 900. Its urban centre contained pyramids, plazas, ballcourts, reservoirs
  and carved monuments. Inscriptions and images reveal a court headed by kings and supported by
  titled officials and religious specialists. A painted room preserves scenes of court life alongside calendrical
  and astronomical calculations, showing that scribes worked close to royal authority. Nearby residential areas and
  limestone quarries formed part of its urban setting. The last dated royal monument belongs to
  889, providing evidence for the kingdom's late survival during the Classic Maya collapse. Its final
  political disappearance is estimated rather than securely dated, and earlier settlement predates this royal period.
