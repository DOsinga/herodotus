description: >-
  The Jemdet Nasr period was an archaeological phase in southern Mesopotamia, approximately 3100
  to 2900 BCE. Its name comes from the Iraqi site where distinctive painted pottery and early
  tablets were excavated. Similar finds occur at Uruk, Nippur, Ur, and other settlements, so the
  label describes shared material practices rather than one kingdom. Large buildings, cylinder
  seals, and accounting tablets show organized institutions managing agricultural produce,
  crafts, and food distributions. Writing became more abstract during this period, moving toward
  the forms of cuneiform. Decorated vessels were uncommon and often associated with higher
  status settings. These discoveries reveal hierarchy and administration but do not securely
  identify rulers or constitutions. The succeeding Early Dynastic period provides clearer
  evidence of separate city states.
