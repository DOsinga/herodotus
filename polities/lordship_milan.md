description: >-
  The Lordship of Milan governed a growing territory in northern Italy from 1259 until
  its elevation to a duchy in 1395. It emerged from the city's communal politics as the
  Della Torre family concentrated authority in the hands of a lord. Their rivals, the
  Visconti, won control under Archbishop Ottone, though struggles between the families
  continued into the early fourteenth century. Visconti rulers expanded beyond Milan
  through war, diplomacy, and the submission of neighboring towns. Wealth from trade and
  manufacturing supported this territorial growth, while conflicts with the papacy and
  other Italian powers shaped its politics. Gian Galeazzo Visconti consolidated the
  family's possessions and obtained a ducal title from King Wenceslaus in 1395, giving
  the government a new princely identity.
