name: "Turk Shahi Kingdom"
description: >-
  The Turk Shahi kingdom governed Kabulistan and Gandhara from about 665 to 822, across parts of
  present Afghanistan and Pakistan. Its royal house replaced the Nezak rulers during struggles against expanding Arab
  armies. Kabul served as a summer seat, while Hund on the Indus was a winter centre.
  The kings supported Buddhism within a region where several religious traditions coexisted. Coins combined inherited Iranian
  forms with royal titles and imagery, providing important evidence for rulers whose histories are otherwise fragmentary.
  The court maintained diplomatic connections with Tang China and repeatedly resisted Muslim invasions, sometimes negotiating tribute
  without surrendering local rule. Around 822, the Brahmin minister Kallar displaced the last Turk Shahi king
  and founded the succeeding Hindu Shahi government there.
