description: >-
  Tayma is a large oasis in northwestern Arabia, on the caravan route between Medina and Dumat
  al-Jandal. This record covers its self-rule outside any empire. After Rome annexed Nabataea
  in 106, Tayma lay beyond Roman reach. A third-century inscription names a man with a Jewish
  name as governor of the town, and Arabic tradition remembers a Jewish community whose
  best-known figure was the sixth-century poet al-Samaw'al, lord of the fortress of al-Ablaq.
  The oasis passed to the caliphs in the seventh century. Later it again ran its own affairs:
  around 1170 Benjamin of Tudela reported that a Jewish prince governed it, and in later
  centuries Arab clans tied to the Shammar held it until the Rashidis of Ha'il took it.
