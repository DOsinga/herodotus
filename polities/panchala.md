description: >-
  Panchala occupied territory in northern India's upper Ganges plain, mainly within modern Uttar
  Pradesh. Its beginnings are placed here around 1100 BCE, although the early chronology
  is approximate. Vedic texts link it closely with the Kurus, and the Mahabharata gives it a
  prominent role. Its territory had
  northern and southern divisions, associated with Ahichchhatra and Kampilya. The Nanda
  rulers of Magadha absorbed Panchala around the middle of the fourth century BCE, and it
  passed to the Mauryas. After their fall, independent kings ruled again from Ahichchhatra.
  They are known almost only from their copper coins, which name rulers such as Agnimitra,
  Indramitra and Bhanumitra beside images of the gods. The Kushans held the region for a
  time. The last king, Achyuta, was defeated by Samudragupta around 350 CE.
