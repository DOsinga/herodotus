description: >-
  The Principality of Capua governed part of southern Italy around the Volturno valley and
  Terra di Lavoro. Its separate government began when Pando broke from Salerno in 861.
  Lombard rulers competed with neighbouring princes, bishops and outside powers, sometimes
  combining Capua with Benevento under shared rulers while maintaining local government.
  Norman Richard of Aversa conquered the principality in 1058, replacing its Lombard
  ruling house. The Norman princes became important partners and rivals of the papacy, but
  their position weakened as Roger II consolidated his Sicilian kingdom. Roger seized
  Capua in 1134. Robert II briefly recovered power in 1137 and again in 1155, creating
  interruptions rather than continuous independence. Sicilian conquest in 1156 finally
  ended the separate court and government there.
