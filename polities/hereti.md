description: >-
  Hereti occupied the southeastern Caucasus around Shaki, covering areas now divided between
  eastern Georgia and northwestern Azerbaijan. This record follows a separate principality
  from 787 and the kingdom established when Grigol Hamam took a royal title in 893. Local
  rulers governed a frontier shaped by Georgian, Armenian and Caucasian Albanian
  connections, while dealing with Arab and neighbouring powers. Towns traded toward Armenia
  and Persia. Hereti fought with Kakheti and Abkhazia and lost eastern holdings to stronger
  neighbours. Queen Dinar is associated with a shift from Oriental Orthodox Christianity to
  the Georgian Orthodox confession. The late succession dates remain disputed. The record
  ends with Bagrat III's annexation in 1010; later combined Kakheti and Hereti governments
  are not treated as unchanged continuity.
