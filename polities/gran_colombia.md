description: >-
  Gran Colombia was the republic formed in 1819 during the struggle against Spanish rule in
  northern South America. Governed from Bogotá, it united territories corresponding chiefly to
  Colombia, Venezuela, Ecuador, and Panama. Simón Bolívar became president, while Francisco de
  Paula Santander helped direct civil administration. The 1821 constitution created national
  institutions intended to hold the liberated regions together under a strong central government.
  Independence campaigns gave the union prestige, but distance, regional interests, and disputes
  over presidential power strained it. Bolívar's efforts to preserve unity increasingly
  conflicted with opponents seeking constitutional arrangements or greater local autonomy.
  Venezuela and Ecuador separated in 1830, and the remaining union dissolved in 1831. Its central
  territory continued as New Granada, predecessor of modern Colombia.
