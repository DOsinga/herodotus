description: >-
  The Shaki Khanate was a khanate in the southern foothills of the Greater Caucasus, in
  present-day northwestern Azerbaijan, which governed itself from 1743 until it accepted
  Russian protection in 1805. Haji Chalabi, a local notable, revolted against Nader Shah in
  1743 and held Shaki against the shah's army. He went on to fight Georgia and his
  neighbours, and for a time controlled parts of Shirvan. His successors ruled from Shaki
  and, after floods destroyed the old town in 1772, from Nukha, where they built the khan's
  palace that still stands. Family feuds and interventions by Quba, Karabakh and Georgia
  unsettled the dynasty in the late eighteenth century. Salim Khan accepted Russian
  protection in 1805, and Russia abolished the khanate in 1819.
