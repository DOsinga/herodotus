description: >-
  Coosa was a Muskogean speaking chiefdom in the southeastern region of present day
  United States, represented here from 1400 to 1560. Its main settlement, known
  archaeologically as Little Egypt, stood beside the Coosawattee River in northwestern
  Georgia. A chief based there exercised influence over smaller chiefdoms stretching
  into Tennessee and Alabama. Platform mounds, a central plaza, pottery, and village
  remains connect its communities with the wider Mississippian cultural world. Hernando
  de Soto's expedition entered Coosa in 1540, leaving accounts of its ruler and
  settlements. European contact brought violence and diseases that contributed to
  population loss and political disruption. The chiefdom declined during the sixteenth
  century, but surviving communities continued elsewhere, including settlements along
  the Coosa River in what became Alabama.
