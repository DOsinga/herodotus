description: >-
  Tế Giang was a small independent domain in the Red River region of present northern Vietnam, ruled by Lã Đường during the brief tenth century warlord period. Its separate government is estimated here to have emerged around 965, when the weakening Ngô order left regional military leaders competing for power. Lã Đường occupied the area now associated with Văn Giang in Hưng Yên Province and adopted the title Lã Tá Công. The surviving account is chiefly biographical and gives little detail about his administration, territory, or court. His authority rested on control of this local centre rather than a claim to govern all Vietnam. Đinh Bộ Lĩnh defeated him by 968, incorporating Tế Giang into the new unified Đinh kingdom.
