description: >-
  Qatna was a Bronze Age kingdom near modern Homs in western Syria,
  flourishing between approximately 2000 and 1340 BCE. Amorite kings ruled from a
  large palace, directing a city and its fertile surroundings. The kingdom stood
  on trade routes joining Mesopotamia, the Mediterranean coast, and inland Syria, and
  maintained diplomatic ties with Mari and other regional powers. Rivalry with Yamhad
  limited its ambitions during the early second millennium. Later, Qatna faced the
  competing influence of Mitanni, Egypt, and the Hittites. Excavated archives, fine objects,
  and royal tombs reveal its court, international contacts, and ancestor rituals. Hittite
  campaigns destroyed the kingdom in the fourteenth century BCE. Settlement continued or
  returned afterward, but the Bronze Age monarchy did not survive that upheaval.
