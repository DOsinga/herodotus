description: >-
  Shanshan was an oasis kingdom near Lop Nur at the eastern end of the Tarim Basin, in
  present Xinjiang. Its name dates from 77 BCE, when a Han envoy killed the ruler of Loulan
  and the Chinese emperor installed his brother as king. The kingdom included settlements
  along routes connecting China with Central Asia, making it a focus of competition between
  Chinese governments and the Xiongnu. Local kings governed a landscape dependent on
  irrigation and shifting river channels. Documents written in Gandhari and the Kharosthi
  script preserve details of administration and daily life. Buddhism received royal support
  and attracted monks. Invasion and environmental change weakened its centres; Northern Wei
  conquest in 445 brought government through officials appointed by that dynasty.
