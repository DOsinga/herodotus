name: "Ganzhou Uyghur Kingdom"
description: >-
  The Ganzhou Uyghur Kingdom governed part of the Hexi Corridor in northwestern China from
  894 to 1036. Its rulers were Uyghur khagans, descended from communities that moved
  southwest after the earlier steppe empire collapsed. Ganzhou, modern Zhangye, lay on
  routes linking China with Central Asia. The kingdom maintained diplomatic and marriage
  ties with the neighboring Guiyi government at Dunhuang and exchanged tribute and trade
  with Chinese dynasties without losing its own royal government. Buddhist monks and nuns
  served as envoys, reflecting the kingdom's religious culture. Warfare with the Tanguts and
  Khitans gradually weakened its position. Tangut forces captured Ganzhou in 1028, but
  surviving Uyghur rule elsewhere continued until the remaining realm was annexed in 1036,
  ending this regional political entity.
