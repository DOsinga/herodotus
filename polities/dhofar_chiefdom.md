description: >-
  Dhofar is the monsoon-watered coast and mountain country of southern Arabia, long the main
  source of frankincense. This regional record covers two periods without an outside ruler.
  From around 1000 the port of Zafar, whose ruins lie at al-Baleed near Salalah, grew rich
  exporting incense and horses. It was governed by its own lords, first the Manjawi and then
  the Habudi family, until the Rasulid sultan of Yemen conquered it in 1279. The second period
  begins after the end of Muhammad Aqil's rule in 1829, when the Kathiri and Qara tribes and
  passing strongmen held the region. Among these was Sayyid Fadhl ibn Alawi, who arrived from
  Mecca in 1875 and ruled for a few years until local sheikhs drove him out. The sultans of
  Muscat took Dhofar in 1879.
