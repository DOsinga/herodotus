description: >-
  The State of Slovenes, Croats and Serbs existed briefly in 1918 as Austria-Hungary broke
  apart. A National Council in Zagreb claimed authority over the former empire's South
  Slavic lands, including Slovenia, Croatia and Bosnia and Herzegovina. It was a
  provisional state rather than the existing Serbian kingdom, despite the shared name of
  one of its peoples. Its leaders sought to replace Habsburg rule while facing military
  disorder, social unrest and Italian advances along the Adriatic. They lacked secure
  international recognition and the resources to defend their territorial claims.
  Negotiations with Serbia therefore moved quickly towards union, amid disagreement about
  safeguards for regional autonomy. On 1 December it joined Serbia in the Kingdom of
  Serbs, Croats and Slovenes, later renamed Yugoslavia.
