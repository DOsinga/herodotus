description: >-
  The Kingdom of Ladakh, earlier known as Maryul, occupied the upper Indus valleys and neighboring
  Himalayan lands from about 930 to 1842. It emerged during the fragmentation of the Tibetan Empire
  under Lhachen Palgyigon, whose family established several western Tibetan kingdoms. Initially
  centered at Shey, the monarchy later developed Leh as a major royal and trading center. The
  Namgyal rulers took power in the fifteenth century and supported Tibetan Buddhist monasteries,
  palaces, and religious learning. Ladakh's position connected Tibet, Kashmir, and Central Asia,
  bringing commerce as well as conflict. Seventeenth-century wars with Tibet required Mughal
  assistance and a negotiated settlement. Dogra campaigns under Sikh authority conquered the
  kingdom, and the settlement of 1842 confirmed the end of its independent rule.
