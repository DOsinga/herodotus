description: >-
  Agrippa I ruled a growing Herodian client kingdom in the southern Levant from 37 to 44 CE.
  Caligula first granted him territories formerly held by Philip, northeast of the Sea of
  Galilee. In 39 he received Galilee and Perea after Herod Antipas was exiled; Claudius
  added Judea and Samaria in 41. The resulting monarchy approached the extent of his
  grandfather Herod the Great's realm, with Jerusalem again under a local king. Agrippa
  depended on friendships at the Roman court while governing through his own royal
  administration. He cultivated support among Jewish subjects and intervened during
  Caligula's threat to place an imperial statue in the Jerusalem Temple. His death in 44
  brought direct Roman government under procurators rather than royal succession.
