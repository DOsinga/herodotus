description: >-
  The Viscounty of Bourges was a feudal lordship in Berry, centred on the city of Bourges and
  recorded here from 926 to 1101. King Rudolph abolished the county of Bourges in 926 and
  replaced it with a viscounty held directly from the crown. Over the following century the
  viscounts, who also held lands at Dun and elsewhere in Berry, became independent lords in
  the feudal politics of central France, while the archbishops of Bourges kept great influence
  in the city. The last viscount, Odo Arpin, sold Bourges and Dun to King Philip I for sixty
  thousand shillings and left on the Crusade of 1101. Bourges then became the main royal
  stronghold south of the Loire.
