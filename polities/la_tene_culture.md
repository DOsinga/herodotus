description: >-
  La Tène culture spread across much of western and central Europe during the later Iron
  Age, beginning around 450 BCE. Named after finds beside Lake Neuchâtel in Switzerland, it
  followed the Hallstatt tradition and developed distinctive metalwork with flowing curves,
  spirals, and stylized animals. Iron weapons, ornaments, burials, settlements, and deposits
  in water reveal connected communities, not a single government or unified Celtic empire.
  Trade and contact with Mediterranean peoples influenced its development, while local
  societies retained differences. Later fortified towns became important centres of
  production and exchange. This record represents the archaeological culture until about 50
  BCE, when Caesar's conquest transformed Gaul. Related traditions elsewhere continued
  beyond that endpoint, and individual historically known states require their own separate
  records.
