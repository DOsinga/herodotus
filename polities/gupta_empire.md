description: >-
  The Gupta Empire developed in northern India from an early royal house conventionally dated
  around 240 CE and lasted until about 550. Pataliputra is traditionally identified as its
  principal capital. Chandragupta I strengthened the dynasty, and Samudragupta extended its
  influence through conquest and relationships with subordinate rulers. Chandragupta II presided
  over another period of expansion. Imperial kings governed through officials and regional
  authorities, combining direct rule with tribute from other courts. Sanskrit literature,
  sculpture, temple building, mathematics, and astronomy flourished during the Gupta age,
  although prosperity was uneven and the golden age label is debated. Hun invasions, regional
  breakaways, and weakening central power reduced the empire. Vishnugupta is among its last
  attested rulers; residual Gupta governments may have survived later.
