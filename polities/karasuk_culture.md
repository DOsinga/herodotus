description: >-
  The Karasuk culture describes late Bronze Age communities of southern Siberia and neighbouring
  regions, dating approximately from 1500 to 800 BCE. The Minusinsk Basin provides its
  representative centre, but related finds extend across a much wider zone. This is an
  archaeological grouping, not evidence for a single kingdom or known central government.
  Settlements, graves and tools indicate communities combining farming with livestock keeping,
  with greater mobility than some preceding Andronovo groups. Skilled metalworkers produced
  distinctive curved bronze knives, decorated handles and horse equipment. The dead were placed
  in stone burial chambers, often within square enclosures beneath mounds. Similar objects and
  pottery suggest contacts toward Mongolia and China. In the Minusinsk area, the later Tagar
  culture followed these Bronze Age communities.
