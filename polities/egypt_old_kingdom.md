description: >-
  The Old Kingdom united Egypt's Nile valley under kings based at Memphis from about
  2686 to 2181 BCE. Royal authority drew on the belief that the king upheld divine
  order, while officials collected taxes and directed labor across provincial
  districts. Djoser's stepped pyramid at Saqqara began an age of stone construction,
  followed by the pyramids of Sneferu, Khufu, Khafre, and Menkaure. Expeditions brought
  copper from Sinai, stone from quarries, and goods from Nubia and trading partners.
  During the Fifth and Sixth Dynasties, provincial governors gained wealth and power.
  Succession troubles and poor Nile floods contributed to fragmentation. This record
  ends with the Sixth Dynasty; the Memphite kings who followed continued a reduced
  royal government rather than restoring earlier centralized authority.
