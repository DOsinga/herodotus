description: >-
  Nakhon Lampang was a city-state in the Wang valley of northern Thailand, ruled by the line
  of Thipchang that became the Chet Ton dynasty. As Burmese control of Lan Na weakened, the
  townspeople chose the hunter Thipchang to expel the lord of Lamphun, and he ruled Lampang
  on his own account from 1732 to 1759. After a usurper's short rule the Burmese retook the
  town in 1763. In 1775 Thipchang's grandson Kawila rose against Burma and joined Taksin's
  capture of Chiang Mai, and Lampang became a tributary of Siam. It served as the main
  frontline citadel while Chiang Mai lay empty, and its rulers kept their own courts and
  forces. Siam ended the state in 1899, when Lampang became part of Monthon Phayap.
