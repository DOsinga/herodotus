  Jamaica is an island country in the Caribbean that became independent from Britain in 1962,
  with Kingston as its capital. It retained a constitutional monarchy and parliamentary government, with elected
  representatives and a prime minister responsible to parliament. Independence followed centuries of Spanish and then
  British colonial rule, during which enslaved Africans supplied labour for plantation agriculture. Their descendants helped
  shape the island's language, religious life, and culture. Since independence, tourism, bauxite mining, agriculture, and
  services have supported the economy, alongside substantial migration and overseas family ties. Jamaica has exerted
  cultural influence far beyond its size through reggae, Rastafari, and sporting achievements. Political competition and
  economic difficulties have accompanied continuity in its national institutions and its membership of the Commonwealth.
