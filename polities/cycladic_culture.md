description: >-
  Cycladic culture describes Bronze Age communities on the Cyclades islands of the
  Aegean Sea, represented here by their early development from approximately 3200 to
  2000 BCE. Archaeologists recognise related pottery, stone tools, graves, and marble
  sculpture across islands rather than evidence of one state. The best known objects are
  flat human figurines, often female, whose uses remain uncertain. People grew grain,
  raised livestock, fished, worked copper, and exchanged goods by sea. Obsidian and
  pottery help trace connections among island settlements and with Crete and mainland
  Greece. Ship images provide evidence of seafaring without surviving boats. Differences
  between settlements and burial customs caution against assuming common rulers. Around
  2000 BCE the early sequence gave way to changing Middle Cycladic cultural traditions.
