description: >-
  The Atabegs of Yazd were a local dynasty that ruled the desert city of Yazd in central Iran
  from about 1141 to 1319. They followed the Kakuyids, to whom they were linked by marriage, and
  despite their Turkish title the early rulers bore Persian names. Most of the atabegs paid
  tribute first to the Seljuks and then to the Mongol Ilkhans, keeping their own government in
  return. Under them Yazd prospered on the caravan routes across the Iranian plateau and as a
  centre of silk weaving, and Marco Polo, passing through in 1272, called it a noble city busy
  with trade. The line ended when Mubariz al-Din Muhammad, founder of the Muzaffarid dynasty,
  overthrew the last atabeg and took the city for the Ilkhan.
