description: >-
  The Bactria–Margiana Archaeological Complex, called the Oxus civilization, occupied Central
  Asian oasis regions approximately between 2400 and 1700 BCE. Sites lie chiefly in modern
  Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan, and northern Afghanistan, with Gonur Depe among the largest centres.
  Communities cultivated wheat and barley using irrigation, kept livestock, and built substantial
  settlements of mudbrick. Fortified compounds, workshops, elaborate burials, bronze objects, and
  stone jewellery reveal specialised production and marked differences in wealth. Connections
  reached the Iranian plateau, Indus region, and Eurasian steppe. Archaeologists debate whether
  particular buildings were palaces or temples, and neither rulers nor a unified state are
  securely known. During the later period, major centres contracted and local traditions changed.
  Its estimated dates describe archaeological development rather than a documented dynasty.
