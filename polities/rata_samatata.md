description: >-
  The Rata kingdom was a royal government in Samatata, the southeastern Bengal region now largely within Bangladesh. Its approximate span here, 650 to 750, reflects limited evidence rather than securely known founding and extinction dates. The court ruled from Devaparvata, associated with the Mainamati area near Comilla, separately from the contemporary Khadga government. Its clearest witness is Sridharanarata's Kailan copper plate, dated around 664 to 675. The grant records royal land donations supporting Buddhist worship, religious study and monastic needs, revealing officials and organised administration. His father Jivadharanarata also belonged to this ruling family. Samatata's rivers and coastal connections placed such courts within a wider Bengal trading world. The Ratas' exact territory and the circumstances ending their rule remain uncertain.
