description: >-
  Thinis represents a chiefdom in Upper Egypt around 3300 to 3100 BCE, associated with the
  early royal burials at nearby Abydos. Its dates and political boundaries are
  reconstructed, not preserved in a contemporary account of a named state. The town itself
  has not been securely located, and later writers provide much of its recorded identity.
  Archaeological evidence from Abydos reveals powerful leaders, elaborate tombs and
  growing control of goods and labour. Thinis is reconstructed as one of several competing
  Upper Egyptian centres, alongside Naqada and Nekhen. Its rulers ultimately absorbed
  rivals and helped unite Egypt. The precise order of these changes remains uncertain.
  Around 3100 BCE its leading position passed into the unified monarchy conventionally
  represented by Egypt's First Dynasty.
