description: >-
  The Prince Bishopric of Münster was an ecclesiastical principality in northwestern
  Germany, governed separately from 1250 until Prussian occupation in 1802. Its bishop held
  both religious office and territorial power, but the state was smaller than the diocese
  under his spiritual care. The cathedral chapter elected the bishop, while nobles and the
  city shared influence over matters such as taxation. Purchases and political agreements
  expanded its lands, producing northern and southern territories separated by other states.
  The Reformation brought religious conflict, yet Catholic government ultimately prevailed.
  The city repeatedly sought independence from its bishop, but without success. Later
  bishops sometimes held several church principalities together. Prussian troops took
  control before the formal secularisation of 1803, which divided the ecclesiastical lands.
