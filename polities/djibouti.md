  Djibouti has been an independent republic in the Horn of Africa since 1977,
  when a referendum ended French rule over the Territory of the Afars and the
  Issas. Its capital and principal port, also named Djibouti, stand near the
  entrance to the Red Sea. A president heads the state, supported by a prime
  minister and elected national assembly, while the ruling political
  organization has long dominated public life. Somali and Afar communities form
  major parts of the population. Disputes over representation contributed to
  civil conflict in the 1990s, followed by peace and power-sharing agreements.
  Port services, links with landlocked Ethiopia, and foreign military bases give
  the small country considerable strategic importance. It continues within the
  borders inherited at independence today.
