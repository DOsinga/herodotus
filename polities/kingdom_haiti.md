description: >-
  Henri Christophe governed northern Haiti from the division following Dessalines's death
  in 1806 until 1820. Initially president of the State of Haiti, he proclaimed himself
  King Henri I in 1811. His capital, Cap-Henri, is today's Cap-Haïtien. The northern state
  rivalled Alexandre Pétion's southern republic while defending the independence won in
  the Haitian Revolution. Christophe built Sans-Souci Palace and the Citadelle Laferrière,
  created a titled nobility and supported schools. His government concentrated power in
  the monarch and required agricultural labour to sustain plantation production. These
  demands helped provoke opposition among workers and soldiers. After a stroke and a
  military revolt, Christophe killed himself in October 1820. Jean-Pierre Boyer then
  brought the north under the republic, ending Haiti's division into rival governments.
