description: >-
  Yue was a kingdom in the lower Yangtze region of eastern China,
  centred initially on Kuaiji in present Zhejiang. Its beginnings are uncertain; the
  date around 700 BCE is an estimate rather than a documented foundation.
  Hereditary kings governed a society distinguished by boats, bronze weapons, and customs
  different from those of the northern Chinese states. Yue became prominent through
  warfare with neighbouring Wu. King Goujian recovered from defeat and conquered Wu
  in 473 BCE, shifting the court north and extending Yue's influence. Its
  rulers then took part in interstate struggles during the Warring States period.
  Chu conquered the kingdom, with 306 BCE used here although 333 BCE
  is also reported. Yue identities and successor communities persisted after that conquest.
