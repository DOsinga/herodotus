description: >-
  The Chernyakhov culture spread across lands north of the Black Sea, including modern
  Ukraine, Moldova, and Romania, during the early centuries CE. This record uses approximate
  dates of 200 to 400 and a representative point in the middle Dnieper region.
  Archaeological remains show settled cereal farmers, livestock keeping, metalworking, and
  widespread wheel-made pottery. Cemeteries contain both cremations and burials, often with
  ornaments, combs, and vessels but few weapons. Roman imports demonstrate trade across the
  imperial frontier. The culture is associated with Gothic-led populations, yet its material
  traditions also reflect local Dacian and Sarmatian communities. It should not be read as a
  single documented kingdom. Hunnic invasions disrupted existing political networks and
  contributed to its decline, while some local traditions survived.
