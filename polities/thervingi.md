description: >-
  The Thervingi were a Gothic people who held the lands north of the lower Danube, from
  the Carpathians to the Dniester, between the late third century and 376. They are first
  named in 291, a few years after Rome abandoned its province of Dacia, and the Sântana de
  Mureș–Chernyakhov culture, named in part after a Transylvanian site, shows Gothic
  settlement across the region. The Thervingi were a confederation of chiefdoms that could
  unite under a war leader, called a judge, such as Athanaric, who fought the emperor
  Valens from 367 to 369 and persecuted Gothic Christians. When the Huns arrived in 376,
  most Thervingi under Fritigern crossed the Danube into the Roman Empire.
