  The Kingdom of Great Britain united England, including Wales, with Scotland in 1707.
  Their monarchies had shared a ruler since 1603, but the Acts of Union created one
  kingdom and one parliament at Westminster. Scotland retained distinct legal, educational
  and church institutions. A constitutional monarchy developed alongside cabinet
  government and growing parliamentary influence, although voting rights remained
  restricted. Hanoverian succession in 1714 and defeated Jacobite rebellions secured the
  new ruling order. Naval warfare, overseas commerce and colonial expansion made Britain a
  major global power, while industrial change transformed production at home. The American
  colonies broke away during the eighteenth century. Great Britain remained separate from
  Ireland until legislation enacted in 1800 created the United Kingdom of Great Britain
  and Ireland.
