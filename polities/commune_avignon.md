description: >-
  The Commune of Avignon was the self-governing city of Avignon on the Rhône, recorded here
  from 1129 to 1251. Avignon lay on the line between the lands of the counts of Toulouse and
  of Provence, and both claimed it. Consuls governed the city from about 1129, and in the
  thirteenth century elected podestàs on the Italian model held executive power. The commune
  built a bridge across the Rhône between 1171 and 1185. Allied with Raymond VII of Toulouse
  during the Albigensian Crusade, Avignon refused passage to Louis VIII, who besieged and took
  it in 1226 and razed its walls. The city recovered its self-government, but in 1251 it
  submitted to Alphonse of Poitiers and Charles of Anjou.
