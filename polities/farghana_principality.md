description: >-
  The Principality of Farghana occupied the fertile Fergana valley in Central Asia, with its court at Akhsikath. Its beginning around 600 is estimated; the native royal line is better documented in the eighth century. Rulers used titles such as ikhshid and dehqan and maintained their own government amid competing outside powers. Western Turkic, Tang Chinese, and Arab influence all reached the valley. Umayyad campaigns brought invasions and changing alliances, while Ferghana's rulers sometimes supported the Turgesh against Arab armies. They also sought Tang assistance and joined the Chinese side at Talas in 751. Local rule survived these struggles into Abbasid times. In 819, the Abbasid governor appointed Ahmad ibn Asad over Ferghana, replacing the court with a new provincial administration.
