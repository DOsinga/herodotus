description: >-
  Minoan civilization developed on Crete during the Bronze Age, represented here from about 3100 to 1450 BCE. Archaeologists identify it through settlements, pottery, frescoes, seals, burial customs, and complexes conventionally called palaces. Knossos, Phaistos, Malia, and Zakros housed workshops, storerooms, shrines, and administrative activities, but the people who governed these centers remain unknown. Their Linear A writing has not been deciphered, limiting what can be said about political institutions. Maritime contacts linked Crete with the Aegean islands, Egypt, and the eastern Mediterranean. Art often depicts ceremonies, animals, and scenes associated with the sea. Destruction affected many centers during the fifteenth century BCE. This record ends with the Mycenaean takeover of Knossos, although Minoan traditions continued afterward in art and religion.
