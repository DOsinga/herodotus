description: >-
  The Rozvi Empire ruled much of the Zimbabwean plateau from the late seventeenth century
  into the nineteenth, with this record spanning 1683 to 1866. Changamire Dombo built its
  power through military victories, cattle wealth, and the conquest of the Butua kingdom.
  His successors governed from centres including Danamombe, maintaining the region's
  tradition of monumental stone building. A king, or mambo, worked through provincial
  chiefs, royal relatives, and court advisers. Farming, cattle keeping, mining, and trade
  supplied wealth and tribute. Rozvi armies challenged Portuguese influence and helped expel
  Portuguese traders from parts of the interior. Succession struggles later weakened central
  authority, while invading southern African groups broke its power. Surviving royal
  resistance ended as Ndebele rule consolidated over the former heartland.
