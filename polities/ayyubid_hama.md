description: >-
  The Ayyubid Emirate of Hama was a Syrian principality centred on the Orontes River city of
  Hama. From 1193, it governed its own affairs under a local branch of Saladin's family,
  descended from his nephew al-Muzaffar Umar. Its rulers participated in the alliances and
  rivalries of the wider Ayyubid world. Unlike most Syrian Ayyubid states, Hama survived the
  Mongol invasion and the subsequent Mamluk takeover because its ruler supported the
  Mamluks. Direct Mamluk government interrupted the emirate between 1299 and 1310. The
  sultan then restored Ayyubid rule under Abu al-Fida, remembered as a historian and
  geographer. His son succeeded him but lost Mamluk favour. In 1341, his removal ended the
  dynasty's government at Hama and brought direct Mamluk administration there.
