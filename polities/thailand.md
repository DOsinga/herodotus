  Thailand is a Southeast Asian state centred on Bangkok, recorded here from the 1932 revolution that ended Siam's absolute monarchy. A constitution retained the king while transferring governing power to new political institutions. Military leaders became dominant, and the country adopted the name Thailand in 1939. During the Second World War its government allied with Japan, while the Free Thai movement supported the Allies. Postwar governments built close American ties and promoted economic growth, industry and tourism. Parliamentary elections have alternated with military coups, rewritten constitutions and periods of authoritarian rule. The monarchy remains central to national identity and political debate. This record keeps these changes within one continuing constitutional kingdom, rather than treating each government as a new state.
