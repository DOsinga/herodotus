  Bolivia is a South American republic established in 1825 after the independence wars against
  Spain. It combines Andean highlands with eastern lowlands; Sucre is its constitutional capital,
  while the national government works in La Paz. Named for Simón Bolívar, the new state faced
  unstable leadership and conflicts with its neighbours. Defeat in the War of the Pacific
  deprived it of its coast, and the Chaco War brought further territorial losses. Mining
  interests shaped politics until the 1952 revolution widened voting rights, nationalised major
  tin mines and introduced land reform. Military governments later alternated with civilian rule
  before democratic government returned in 1982. The 2009 constitution recognised Bolivia as a
  plurinational state, giving greater prominence to indigenous peoples and their rights.
