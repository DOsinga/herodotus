description: >-
  Yin was a short-lived monarchy in northern Fujian, China, existing separately from 943
  to 945. Its founder and only emperor, Wang Yanzheng, belonged to the ruling family of
  neighbouring Min. Conflict among that family's members after Wang Shenzhi's death
  weakened Min and eventually produced an open break. Wang Yanzheng established his own
  court at Jian Prefecture, modern Jian'ou, controlling the northwestern part of the
  former kingdom. He adopted an imperial title, but his new state remained closely tied
  to Min's succession struggles rather than forming a durable regional order. In 945 he
  took the Min throne and reunited Yin's territory with it. Southern Tang conquered the
  reunited government later that year. Yin therefore represents a division within
  Fujian's tenth-century politics.
