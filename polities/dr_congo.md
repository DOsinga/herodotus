  The Democratic Republic of the Congo is a Central African state independent from Belgium since
  1960, with its capital at Kinshasa. Independence quickly brought army mutiny, regional
  secessions, foreign intervention, and the killing of Prime Minister Patrice Lumumba. Mobutu
  seized power in 1965, established authoritarian rule, and renamed the country Zaire in 1971.
  Laurent Kabila's rebellion overthrew him in 1997 and restored the current name. Wars drew in
  neighbouring armies and armed groups, causing widespread displacement and lasting insecurity,
  especially in the east. The 2006 constitution established a president, prime minister, and two
  chamber parliament. Its river basin and mineral resources shape both economic opportunities and
  struggles over power. Despite these changes of name and regime, the state continues today.
