description: >-
  The Kingdom of Gilgit was a Buddhist monarchy in the mountain valleys of present
  northern Pakistan, represented here from about 550 to 755. Its Patola Shahi rulers
  occupied a strategic position on routes connecting the Indian subcontinent, Central
  Asia, and China. Royal inscriptions, donated bronze Buddhas, and the Gilgit manuscripts
  show a court closely involved in Buddhist patronage. Chinese accounts called Gilgit
  Lesser Bolu, distinguishing it imperfectly from neighboring Baltistan. Competition
  between Tang China and the Tibetan Empire repeatedly shaped its diplomacy and survival.
  Gao Xianzhi captured and deported its ruler in 747, but the local dynasty subsequently
  returned. The final Tibetan takeover is estimated around 755, after Tang withdrawal;
  both the restoration and ending remain uncertain in surviving sources.
