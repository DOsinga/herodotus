description: >-
  The Hammudid Caliphate was the rule of an Arab family of Idrisid descent who claimed the
  caliphate of Córdoba during the civil war that broke up Umayyad al-Andalus. The brothers Ali
  and al-Qasim ibn Hammud had been made governors of Ceuta, Tangier and Algeciras. In 1016 Ali
  marched on Córdoba, executed the caliph Sulayman and took the title himself. After his
  murder in 1018 his brother al-Qasim and his son Yahya fought over the throne. The people of
  Córdoba drove the family out in 1023 and restored Umayyad princes, though Yahya briefly
  returned in 1025. The Hammudids kept Málaga, Algeciras and the African ports, and from 1026
  Yahya ruled from Málaga as the head of a taifa that still claimed the caliphate.
