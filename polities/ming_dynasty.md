description: >-
  The Ming dynasty ruled China from 1368 to 1644 after Zhu Yuanzhang overthrew Mongol Yuan
  authority and became the Hongwu emperor. Its government combined a powerful hereditary
  emperor with a large civil bureaucracy whose officials were recruited through
  examinations. The capital began at Nanjing before the Yongle emperor moved the main court
  to Beijing. Early Ming expansion included Zheng He's maritime expeditions, while later
  rulers concentrated on defending northern frontiers. Commercial growth, printing, cities
  and overseas silver reshaped society, alongside flourishing painting, literature and
  porcelain production. Financial strain, famine, rebellion and war with the Manchus
  undermined the state. Beijing fell to Li Zicheng in 1644, and the Chongzhen emperor died.
  Ming loyalist courts continued farther south as the Southern Ming.
