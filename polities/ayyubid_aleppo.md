description: >-
  The Ayyubid Emirate of Aleppo governed northern Syria as a separate hereditary domain
  after Saladin's death in 1193. His son al-Zahir Ghazi controlled Aleppo and nearby
  lands, acknowledging senior relatives while conducting his own affairs. The emirate
  competed and cooperated with other Ayyubid courts, crusader states, and neighbouring
  rulers. Its government relied on military commanders, judges, and court officials.
  Repeated minority successions gave regents an important role, notably Dayfa Khatun
  during the childhood of an-Nasir Yusuf. After the Mamluks displaced the Ayyubids in
  Egypt in 1250, an-Nasir extended his authority to Damascus and sought unsuccessfully to
  recover Egypt. Mongol expansion finally overwhelmed his position. Hulagu's forces
  captured Aleppo in 1260, ending the emirate and bringing the city under Mongol control.
