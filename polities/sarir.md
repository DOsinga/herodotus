description: >-
  Sarir was a mountain kingdom in present-day Dagestan, emerging around the sixth
  century and breaking apart around the early twelfth. Its rulers governed from
  Humraj, tentatively identified with Khunzakh, and associated their authority with a
  legendary golden throne inherited from Persian kings. Medieval writers described a
  Christian royal court, although religious practice across the countryside was more
  varied. Sarir allied with the Khazars during Arab campaigns in the Caucasus, then
  paid tribute without losing its own kings. Later it fought neighbouring Muslim
  rulers at Derbent and Shirvan and developed close ties with Christian Alania through
  royal marriages. Pressure from neighbouring powers and internal disputes contributed
  to fragmentation. The approximate endpoint here marks political disintegration, not
  the disappearance of local Christianity.
