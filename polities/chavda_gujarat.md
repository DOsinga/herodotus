description: >-
  The Chavda kingdom governed part of present day Gujarat in western India,
  conventionally dated from about 690 to 942. Its early centre was Panchasar, while
  later rulers held court at Anahilavada, modern Patan. Vanraja is traditionally
  credited with founding that city in either 746 or 765 and establishing the kingdom's
  known royal line. Much of its history comes from later accounts written under the
  succeeding Chaulukyas, so lengthy reigns, genealogies, and founding stories require
  caution. Inscriptions confirm that Chavda rulers belonged to the political landscape
  of early medieval Gujarat. The kingdom was a monarchy amid competing regional powers
  and religious communities. According to the traditional succession account, Mularaja
  overthrew his maternal uncle Samantsimha in 942 and founded the Chaulukya kingdom.
