description: >-
  The Sultanate of Ifat was a Muslim kingdom in the eastern Horn of Africa, recorded here from
  1275 to 1403. Its Walashma rulers linked the inland districts of eastern Shewa with Zeila, a
  port connecting regional commercial trade to the Red Sea and Arabia. Farming towns and
  commercial routes supported a population speaking several languages. The sultans repeatedly
  fought Ethiopia's Christian emperors, sometimes submitting and sometimes renewing resistance.
  These wars also brought struggles within the ruling family and shifts toward eastern centres.
  Sa'ad ad-Din II's defeat and death ended this phase of Walashma power, although accounts differ
  over the campaign's precise date and circumstances. His family's political tradition continued
  in the later Adal Sultanate, rather than disappearing with Ifat's fall.
