description: >-
  Bit Agusi was an Aramaean kingdom in northern Syria, established around 900 BCE and ruled
  from Arpad. Its territory included Aleppo and towns between the northern Syrian plain and
  the approaches to Hamath. Named for its founder Gusi, it belonged to the patchwork of
  kingdoms that followed the collapse of Bronze Age empires. Its kings alternated between
  paying Assyrian tribute and building alliances against Assyrian expansion. Treaties
  associated with King Mati-ilu survive at Sefire, offering rare evidence of Aramaean
  diplomacy, sworn obligations, and royal authority. Support from Urartu could not protect
  Arpad when Tiglath-Pileser III invaded. After a siege lasting three years, Assyria captured
  the capital in 740 BCE and replaced the kingdom with provincial government under its own
  officials.
