description: >-
  Bronze Age Britain describes prehistoric communities across Britain from roughly 2500 to
  800 BCE, rather than a single kingdom or unified government. Archaeology traces the
  arrival of Beaker pottery, new burial customs, and migrants from continental Europe
  alongside the introduction of copper and bronze working. Stonehenge's later construction
  and rich graves in Wessex reflect changing ceremonial practices and differences in
  wealth. Farmers cultivated land, raised animals, and increasingly lived in settled
  landscapes divided by field boundaries. Metalworkers produced tools, weapons, ornaments,
  and objects deposited in hoards or wet places. British tin helped connect these
  communities with wider European exchange networks. Their spoken languages and political
  arrangements remain uncertain. The conventional ending here marks the transition towards
  the British Iron Age.
