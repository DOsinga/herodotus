description: >-
  The Kingdom of Cambodia continued Khmer government after the political centre shifted away from
  Angkor, conventionally beginning in 1431 and lasting until French protection in 1863. Kings
  established courts around Chaktomuk, Longvek, and later Oudong, closer to river routes and
  maritime trade. Theravada Buddhism remained central to society and royal authority. Scarce early
  sources make the details of the move from Angkor uncertain. Longvek prospered in trade before a
  Siamese conquest in 1594. Thereafter, dynastic disputes and pressure from Siam and Vietnam
  repeatedly weakened the monarchy and reduced its territory, especially in the Mekong delta.
  Vietnam governed it directly from 1834 until a revolt in 1841. Norodom's acceptance of a French
  protectorate in 1863 ended the kingdom's independent direction of foreign relations.
