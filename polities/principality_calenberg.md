description: >-
  Calenberg was a Welf principality in northern Germany, established in 1432 from a division
  of Brunswick lands. Named after Calenberg Castle, it included territory along the Leine
  and later incorporated Gottingen. Its princes governed within the Holy Roman Empire, where
  repeated family partitions and inheritances reshaped neighbouring states. Elisabeth of
  Brandenburg helped introduce the Lutheran Reformation during her regency. In 1584 the ruling
  line ended, and government passed to Brunswick-Wolfenbuttel, creating the gap shown in
  this record. Calenberg became separately governed again in 1635 under a Luneburg branch.
  Duke George moved the court to Hanover in 1636, strengthening the city's place in the
  principality. Ernest Augustus received electoral rank in 1692, turning these lands into
  the nucleus of Hanover's electorate.
