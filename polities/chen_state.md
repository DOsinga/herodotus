description: >-
  Chen was a hereditary state in the plains of eastern Henan, China, with its capital at
  Wanqiu, near present-day Huaiyang. It is recorded as governing separately from 770 to 479
  BCE, after the Zhou royal court moved east. Its earlier fief was traditionally granted to
  Duke Hu, whose family claimed descent from the legendary emperor Shun. During the Spring
  and Autumn period, Chen stood between stronger neighbors and became vulnerable to Chu's
  expansion. Noble rivalries and disputes over succession repeatedly destabilized its court.
  Its ruling lineage also had lasting influence beyond the kingdom: a branch settled in Qi
  and became the Tian family, which eventually replaced Qi's original rulers. Chu conquered
  Chen in 479 BCE, absorbing its territory and capital.
