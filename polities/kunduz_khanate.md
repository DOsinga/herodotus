description: >-
  The Khanate of Kunduz was an Uzbek state in the valley of the Kunduz River in northeastern
  Afghanistan, led by the Qataghan tribal confederation, whose chiefs had dominated the region
  since the eighteenth century. Mir Muhammad Murad Beg made it a regional power early in the
  nineteenth century. He gathered the Uzbek tribes, raided and resettled the Tajik and Hazara
  villages around him, extended his authority over Khulm and Balkh, and in 1829 conquered
  Badakhshan, whose people suffered heavily. His slave raids made Kunduz feared, and he held the
  English traveller William Moorcroft for ransom in 1824. Pressure from Dost Mohammad Khan of
  Kabul cost him Badakhshan in the late 1830s, and after his death the khanate declined until
  Afghan forces annexed it in 1859.
