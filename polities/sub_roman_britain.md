description: >-
  Sub-Roman Britain was the lowland and northern Britain of the civitates and their successors
  between the end of Roman rule and the first recorded kingdoms, recorded here as a chiefdom
  from 410 to 570. After Constantine III took the last field army to Gaul in 407, the cities
  were told in 410 to see to their own defence. Local magistrates, landowners and warlords
  then governed, and writers such as Gildas name tyrants and a council that hired Saxon
  mercenaries. Germanus of Auxerre found an organised Christian community at Verulamium in
  429. Towns shrank, coinage and mass-produced pottery disappeared, and villas were abandoned.
  British and Anglo-Saxon kingdoms emerged region by region, and the last enclaves of the
  south fell after 570.
