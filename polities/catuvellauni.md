description: >-
  The Catuvellauni were a British people and kingdom north of the Thames, centred initially
  around Verlamion near modern St Albans. Their independent rule is represented from
  approximately 50 BCE until the Roman invasion of 43 CE. Coins and scattered classical
  accounts reveal rulers including Tasciovanus and Cunobelinus, whose power extended into
  neighbouring territories. Cassivellaunus, who resisted Caesar, is often associated with
  them, although his tribal identity is uncertain. Cunobelinus ruled from Camulodunon,
  modern Colchester, and maintained connections with the Roman world through trade and
  coinage. After his death, his sons Caratacus and Togodumnus led opposition to the invading
  army. Roman conquest ended the kingdom's independence, but its name survived in a
  community based at Verulamium, a town beside earlier Verlamion.
