description: >-
  Pre-Pottery Neolithic B describes archaeological communities in the Levant and upper Mesopotamia between approximately 8800 and 6500 BCE. It is a cultural phase, not a known kingdom or unified government. Kathleen Kenyon identified it while excavating Jericho, one of its important settlements. People increasingly relied on cultivated crops and domesticated livestock, although hunting and gathering continued. Rectangular houses, polished lime plaster floors and distinctive flint blades distinguish many sites from earlier villages. Some communities removed skulls from burials and modelled faces over them with plaster, providing evidence of elaborate mortuary practices. Exchange carried materials such as obsidian over considerable distances. Regional settlement patterns changed toward the end of the period, leading into later Neolithic communities with differing traditions and pottery.
