description: >-
  Mesolithic Britain describes hunter-gatherer communities after the last Ice Age, approximately
  9600 to 4000 BCE, not a united political state. Warming conditions brought expanding woodland
  and changing animal populations. People hunted, fished, gathered plants, and used small flint
  blades fitted into tools and weapons. Star Carr in Yorkshire preserves timber structures,
  worked antler, and other unusually rich evidence of daily activity beside a former lake.
  Communities varied across Britain, and surviving remains do not establish named rulers or a
  common government. Rising seas gradually drowned Doggerland, severing the land connection with
  continental Europe. Later arrivals brought farming, livestock, and new settlement practices
  around 4000 BCE. The Neolithic transition substantially changed Britain's population and
  economy, although its pace varied between regions.
