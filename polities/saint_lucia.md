  Saint Lucia is an island state in the eastern Caribbean, independent from the United
  Kingdom since 22 February 1979. Its capital, Castries, is a port on the western coast. The
  country is a parliamentary constitutional monarchy: a governor general represents the
  monarch, while a prime minister and cabinet govern through an elected House of Assembly
  and appointed Senate. French and British colonial rivalry repeatedly changed control of
  the island before British rule became permanent in 1814. That history survives in English
  public administration and widely spoken French based Creole. Independence followed a
  period of associated statehood with Britain. Tourism has become a economic activity
  alongside agriculture, with the volcanic Pitons among its landmarks. Saint Lucia remains a
  separate Commonwealth realm.
