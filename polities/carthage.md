description: >-
  Carthage began as a Phoenician settlement on the coast of present Tunisia,
  traditionally founded by colonists from Tyre in 814 BCE. Its harbour and
  commercial connections helped it become the leading power of the western Mediterranean.
  The city governed surrounding African lands and overseas possessions through magistrates, councils,
  and families; elected suffetes became its chief civilian officers. Maritime trade and
  agriculture supported armies that combined citizen forces with allied and hired soldiers.
  Carthage fought Greek cities in Sicily before its rivalry with Rome produced
  three Punic Wars. Hannibal's invasion of Italy nearly broke Roman power, but
  defeat stripped Carthage of its empire. In 146 BCE Roman forces captured
  and destroyed the city, ending the state and taking its African territory.
