description: >-
  The early Duchy of Thuringia was a separately governing realm on the eastern edge of Frankish power from 642 to
  about 719. It developed from a frontier command established by the Austrasian king Dagobert, who appointed Radulf to defend against
  Slavic incursions. Radulf later rebelled and defeated a royal expedition led by Sigebert III. He then made treaties with neighbouring
  peoples and claimed royal standing while nominal Frankish ties persisted. The Hedenid dukes continued government, with Würzburg representing their later
  court on the Main rather than the whole earlier Thuringian kingdom. Their patronage supported Christian missionary activity. The rise of
  the Pippinid family reduced their independence, and conflict with Charles Martel around 717 to 719 brought their autonomous rule down.
