description: >-
  Cao was a hereditary state centred on Taoqiu, near modern Dingtao in Shandong, on the North China Plain. Its ruling family traced descent to Shu Zhenduo, a brother of the Zhou dynasty's founding king. This record follows its separate government from 770 BCE, after the Zhou court moved east and royal control weakened. During the Spring and Autumn period, Cao was caught between stronger neighbours, especially Jin, Chu, and Song. Duke Gong's treatment of the future Duke Wen of Jin became part of accounts explaining Jin's attack on Cao around the battle of Chengpu. Succession struggles further weakened the ruling house. In 487 BCE, Song conquered Cao, captured its final ruler, and ended the state's independent political existence in China.
