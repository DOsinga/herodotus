  Japan's present constitutional state dates here from 1947, when a new postwar constitution took
  effect. The island country lies off East Asia, with Tokyo as its capital and seat of
  government. The emperor became a symbol of the state, while an elected Diet and cabinet headed
  by a prime minister exercised political power. Japanese institutions continued operating during
  the Allied occupation, which ended in 1952. The constitution renounced war, although Japan
  subsequently developed Self-Defense Forces and maintained a security alliance with the United
  States. Rapid industrial growth made it a major economic power, followed by prolonged
  difficulties after the asset bubble collapsed. Earthquakes and the 2011 tsunami exposed lasting
  environmental risks. The parliamentary monarchy continues within the same constitutional
  framework.
