description: >-
  The Margraviate of Meissen was a hereditary territorial principality along the Elbe
  in what is now Saxony. This record covers its separate government from 1250 to 1423,
  rather than its earlier life as an imperial frontier province. The Wettin family
  made Meissen the centre of expanding lands, combining inherited rights, settlement,
  and military power. Henry the Illustrious secured Thuringian possessions after the
  succession conflict ending in 1264. Later margraves resisted attempts by German
  kings to take their territories, notably winning the Battle of Lucka in 1307. The
  dynasty repeatedly divided its holdings among relatives. In 1423 Frederick the
  Warlike received Saxe-Wittenberg and the right to help elect the emperor, bringing
  Meissen into the enlarged Wettin state known as electoral Saxony.
