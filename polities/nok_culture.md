description: >-
  Nok culture describes prehistoric communities in central Nigeria, known from
  archaeological remains dated roughly between 1500 BCE and the beginning of the Common
  Era. It takes its name from the village where distinctive terracotta sculptures were
  discovered in 1928. These hollow figures depict people and animals, often with elaborate
  hairstyles, jewellery, and shaped facial features. Their original uses remain uncertain,
  though some finds suggest ritual or funerary settings. Settlements, pottery, plant
  remains, and furnaces show farming communities that cultivated millet and developed
  ironworking. Shared artistic styles reveal connections between sites, but do not
  establish a single kingdom, capital, or known ruling dynasty. Looting has destroyed
  archaeological context. Both the culture's beginnings and its poorly dated disappearance
  remain matters of research.
