description: >-
  The Traikutaka Kingdom ruled Aparanta, northern Konkan, and neighbouring parts of western India from approximately 388 to 550. Its name refers to a mountain with three peaks, but the location of its capital remains disputed. Coins and inscriptions provide the main evidence for its kings, including Indradatta, Dahrasena, Vyaghrasena, Madhyamasena, and Vikramasena. Their silver coinage closely followed Western Satrap designs, preserving traces of that earlier monetary tradition. Dahrasena performed a royal horse sacrifice, and the dynasty is associated with devotion to Vishnu. Expansion brought conflict with the Vakatakas, while later rulers also faced Vishnukundin influence. These outside powers did not necessarily remove the local court. The later royal sequence, including Vikramasena's inscription of 533, supports continuation beyond the fifth-century cutoff.
