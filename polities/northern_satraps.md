description: >-
  The Northern Satraps were Indo-Scythian rulers who governed Mathura and the surrounding
  Ganges–Yamuna region of northern India from about the middle of the first century BCE. They used
  the Persian title of satrap or great satrap, inherited from the Indo-Scythian kingdom in the
  northwest, but acted as independent rulers after its decline. The best known were Rajuvula and
  his son Sodasa, whose coins and inscriptions record donations to Buddhist and Jain
  establishments. Mathura flourished as a centre of trade and religious art during their rule, and
  its sculptors developed styles that later shaped Kushan art. The Kushans extended their power
  over Mathura in the late first century CE, ending the satraps' government.
