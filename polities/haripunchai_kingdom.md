description: >-
  Haripunchai was a Mon kingdom centred on Lamphun in northern Thailand, lasting into
  the late thirteenth century. Its beginnings are estimated here around 660, following
  chronicles that associate its foundation with Queen Camadevi, a princess from Lavo.
  Other interpretations place the foundation later. Kings governed a fertile basin and
  supported Buddhist monasteries, religious buildings, and artistic traditions linked to
  the wider Dvaravati world. Mon inscriptions provide firmer evidence for its later
  history, recording donations and construction. The kingdom maintained a distinct court
  while neighbouring Tai powers expanded. Mangrai captured Lamphun and incorporated
  Haripunchai into Lan Na, conventionally dated here to 1292, although 1281 also appears
  in accounts. Its Buddhist culture and Mon heritage continued to influence the region
  after conquest.
