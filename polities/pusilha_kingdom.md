description: >-
  Pusilha was a Maya kingdom in southern Belize, positioned between river valleys linking the central lowlands with Honduras and the Caribbean. Royal inscriptions place its court's beginnings around 570, while the last surviving dated monument belongs to 798. Kings associated with its own emblem glyph governed an urban centre containing ceremonial buildings, residences, and carved stone monuments. Its location encouraged exchanges of goods and ideas with centres such as Copan, Quirigua, Caracol, and Tikal. Similar pottery once suggested dependence on powerful neighbours, but inscriptions and archaeology instead support a separate royal government. The court's end around 850 is an estimate, not a documented conquest. Changes in ceramics and buildings indicate that people continued living there after royal monument production ceased.
