  Transnistria is a separately governed territory along the eastern side of the Dniester River,
  internationally regarded as part of Moldova. Its authorities emerged in 1990 during the Soviet
  Union's breakup, opposing political changes in the Moldavian republic. Fighting with Moldovan
  forces in 1992 ended in a ceasefire that left the territory under its own administration,
  centred on Tiraspol. It maintains a president, government, parliament, security forces,
  currency, and other state institutions, but lacks international recognition. Russian troops and
  support have played an important role in its survival. Its population includes Moldovans,
  Russians, and Ukrainians, and its official culture preserves many Soviet symbols. Negotiations
  have not resolved its status or produced reintegration, so separate government continues
  despite Moldova's claim to sovereignty.
