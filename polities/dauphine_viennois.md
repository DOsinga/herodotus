description: >-
  The Dauphiné of Viennois was a principality in the western Alps and the Rhône valley of
  southeastern France, centred on Grenoble. Its rulers began as the counts of Albon, who gathered
  lands around Grenoble and Vienne from the eleventh century. Guigues IV took the nickname
  Dauphin in the twelfth century, his successors made it their title, and their lands became
  known as the Dauphiné. The state lay inside the Kingdom of Arles and so within the Holy Roman
  Empire, but the dauphins governed it as their own, with a council at Grenoble and a university
  founded there in 1339. Humbert II, deep in debt and without an heir, sold the Dauphiné to the
  French crown in 1349, and the title passed to the heir to the throne.
