description: >-
  The Ammarid Emirate was a small Shia state on the Lebanese coast, ruled from Tripoli by
  the Banu Ammar, a family of judges of Kutama Berber descent. Around 1065 the Fatimid
  governor of the town died and the qadi Amin al-Dawla took power for himself, holding the
  coast from Akkar down to Jubayl. His nephew Jalal al-Mulk kept the city free by balancing
  between the Fatimids in Egypt and the Seljuks inland, took Jableh from the Byzantines in
  1081, and made Tripoli a centre of learning with a large library. Under his brother Fakhr
  al-Mulk the emirate also held Tartus and Arqa, but the First Crusade brought constant war.
  The Crusaders took Tartus in 1102 and Jubayl in 1104, and Tripoli itself fell in 1109.
