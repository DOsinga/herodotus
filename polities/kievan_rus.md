description: >-
  Kievan Rus' united lands along eastern Europe's river routes under princes of the Rurik dynasty,
  with Kyiv becoming their principal centre in 882. Its rulers drew wealth from tribute, commerce,
  and military expeditions, linking Baltic and Black Sea networks. Prince Vladimir's adoption of Christianity in
  988 strengthened ties with Byzantium and helped shape religious and literary traditions. Princes, their armed
  followers, local elites, and urban assemblies all influenced government. Yaroslav the Wise fostered dynastic alliances
  and legal development, but succession arrangements encouraged rivalry among branches of the ruling family. This
  record ends with Mstislav the Great's death in 1132, when regional principalities increasingly governed themselves.
  Kyiv remained important, while separate Rus' states continued many institutions and traditions of this realm.
