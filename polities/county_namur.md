description: >-
  The County of Namur was a small principality in the southern Low Countries, centred on the town
  of Namur where the Sambre meets the Meuse. Counts are recorded from the tenth century, and the
  county passed through the houses of Luxembourg, Hainaut and Courtenay before Guy of Dampierre,
  count of Flanders, bought it in 1263. This record follows its separate government from 1250,
  when imperial authority faded. The counts ruled from the castle above the confluence and drew
  income from the Meuse trade and the iron and stone of the Ardennes. John III, the last count of
  the Dampierre line, sold the county to Philip the Good of Burgundy in 1421 while keeping it for
  life, and Burgundian rule began at his death in 1429.
