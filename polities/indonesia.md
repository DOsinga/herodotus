  Indonesia is an archipelagic republic stretching between the Indian and Pacific oceans, proclaimed independent by Sukarno and Mohammad Hatta in August 1945. During the revolution, its government controlled substantial parts of Java and Sumatra while resisting Dutch attempts to restore colonial rule. Dutch recognition followed in 1949, and a brief federal arrangement gave way to a unitary republic in 1950. Parliamentary politics yielded to Sukarno's Guided Democracy, then to Suharto's authoritarian New Order after violent upheaval and mass killings in the 1960s. Suharto's fall in 1998 opened a period of democratic reform, decentralisation, and presidential elections. Jakarta has long been its national governing centre. Diverse islands, languages, and religious communities remain joined by national institutions and a shared Indonesian language.
