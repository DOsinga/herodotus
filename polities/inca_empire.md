description: >-
  The Inca Empire expanded from Cusco in the Andes between 1438 and 1533, becoming the
  largest state in precolonial America. Pachacuti and his successors incorporated peoples
  through warfare, negotiation and resettlement. The Sapa Inca ruled through nobles,
  officials and local leaders, organising the empire into four regions. Labour obligations
  supplied armies, roads, terraces and storehouses, while knotted quipu recorded
  information. A network of mountain roads connected communities across parts of modern
  Peru, Ecuador, Bolivia, Chile and Argentina. Epidemics and a succession war between
  Huascar and Atahualpa weakened the state before Spanish arrival. Francisco Pizarro
  captured Atahualpa in 1532, and Spanish forces entered Cusco in 1533. Inca resistance
  continued elsewhere, but the empire's central government had fallen to the Spanish
  conquerors.
