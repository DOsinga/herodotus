description: >-
  The County of Provence was a largely self-governing principality in southeastern France,
  between the lower Rhône, the Alps and the Mediterranean. Count William I, called the Liberator,
  drove the Saracens from their base at Fraxinet around 972, and his family then governed the
  region with little regard for the kings of Burgundy or the emperors who later claimed it. By
  marriage the county passed in 1112 to the counts of Barcelona, who ruled it from Aix, and in
  1246 to Charles of Anjou, later king of Naples. The Angevin counts governed Provence through
  seneschals and a chamber of accounts at Aix, and René of Anjou held court there in the
  fifteenth century. After Charles of Maine died in 1481 the county passed to Louis XI of France.
