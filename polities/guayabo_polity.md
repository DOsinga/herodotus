description: >-
  Guayabo was a chiefly centre on the southern slopes of Turrialba Volcano in present
  Costa Rica. This record covers its developed political centre from about 500 to its
  abandonment around 1400, rather than the much earlier first occupation. Archaeologists
  have uncovered raised stone platforms, paved roads, tombs, carved stones, and
  waterworks, showing organized construction and links with surrounding settlements.
  Circular foundations supported wooden houses with thatched roofs; the largest central
  mound probably carried a building connected with leadership and public activity.
  Aqueducts and drainage channels reveal careful management of water in a rainy landscape.
  These remains support a reconstruction of ranked society, but its rulers, borders, and
  precise institutions are unknown. The reasons for abandonment before Spanish contact
  remain uncertain.
