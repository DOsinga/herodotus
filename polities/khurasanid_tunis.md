description: >-
  Khurasanid Tunis was a principality centred on Tunis, in present Tunisia, ruled by the Banu
  Khurasan, a Sanhaja Berber family. When the Zirids abandoned Kairouan for Mahdia in 1057
  under pressure from the Banu Hilal, Tunis asked the Hammadid emir for a governor, and he
  appointed Abd al-Haqq ibn Khurasan. Abd al-Haqq and his descendants governed the city in
  their own right, with the title of shaykh and at first with a council of notables, while
  recognising the Hammadids or the Zirids in turn. They paid the Arab tribes for peace and
  built walls, a palace and a mosque, and Tunis grew in importance. The Hammadids annexed the
  city from 1128 to 1148. The Almohads ended Khurasanid rule in 1159.
