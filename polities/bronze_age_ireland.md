description: >-
  Bronze Age Ireland describes the island's prehistoric communities during a conventional
  span from about 2500 to 500 BCE, rather than a single state. Early copper working at
  places such as Ross Island preceded the use of bronze made with imported tin. Craftspeople
  produced axes, swords, vessels and trumpets, while native gold supported distinctive
  ornaments including lunulae and later elaborate collars. These objects connect Ireland
  with exchange networks reaching Britain and continental Europe. Burial customs changed,
  with stone cists, cremation urns and continued use of older monuments. Settlements and
  rich deposits suggest differences in wealth, but named rulers and political boundaries are
  unknown. The transition to ironworking was gradual, and newer dating often places its
  beginnings earlier than the traditional endpoint.
