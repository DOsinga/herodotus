description: >-
  The Umayyad Caliphate was an Islamic empire governed from Syria between 661 and 750.
  Mu'awiya established its ruling line after the first Muslim civil war, making Damascus the
  principal capital and turning the caliphate towards hereditary succession. Caliphs
  appointed provincial governors and relied heavily on Syrian armies, while ruling
  populations with many languages and religions. Conquests carried their authority across
  North Africa into Iberia, and eastwards into Central Asia and Sindh. Abd al-Malik
  strengthened the central government, promoted Arabic administration and introduced
  distinctive Islamic coinage. Civil wars, tribal rivalries and revolts strained this realm.
  The Abbasid revolution overthrew it in 750. A surviving Umayyad prince later founded a
  separate state at Córdoba, rather than continuing government of the eastern empire.
