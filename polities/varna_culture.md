description: >-
  The Varna culture flourished along the western Black Sea coast in present day Bulgaria,
  approximately between 4600 and 4200 BCE. It is an archaeological culture, not a
  documented kingdom, and its government remains unknown. Settlements and cemeteries
  reveal skilled copper working, pottery production and wide exchange networks. The Varna
  cemetery is especially famous for exceptionally early gold ornaments, together with
  copper tools, flint blades and imported shells. Wealth was distributed unevenly among
  graves, suggesting pronounced social ranking. Some richly furnished symbolic burials
  contain no body, while other graves include objects interpreted as signs of authority.
  These finds show distinctions in status without identifying named rulers or
  institutions. The disappearance of this cultural pattern does not provide a precise
  political ending.
