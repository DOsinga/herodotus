description: >-
  Michelsberg culture was a Late Neolithic archaeological tradition spread across western central
  Europe between approximately 4400 and 3500 BCE. Named after the Michaelsberg hill near
  Untergrombach in Germany, it extended along the Rhine into neighbouring France, Belgium, and
  the Netherlands. Its communities grew cereals, raised cattle and other livestock, and
  supplemented farming with hunting. Tulip shaped beakers help archaeologists trace its
  development. Large settlements often had earthwork enclosures, while timber buildings and
  storage pits reveal everyday life. Some sites show differences in wealth and access to imported
  objects, but no common state or named rulers are known. Burial practices remain unevenly
  understood. The tradition gave way to successors, including Wartberg, with evidence of
  continuity rather than one documented political collapse.
