description: >-
  The Kartid state ruled from Herat in Khorasan between 1244 and 1381, covering parts of modern Afghanistan and neighboring lands. Its Sunni Muslim rulers belonged to a family connected with the earlier Ghurids and usually bore the title malik. They preserved a regional court while accepting Mongol supremacy, first within the Mongol Empire and then under the Ilkhanate. Relations with Mongol rulers alternated between cooperation, suspicion, and armed conflict. After the Ilkhanate broke apart in 1335, the Kartids expanded their authority and competed with other Khorasan powers, including the Sarbadars. Herat remained their political center throughout these changes. Timur captured the city in 1381 and ended its regional government; surviving members of the ruling family were subsequently imprisoned and killed.
