description: >-
  The zipazgo of Bacatá was the southern Muisca confederation on the Bogotá savanna in the eastern
  highlands of present Colombia. Its ruler, the zipa, held a paramount position over allied and
  subject chiefdoms such as Zipaquirá, Fusagasugá and Guatavita, which Zipa Nemequene brought
  under his control. Muisca communities farmed maize, potatoes and quinoa, and Bacatá controlled
  the salt springs of Zipaquirá, whose salt was traded far beyond the plateau. Its rulers fought
  repeated wars with the zaque of Hunza to the north and with the Panche to the west. Gold
  offerings in lakes such as Guatavita inspired the European legend of El Dorado. Spanish forces
  under Gonzalo Jiménez de Quesada defeated Zipa Tisquesusa in 1537 and founded Santafé de Bogotá
  in 1538.
