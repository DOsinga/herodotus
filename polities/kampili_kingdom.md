description: >-
  The Kampili Kingdom was a Hindu monarchy near the Tungabhadra River in what is now northeastern Karnataka, India. Recorded here from 1300, it grew from the power of Singeya Nayaka, a former Hoysala commander, and was ruled by his son Kampilideva. Its fortified centres lay near Ballari and Hampi, where Kannada inscriptions preserve evidence of royal activity and temple building. Kampili emerged amid competition between southern kingdoms and the advancing Delhi Sultanate, rather than controlling a broad South Indian empire. Kampilideva opposed Delhi's claims, but Muhammad bin Tughluq's forces destroyed his kingdom in 1327. Accounts describe the king's death and mass immolation during the final defeat. The region later helped provide the setting for the rise of Vijayanagara in 1336.
