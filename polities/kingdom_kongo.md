description: >-
  The Kingdom of Kongo developed around 1390 in west central Africa, extending across parts of
  present Angola and the two Congo republics. Its capital, Mbanza Kongo, concentrated royal power,
  markets, and a court of nobles. A king known as the manikongo governed provinces through
  appointed officials and negotiated succession with families. Portuguese contact began in the
  late fifteenth century, and royal conversion made Catholic Christianity an important part of
  court life. Relations included diplomacy and trade, but slavery and Portuguese expansion also
  caused conflict. Defeat at Mbwila in 1665 triggered decades of civil war before reunification.
  The monarchy survived with reduced reach into the nineteenth century. This record ends in 1888,
  when Pedro V accepted Portuguese protection; the crown was abolished in 1914.
