description: >-
  The Shah-Armens were a Turkish dynasty that governed the country around Lake Van in
  eastern Anatolia from 1100 to 1207, with their capital at Ahlat on the lake's northwestern
  shore. The name means kings of Armenia. Sökmen al-Qutbi, a commander in Seljuk service,
  took Ahlat in 1100, and his descendants ruled most of the present provinces of Bitlis and
  Van and parts of Muş. They acknowledged the Seljuk sultans but governed their own state,
  fighting the Kingdom of Georgia to the north and taking part in the wars against the
  Crusaders. Under Sökmen II, who reigned for more than half a century, Ahlat grew into a
  rich trading city. After the line died out, slave commanders held the city until the
  Ayyubid prince al-Awhad took Ahlat in 1207.
