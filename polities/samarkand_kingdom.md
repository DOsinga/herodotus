description: >-
  The Kingdom of Samarkand was a Sogdian monarchy in the fertile valley of the
  Zeravshan River, in present Uzbekistan. Its beginnings here are estimated around 600,
  before Shishpir established the Ikhshid ruling line during the seventh century.
  Samarkand's merchants linked Central Asia with distant markets, while royal coins and
  the Afrasiab palace paintings preserve evidence of its court and international
  contacts. Its kings negotiated between Turkish powers, Tang China, and the expanding
  Arab caliphate. Qutaiba's conquest in 712 reduced local authority without immediately
  eliminating the dynasty. Gurak maintained a smaller court at Ishtikhan through
  shifting alliances. His successor Turgar continued issuing coins until Abbasid rule
  displaced the native government around 755, a date that remains approximate in the
  surviving evidence.
