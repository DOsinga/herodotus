description: >-
  The Ōgigayatsu Uesugi were a branch of the Uesugi family who governed Sagami and much of
  Musashi in the Kantō plain of eastern Japan, from the Ōnin War until their destruction at
  Kawagoe in 1546. With their cousins of the Yamanouchi line they had served as deputies of the
  Kamakura government and fought the Kantō kubō after 1455. Their retainer Ōta Dōkan built the
  castles of Edo and Kawagoe in 1457, and the Ōmori family held Odawara for them. From 1487 the
  two Uesugi lines fought each other, which let Hōjō Sōun take Odawara and then all of Sagami
  by 1516. The Hōjō went on to take Edo in 1524 and Kawagoe in 1537. Uesugi Tomosada died in
  the failed siege of Kawagoe in 1546.
