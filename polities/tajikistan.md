  Tajikistan is a mountainous, landlocked republic in Central Asia, independent since
  the Soviet Union broke apart in 1991. Its capital is Dushanbe, and Tajik, a form of
  Persian, distinguishes it linguistically from most neighboring states. Independence
  was followed by a destructive civil war between the government and a varied
  opposition, ending with a peace agreement in 1997. Presidential rule subsequently
  became increasingly concentrated and authoritarian. The Pamir Mountains dominate the
  eastern region, while western valleys support farming and much of the population.
  Cotton, aluminum, hydroelectric power, and money sent home by migrant workers have
  shaped its economy. The state has continued through these political changes,
  balancing relations with Russia, China, and nearby countries while guarding its long
  frontier with Afghanistan.
