description: >-
  The Mutawakkilite Kingdom of Yemen emerged in northern Yemen in 1918 after
  Ottoman withdrawal. Imam Yahya combined the authority of a Zaydi leader with
  royal government, ruling from Sanaa through officials, family members, and negotiated relationships
  with tribes. The monarchy sought recognition abroad while resisting outside influence and
  preserving a conservative order. Disputes with Saudi Arabia led to war in
  1934, while British control around Aden limited its claims in the south.
  Yahya's assassination in 1948 brought a constitutional revolt, defeated by his son
  Ahmad. Reform and opposition continued. After Ahmad's death, army officers overthrew his
  successor al-Badr in 1962 and proclaimed the Yemen Arab Republic. Royalist resistance
  survived during the ensuing civil war, but the kingdom's government had fallen.
