description: >-
  After the assassination of the emperor Langdarma in 842, the Tibetan Empire broke apart in
  a civil war between the princes Yumtän and Ösung, followed by revolts against what was
  left of royal authority. In central Tibet, the provinces of Ü and Tsang, government passed
  to regional lords: Yumtän's descendants held parts of Ü around Lhasa, a line descended
  from Ösung held the Yarlung valley, and noble clans ruled other districts from their own
  fortresses with private armies. From the late tenth century these lords became patrons of
  the reviving monasteries, and schools such as the Kadam, Sakya and Kagyu grew up under
  clan families. No ruler held the whole region until the Mongols imposed their authority in
  the 1240s.
