description: >-
  The Kingdom of Hejaz ruled western Arabia from 1916 to 1925 under the Hashemite family,
  with Mecca its capital. Hussein bin Ali, the Ottoman-appointed sharif of Mecca,
  proclaimed independence during the Arab Revolt and became king. His monarchy controlled
  Islam's pilgrimage centres and drew authority from his family's descent from Muhammad.
  British assistance helped the revolt, but Hussein's hopes for a much larger Arab kingdom
  conflicted with the postwar mandate settlements. Disputes over Palestine and the
  mandates damaged relations with Britain. Meanwhile Ibn Saud's forces expanded from
  central Arabia. Hussein abdicated in 1924, leaving his son Ali to defend the remaining
  kingdom from Jeddah. Saudi conquest ended its separate government in 1925 and
  incorporated its territories into Ibn Saud's dominions.
