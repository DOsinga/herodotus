description: >-
  The Sukhothai Kingdom was a monarchy centered in north-central Thailand, represented here from 1238 to 1438. Its formation is traditionally associated with Si Inthrathit's rise, although the earlier history and meaning of that event remain debated. Royal authority operated through relationships with neighboring towns rather than uniform control within fixed borders. Sukhothai and Si Satchanalai formed connected centers of power, trade, and religious activity. Ram Khamhaeng's reign became especially important in later accounts of Thai kingship and writing. Theravada Buddhism shaped royal patronage, sculpture, and temple architecture, while ceramic production linked the region with wider markets. Influence declined as surrounding centers strengthened and Ayutthaya intervened. Following its last king's death in 1438, Sukhothai passed under Ayutthaya, ending its separate government.
