  Finland is a northern European state bordering Sweden, Norway, and Russia, with Helsinki
  as its capital. It declared independence in 1917 after more than a century as an
  autonomous grand duchy within the Russian Empire. A bitter civil war followed in 1918,
  and a republican constitution was adopted in 1919. Its elected civilian government
  developed into a parliamentary republic with an elected president and legislature.
  Finland preserved its independence through the Winter War and Continuation War against
  the Soviet Union, although it lost territory. Postwar policy combined close economic
  ties with the West and careful relations with its eastern neighbor. Industrial growth
  and a broad welfare system transformed society. Finland joined the European Union in
  1995 and NATO in 2023.
