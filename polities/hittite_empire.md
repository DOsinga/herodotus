description: >-
  The Hittite state developed in central Anatolia around 1650 BCE and lasted until about
  1180 BCE, encompassing its Old Kingdom and later imperial phases. Hattusa was its main
  capital, with archives preserving royal correspondence, laws, rituals and treaties. Kings
  governed through a royal household, officials and dependent rulers, while queens could
  hold religious and political authority. Expansion brought much of Anatolia and northern
  Syria under Hittite control. Competition with Egypt culminated in the battle of Kadesh and
  a subsequent peace treaty, one of antiquity's best preserved diplomatic agreements. The
  kingdom faced succession disputes, warfare and disruption near the end of the Bronze Age.
  Hattusa was abandoned as central government collapsed, though successor kingdoms continued
  Hittite traditions in Syria and Anatolia.
