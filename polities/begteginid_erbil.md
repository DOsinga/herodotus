description: >-
  The Begteginids were a Turkmen family that governed Erbil, the old Arbela east of Mosul, for
  most of the twelfth and early thirteenth centuries. Zayn al-Din Ali Kuchuk, a commander of the
  Zengids, received the city around 1144 and kept it as his own lordship while serving the Zengid
  rulers of Mosul. After his death in 1168 an atabeg set aside his elder son Gökböri in favour of
  a younger brother, and Gökböri spent years in Zengid and then Ayyubid service, fighting for
  Saladin against the crusaders. In 1190 Saladin gave Erbil back to him, and Gökböri ruled it
  until his death in 1233, remembered for his hospitals and charitable foundations and for
  popularising the celebration of the Prophet's birthday. Having no sons, he left Erbil to the
  Abbasid caliph.
