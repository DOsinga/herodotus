description: >-
  The Jaunpur Sultanate was a Muslim monarchy in the middle Ganges region of northern
  India, established in 1394 as Delhi's Tughlaq government weakened. Its founder, Malik
  Sarwar, had served as a royal minister before becoming governor and building an
  independent court at Jaunpur. His successors, known as the Sharqi dynasty, expanded
  into surrounding territories in present day Uttar Pradesh and Bihar. Ibrahim Shah
  encouraged scholarship and religious education, helping Jaunpur gain a reputation as
  an intellectual centre. Distinctive mosques, including the Atala Mosque, survive from
  this period. The sultans repeatedly contested Delhi's rulers for supremacy. Bahlul
  Lodi defeated Husain Shah and took Jaunpur in 1479, ending the independent government
  represented here, although Sharqi resistance and claims continued afterward for years.
