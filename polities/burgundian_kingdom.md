description: >-
  The Burgundians established a kingdom near the Rhine in 411, then rebuilt their realm in eastern
  Gaul after its destruction in 437. Roman authorities settled survivors in Sapaudia around Lake
  Geneva in 443. Their kings expanded into the Rhône and Saône valleys, with Lyon becoming the
  royal seat. Burgundian rulers also held Roman military offices, drawing on imperial institutions
  while governing a mixed Burgundian and Roman population. Gundobad's law code addressed relations
  between these communities. Initially associated with Arian Christianity, the royal house later
  adopted the faith of its Catholic neighbours. Rivalries within the dynasty and pressure from the
  Franks weakened the kingdom. Frankish conquest in 534 ended its separate government, although
  Burgundy survived as a regional name and identity.
