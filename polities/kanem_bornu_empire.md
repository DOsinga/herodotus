description: >-
  Kanem–Bornu was a long-lived monarchy around Lake Chad, traditionally traced to about 700 and ending
  here with Rabih's conquest in 1893. Early rulers governed Kanem from Njimi and used the
  title mai. Conversion to Islam strengthened connections with North Africa and wider trading networks. Warfare
  and pressure from the Bilala drove the ruling house west into Bornu, where Ngazargamu became
  a capital. Rulers controlled agricultural lands and Saharan routes carrying salt, slaves, and other goods.
  Idris Alooma expanded military strength and administration during the sixteenth century. In the nineteenth century,
  the al-Kanemi family displaced the older royal line. Rabih's invasion destroyed the independent government, although
  related traditional emirates later survived under colonial rule and within Nigeria as non-sovereign local institutions.
