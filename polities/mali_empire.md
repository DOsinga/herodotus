description: >-
  The Mali Empire arose in West Africa around 1235 after Sundiata Keita's victory over the Sosso
  ruler at Kirina. Its Mandinka heartland lay along the upper Niger, and its authority eventually
  reached across large parts of the western Sahel. The mansa ruled through court officials,
  provincial governors and subordinate local rulers, supported by tribute and commerce. Goldfields
  and routes carrying salt and other goods linked Mali to North African markets. Mansa Musa's
  pilgrimage to Mecca in 1324 made its wealth famous abroad. Cities including Timbuktu became
  centres of Islamic learning, alongside older local religious traditions. Succession struggles
  and the growth of Songhai reduced imperial power. The remaining kingdom fragmented during the
  seventeenth century, with 1670 marking its conventional endpoint here.
