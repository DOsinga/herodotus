description: >-
  The Haixi Jurchens were the middle group of Jurchen tribes recognised by the Ming, living
  along the Songhua River and its tributaries in what is now Jilin and southern
  Heilongjiang. From the early fifteenth century the Ming enrolled their chiefs as
  commanders of frontier guards, granting hereditary titles and licences to trade at the
  horse markets of Kaiyuan. The titles did not bring Ming government: each chief led his
  own clans, fought his neighbours and sometimes raided Liaodong. Through the fifteenth and
  sixteenth centuries many Haixi groups moved south towards the Ming frontier, and Mongol
  influence on their language and customs was strong. By the middle of the sixteenth
  century they had regrouped into four rival states, Hada, Ula, Hoifa and Yehe, later known
  together as the Hūlun.
