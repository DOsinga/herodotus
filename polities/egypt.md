  Modern Egypt is represented from its formal independence in 1922, centred on the Nile valley
  and delta, with Cairo as its capital. British influence persisted after independence,
  especially around the strategically important Suez Canal. The Free Officers overthrew the
  monarchy in 1952, and a republic was proclaimed the following year. Under Gamal Abdel Nasser,
  Egypt nationalised the canal, pursued Arab nationalism and briefly united with Syria in the
  United Arab Republic while retaining its government. Anwar Sadat later changed economic policy
  and made peace with Israel. Long presidential rule under Hosni Mubarak ended during the 2011
  uprising, followed by further upheaval and renewed military influence. Egypt remains a republic
  whose presidency dominates national politics, linking African and eastern Mediterranean
  affairs.
