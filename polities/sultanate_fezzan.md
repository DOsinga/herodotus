description: >-
  The Sultanate of Fezzan was ruled by the Awlad Muhammad dynasty from the oasis town of
  Murzuk in southwestern Libya, from about 1550 to 1812. Its founder, Muhammad al-Fasi, was
  remembered as a sharif or pilgrim from the west who was invited to defend the oases from
  raiders. The sultans controlled the Fezzan oases and the caravan routes linking the
  Mediterranean with Bornu and Lake Chad, and Murzuk grew rich on the trade in slaves and
  goods. The Ottoman governors of Tripoli repeatedly invaded, killed sultans and demanded
  tribute, and Ottoman garrisons held Murzuk at times, but the dynasty kept returning to
  power and governed the oases itself. Muhammad al-Mukni, acting for the Karamanli rulers of
  Tripoli, ended the dynasty around 1812.
