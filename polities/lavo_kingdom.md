description: >-
  Lavo was a kingdom centred on Lavapura, now Lopburi in central Thailand. Its traditional
  foundation is dated to 648, though early chronology rests partly on later accounts.
  Local kings ruled amid the Buddhist communities associated with Dvaravati, later drawing
  strongly on Khmer language, religion and court culture. The kingdom's position connected
  the Chao Phraya basin with inland and coastal trading routes. Angkor's expansion
  interrupted separate government around 1001 and again from 1181, with conventional
  restorations placed in 1052 and 1218. This record follows the Lopburi court rather than
  every neighbouring city or the separate Ayodhya government. Lavo's rulers became closely
  involved in the rise of Ayutthaya. Incorporation in 1388 ended its separate political
  role, while Lopburi remained an important city.
