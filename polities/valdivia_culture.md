description: >-
  The Valdivia culture developed along Ecuador's Pacific coast, especially the Santa Elena peninsula. Its span is estimated here from about
  3500 to 1800 BCE. Archaeological remains show settled communities with houses arranged around open central spaces, combining fishing, gathering, and
  hunting with farming. Cultivated crops included maize, beans, squash, and cotton, which supplied material for textiles. Valdivia is especially known
  for early pottery and small human figurines, often depicting women with modelled hairstyles. Changes in ceramics show a long local
  development rather than the history of a single state. The evidence does not identify kings or a shared government across
  all settlements. Later regional traditions, including Machalilla, followed Valdivia, while its communities contributed to the development of settled coastal life.
