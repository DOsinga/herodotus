description: >-
  The Andronovo culture describes Bronze Age archaeological communities across the
  Eurasian steppe, especially Kazakhstan, southern Siberia, and areas east of the Urals.
  This record uses approximately 1800 to 1450 BCE for its main phase, while wider
  definitions cover a longer period. Excavated settlements, cemeteries, decorated pottery,
  and bronze objects reveal shared practices without establishing a single government.
  Communities raised cattle, sheep, and horses, cultivated some crops, and worked copper
  and bronze. Burial customs varied, including both inhumation and cremation, and local
  groups differed within the broader complex. Its relationship with the earlier Sintashta
  culture and later regional traditions remains part of archaeological debate. Andronovo
  evidence is often connected with Indo-Iranian language dispersal, but material
  similarities alone cannot identify political borders.
