description: >-
  The Sumpa Kingdom lay in northeastern Tibet around the upper Yangtze and neighbouring highlands. Its early chronology and location are
  approximate: this record begins around 300, associating the community with Supiya mentioned in Kharosthi documents. Chinese and Tibetan accounts describe
  a people whose herds and mountain routes mattered to powers. Tibetan expansion brought them under tribute and increasing control, but
  local kings did not disappear immediately. Their people also supplied troops to Tibetan frontier armies. Tang records name King Molingzan
  in the mid eighth century and describe his attempt to seek a Chinese alliance. Tibetan authorities killed him, while his
  son escaped to China. Around 750 marks the estimated destruction of that local court, not the disappearance of the people.
