description: >-
  Brabant was a duchy in the Low Countries, spanning parts of modern Belgium and the
  southern Netherlands. Its separately governing period is represented here from 1250 until Burgundy absorbed it
  in 1430, although the ducal title was older. Hereditary dukes ruled alongside influential nobles, clergy,
  and prosperous towns, including Leuven, Brussels, Antwerp, and 's-Hertogenbosch. Trade and textile production gave urban
  communities leverage over taxation and princely policy. John I's victory at Worringen in 1288 strengthened
  Brabant and secured Limburg. The Joyous Entry of 1356 set out privileges that constrained the
  duke and became an constitutional reference. Succession disputes and marriage brought the ruling house closer
  to Burgundy. Philip the Good inherited Brabant in 1430, incorporating it into his larger state.
