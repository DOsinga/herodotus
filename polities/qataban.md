description: >-
  Qataban was an ancient South Arabian kingdom centred on Timna in the Bayhan valley of present Yemen. This record represents its later history from roughly 400 BCE to 200 CE, with both boundaries approximate. Kings governed a territory sustained by irrigated agriculture and routes carrying incense and other goods across Arabia. Inscriptions in the Qatabanian language record rulers, religious dedications and commercial regulations, offering evidence beyond later historical accounts. The deity Amm held a prominent place in worship. Qataban competed and cooperated with neighbouring Saba, Hadramawt and other kingdoms as regional power shifted. Its reach and prosperity varied over time, and the capital eventually declined. Hadramawt conquered the remaining kingdom in the late second century, ending its separate royal government.
