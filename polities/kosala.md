description: >-
  Kosala was a kingdom of northern India, centred on the modern Awadh region and extending
  toward the Himalayan foothills. This record covers its early independent monarchy from
  about 700 BCE until its estimated conquest by Magadha in 491 BCE. Shravasti served as a
  major royal capital, while Ayodhya also held an important place in its history and
  traditions. Kosala became one of the strongest mahajanapadas, expanding over neighbouring
  communities and competing with Magadha. King Prasenajit appears prominently in Buddhist
  accounts as a contemporary of the Buddha. The region supported growing towns, iron use,
  and rice farming, and was important to Buddhism and Jainism. Conflict and succession
  struggles weakened the kingdom before Magadha finally absorbed it during the reign of
  Ajatashatru.
