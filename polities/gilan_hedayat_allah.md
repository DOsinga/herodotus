description: >-
  Gilan, the rice and silk country on the southwestern shore of the Caspian, was governed by a
  local family from Fuman in the decades after Nader Shah's death. In 1749 the tribal chief Agha
  Jamal Fumani, who claimed descent from the Eshaqvand rulers of western Gilan, seized the
  province together with an ally. After he was killed in 1752 the Qajar chief Mohammad Hasan Khan
  appointed his son Hedayat-Allah governor, and Hedayat-Allah ruled from Rasht as a semi-
  independent prince. Karim Khan Zand replaced him in 1760 and later made a cousin governor, but
  restored him in 1767 under Zand suzerainty and married into his family. When Agha Mohammad Khan
  Qajar invaded Gilan in 1786, Hedayat-Allah fled and was killed, and the province passed to the
  Qajars.
