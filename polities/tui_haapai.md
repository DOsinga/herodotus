description: >-
  The Tuʻi Haʻapai were the rulers of the central Tongan island group of Haʻapai during the Tongan
  civil wars, the line from which the modern Tongan monarchy grew. Tupoutoʻa, a son of the
  murdered Tuʻi Kanokupolu Tukuʻaho, ruled Haʻapai from 1808 and claimed his father's title,
  though most chiefs refused to recognise him. His son Tāufaʻāhau received a disputed inheritance
  of land in Haʻapai in 1820 and made it his base. He converted to Methodism, allied with the
  missionaries and in 1833 received Vavaʻu from Fīnau ʻUlukālala III. In 1839 he issued the Vavaʻu
  Code, his first written law. When he gained the title Tuʻi Kanokupolu in 1845, he ruled a united
  Tonga as George Tupou I.
