description: >-
  The Zayyanid Kingdom of Tlemcen ruled parts of northwestern Algeria from the thirteenth to sixteenth centuries. It emerged as Almohad authority weakened, under a Berber dynasty whose sultans governed from Tlemcen. The capital sat between Morocco and Ifriqiya and connected Mediterranean commerce with routes into the Sahara. Its merchants, scholars and craftsmen helped make it an important urban centre. Zayyanid rulers repeatedly fought the Marinids of Morocco and the Hafsids of Tunis, sometimes losing their capital or accepting their rivals' overlordship. Dynastic disputes and Spanish expansion along the coast weakened the kingdom further. Ottoman intervention from Algiers ended Zayyanid government in the sixteenth century, with 1556 used here for its absorption. Tlemcen remained a regional city after the dynasty disappeared.
