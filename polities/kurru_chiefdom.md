description: >-
  The Kurru chiefdom stands for the elite of Upper Nubia who ruled the Napata region after
  Egypt's New Kingdom withdrew from Kush around 1069 BCE, and whose descendants became the
  kings of Kush. Its main evidence is the cemetery at el-Kurru, between the third and fourth
  cataracts of the Nile. The oldest graves there are Nubian tumuli, rock-cut pits under round
  mounds of gravel, later given enclosure walls and offering chapels, and then followed by
  square tombs that were probably small pyramids. No early tomb carries a name. George Reisner
  and later Timothy Kendall dated the first burials to the mid-ninth century BCE, while László Török and
  others push them back to about 1070 BCE. The line leads to Alara and Kashta, whose tombs are
  thought to be there.
