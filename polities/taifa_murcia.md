description: >-
  The Taifa of Murcia was a Muslim kingdom centred on the irrigated Segura valley in southeastern
  Spain. It governed separately during five periods between 1011 and 1266, interrupted by control from
  neighbouring taifas and the Almoravid and Almohad empires. Murcia's fortifications and agricultural
  hinterland supported its rulers. The most prominent twelfth century regime was that of Ibn
  Mardanish, whose Murcia-based state resisted Almohad expansion from 1147 until 1172. Independent
  government returned as Almohad authority weakened in the thirteenth century. The Treaty of Alcaraz
  in 1243 placed Murcia under Castilian protection but left Muslim rulers administering local affairs.
  Following the revolt of 1264 and its suppression, direct incorporation into Castile in 1266 ended
  the taifa, unlike the continuing Castilian territorial title.
