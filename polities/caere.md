description: >-
  Caere was an Etruscan city-state near the Tyrrhenian coast of central Italy, at modern Cerveteri. Its development from about 750 BCE produced a wealthy urban centre with ports connecting it to Mediterranean trade. It belonged to the Etruscan League, but managed its own affairs rather than forming part of a unified Etruscan kingdom. Rich chamber tombs at the Banditaccia cemetery reveal families, goods and changing styles of houses and burial. The port sanctuary at Pyrgi left inscriptions documenting links between Etruscan and Phoenician religious traditions. Caere maintained close, sometimes friendly relations with Rome, including sheltering Roman religious possessions during the Gallic attack. Roman pressure eventually reduced its independence. In 273 BCE, Rome confiscated half its territory and absorbed the city.
