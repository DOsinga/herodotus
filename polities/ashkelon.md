description: >-
  Ashkelon was a coastal city state of the Philistines in the southern Levant, near
  present Israel's Mediterranean shore. This record begins with an estimated Philistine
  settlement around 1175 BCE, rather than the city's earlier Canaanite history. It
  belonged to the five principal Philistine cities, each governed by its own ruler. Its
  harbour and position between Egypt and inland routes supported trade across the eastern
  Mediterranean. Archaeology reveals changing pottery traditions, substantial buildings
  and commercial activity, while written sources document dealings with neighbouring
  kingdoms and Assyrian emperors. Ashkelon's kings sometimes resisted imperial demands and
  sometimes paid tribute. Nebuchadnezzar captured and destroyed the city in 604 BCE. Rebuilt
  as the Hellenistic city of Ascalon, it broke away from the Seleucids in 104 BCE and governed
  itself until Pompey's settlement of Syria.
