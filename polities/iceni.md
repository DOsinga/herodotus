description: >-
  The Iceni were a British people whose kingdom occupied much of present Norfolk and nearby eastern
  England. Its beginning around 50 BCE is approximate, supported by archaeological and coin evidence
  rather than a recorded foundation. Local kings issued distinctive coins, and the kingdom initially
  survived Rome's conquest of Britain as an allied client state. Roman interference prompted
  resistance in 47 CE, but Prasutagus continued to rule. After his death, Roman officials seized his
  estate despite arrangements intended to protect his daughters. His widow Boudica led a wider
  uprising that destroyed Roman towns, including Londinium and Camulodunum. Roman forces defeated the
  rebels in 61. The kingdom then disappeared into Roman provincial rule, although the Iceni remained a
  recognized community under occupation.
