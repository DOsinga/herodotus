description: >-
  The County of Hainaut lay in the southern Low Countries, around Mons and Valenciennes, on both
  sides of the present border between Belgium and France. It began as a Frankish district named
  after the river Haine and was ruled by hereditary counts from the tenth century. This record
  follows its separate government from 1250, when imperial authority faded. The counts of the
  Avesnes family inherited Holland and Zeeland in 1299, and the counties then shared their rulers
  while keeping their own estates and officials. Hainaut's towns grew rich on cloth and its
  countryside on grain and coal, and Mons became the main seat. The Wittelsbach heiress
  Jacqueline lost a long war with her cousin Philip the Good and handed Hainaut to him by the
  Treaty of Delft in 1433.
