description: >-
  The Rustamid Imamate was an Ibadi Muslim state centered on Tahert in present Algeria from 777 to 909. Abd al-Rahman ibn Rustam, of Persian origin, was elected imam, but subsequent rulers came from his family. Their religious authority connected Berber communities across the central Maghreb and beyond, without implying equally direct control everywhere. Tahert developed into a commercial and scholarly center linked to caravan routes. Its population included Christians and Muslims of different traditions, alongside the ruling Ibadi community. Succession struggles and declining power weakened the state in its later decades. Fatimid forces captured Tahert in 909, ending the imamate's territorial government. Ibadi refugees continued their religious communities elsewhere, notably around Ouargla and later Mzab, without preserving the former state.
