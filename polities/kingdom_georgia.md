description: >-
  The Kingdom of Georgia united Georgian lands in the Caucasus under Bagrat III in 1008
  and continued through centuries of expansion, invasion, and fragmentation. Its Bagrationi monarchs governed alongside powerful
  nobles and the Georgian Church. David IV strengthened royal institutions and captured Tbilisi, while Queen
  Tamar's reign marked a high point in territorial influence, trade, and cultural patronage. Churches, monasteries,
  and Georgian literature reflect the resources of this Christian kingdom. Mongol domination weakened it during
  the thirteenth century, but George V restored royal strength. Later plague, Timur's invasions, and renewed
  regional warfare damaged the country. Dynastic disputes and assertive provincial rulers divided its lands. This
  record ends in 1478, with Kartli carrying forward the eastern royal government amid that fragmentation.
