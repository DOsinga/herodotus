description: >-
  Gabon became independent from France in 1960 on the Atlantic coast of central Africa,
  with Libreville as its capital. The new republic developed a strong presidency under
  Léon M'ba. French troops restored him after a coup in 1964, illustrating the close
  relationship with the former colonial power. Omar Bongo succeeded him in 1967 and
  governed for decades, first through one party rule and later through a multiparty
  system. His son Ali followed him in 2009 before military officers removed the
  government in 2023. These changes did not end the state. Oil exports brought revenue,
  alongside timber and manganese, but wealth remained unevenly shared. Extensive
  equatorial forests and protected areas distinguish Gabon, where economic
  diversification remains an important national challenge today.
