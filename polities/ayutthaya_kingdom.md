description: >-
  The Ayutthaya Kingdom ruled much of present Thailand from 1351 to 1767, with its main
  capital on the Chao Phraya river system. Its kings initially headed a loose network of
  cities and tributary rulers, later building a stronger court administration and
  controlling labour through ranked officials. Buddhism shaped royal authority, while rice
  farming and overseas commerce supported the state. Chinese, Persian, Japanese and European
  merchants made the capital an international trading centre. King Narai pursued diplomacy
  with France and other foreign powers during the seventeenth century. Warfare with
  neighbouring Burmese kingdoms repeatedly threatened Ayutthaya, despite periods of recovery
  and prosperity. In 1767 a Konbaung army captured and destroyed the capital, ending the
  kingdom and leaving Siam divided among rival leaders.
