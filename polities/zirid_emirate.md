description: >-
  The Zirid Emirate governed Ifriqiya, centred on present-day Tunisia, from 972 until Norman conquest in
  1148. Its hereditary Sanhaja Berber rulers initially acknowledged the Fatimid caliphs while managing their own
  lands. The court first operated around Kairouan, supporting a region with farming, crafts, and Mediterranean
  trade. Territorial divisions produced a separate Hammadid state farther west. In the eleventh century, the
  Zirids rejected Fatimid allegiance and embraced Sunni affiliation. The advance of Banu Hilal groups, military
  defeats, and weakening provincial control reduced their inland authority. The rulers moved to the coastal
  stronghold of Mahdia in 1057, increasingly relying on maritime connections. Roger II's forces from Sicily
  captured Mahdia in 1148, ending the emirate and bringing its remaining possessions under Norman control.
