description: >-
  Hoabinhian names an archaeological tradition of mainland Southeast Asian foragers,
  represented here approximately from 10000 to 2000 BCE. Named after discoveries in
  Vietnam's Hòa Bình province, it is recognised chiefly through stone tools made by flaking
  rounded cobbles, often on one face. Caves, rock shelters and shell deposits preserve
  evidence of hunting, fishing and gathering plants across varied environments. Grinding
  stones, bone implements and later pottery occur in some assemblages. These finds do not
  establish a single people, government or territory, and archaeologists debate how widely
  the label should be applied. Individual sites have different chronologies, with some
  evidence extending beyond this record's estimated span. Changes toward farming and new
  tool traditions happened unevenly rather than through one political collapse.
