description: >-
  Gregory's African State was the imperial government established in Byzantine North
  Africa after Gregory the Patrician rebelled against Constans II in 646. Gregory had
  been exarch of Africa, combining military and civil authority over provinces centered
  on modern Tunisia. Religious conflict helped fuel the break: he defended Chalcedonian
  Christianity against the emperor's support for Monothelitism. Claiming the imperial
  title, he drew on local Roman and Berber support and governed independently of
  Constantinople. In 647 an Arab army invaded from Egypt. Gregory confronted it at
  Sufetula, where his forces were defeated and he was killed according to the account
  generally accepted by historians. The invaders withdrew after taking tribute. Africa
  returned to Byzantine allegiance, rather than becoming a permanent caliphal
  possession.
