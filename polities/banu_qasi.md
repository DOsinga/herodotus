description: >-
  The Banu Qasi were a family of local converts to Islam who ruled the Upper March of al-
  Andalus, in the Ebro valley of northeastern Spain, from Tudela. They claimed descent from a
  Visigothic count, Cassius, and intermarried with the kings of Pamplona. Musa ibn Musa was so
  powerful in the 850s that he called himself the third king of Spain, but the emir stripped
  him of his offices after his defeat at Albelda. In 872 his sons rose again, seized Zaragoza,
  Tudela and Huesca, and ruled independently of Córdoba, allying with Pamplona, Asturias and
  the Catalan counts as it suited them. Defeat by Pamplona in 907 began their decline. In 924
  Abd al-Rahman III removed the family from Tudela and took them to Córdoba.
