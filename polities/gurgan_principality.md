name: "Principality of Gurgan"

description: >-
  After the fall of the Sasanian Empire, Gurgan, the fertile plain at the southeastern corner of
  the Caspian Sea, kept its independence for more than sixty years under Turkic princes known by
  the name or title of Sul. Arab armies reached the region in the 650s and the local rulers
  agreed to pay tribute, but they paid irregularly and held the country with their own troops,
  and the province remained largely Zoroastrian. The princes guarded the frontier between the
  settled lands of Tabaristan and Khorasan and the Turkic nomads of Dihistan and the steppe to
  the north. In 716 the Umayyad governor Yazid ibn al-Muhallab led a large campaign against the
  Caspian principalities, conquered Gurgan and settled Arab troops there, bringing the province
  under the caliphate.
