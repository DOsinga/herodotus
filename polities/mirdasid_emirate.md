description: >-
  The Mirdasid Emirate was an Arab state based in Aleppo that governed northern Syria and
  the western Jazira for most of the period from 1024 to 1080. Its rulers came from the Banu
  Kilab, a Bedouin tribe of the Syrian steppe, and their power rested on the tribe's
  horsemen rather than on a standing army of slave soldiers. Salih ibn Mirdas took Aleppo
  from its Fatimid governor in 1024. The emirs balanced the Fatimid caliphate to the south
  against the Byzantine Empire to the north, acknowledging one or the other as it suited
  them. The Fatimids held Aleppo again from 1038 to 1042. Family quarrels
  and the arrival of the Turkmen weakened the emirate, and in 1080 the last emir handed
  Aleppo to the Uqaylid ruler of Mosul.
