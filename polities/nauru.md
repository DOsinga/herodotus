  Nauru is a island republic in the central Pacific, independent since 1968. Australia
  administered the preceding trust territory, with Britain and New Zealand also involved in its
  colonial arrangements. The elected parliament chooses a president from its members, combining
  the roles of head of state and head of government. Government offices are concentrated in Yaren,
  although the country has no official capital. Phosphate mining brought revenue but severely
  damaged the island's interior. Declining deposits and financial difficulties later forced the
  state to seek other income, including agreements with Australia involving asylum processing.
  Most residents live along the coastal strip. Nauru remains a sovereign state, participating in
  international organisations while facing environmental pressures and the challenges of a limited
  economic base.
