description: >-
  Western Liang was a monarchy in northwestern China's Hexi Corridor from 400 to 421, one
  of the states conventionally called the Sixteen Kingdoms. Li Gao established it after
  breaking with Northern Liang, supported by local Han Chinese elites. Its rulers used the
  title duke rather than emperor. Dunhuang was the first capital; Li Gao moved his court
  to Jiuquan in 405 to strengthen pressure on his eastern rival. Control of oasis towns
  supported government along the routes linking China with Central Asia. Diplomatic
  submissions to larger powers did not remove its own ruling court. After Li Gao died, his
  successor Li Xin lost Jiuquan to Northern Liang in 420. Li Xun briefly continued
  resistance at Dunhuang before conquest in 421.
