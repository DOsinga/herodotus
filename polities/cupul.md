description: >-
  Cupul was one of the largest and most densely populated Maya provinces of the Yucatán
  Peninsula, in the east around the town of Saci, where Valladolid now stands. It took shape
  in the mid fifteenth century after the fall of Mayapan and included the ruins of Chichén
  Itzá and the sacred cenote that drew pilgrims. Unlike some neighbouring provinces, Cupul
  was not ruled by a single supreme lord, and its towns were governed by leaders of the
  Cupul lineage who acted together in war. The province resisted the Spanish fiercely.
  Francisco de Montejo's first expedition was driven out of Chichén Itzá in the 1530s, and
  only in 1543 did Spanish forces take the province and found Valladolid there.
