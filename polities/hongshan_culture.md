description: >-
  The Hongshan culture flourished in northeastern China's West Liao River basin approximately between 4700 and 2900 BCE. Archaeological sites extend across parts of Inner Mongolia and Liaoning, with the culture named after a site near Chifeng. Communities cultivated millet and also raised animals, hunted, and gathered food. Their known remains include finely worked jade ornaments, coiled dragon forms, pottery, and elaborate ritual sites. At Niuheliang, stone tombs, ceremonial platforms, and a building containing clay figures suggest organized religious activity and differences in social standing. These finds reveal complex communities, but do not identify a named state, royal dynasty, or uniform government. Hongshan traditions changed into later regional cultures, while aspects of its jade working and ritual practices influenced subsequent societies.
