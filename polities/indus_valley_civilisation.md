description: >-
  The Indus Valley Civilisation developed across present Pakistan and northwestern India from about 3300 BCE. Its mature urban phase began around 2600 BCE, producing large cities including Harappa and Mohenjo-daro. Brick buildings, planned streets, wells, drains, seals, and standardised weights reveal skilled craftspeople and extensive coordination. Farmers supported settlements growing wheat, barley, and other crops, while traders connected the region with Afghanistan, the Persian Gulf, and Mesopotamia. Its signs remain undeciphered, and neither rulers nor a system of government can be identified. Shared material practices do not prove that one state ruled every city. Around 1900 BCE, major urban centres declined as settlement patterns changed. Regional cultures, including Cemetery H in Punjab, continued elements of earlier life after that transition.
