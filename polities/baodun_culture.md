description: >-
  The Baodun culture developed on the Chengdu Plain in China's Sichuan Basin, approximately 2800 to
  1800 BCE in this record. Archaeologists recognize it through related settlements, pottery and
  agricultural remains rather than written accounts of a named state. Baodun, the largest known
  settlement, had inner and outer earth walls; several neighboring sites also possessed double
  enclosures. Pebbles covering settlement walls are a distinctive feature of the culture. People lived
  in houses made with woven branches and mud, growing rice and foxtail millet alongside other plants.
  The substantial walls show organized labor, but they do not identify rulers or establish how
  communities were governed. Dating remains approximate because radiocarbon evidence is limited. The
  later Sanxingdui tradition followed these communities in Sichuan.
