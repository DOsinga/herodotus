description: >-
  The Samarra culture describes farming communities in central Mesopotamia, roughly between 5500 and
  4800 BCE, rather than a known kingdom. Named for finds at Samarra in modern Iraq, it is also
  documented at Tell es-Sawwan, Tell Shemshara, and other settlements. Archaeologists recognize it
  especially through fine painted pottery bearing geometric patterns, birds, and other stylized
  animals. Evidence at Tell es-Sawwan suggests organized cultivation and irrigation, including the
  growing of flax. Houses, burials, and craft remains show settled communities with developed social
  practices, but they do not identify named rulers or a single common government. Samarran material
  overlapped with neighboring Hassuna and Halaf traditions and contributed to the later Ubaid world.
  Similar pottery elsewhere shows connections, not necessarily shared political control.
