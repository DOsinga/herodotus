description: >-
  The Argaric culture flourished in southeastern Spain during the Early Bronze Age, approximately 2200 to 1550 BCE. Named after El
  Argar in Almería, it extended across Murcia and parts of Granada and Jaén. Excavated hilltop settlements, fortifications, workshops and graves
  reveal a society with strong differences in wealth. Copper and bronze weapons, silver ornaments and distinctive plain pottery were important
  products. People grew cereals, raised livestock and made textiles from wool and flax. Unlike earlier collective tombs, Argaric burials often
  placed individuals beneath houses, sometimes inside large jars. Rich female burials have prompted debate about women's authority. Archaeologists disagree over
  whether these communities formed one state. Their collapse and replacement by less uniform regional traditions remain subjects of continuing research.
