description: >-
  Izamal was a major Maya political and religious centre in northern Yucatan, represented
  here from about 200 BCE to 1000 CE. Large pyramids, extensive settlement and raised
  causeways connecting neighbouring centres show the scale of its regional influence. Its
  builders used dressed stones and a distinctive architectural style also found in
  surrounding towns. Although monumental remains reveal organised authority, the ruling
  history is less clearly documented than at cities with abundant readable royal
  inscriptions. Construction flourished from the Protoclassic through the Late Classic
  periods. During the Terminal Classic, Izamal lost much of its earlier importance as
  Chichen Itza rose. It remained occupied and became a renowned pilgrimage destination, but
  those later religious roles do not establish continuity of its government.
