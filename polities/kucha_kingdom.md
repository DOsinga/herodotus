description: >-
  The Kingdom of Kucha occupied an oasis along the northern edge of the Taklamakan Desert in
  present day Xinjiang. Its beginnings are estimated here around 200 BCE, and its separate
  royal government ended with Tang conquest in 648. Han accounts identify Kucha as a
  populous kingdom engaged in shifting relations with Chinese courts and steppe powers. Its
  own kings maintained a rich oasis economy based on irrigated farming and Silk Road
  commerce. The population spoke Kuchean, or Tocharian B, while other languages served
  religious and administrative purposes. Buddhist monasteries, the nearby Kizil cave
  paintings, and the translator Kumarajiva reflect its cultural importance. Kuchean music
  also influenced Chinese court entertainment. Tang military administration replaced the
  kingdom's government after capturing its capital.
