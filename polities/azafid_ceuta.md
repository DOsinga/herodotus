description: >-
  Azafid Ceuta was the city state of the Banu al-Azafi, a family of scholars and notables who
  governed Ceuta, on the African shore of the Strait of Gibraltar, in the thirteenth and early
  fourteenth centuries. As Almohad authority collapsed, Abu al-Qasim al-Azafi expelled the
  caliph's governor and took control of the city. The family ruled it as hereditary lords, at
  times acknowledging the Hafsids or the Marinids, and Ceuta prospered from trade with Genoa
  and the Christian ports. A rising against the Marinids in 1304 ended when the fleet of
  Nasrid Granada took the city in 1306. The Marinids recovered Ceuta in 1309 and soon restored
  the Azafids. Disorder after Yahya al-Azafi's death led the Marinids to take direct control
  in 1327.
