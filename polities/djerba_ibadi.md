description: >-
  Ibadi Djerba was the self-governing island of Djerba off southern Tunisia after the collapse
  of Zirid authority, recorded here as a chiefdom from 1057 to 1135. Most islanders followed
  the Ibadi branch of Kharijite Islam, which rejected hereditary rule, and their communities
  were led by councils of religious scholars and village notables rather than by a prince. The
  Bedouin tribes that overran the mainland did not reach the island. Djerba's sailors turned
  to piracy against the shipping of Ifriqiya, Sicily and southern Italy. In response Roger II
  of Sicily sent a fleet that conquered the island in 1135, and the Normans held it, putting
  down a revolt in 1154, until the Almohad conquest of 1160.
