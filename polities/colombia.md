  Colombia occupies northwestern South America, with Caribbean and Pacific coasts and a
  capital at Bogotá. This record begins in 1831, when the breakup of Gran Colombia left the
  Republic of New Granada as a separate state. Its nineteenth century governments repeatedly
  changed names and moved between centralized and federal arrangements before adopting the
  Republic of Colombia in 1886. Liberal and Conservative rivalries produced civil wars, and
  Panama separated in 1903. Coffee exports helped shape the economy, while twentieth century
  political violence developed into a prolonged conflict involving guerrillas, state forces,
  and paramilitary groups. Colombia is now a presidential republic with elected national
  legislative institutions under its 1991 constitution. Its continuing history combines
  democratic government, regional diversity, and unevenly achieved peace.
