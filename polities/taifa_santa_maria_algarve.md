description: >-
  The Taifa of Santa Maria do Algarve was a small Muslim kingdom in what is now southern
  Portugal, centred on the town of Shantamariyya al-Gharb, present Faro. It was one of the
  taifa states that appeared as the caliphate of Córdoba broke apart. Its rulers were the Banu
  Harun, a local Muslim family of Iberian descent. Said ibn Harun made himself independent
  around 1018 and ruled until about 1041, and his son Muhammad al-Mutasim followed him. The
  kingdom held the eastern Algarve coast between the larger taifas of Seville, Badajoz and
  Silves. In 1051 al-Mutadid of Seville conquered it, as he absorbed one small neighbour after
  another. The family stayed on as the town's judges until the Portuguese conquest of 1249.
