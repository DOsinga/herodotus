description: >-
  Merimde culture describes Neolithic village life in Egypt's western Nile Delta during
  the fifth millennium BCE, represented here by estimated dates of 4800 to 4100 BCE. Its
  principal known settlement, Merimde Beni Salama, lies northwest of modern Cairo.
  Excavated layers show changes from lighter occupation to a larger community with
  storage areas. Residents grew crops and kept animals, supplementing farming with
  fishing and hunting. Small round or oval huts, pottery, stone tools, and burials
  provide the evidence for everyday life. Some bodies were buried within the settlement,
  and children were especially common among these graves. A clay head is an unusual
  early human representation. These remains do not establish a kingdom, named leaders,
  or a single documented system of government.
