description: >-
  Tikal was a Maya kingdom in the tropical lowlands of present northern Guatemala, with dynastic government dated approximately from 100 to 900 CE. Its kings claimed sacred authority, celebrated victories and rituals on carved monuments, and built large temples and palace complexes. The early dynasty traced its origins to Yax Ehb Xook. A political upheaval in 378 connected the court with powerful figures associated with Teotihuacan. Tikal later competed with Calakmul through wars, alliances, and influence over neighboring cities. Defeat in 562 interrupted its prominence, but renewed expansion followed under Jasaw Chan Kawiil. The last known royal monument dates to 869. Population loss and the collapse of royal institutions followed; scattered later habitation does not demonstrate that the kingdom continued.
