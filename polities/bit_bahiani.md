description: >-
  Bit Bahiani was an Aramean kingdom on the upper Khabur river in what is now northeastern
  Syria, with its capital at Guzana, the modern Tell Halaf. Like other Aramean states it was
  named after the founder of its ruling house, Bahian. It arose as Aramean groups settled
  the Khabur basin once Middle Assyrian control of the region had faded. Its best known
  ruler, Kapara, built a palace at Guzana decorated with carved stone reliefs and large
  statues, which were excavated by Max von Oppenheim in the early twentieth century. The
  kingdom submitted to Adad-nirari II of Assyria in 894 BCE. Its rulers then governed as
  Assyrian governors, as the bilingual statue of Hadad-yith'i shows, until Guzana was made a
  regular province in 808 BCE.
