description: >-
  Scotland developed in northern Britain from the kingdom of Alba, with 843 conventionally marking its foundation. Its monarchs gradually extended authority over neighbouring territories, while powerful nobles, towns, and the church remained important in government. Parliament emerged as an institution representing the kingdom's estates. Wars against England in the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries established a tradition of contested independence. The Reformation replaced the established Catholic church with a Protestant settlement in 1560. In 1603, Scotland and England acquired the same monarch but retained separate governments. English conquest interrupted Scottish independence from 1651 until the restoration of the monarchy in 1660. The Acts of Union in 1707 created Great Britain, ending the separate kingdom while preserving Scotland's legal and church institutions.
