description: >-
  The Aghlabid Emirate governed Ifriqiya, centred on present-day Tunisia and extending into
  eastern Algeria and western Libya, from 800 to 909. Ibrahim ibn al-Aghlab received hereditary
  authority from the Abbasid caliph but established a government that managed its own affairs
  while acknowledging distant overlordship and paying tribute. Kairouan was the urban and
  religious centre; the court occupied nearby al-Abbasiyya and later Raqqada. Emirs faced revolts
  by Arab troops and tensions with local religious scholars and Berber communities. They built
  irrigation works, fortifications, and mosques, while fleets carried warfare into the
  Mediterranean. The conquest of Sicily began in 827 and advanced over decades. A movement backed
  by Kutama Berbers overthrew the dynasty in 909, establishing Fatimid power in its former
  heartland.
