description: >-
  The County of Boulogne was a feudal principality on the Channel coast of northern France,
  centred on Boulogne-sur-Mer and recorded here from 964 to 1214. The counts of Flanders held
  the region in the early tenth century, and a separate line descended from Adelolf ruled
  Boulogne from 964. Eustace II fought for William the Conqueror at Hastings, and his sons
  Godfrey of Bouillon and Baldwin led armies on the First Crusade and became rulers of
  Jerusalem. Through Countess Matilda the county passed to Stephen of Blois, king of England,
  and later to the house of Alsace. Count Renaud of Dammartin joined the coalition against
  Philip II and was captured at Bouvines in 1214, after which the county came under royal
  control.
