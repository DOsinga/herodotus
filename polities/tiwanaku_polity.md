description: >-
  Tiwanaku was a political and ceremonial centre in the southern Lake Titicaca basin of present
  day Bolivia, flourishing approximately from 600 to 1000. Monumental stone buildings, carved figures and decorated
  pottery drew people into a shared religious world. Raised field farming supported communities around the
  city, while llama caravans linked the highlands with distant settlements. Scholars disagree over whether its
  organisation resembled a central state or a network of powerful lineages and locally governed communities.
  Tiwanaku inspired colonies and cultural practices in Peru and Chile, but those connections do not
  prove continuous territorial rule. Around the end of its main period, urban and ceremonial activity
  declined. The archaeological evidence cannot identify a ruling dynasty or one certain cause of collapse.
