description: >-
  The Tsangpa regime ruled from Shigatse in west central Tibet between 1565 and
  1642. Karma Tseten, formerly a retainer and governor under the Rinpungpa, seized
  power and established a hereditary royal government. His successors expanded
  beyond Tsang into other parts of Tibet amid competition between aristocratic
  houses and Buddhist schools. They supported the Karma Kagyu tradition, while
  also patronising other religious communities, and opposed the power of the Gelug
  school and the Dalai Lamas. Political and religious rivalries became closely
  entangled with Mongol intervention. The final ruler, Karma Tenkyong, faced Gushi
  Khan's Khoshut army, which defeated the Tsangpa in 1642. Authority then passed
  to the Ganden Phodrang government associated with the Fifth Dalai Lama, ending
  the dynasty's separate royal rule.
