description: >-
  The Akita clan, known until about 1570 as the Andō, governed northern Dewa province on the
  Sea of Japan coast of northern Honshu from Minato Castle at the mouth of the Omono River, in
  what is now the city of Akita. Claiming descent from Abe no Sadato, the Andō had managed
  trade with the Ainu and the island of Ezo for the Hōjō regents of Kamakura from Tosaminato in
  Tsugaru. Driven out of Tsugaru by the Nanbu in the fifteenth century, they crossed into Dewa,
  where the Minato and Hiyama lines held the coast. Andō Chikasue of the Hiyama line united
  both houses around 1570 and took the name Akita. His son Sanesue submitted to Toyotomi
  Hideyoshi in 1590 and kept Akita as an administrator of the regime.
