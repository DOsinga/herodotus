description: >-
  Egypt's Memphite government survived the collapse of the Old Kingdom under a
  succession of poorly documented kings, conventionally dated here to about 2181–2160
  BCE. These rulers retained the titles and religious claims of pharaohs, but their
  practical authority contracted as provincial governors became independent. Memphis
  remained their likely seat, while alliances with powerful officials at Coptos helped
  sustain the royal house. Later king lists preserve many names, though the order, reign
  lengths, and even the division between the Seventh and Eighth dynasties remain
  uncertain. The Seventh Dynasty may never have existed as a separate ruling house.
  Surviving decrees and scattered monuments offer firmer evidence than later stories.
  Eventually rulers based at Herakleopolis displaced the Memphite kings and established
  another monarchy.
