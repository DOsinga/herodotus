description: >-
  Siwa is an oasis in the Western Desert of Egypt, whose Berber-speaking people lived largely
  outside the control of any state between the Arab conquest of Egypt and 1820. In the eleventh
  century the geographer al-Bakri found only Berbers there, al-Idrisi in the twelfth a Berber
  majority with some Arabs, and al-Maqrizi, visiting in the fifteenth, compared their speech to
  that of the Zenata. The people lived in Shali, a walled town of salt and mud brick on a rock
  outcrop, whose old mosque is dated to 1203. Accounts from around 1800 describe affairs run by
  a council of family heads, the ajwad, split into rival factions. Muhammad Ali's expedition
  annexed Siwa in 1820, though his agent was killed in 1838.
