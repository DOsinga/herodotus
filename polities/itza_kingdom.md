description: >-
  The Itza kingdom was a Maya state in northern Guatemala's Peten region, represented here from 1441 until Spanish conquest in 1697. Its capital, Nojpeten, stood on an island in Lake Peten Itza, now occupied by the town of Flores. Kings bearing the Kan Ek title ruled a community whose temples and ceremonial buildings continued Maya religious traditions. The island's position and surrounding forests helped preserve independence while Spanish power expanded elsewhere. Visits by explorers and missionaries brought negotiations but did not immediately establish colonial government. Rivalry with neighbouring Maya groups, the Kowoj, also shaped the kingdom's politics. In 1697 Martin de Ursua's forces attacked across the lake and captured Nojpeten. Its fall ended the last independent Maya kingdom resisting conquest.
