  Malaysia is a Southeast Asian federation spanning the Malay Peninsula and northern Borneo,
  separated by the South China Sea. It was formed in 1963 when independent Malaya joined with
  Sabah, Sarawak, and Singapore; Singapore left in 1965. A parliamentary government operates
  within a federal constitutional monarchy. The national monarch is chosen in rotation from the
  rulers of nine Malay states, while other states have governors. Kuala Lumpur is the national
  capital and parliamentary seat, and Putrajaya houses much of the federal administration. Malay,
  Chinese, Indian, and Indigenous communities contribute to a varied society with Islam as the
  official religion. The federation emerged amid confrontation with Indonesia and has since
  combined industrial growth with debates over ethnicity, regional rights, and representation.
