description: >-
  The Jianzhou Jurchens were the southern group of Jurchen tribes recognised by the Ming,
  living in the mountains between Liaodong and the Yalu River. In 1403 the Ming made the
  Huligai chief Ahacu commander of the Jianzhou Guard, and Möngke Temür of the Odoli soon
  headed a second guard near the Tumen. Both groups moved south and west over the following
  decades, settling in the valleys of the Hun and Suksuhu rivers by the 1440s. Their chiefs
  held hereditary Ming titles and traded at Fushun and Kaiyuan, but they governed their own
  people, raided Ming and Korean borders and suffered punitive campaigns in 1467
  and 1478. Under Wang Gao in the sixteenth century they formed a loose confederation, which
  broke up under Ming attacks before Nurhaci reunited them.
