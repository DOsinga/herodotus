description: >-
  The Khadga kingdom governed Vanga in eastern Bengal during the seventh and early eighth
  centuries, conventionally dated from 625 to 716. Its court at Karmanta Vasaka is usually placed
  near modern Comilla in Bangladesh. The rulers were Buddhist monarchs who used royal rather than
  imperial titles, emerging between Gauda's decline and the later rise of the Palas. Their
  authority expanded into Samatata, without making every neighbouring eastern Bengali kingdom part
  of the same state. Named kings include Khadgodyama, Jatakhadga, Devakhadga and Rajabhatta, with
  Queen Prabhavati also appearing in the dynasty's history. The sequence depends on limited
  evidence. Later Buddhist rulers governed the region, but the end of Khadga power after its last
  named king is uncertain despite the conventional date.
