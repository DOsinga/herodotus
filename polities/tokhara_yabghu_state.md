description: >-
  The Tokhara Yabghu State governed parts of Tokharistan around the upper Oxus, in present Afghanistan and neighbouring Central Asia, from
  625. Its hereditary rulers were Western Turks who used the title yabghu and initially ruled from Kunduz. Their authority linked
  numerous local principalities rather than replacing every regional court. Buddhism remained prominent, and coinage and Chinese accounts illuminate the state's
  connections across the region. The yabghus acknowledged Western Turk and later Tang supremacy while conducting their own affairs. Arab expansion
  disrupted this arrangement: Qutaiba deported the ruler in 710. A restored court appears by 718, operating from Badakhshan while Arabs
  held Balkh. Its last recorded embassy dates to 758; that year marks an estimated end, not a documented final conquest.
