description: >-
  Erfurt was the largest city of Thuringia, a trading town on the Gera that dealt in woad, the
  blue dye grown in the surrounding countryside. Its lords were the archbishops of Mainz, but from
  the thirteenth century the city council bought up their rights and governed Erfurt as if it were
  a free city, though it never gained that status in law. The city built up a territory of more
  than eighty villages, joined the Hanseatic League and founded a university that opened in 1392,
  where Martin Luther later studied. Erfurt adopted the Reformation, with its Catholic minority
  protected by Mainz. A Swedish garrison held it from 1631 to 1650. In 1664 the archbishop, with
  French and Rhenish troops, besieged the city and forced it to submit.
