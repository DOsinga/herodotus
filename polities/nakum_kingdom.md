description: >-
  Nakum was a Maya royal centre beside the Holmul River in northeastern Guatemala, near Tikal, Yaxha
  and Naranjo. The period from about 250 to 950 CE estimates its Classic and Terminal Classic
  political life, rather than its entire settlement history. Palaces, temples, plazas, tombs and
  carved monuments reveal an elite court, although its rulers and relations with larger neighbours
  remain incompletely understood. Archaeological evidence connects Nakum with both Naranjo and Tikal
  at different times; those links do not necessarily establish direct outside government. Its river
  position provided access to trade and communication routes through the Petén lowlands. Nakum
  expanded as several nearby capitals declined, investing in buildings during the ninth century before
  its own court disappeared and the settlement diminished.
