  Sudan became independent in 1956 after Anglo-Egyptian rule, with Khartoum at the meeting of the Blue and White Nile as its capital. The modern republic occupies northeastern Africa, including Nile farming regions, desert lands and a Red Sea coast. Parliamentary governments repeatedly gave way to military regimes, and struggles over religion, regional power and resources shaped its politics. Two long wars between northern governments and southern movements ended with South Sudan's independence in 2011. Conflict in Darfur brought further destruction and displacement. Omar al Bashir's overthrow in 2019 opened a contested transition, followed by a military coup in 2021. War between the army and Rapid Support Forces began in 2023, severely disrupting government and civilian life across the continuing state.
