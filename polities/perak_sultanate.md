description: >-
  The Perak Sultanate was a Malay state along the Perak river on the west coast of the Malay
  Peninsula. When Malacca fell to the Portuguese, the son of its last sultan, Muzaffar Shah, was
  invited to rule Perak and became its first sultan in 1528. The state's wealth came from tin, which
  drew traders from India, the Dutch East India Company and later the Chinese. Perak came under the
  influence of Aceh for much of the seventeenth century and later resisted Siamese attempts to
  dominate it, with British help from Penang. Disputes over the succession and wars between Chinese
  mining societies in the 1870s led to the Pangkor Treaty of 1874, under which the sultan accepted a
  British Resident.
