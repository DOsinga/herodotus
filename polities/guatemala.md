description: >-
  Guatemala became a separate Central American state in 1839 as the regional federation
  broke apart, and formally declared itself a republic in 1847. Guatemala City remained
  its capital. Rafael Carrera built a conservative government, followed by liberal
  rulers who promoted coffee exports and concentrated land and political power.
  Dictatorships dominated much of its later history. The revolution of 1944 introduced
  elected reform governments, but a United States backed coup removed Jacobo Árbenz in
  1954. Civil war from 1960 to 1996 brought massacres, displacement, and genocide
  against Maya communities. Peace agreements ended the fighting without resolving deep
  inequalities. The country continues as a presidential republic, with an Indigenous
  population whose languages and traditions remain central to national life alongside
  Spanish culture.
