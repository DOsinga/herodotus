description: >-
  Al-Yamama is the old name for the district of central Arabia around Wadi Hanifa and al-Kharj,
  whose oasis towns grew dates and wheat. This regional record covers it after the
  Ukhaydhirids, the Zaydi emirs whom Nasir Khusraw found ruling there in 1051. Clans of the
  Banu Hanifa stayed strong in Hajr, the chief town, and in Manfuha, while families from Tamim
  and other tribes took over many villages. Ibn Battuta met a Hanafi emir of Hajr in the
  fourteenth century. In 1446 the clan of Mani' al-Muraydi founded Diriyah on land granted by
  the ruler of Hajr. The Uyunid, Usfurid and Jabrid rulers of eastern Arabia at times claimed
  overlordship. The record ends when Dahham ibn Dawwas made himself master of Riyadh.
