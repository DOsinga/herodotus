description: >-
  The Maykop culture was an Early Bronze Age archaeological culture north of the Caucasus,
  represented here by estimated dates of 3700 to 3000 BCE. Its name comes from a richly
  furnished burial mound at Maykop in present southern Russia. Settlements and graves reveal
  farming communities with cattle and pigs, skilled metalworking, and connections across the
  Caucasus and neighbouring steppe. Large burial mounds contained gold and silver objects,
  tools, weapons, and striking animal images, showing substantial differences in wealth.
  Some sites preserve early wooden wagon wheels. These remains do not identify a single
  kingdom, known rulers, or a uniform system of government. Maykop overlapped with nearby
  cultures and changed over time, leaving a material legacy important to studying Bronze Age
  exchange.
