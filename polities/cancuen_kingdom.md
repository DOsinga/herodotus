description: >-
  The kingdom of Cancuen was a Late Classic Maya state beside the Pasión River in present
  day Guatemala, flourishing approximately from 650 to 800. Its rulers controlled a
  strategic point connecting river traffic with routes towards the highlands. Jade,
  obsidian, and pyrite working made the city an important commercial centre. King Tajal Chan
  Ahk enlarged a palace whose many rooms and courtyards testify to the scale of the royal
  court. Unlike some Maya cities, Cancuen had relatively few monumental temples, but
  possessed ball courts, a marketplace, and riverside facilities. Its political ties
  included other regional kingdoms, without proving permanent shared government. Around 800,
  King Kan Maax and members of the royal household were executed. The city was abandoned
  shortly afterwards.
