description: >-
  Northern Zhou ruled western and then northern China from 557 to 581, with its court at Chang'an.
  The Yuwen family replaced Western Wei after years of exercising military power behind its
  emperors. Early rulers remained under the control of Yuwen Hu until Emperor Wu killed him in 572
  and governed directly. The state combined a military elite with administrative reforms presented
  as revivals of ancient Zhou institutions. Emperor Wu conquered Northern Qi in 577, bringing most
  of northern China under one government. His successors struggled to hold this achievement. Yang
  Jian gained control as regent after Emperor Xuan's death, defeated opposing commanders and took
  the throne in 581. His accession founded Sui, ending Northern Zhou while retaining its
  territorial base.
