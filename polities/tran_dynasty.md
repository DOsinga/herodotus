description: >-
  The Trần dynasty governed Đại Việt in northern and central Vietnam from 1225 to 1400,
  replacing the Lý rulers through a court arranged succession. Its monarchy centred on
  Thăng Long, with royal relatives holding military and administrative posts. Emperors
  often retired while retaining influence beside their successors. Trần armies resisted
  three Mongol invasions, culminating in the destruction of an invading fleet at Bạch
  Đằng in 1288. The court supported Buddhism, while Confucian learning and examinations
  gained importance. Farming, dike building, and Vietnamese literature also developed
  during this period. Relations with Champa brought both territorial gains and costly
  wars. Succession struggles and weakening royal authority allowed the minister Hồ Quý
  Ly to take the throne in 1400 and establish another dynasty.
