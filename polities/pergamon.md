description: >-
  The Kingdom of Pergamon grew around a fortified city in western Anatolia, in
  modern Turkey, from 282 to 133 BCE. Philetaerus broke with Lysimachus and used
  the royal treasury under his care to establish a lasting local government. His
  successors, the Attalids, turned that foothold into a Hellenistic monarchy.
  Attalus I defeated the Galatians, while Eumenes II gained extensive lands
  through alliance with Rome against the Seleucids. Their capital became known for
  its library, steep theatre, temples, and great altar, making sustained royal
  cultural patronage central to its public identity. Pergamon combined Greek civic
  life with dynastic rule over a varied population. The childless Attalus III
  bequeathed his kingdom to Rome in 133 BCE, though resistance delayed full Roman
  possession.
