description: >-
  Urewe culture identifies early Iron Age communities around Lake Victoria, represented here
  approximately from 550 BCE to 500 CE. Its name derives from a Kenyan archaeological site,
  while related finds extend across Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda, Burundi, and neighbouring regions.
  Distinctive pottery includes shaped vessels, geometric decoration, and dimpled bases. Furnaces
  and slag show developed iron smelting techniques, often found alongside the ceramics. These
  communities probably combined farming, including millet and sorghum cultivation, with
  livestock keeping. Connections with Bantu language expansion help explain their wider setting,
  but archaeological similarities do not establish a united state or named rulers. Local
  variations and continuing settlement show a diverse cultural tradition. Its approximate end
  marks an archaeological period, not the disappearance of its population.
