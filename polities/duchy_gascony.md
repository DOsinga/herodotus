description: >-
  The Duchy of Gascony occupied southwestern France between the Garonne, Atlantic, and Pyrenees.
  This record begins around 769, when Lupo's Vasconian government stood apart from Frankish
  administration, rather than with the earlier creation of a Frankish frontier office. Native
  dukes long maintained power despite Carolingian claims and military campaigns. Their lands
  combined Basque communities with areas where the Romance Gascon language developed. Viking
  attacks damaged Bordeaux and monasteries, while ducal marriages linked Gascony closely with
  neighboring Navarre. Over time, smaller counties and lordships gained authority within the
  duchy, weakening its unity. Rival inheritances and conquest brought the remaining ducal
  government under William VIII of Aquitaine by 1063. Later English and French struggles over
  Gascony belonged to a different political setting.
