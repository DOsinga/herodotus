description: >-
  The Yamnaya culture describes pastoral communities across the Pontic and Caspian steppes
  approximately from 3300 to 2600 BCE. Its name refers to pit graves covered by burial
  mounds, often containing bodies sprinkled with ochre. The Lower Volga marker represents
  only part of a broad archaeological distribution, not a known capital. Herding supported
  mobile or partly mobile households, while wagons helped transport possessions across
  grasslands. Pottery, metal tools, animal offerings, and differences in grave goods reveal
  shared customs and unequal social status. Archaeological and genetic research connects
  Yamnaya populations with major movements into Europe and Central Asia. Many scholars
  associate them with the spread of Indo-European languages. Neither those connections nor
  burial evidence establishes a single government for the entire culture.
