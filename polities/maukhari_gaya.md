description: >-
  The Maukhari Kingdom of Gaya was a local monarchy in Magadha, in present Bihar, known mainly from inscriptions of the fifth and sixth centuries. This record uses approximate dates of 450 to 600 rather than a documented founding or conquest. Its rulers belonged to a separate branch from the better known Maukharis of Kannauj. Barabar represents the cave inscriptions associated with Anantavarman, which also name his father Shardulavarman and grandfather Yajnavarman. These texts describe a ruling family, military prestige, and religious gifts, including installations of Hindu images in older caves. The branch probably held feudatory status within the changing Gupta political world, but its surviving evidence is limited. Its estimated end marks the disappearance of a separately attested court here.
