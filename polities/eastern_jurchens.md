description: >-
  The Eastern Jurchens were the Jurchen tribes of the coast and valleys of northeastern
  Korea, in what is now Hamgyong, after the fall of Balhae. Korean sources call them
  Eastern Jurchens to set them apart from the Jurchens of the Yalu. They lived in villages
  under hereditary chiefs with no common ruler, and neither the Liao nor Goryeo governed
  them. Their chiefs sent tribute to Goryeo and accepted its titles, while their
  raiders struck Goryeo's coast, and Goryeo built a long wall against them in the eleventh
  century. From about 1103 the Wanyan chief Wuyashu brought these tribes under his
  leadership. Goryeo occupied the Hamhung plain with nine fortresses in 1107 but handed them
  back in 1109, and the tribes became part of Aguda's Jin state from 1115.
