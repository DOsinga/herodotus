description: >-
  Buganda developed north of Lake Victoria in present-day Uganda, with its beginnings conventionally placed around 1300. Its kings, known as kabakas, built a powerful monarchy supported by appointed chiefs and established clan networks. Farming, especially banana cultivation, sustained dense settlement, while armies and fleets of canoes helped the kingdom expand at its neighbours' expense. The royal court became a centre of regional politics and later attracted traders, missionaries, and European visitors. Religious rivalries and succession conflicts destabilised the kingdom in the late nineteenth century. British protection in 1894 ended its control of foreign affairs and warfare, although the monarchy survived within colonial Uganda. Abolished after independence, the institution was restored in 1993 with a cultural role rather than sovereign powers.
