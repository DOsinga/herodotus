description: >-
  Galatia occupied the highlands of central Anatolia, around modern Ankara, from
  approximately 270 to 25 BCE. Celtic migrants who crossed into Asia Minor settled
  among older local populations and formed three separate tribal territories. Their
  leaders originally governed through tetrarchies rather than a single royal court.
  Galatian warriors served as mercenaries and fought neighbouring Hellenistic kingdoms,
  while their communities gradually adopted Greek customs without immediately losing
  their Celtic language. Ancyra, Pessinus and Tavium were important urban centres.
  Deiotarus eventually brought much of Galatia together as a kingdom during the first
  century BCE. Roman politics increasingly shaped its succession and alliances. After
  the last king, Amyntas, died in 25 BCE, Augustus annexed the territory, replacing its
  rulers with Roman provincial government.
