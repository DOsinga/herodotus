description: >-
  The Hemudu culture developed south of Hangzhou Bay in present Zhejiang, China,
  approximately between 5500 and 3300 BCE. Archaeologists identify it through settlements,
  burials, tools, and distinctive pottery rather than written accounts of a kingdom. Its
  inhabitants built wooden houses on stilts in a wet landscape and cultivated rice while
  also fishing, hunting, and gathering plants. Bone hoes, wooden implements, black
  pottery, carved ivory, jade ornaments, and early lacquer objects show varied skills.
  Sites such as Hemudu and Tianluoshan preserve evidence of rice domestication and
  changing environments. Flooding and saltwater intrusion affected settlements. Political
  institutions and named rulers remain unknown. The dates are estimates, and the later
  Liangzhu culture represents a subsequent development in this broader coastal region of
  China.
