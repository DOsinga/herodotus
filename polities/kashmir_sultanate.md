description: >-
  The Kashmir Sultanate governed the Himalayan Kashmir Valley from Shah Mir's seizure of power in
  1339 until Mughal conquest in 1586. Srinagar was its main capital, commercial centre, and mint.
  Muslim sultans ruled through ministers and court elites, first under the Shah Mir dynasty and
  later the Chaks, who took power in 1561. Persian increasingly shaped administration and
  literature alongside Sanskrit and Kashmiri. Sufi and local religious traditions influenced a
  society containing Muslim, Hindu, and Buddhist communities. Zain-ul-Abidin encouraged religious
  tolerance; Mughal intervention disrupted rule in 1540–1551. Succession disputes and struggles
  among nobles weakened later governments. Akbar's forces captured Srinagar in 1586; Chak
  resistance continued afterward, but the valley's central government passed into the Mughal
  Empire as a conquered province.
