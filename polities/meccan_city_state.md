description: >-
  The Quraysh government of Mecca was an oligarchy of clans in western Arabia,
  conventionally dated here from about 450 to 630. Tradition credits Qusayy ibn Kilab
  with gathering the clans around the Kaaba and organising responsibilities for its
  sanctuary, pilgrims and military affairs. These offices belonged to prominent families
  rather than a royal bureaucracy. Pilgrimage supported the town, while merchants later
  developed caravan routes towards Yemen and Syria and made alliances with neighbouring
  tribes. Muhammad belonged to the Quraysh, but influential Meccans opposed his
  preaching and fought his community after its migration to Medina. A truce temporarily
  eased the conflict. Muhammad's conquest of Mecca in 630 ended the city's separate
  government, although Quraysh families remained important in subsequent Islamic
  politics.
