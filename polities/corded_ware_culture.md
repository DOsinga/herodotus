description: >-
  The Corded Ware culture describes related archaeological traditions across central,
  northern, and eastern Europe, approximately 2900 to 2350 BCE. Its name comes from
  pottery decorated by pressing cords into wet clay. Individual graves, often beneath
  small mounds, commonly contained vessels and polished stone battle axes. Burial
  positions varied by region and sometimes distinguished men from women. Settlements
  and food remains show farming alongside livestock raising, rather than an exclusively
  wandering pastoral life. Ancient DNA indicates substantial ancestry associated with
  steppe populations, but shared objects do not establish one people, language, or
  government. Regional groups included the Scandinavian Battle Axe and northern
  European Single Grave traditions. Saxony-Anhalt represents this wide distribution,
  whose boundaries and dates remain estimates based on archaeological evidence.
