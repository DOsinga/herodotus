description: >-
  The Ghana Empire, called Wagadu by the Soninke, developed in the western Sahel, across
  parts of modern Mauritania and Mali. Its beginnings are uncertain, conventionally placed
  around 300. Kings drew wealth from taxing trade, especially gold moving north and salt
  moving south across the Sahara. Their authority joined a central royal domain with
  dependent communities and tributary rulers. Medieval Arabic accounts describe a
  prosperous court alongside Muslim merchants and officials, while royal religious
  practices remained distinct. Koumbi Saleh is associated with the empire, although its
  identification and early importance are debated. Decline involved changing long distance
  trade patterns and stronger regional rivals. An alleged Almoravid conquest remains
  disputed; this record ends with the Sosso seizure of Koumbi Saleh around 1203.
