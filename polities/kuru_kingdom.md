description: >-
  The Kuru Kingdom arose around 1200 BCE in northern India, centred on the Kurukshetra
  region and the upper Ganges and Yamuna plains. Its beginnings are associated with the
  joining of Bharata and Puru groups during the Vedic period. Kings and Brahmin priests
  developed rituals that strengthened royal authority and helped shape later Hindu
  traditions. Agriculture and settled communities became increasingly important beside
  older pastoral ways of life. Parikshit and Janamejaya are remembered as prominent
  rulers, although surviving traditions mix historical memory with legend. Kuru lost its
  earlier dominance as neighbouring powers grew, surviving as a lesser Mahajanapada by
  about 500 BCE. The Nandas eventually absorbed it around 350 BCE. Its dynastic legends
  remained central to the later Mahabharata epic tradition.
