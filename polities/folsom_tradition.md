description: >-
  The Folsom tradition is an archaeological culture of mobile hunters on North
  America's Great Plains and adjoining regions. Its dates here, approximately 10800 to
  10200 BCE, use calibrated radiocarbon estimates rather than older uncalibrated
  chronologies. Distinctive stone spear points have long grooves, or flutes, carefully
  removed from their surfaces. Finds of these points with extinct bison bones
  demonstrated that people occupied the Americas far earlier than some scholars once
  accepted. George McJunkin discovered the New Mexico site that gave the tradition its
  name. Camps, tools and communal kill sites suggest small groups sometimes gathered
  for coordinated hunts. The evidence does not reveal a state, named rulers or a single
  government. Changing technologies eventually replaced Folsom across its wide
  geographical range.
