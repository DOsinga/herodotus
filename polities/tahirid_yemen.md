description: >-
  The Tahirid Sultanate ruled much of southern Yemen and the Tihama coastal plain from
  1454 to 1517. A local family that had served the Rasulid sultans seized Aden during
  their predecessors' collapse. Its Sunni Muslim rulers used Zabid as a winter capital
  and maintained residences in the southern highlands. They sponsored schools, mosques,
  bridges, waterworks, and the notable Amiriyya religious buildings. Their authority
  faced tribal uprisings and resistance from Zaydi imams farther north, although Amir
  II captured Sanaa in 1504. Portuguese attacks threatened the maritime trade
  supporting Aden. Disagreement over supplies for an Egyptian expedition turned the
  Mamluks against the dynasty. Mamluk forces and Yemeni allies defeated Amir II, ending
  the main sultanate in 1517 despite surviving local Tahirid resistance.
