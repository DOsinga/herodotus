description: >-
  The Emirate of Córdoba ruled much of al Andalus in the Iberian Peninsula from 756 to 929. Abd al
  Rahman I, an Umayyad prince who escaped the Abbasid overthrow of his family in the east,
  established an hereditary monarchy at Córdoba. The emirs governed without submitting to Baghdad,
  although they did not initially claim the caliphal title. Their authority depended on balancing
  Arab, Berber and locally rooted groups, while managing provincial revolts and wars with northern
  Christian kingdoms. Córdoba developed as a political and cultural centre, and its Great Mosque
  became a lasting expression of Umayyad patronage. Abd al Rahman III strengthened central control
  and declared himself caliph in 929, continuing the state under the separately recorded Caliphate
  of Córdoba.
