description: >-
  Hippostratus ruled an Indo-Greek kingdom in the western Punjab during the first century BCE, approximately 65 to 55 BCE in this record. His government is reconstructed chiefly from coins, leaving its borders, capital arrangements, and dynastic relationships uncertain. He followed or partly overlapped Apollodotus II while another Greek ruler governed farther east. Silver issues portray the king wearing a diadem, with reverses showing mounted rulers or a standing goddess; bronze coins use several divine images. Titles meaning Saviour and Great King express his royal claims. The quantity and quality of these issues suggest resources, but cannot supply a detailed political history. He resisted Indo-Scythian expansion before losing to Azes I, whose overstrikes on his coins provide evidence for the takeover.
