description: >-
  The Neo-Babylonian Empire arose in southern Mesopotamia when Nabopolassar rebelled against Assyria in 626 BCE.
  With Babylon as its capital, the monarchy joined the Medes in destroying Assyrian power and
  then extended its authority across much of the former empire's western lands. Nebuchadnezzar II defeated
  Egyptian forces, conquered Jerusalem and deported people from Judah, while rebuilding Babylon with walls, temples
  and ceremonial avenues. Agriculture, temple estates and trade supported an urban society whose records preserve
  extensive administrative and commercial dealings. Later rulers faced succession struggles, and Nabonidus pursued religious policies
  that generated controversy. In 539 BCE, Cyrus of Persia took Babylon and incorporated the kingdom
  into the Achaemenid Empire. Babylon remained an important city after its independent royal government ended.
