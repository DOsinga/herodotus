description: >-
  Wadi al-Qura, the 'valley of villages', was a chain of farming settlements north of Medina on
  the road between the Hejaz and Syria, centred first on Qurh and later on al-Ula. Before Islam
  its villages were held by Jewish and Arab clans. Muhammad's forces raided the valley several
  times and took it in 628, after which it belonged to the caliphate. A Jewish community there
  still wrote to the geonim of Iraq in the eleventh century. Once Abbasid control lapsed, the
  oasis families and the Bedouin around them held the valley themselves. In the thirteenth
  century the walled town of al-Ula replaced Qurh, and the oasis stayed in local hands until
  the Ottoman Hejaz Railway reached it in 1908.
