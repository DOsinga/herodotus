description: >-
  The Ghurid Sultanate grew from the mountain region of Ghur in present Afghanistan during the
  twelfth and early thirteenth centuries. Its hereditary rulers adopted Islam and sponsored
  Persian literature, architecture and court culture. Initially surrounded by stronger Ghaznavid
  and Seljuk powers, they expanded after regional conflicts weakened their neighbours. Ghiyath al-
  Din Muhammad governed the western domains while his brother Muhammad of Ghor led conquests into
  northern India, where commanders established enduring Muslim governments. The minaret at Jam
  survives as an monument of their patronage. This record represents their Afghan centred state
  from about 950 to 1213. After Muhammad's assassination in 1206, succession disputes and pressure
  from Khwarazm fractured royal power. Khwarazmian conquest ended the remaining Ghurid governments
  in the following years.
