description: >-
  The Hopewell tradition linked many communities in eastern North America between approximately 100 BCE and 500 CE, especially in the Ohio and Illinois valleys. It was not a single people or state. Archaeologists identify shared earthwork construction, burial practices and exchange networks among otherwise distinct local societies. Mound City in Ohio preserves one ceremonial complex. Communities combined cultivation with hunting, fishing and gathering, usually living in small settlements rather than cities. Copper, mica, shells and obsidian travelled long distances and became crafted objects placed in burials. Monumental enclosures and mounds required organised labour, but evidence does not establish a central government over the network. These ceremonial and exchange patterns diminished after about 500, while local populations continued developing other traditions.
