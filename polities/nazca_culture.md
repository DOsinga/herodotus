description: >-
  Nazca culture developed on Peru's dry southern coast, building on earlier Paracas
  traditions. This record covers approximately 100 BCE to 400 CE, emphasizing the period
  associated with Cahuachi before later regional government is represented separately.
  Archaeologists know Nazca communities through painted pottery, textiles, burials,
  settlement remains, and the ground drawings called geoglyphs. Cahuachi was a ceremonial
  centre of mounds and plazas, where gatherings and offerings linked communities. Farming
  in river valleys supported maize, beans, squash, and crops despite the desert setting.
  Evidence suggests local chiefdoms and religious leadership rather than a single kingdom.
  Named rulers and precise political boundaries are unknown. Cultural traditions continued
  after Cahuachi's importance declined, so the record's endpoint is not the disappearance
  of every Nazca community.
