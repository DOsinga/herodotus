description: >-
  The Early Kingdom of Nepal represents royal government in the Kathmandu Valley from
  approximately 400 to 1482, not the territory of modern Nepal. Licchavi rule is
  securely documented by Manadeva's inscription of 464, while earlier beginnings remain
  uncertain. Kings, court officials, and powerful families governed a valley linked
  through trade and pilgrimage with India and Tibet. Hindu and Buddhist traditions
  developed together, supported by temples, monasteries, and skilled artisans. The
  poorly documented centuries after the Licchavis are followed here by the Malla rulers
  rather than treated as separate states. Later Malla kings promoted social reforms and
  distinctive urban architecture. After Yaksha Malla's death in 1482, the valley
  divided into rival kingdoms, ending the shared political identity represented in this
  record.
