description: >-
  Djenné-Djenno was an early town in the Inland Niger Delta of present-day Mali. Settlement
  began around 250 BCE, but this record follows its urban development from approximately 300
  CE until abandonment around 1400. Archaeological finds show rice cultivation, fishing,
  livestock keeping, ironworking, and trade across the surrounding floodplain and beyond.
  The town expanded alongside neighbouring settlements with specialised activities,
  challenging older assumptions that West African urban life depended on outside founders.
  Pottery, imported materials, and terracotta figures reveal a local society, but they do
  not identify its rulers or establish a known form of government. It is therefore
  represented as an archaeological culture rather than a documented state. Its inhabitants
  eventually moved toward the later city of Djenné, close by.
