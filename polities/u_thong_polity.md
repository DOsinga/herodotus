description: >-
  U Thong was an early urban centre in the western Chao Phraya basin of present Thailand, near
  modern Suphan Buri. Its separate political existence is estimated from around 300 to 1000,
  reflecting archaeological growth and decline rather than a known royal chronology.
  Settlement long preceded the Dvaravati period, while a large moat, brick structures, and
  religious remains show increasing organisation. Finds include a Roman coin, suggesting
  connections reaching far beyond the town. A seventh-century copper inscription names
  Harshavarman and royal succession, although the city's native political identity remains
  uncertain. Buddhist monuments and evidence of other religious practices place it within the
  wider exchange networks of early mainland Southeast Asia. Its decline cannot securely be
  tied to Ayutthaya's much later founder.
