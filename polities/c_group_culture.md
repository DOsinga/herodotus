description: >-
  C-Group culture is an archaeological tradition of Lower Nubia, in southern Egypt and northern Sudan, dated here approximately from 2400 to 1500 BCE. Its modern name was assigned by archaeologist George Reisner, not preserved from the people themselves. Cemeteries, modest settlements, and distinctive decorated pottery provide most of the evidence. Cattle had economic and symbolic importance, alongside farming and other activities. Round graves surrounded by stone circles later developed more elaborate chambers and offering spaces. These remains do not identify a single kingdom or a known succession of rulers. Communities interacted with Egypt and Kerma while keeping recognisable material traditions. Egyptian forts occupied Lower Nubia during much of the Middle Kingdom, and later Egyptian expansion changed the region's cultural landscape.
