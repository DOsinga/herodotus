description: >-
  The kingdom of Emesa was a small Arab kingdom centred on Emesa, modern Homs, in the Orontes
  valley of western Syria. Its kings, the Emesene dynasty, descended from Sampsiceramus I, a
  tribal chief whom Pompey confirmed as ruler when Rome took Syria in 64 BCE. They were also
  priests of the sun god Elagabal, whose black stone was worshipped in the city's temple. As Roman
  client kings they sent troops to Roman wars and married into other client dynasties, while
  governing their own territory. Their rule was interrupted under Augustus and later restored. The
  kingdom was annexed into the Roman province of Syria in the reign of Vespasian, but the priestly
  family later gave Rome the empresses of the Severan dynasty.
