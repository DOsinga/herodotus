description: >-
  The Duchy of Apulia and Calabria grew from Norman conquests in southern Italy. It began
  as a county in 1043 under William of Hauteville, based at Melfi, and became a duchy
  under Robert Guiscard in 1059. Its rulers governed through a network of barons while
  claiming legitimacy from papal recognition. Expansion displaced Byzantine power and
  brought Lombard territories under Norman control, creating a realm with Latin and Greek
  religious traditions. Salerno became the principal seat after its conquest in 1076 to
  1077. Guiscard's successors faced local lords and disputes within the Hauteville family.
  Roger II of Sicily inherited the duchy in 1127. In 1130 its lands joined his new Kingdom
  of Sicily, ending its history as a separate ducal government.
