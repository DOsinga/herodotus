description: >-
  The A-Group culture developed along the Nile in Lower Nubia, between the First and Second
  Cataracts, approximately 3800 to 2800 BCE. Its communities combined herding with fishing, hunting, gathering, and
  eventually farming. Archaeologists identify them chiefly through cemeteries, distinctive pottery, and objects exchanged with Egypt.
  Fine black-topped vessels and exceptionally rich graves at Qustul show skilled craftspeople and differences in
  wealth. These burials have prompted debate about powerful local leaders, but they do not establish
  one government ruling the whole region. Nubian traders supplied materials such as ivory, ebony, and
  gold while receiving Egyptian goods. The culture's decline overlapped with expanding Egyptian activity, though its
  causes and precise timing remain uncertain. Much surviving evidence now lies beneath Lake Nasser's waters.
