description: >-
  Salzburg was an Alpine territory governed by prince-archbishops, centered on the
  city of Salzburg and the Salzach valley. This record follows its separate
  government from 1250 through its secular successor's annexation in 1805. The
  archbishops combined spiritual office with authority over towns, estates and
  mountain districts within the Holy Roman Empire. Salt production and trade
  supported their wealth, while building programs gave Salzburg its baroque
  appearance. Religious policy included the expulsion of thousands of Protestants in
  1731. The court also supported music, during Mozart's lifetime, although relations
  with patrons could be difficult. Secularization in 1803 removed the archbishop's
  temporal power and granted the territory to Ferdinand, the former grand duke of
  Tuscany. Austria absorbed his Electorate of Salzburg in 1805.
