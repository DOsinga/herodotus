description: >-
  The Uruk period describes an archaeological culture of Mesopotamia, approximately 4000 to 3100
  BCE, rather than a single documented kingdom. Named after Uruk in southern Iraq, it followed
  the Ubaid period and saw settlements grow into large cities. Monumental buildings, specialized
  workshops, irrigation, and standardized pottery reveal increasingly complex economic
  organization. Cylinder seals and early clay tablets helped track goods and labour; the
  earliest writing developed chiefly for administration. Uruk's institutions clearly commanded
  substantial resources, but their precise government remains unknown. Related objects and
  buildings spread into Syria, Anatolia, and Iran through networks whose balance of trade,
  migration, and local imitation is debated. Its later developments lead into the Jemdet Nasr
  period and subsequent Mesopotamian city states in the region.
