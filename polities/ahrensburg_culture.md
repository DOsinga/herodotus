description: >-
  The Ahrensburg culture describes mobile hunting communities on northern Europe's plains near
  the end of the Ice Age, approximately 10900 to 9700 BCE. Its name comes from sites near
  Ahrensburg, northeast of Hamburg, where excavations recovered distinctive flint points and
  animal remains. These communities lived during the cold Younger Dryas and the transition toward
  warmer conditions. Reindeer hunting was especially important: Stellmoor preserved numerous
  bones and wooden arrow shafts, giving unusually clear evidence for bows and arrows. Finds
  extend into Scandinavia, Poland, and lands now submerged beneath the North and Baltic seas.
  Camps and stone arrangements suggest temporary shelters, not urban government. Archaeological
  similarities identify a material tradition rather than a known state. Later hunting cultures
  followed as environments changed.
