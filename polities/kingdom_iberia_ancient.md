description: >-
  The Kingdom of Iberia occupied eastern Georgia in the Caucasus, where it was also known
  as Kartli. Tradition dates its monarchy to Pharnavaz around 302 BCE, although the
  earliest royal chronology remains uncertain. Mtskheta was a royal and religious centre.
  Iberian kings balanced relations with neighbouring Armenia and the competing Roman and
  Iranian empires, sometimes accepting their protection or tribute demands while retaining
  local government. Christianity became a royal religion under Mirian III in the fourth
  century, strongly shaping the country's later identity. Sasanian pressure grew during
  subsequent centuries, despite resistance by rulers such as Vakhtang Gorgasali. In 580
  the Sasanians abolished the kingship and imposed a marzpan. A later presiding
  principality continued Georgian political life under a different form.
