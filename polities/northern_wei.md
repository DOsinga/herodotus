description: >-
  Northern Wei was an imperial state in northern China from 386 to 535, founded by the Tuoba branch of the Xianbei. Its emperors defeated rival kingdoms and unified the north in 439. The court first governed from northern capitals before moving to Luoyang in 493. Land allocation and household administration strengthened imperial control, while Emperor Xiaowen encouraged Chinese court customs and adopted the surname Yuan. Buddhist patronage produced major cave temples at Yungang and Longmen. Tensions between frontier soldiers and the Chinese court helped fuel rebellions and military domination of politics. An eastern court separated in 534; the remaining western court moved to Chang'an. Xiaowu's death and the establishment of Western Wei in 535 ended Northern Wei's final independent court.
