description: >-
  Polada culture describes Bronze Age communities in northern Italy, especially around Lake
  Garda and nearby lakes in Lombardy, Veneto and Trentino. Its approximate span here is 2200
  to 1600 BCE. The name comes from finds recovered from the Polada peat bog during
  nineteenth-century drainage work. Settlements often used timber platforms and pile
  dwellings beside water, where wet conditions preserved objects rarely surviving elsewhere.
  Pottery, stone tools, bronze weapons and ornaments show a changing balance between older
  crafts and metalworking. Farming, livestock keeping, hunting and fishing supported daily
  life, while artifacts indicate contacts across the Alpine region. These shared material
  traditions do not identify a single state, capital or known ruling family. Later Bronze
  Age settlement traditions developed from this background.
