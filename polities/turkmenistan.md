description: >-
  Turkmenistan became independent in 1991 as the Soviet Union dissolved, establishing a
  presidential republic in Central Asia. Its territory stretches across the Karakum Desert
  between the Caspian Sea, Iran, Afghanistan, Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan, with Ashgabat as
  capital. Saparmurat Niyazov carried his authority from the Soviet period into independence,
  concentrating power and building an extensive personal cult. His successors maintained
  tightly controlled government, restrictions on opposition and very limited press freedom.
  The country adopted permanent neutrality as a central principle of foreign policy. Large
  natural gas reserves underpin its economy and relations with foreign customers, alongside
  cotton farming and state directed development. Changes of leaders have not created a
  separate state: the republic continues within the borders inherited from Soviet
  Turkmenistan.
