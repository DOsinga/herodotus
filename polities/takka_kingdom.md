description: >-
  The Takka Kingdom was a Hindu state in the central Punjab, with its capital at Sakala, the
  ancient city under modern Sialkot, recorded here from the decline of Alchon power around 570
  until the Hindu Shahis took over northern Punjab around 965. The Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang, who
  visited in the 630s, described it as stretching from the Indus to the Beas, with Multan and a
  hill state near Sialkot among its dependencies. The Korean monk Hyecho passed through in the
  early eighth century. Arab writers knew it as al-Taqa and described a ruler on good terms with
  his neighbours. Later its Gurjara princes faced Kashmir, whose king Shankaravarman defeated
  their ruler Alakhana around 890. Little is known of its kings, and both of its dates are
  estimates.
