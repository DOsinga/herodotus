description: >-
  The Funj Sultanate ruled from Sennar on the Blue Nile between 1504 and 1821, covering much of present Sudan and adjoining regions. Amara Dunqas established its monarchy after the decline of the medieval Nubian kingdoms. Funj sultans governed a diverse population through subordinate chiefs and rulers, with authority varying across their lands. The court adopted Islam, while Sufi teachers, merchants and migration encouraged its wider spread and the growing use of Arabic. Agriculture, livestock, gold and caravan trade sustained the kingdom. It resisted Ottoman expansion and reached its greatest influence in the seventeenth century. Hamaj commanders took effective power in 1762, leaving sultans dependent on regents. Civil conflict weakened the state before Muhammad Ali's Egyptian forces conquered Sennar in 1821.
