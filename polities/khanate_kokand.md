name: Khanate of Kokand
description: >-
  The Khanate of Kokand emerged in the Fergana Valley in 1709 as Bukhara's authority weakened. Shahrukh Biy, a leader of the Uzbek Ming tribe, established a fortified centre near Kokand, and his descendants expanded a hereditary Muslim monarchy. Rulers initially used the title biy before adopting khan. Irrigated agriculture, urban markets, and regional trade supported a realm that eventually included Tashkent and territories inhabited by Kyrgyz and Kazakh communities. Expansion brought repeated conflicts with Bukhara, Qing authorities, and local rivals. Court patronage fostered literature and architecture, but succession struggles and resistance to taxation weakened government. Russian conquest stripped away northern possessions during the nineteenth century. Following rebellion and military intervention, Russia abolished the khanate in 1876 and directly annexed Fergana.
