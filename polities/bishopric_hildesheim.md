description: >-
  The Prince-Bishopric of Hildesheim was an ecclesiastical principality in Lower Saxony, between
  the Leine and the Oker south of Hanover. The see was founded by Louis the Pious in 815, and its
  bishops built up a secular territory that they ruled as imperial princes from the thirteenth
  century. The cathedral chapter elected the bishops, and the city of Hildesheim, a Hanseatic
  member, kept wide rights of its own. In the Hildesheim Diocesan Feud of 1519 to 1523 the
  neighbouring Guelph dukes seized most of the bishopric, leaving only a small remnant around the
  city, and the lost lands were returned only in 1643. The prince-bishopric stayed Catholic while
  much of its population became Lutheran. Prussia took it over when the church states were
  secularised in 1802.
