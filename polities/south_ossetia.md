  South Ossetia is a partly recognized republic in the South Caucasus, with its
  capital at Tskhinvali. It emerged as a separately governing territory during
  conflict with Georgia, declaring independence in 1992 after the dissolution of the
  Soviet Union. Its institutions include a president, government and parliament, but
  most countries regard its territory as part of Georgia. Ossetian and Russian are
  official languages. A ceasefire initially left control divided between local
  authorities and Georgian forces. War in 2008 brought Russian military
  intervention, recognition by Russia and the removal of Georgian control from the
  contested areas. South Ossetia depends heavily on Russian security and financial
  support. Its mountain location and the Roki Tunnel connect it with North Ossetia
  across the Caucasus range.
