description: >-
  The Adal Sultanate was a Muslim state in the Horn of Africa, recorded here from 1415 to 1577. It
  connected the eastern Ethiopian interior with ports such as Zeila and later centred its court at
  Harar. Walashma sultans held hereditary titles, but military emirs increasingly exercised the
  real power. Its population included several communities linked by farming, pastoralism and trade
  across the Red Sea. Conflict with Christian Ethiopia shaped its history, especially Imam Ahmad
  ibn Ibrahim al Ghazi's sixteenth century campaigns. Ottoman weapons and assistance supported his
  armies, while Portuguese soldiers aided Ethiopia. Ahmad's death in 1543 checked Adal's
  expansion. Warfare, internal struggles and Oromo advances weakened the state, whose later
  political successors included governments at Harar and in Aussa.
