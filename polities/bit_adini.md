description: >-
  Bit Adini was an Aramaean kingdom on the middle Euphrates in northern Syria, with its
  royal capital at Til Barsip, modern Tell Ahmar. Its beginning around 1000 BCE is an
  estimate; it is clearly recorded during the tenth and ninth centuries BCE. The kingdom
  controlled river crossings and lands extending toward the Balikh and westward into Syria.
  Its cultural setting combined Aramaean communities with older regional traditions,
  including inscriptions in Hittite hieroglyphs. Assyrian accounts describe tribute,
  diplomatic exchanges, and resistance to imperial campaigns. The best known ruler, Ahuni,
  defended his territory against repeated attacks by Shalmaneser III. Assyria eventually
  captured the capital and annexed the kingdom in 856 BCE, turning a former rival into part
  of its expanding provincial system.
