description: >-
  Âu Lạc was an early kingdom associated with northern Vietnam and adjoining parts of southern
  China. Traditional chronology places it between 257 and 179 BCE under An Dương Vương, also
  called Thục Phán. Accounts describe the joining of Âu Việt and Lạc Việt communities after
  the overthrow of Văn Lang's rulers. Its centre was Cổ Loa, near modern Hanoi, where
  extensive earthworks reveal the ability to organise labour and defend a settlement. Bronze weapons and
  archaeological remains complement stories preserved much later, but cannot confirm every royal narrative or date.
  Legends connect its fall with a magical crossbow and betrayal within the king's family. The
  kingdom succumbed to Zhao Tuo's Nanyue, while local leaders continued under the conquerors' wider authority.
