description: >-
  The Princely Abbey of Fulda was the territory of the Benedictine abbey of Fulda in eastern
  Hesse, founded in 744 by Sturm, a disciple of Saint Boniface, whose tomb made it one of the
  great monasteries of the Frankish world. Its abbots ranked as princes of the Empire from 1220
  and ruled a compact territory between the Vogelsberg and the Rhön, governed from the town of
  Fulda with the help of a chapter drawn from the nobility. The abbey lost ground to its knights
  and neighbours in the late Middle Ages but recovered under the Counter-Reformation. In 1752 the
  abbots became prince-bishops. Fulda was secularised in 1802 and given to William Frederick of
  Orange-Nassau as compensation for his lost Dutch lands.
