description: >-
  The Peiligang culture describes early farming communities in Henan, China, dating
  approximately from 7000 to 5000 BCE. Named after a site near Xinzheng discovered in 1977, it
  is known through settlements, burials, pottery, and stone tools rather than written accounts
  of rulers. People cultivated millet, raised pigs, hunted deer and wild boar, and caught fish
  from nearby rivers. Grinding equipment and sickles show how grain was processed and
  harvested. Villages often separated living spaces from burial grounds, while pottery served
  cooking and storage needs. Archaeologists generally see limited social hierarchy, not
  evidence of a unified state. Jiahu shares many features, including striking early finds, but
  its relationship to the main group remains debated. Later Yangshao traditions followed in
  the region.
