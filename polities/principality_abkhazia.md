description: >-
  The Principality of Abkhazia was a feudal state on the eastern Black Sea coast ruled by the
  Shervashidze princes, who had governed Abkhazia as dukes of the Georgian kingdom since the
  twelfth century. It emerged as a separate principality after the rebellion against King George
  VIII in 1463, when the victorious lords divided western Georgia among themselves. The Abkhaz
  princes were nominally vassals of the Dadiani of Mingrelia, but fought them over the border and
  governed their coastal and mountain lands themselves. From the 1570s the Ottomans held the
  fortress of Sukhumi, and many of the princes and their subjects turned to Islam, while the
  princes ruled from their palace at Lykhny. In 1810 Prince Giorgi Shervashidze placed the
  principality under Russian protection, and Russia abolished it in 1864.
