description: >-
  The County of Tripoli was a crusader state along the eastern Mediterranean coast,
  covering northern Lebanon and parts of western Syria. Its capital fell to Frankish
  forces in 1109 after a siege begun by Raymond of Toulouse. Bertrand, Raymond's son,
  then ruled as count. A feudal monarchy, the county combined noble lordships,
  fortified towns, and estates held by military orders. Italian trading communities
  gained privileges in its ports, while local Christian and Muslim populations
  continued living under Latin rulers. Its counts became closely connected with the
  princes of Antioch, sometimes sharing a ruler, and negotiated shifting alliances with
  neighbouring powers. Internal disputes weakened its final years. Sultan Qalawun's
  Mamluk army captured Tripoli in 1289, ending the county's government and
  independence.
