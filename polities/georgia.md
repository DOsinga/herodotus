  Georgia is a republic in the South Caucasus, between the Black Sea and the mountains bordering Russia. The state restored independence in 1991 as the Soviet Union dissolved, with Tbilisi as its capital. Its early years brought a coup, civil war and conflicts in Abkhazia and South Ossetia that displaced many people. The Rose Revolution of 2003 replaced the government and prompted institutional and economic reforms. War with Russia in 2008 further entrenched the separation of the two disputed regions from Georgian control. Constitutional changes shifted executive power towards a prime minister responsible to parliament. Georgia's foreign relations have centred on security, trade and closer ties with European institutions. Its earlier kingdoms and brief twentieth-century republic remain distinct historical predecessors.
