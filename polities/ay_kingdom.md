description: >-
  The Ay kingdom occupied the southwestern tip of India, including southern Kerala,
  Nanjinad, and parts of the Western Ghats. Its earliest chiefs appear in Sangam poetry,
  making the starting date around 300 BCE an estimate rather than a documented foundation.
  Early rule was a chieftaincy; later rulers maintained a royal court associated with the
  port of Vizhinjam. Elephants, hill routes, fertile lowlands, and coastal trade helped
  define their territory and resources. The Ays navigated pressure from the Cheras and
  Pandyas, sometimes acknowledging powerful neighbours while retaining their own
  government. Inscriptions provide firmer evidence than the early literary traditions.
  Around 920, after the period of Vikramaditya Varaguna, incorporation of the principal
  territory ended this kingdom; later Ay branches were separate histories.
