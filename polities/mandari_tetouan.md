description: >-
  Mandari Tétouan was the city-state of Tétouan in northern Morocco, governed by the Mandari
  family of Granadan refugees and recorded here from 1484 to 1554. Castile had destroyed
  Tétouan in 1399, and the site stayed largely deserted until Ali al-Mandari, a commander from
  Granada, rebuilt and fortified it in the 1480s with Andalusian settlers. The town became a
  base for corsairs raiding the Spanish and Portuguese coasts and a refuge for Muslims leaving
  Spain. Sayyida al-Hurra, the widow of a later Mandari governor, ruled it from 1515 to 1542
  and negotiated with Spain on her own account. The Mandari recognised the Wattasid sultans of
  Fez without giving up control, and the Saadis took over in 1554.
