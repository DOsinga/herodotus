description: >-
  Maingmaw was a large walled Pyu centre on the Kyaukse plains of present
  Myanmar. This record estimates its political life from 100 to 1050, rather
  than assigning precise dates to an uncertain royal sequence. Also associated
  with the modern village of Pinle, the city had a circular outer plan and two
  inner enclosures, one square and one round. These works suggest organised
  authority and the ability to direct labour, but its rulers and institutions
  are poorly known. The wider Pyu world shared Buddhist traditions, farming, and
  commercial connections without necessarily forming one united state. A canal
  crosses the site, although its age is unconfirmed. Maingmaw's incorporation
  into Pagan during eleventh century expansion provides the estimated endpoint
  used for this record.
