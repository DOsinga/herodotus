description: >-
  Xiliangfu, also called the Liugu or Six Valleys Tibetans, was a Tibetan tribal
  confederation that ruled Liangzhou, the modern Wuwei, at the eastern end of the Hexi
  Corridor in Gansu. Liangzhou slipped from the control of the Guiyi Circuit of Dunhuang
  around 881, and by 906 the local Tibetan tribes governed the city and its valleys
  themselves. Their chiefs sold horses to the Five Dynasties and Song courts and accepted
  Chinese titles, but no Chinese official ruled the city. From 996 the Tanguts raided
  Liangzhou, and in 1003 Li Jiqian took it but was killed in a Tibetan ambush. Under
  Panluozhi and his brother Siduodu the confederation held out until the Tanguts captured
  Liangzhou in 1015. A year later the Ganzhou Uyghurs drove them out.
