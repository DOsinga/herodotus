description: >-
  The Third Imamate of Oman was an Ibadi state in the interior of Oman, formed in 897 after
  the Abbasid army had conquered the country in 893 and ended the second imamate. As before,
  the imams were chosen by the Ibadi scholars and tribal leaders and confirmed by public
  oath, rather than inheriting the office, and they ruled from inland towns such as Nizwa
  through local governors. Their power depended on the support of the tribes, and quarrels
  between tribal factions over the choice and removal of imams weakened the state. The
  imamate faded in the 940s. Qarmatian raids from eastern Arabia struck Oman between 931 and
  934, and in 967 the Buyids conquered the country.
