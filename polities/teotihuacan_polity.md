description: >-
  Teotihuacan was a major urban state in the Basin of Mexico, represented here from about 100
  BCE to 650 CE. Its planned streets, pyramids, and dense apartment compounds supported one of
  the ancient Americas' largest cities. Residents included communities from different regions,
  connected through trade, craft production, and religious life. Obsidian working and exchange
  helped carry its influence across Mesoamerica, while monuments in the Maya region record
  interventions associated with Teotihuacan. Its government remains disputed: clear royal
  portraits and dynastic inscriptions are absent, and some scholars argue for collective
  leadership. Destruction concentrated on elite buildings during the sixth century suggests
  severe internal conflict. Governing institutions are estimated to have ended by 650,
  although settlement continued after the great city's decline.
