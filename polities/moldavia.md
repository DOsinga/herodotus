description: >-
  Moldavia was a principality between the eastern Carpathians and the Dniester, emerging in
  the fourteenth century and uniting with Wallachia in 1859. Its rulers, called voivodes or
  hospodars, governed with powerful boyars and supported the Orthodox church. Suceava was an
  important early capital before the court moved to Iași. Princes balanced relations with
  Hungary, Poland, and the Ottoman Empire, sometimes fighting neighbours and sometimes
  accepting tribute obligations. Stephen the Great became known for military resistance and
  religious foundations. This record excludes the Phanariot period after 1711, when Ottoman
  appointees controlled the principality. Native princes returned in 1822 amid growing
  Russian influence and reform. Alexandru Ioan Cuza's election in both Moldavia and
  Wallachia created the United Principalities, laying foundations for Romania.
