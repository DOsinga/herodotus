  Singapore is an island republic at the southern end of the Malay Peninsula,
  independent since its separation from Malaysia in 1965. The city itself serves as the
  capital and dominates the small state's territory. Its parliamentary system places
  executive government under a prime minister, with a president as head of state. The
  People's Action Party, led initially by Lee Kuan Yew, shaped industrialisation, public
  housing, education, and a strong administrative state. Singapore developed from a
  British trading port into an important shipping, manufacturing, and financial centre.
  Its Chinese, Malay, Indian, and other communities are reflected in four official
  languages. Economic success has accompanied strict political controls and limited
  opposition influence. The republic remains independent despite close commercial ties
  with neighbours.
