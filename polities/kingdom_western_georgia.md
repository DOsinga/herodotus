description: >-
  The Kingdom of Western Georgia governed lands west of the Likhi Range from a seat at
  Kutaisi. David VI Narin established it in 1259 after rebelling against Mongol
  domination and escaping into the west. Its Bagrationi kings claimed Georgian royal
  legitimacy, but powerful nobles in Mingrelia, Guria, Abkhazia, and other districts
  limited their authority. Black Sea ports connected the kingdom with Genoese merchants
  and Trebizond. Rivalry within the royal family weakened it, allowing George V to
  reunite Georgia in 1330. Western kings reappeared during Timur's invasions, ruling
  separately in 1387–1392 and 1396–1401. Both restorations ended with renewed control
  from eastern Georgia. This earlier kingdom is distinct from the later Kingdom of
  Imereti, which emerged from another round of Georgian fragmentation.
