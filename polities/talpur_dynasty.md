description: >-
  The Talpur dynasty ruled Sindh, in present day Pakistan, from 1783 until the British conquest of
  1843. The Talpurs were Baloch chiefs who had served the Kalhoras, and Mir Fateh Ali Khan Talpur
  took power after defeating the last Kalhora ruler at Halani in 1783. The family shared out the
  country between branches, the Mirs of Hyderabad, Khairpur and Mirpur Khas, who governed their own
  shares but acted together. They paid tribute to the Durrani Afghans for much of their rule.
  Treaties with the East India Company in the 1830s opened the Indus to British shipping and brought
  troops into Sindh during the First Anglo-Afghan War. In 1843 Charles Napier defeated the Mirs at
  Miani and Dubba and annexed Sindh, leaving only Khairpur as a princely state.
