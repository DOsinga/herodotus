description: >-
  Mycenaean Knossos was the Greek-speaking palace government established on northern
  Crete after disruptions around 1450 BCE. It occupied the complex that had previously
  served Minoan rulers, combining inherited buildings and traditions with a new
  administrative language. Clay tablets written in Linear B record land, workers,
  livestock, textiles and religious offerings, showing officials overseeing substantial
  resources across the island. These documents provide unusually direct evidence for
  Bronze Age administration, although they do not give a continuous narrative of royal
  politics. The period is represented here as ending around 1375 BCE with destruction of
  the palace. That date remains debated, and some reconstructions place the final
  destruction much later. Settlement at Knossos continued beyond the disappearance of
  this particular palace-based governing system.
