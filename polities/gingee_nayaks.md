description: >-
  The Nayaks of Gingee governed northern Tamil country from a strong hill fortress in southeastern
  India. Established as Vijayanagara governors in the early sixteenth century, they gained increasing independence after
  the imperial defeat at Talikota in 1565. This record follows that separate government until 1649.
  Telugu speaking military and ruling families operated within a largely Tamil region, competing with neighboring
  Nayak courts and the surviving Vijayanagara rulers. Gingee's fortified hills, walls, granaries, and palaces made
  it a military center. Coastal commerce connected its lands with European traders and wider Indian
  Ocean networks. Relations with Tanjavur included warfare and negotiated alliances. The growing power of the
  Deccan sultanates ultimately reached Gingee: Bijapur conquered the fortress in 1649, ending the Nayak state.
