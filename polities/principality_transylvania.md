description: >-
  The Principality of Transylvania governed the eastern Carpathian basin and
  neighboring Partium between 1570 and 1711. It emerged from the eastern Hungarian
  kingdom when John Sigismund exchanged his royal claim for a princely title. Princes
  were elected through a diet dominated by Hungarian nobles, Szekely representatives,
  and Saxon communities. Romanian inhabitants lacked equivalent corporate political
  standing. Usually acknowledging Ottoman suzerainty, the principality nevertheless
  pursued its own alliances and fought to preserve room between Ottoman and Habsburg
  ambitions. Its courts, notably at Gyulafehervar, supported Protestant learning, while
  several Christian confessions received legal recognition. Gabriel Bethlen made it an
  important participant in European conflicts. Habsburg expansion weakened its
  autonomy, and the failure of Rakoczi's rebellion in 1711 ended its separately
  governed existence.
