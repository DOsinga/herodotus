description: >-
  The Badarian culture flourished in Upper Egypt around 4400 to 4000 BCE, before the emergence of the pharaonic state. Named after El-Badari on the eastern bank of the Nile, it is known chiefly from settlements and cemeteries. People cultivated crops, kept cattle, sheep, and goats, and supplemented farming with fishing and hunting. Their fine pottery, often with black rims and rippled surfaces, is particularly distinctive. Graves contained bodies wrapped in mats or skins alongside vessels and other belongings. Differences in burial wealth suggest social distinctions, but the evidence does not identify kings or a common government. Imported shells, stone vessels, and beads reveal wider connections. Badarian traditions contributed to the later predynastic cultures from which ancient Egyptian society took shape.
