description: >-
  The Aceh Sultanate was an Islamic monarchy in northern Sumatra, represented
  here from 1496 to its dissolution under Dutch conquest in 1904. Its capital,
  now Banda Aceh, became a port linking Southeast Asia with the wider Indian
  Ocean. Sultans drew wealth from pepper exports and contested Portuguese power
  around Malacca, while seeking Ottoman connections and promoting Islamic
  scholarship. Ali Mughayat Syah unified neighboring territories, and Iskandar
  Muda expanded royal authority during the early seventeenth century. Four
  successive queens later governed the state, while regional chiefs gained
  influence as central power weakened. Dutch invasion in 1873 began a prolonged
  war. The sultanate ended after its last ruler's surrender and subsequent
  conquest, although armed resistance in Aceh continued beyond the monarchy's
  destruction.
