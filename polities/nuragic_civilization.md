description: >-
  Nuragic civilization developed in Sardinia from approximately 1800 BCE and is represented
  here until Rome's takeover in 238 BCE. Its name comes from nuraghes, the stone towers and
  tower complexes that still mark the island's landscape. Villages, collective tombs, sacred
  wells, bronze figures, and metalworking remains show varied communities with rich ritual
  traditions and Mediterranean contacts. The evidence suggests local leaders and changing
  social hierarchies, but does not establish a single island kingdom or government. Farmers,
  herders, craftspeople, and traders supported settlements such as Su Nuraxi. Phoenician
  settlement and later Carthaginian conquest transformed coastal areas, while much of the
  interior remained outside their control. Roman annexation provides this record's endpoint,
  although interior Sardinian communities resisted Roman authority for centuries afterward.
