name: "Early and Middle Helladic culture"
description: >-
  Early and Middle Helladic culture describes Bronze Age communities on mainland
  Greece, represented here approximately from 3200 to 1600 BCE. It is an
  archaeological grouping based largely on pottery, settlement remains, tools,
  and burials, not a single state. Farmers grew cereals and legumes, raised
  livestock, and developed metalworking and exchange with other Aegean regions.
  Lerna, the representative site, preserves the substantial House of the Tiles
  and later settlements, showing changing construction and social organisation.
  Fortifications and larger buildings suggest differences in wealth and
  authority, but named rulers and detailed governments are unknown. Middle
  Helladic pottery includes burnished Minyan ware and painted vessels, while
  burial practices became more varied. These communities preceded the
  development of the later Mycenaean royal palace states.
