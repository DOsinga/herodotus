description: >-
  Pan Pan was a kingdom on the eastern Malay Peninsula, represented here from around 300 to 775. Its
  location remains disputed: Phunphin in southern Thailand is one proposal, while others place it
  farther south in Malaysia. Chinese histories provide the evidence, recording royal missions from the
  fifth century onward and describing a king, ranked officials, Indian brahmans, temples, and Buddhist
  monks. These reports place Pan Pan within the maritime exchanges linking India, Southeast Asia, and
  China. Its rulers supported religious specialists and sent gifts, including elephants, to Chinese
  courts. Little survives about its internal history or succession. The kingdom eventually disappeared
  into the Srivijayan sphere during the eighth century, although neither a precise conquest date nor
  its borders are secure.
