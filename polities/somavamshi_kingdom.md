description: >-
  The Somavamshi Kingdom arose in the Kosala region of eastern India during the ninth century, conventionally around 850. Its rulers governed from centres including Yayatinagara, identified with Binka, and later Jajpur. Janamejaya's inscriptions provide firmer evidence than the uncertain beginnings of the dynasty. Through warfare, marriage alliances, and conquest, the kingdom expanded from western Odisha into the coastal lands of the Bhauma-Karas around the tenth century. Kings claimed lunar ancestry and supported Brahmanical religion, especially Shiva worship. Their period saw developments in Odisha's temple architecture and a shift away from earlier Buddhist royal patronage. The state weakened amid conflicts with neighbouring powers. Around 1110, the Eastern Ganga ruler Anantavarman Chodaganga conquered its remaining territories and replaced the Somavamshi ruling house.
