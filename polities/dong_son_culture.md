description: >-
  Đông Sơn culture describes archaeological communities centred on northern Vietnam's
  Red River valley, represented here from roughly 1000 to 257 BCE. It is not evidence
  for a single documented government. Its best known finds are bronze drums decorated
  with boats, warriors, musicians, animals and geometric patterns. Their skilled casting
  and wide distribution reveal specialised production and connections across mainland
  and island Southeast Asia. People cultivated rice, raised pigs and water buffalo,
  fished and travelled in dugout canoes. Graves, tools and other bronze objects provide
  further evidence of daily life and social differences. Traditions connect these
  communities with Văn Lang, but that kingdom's chronology is legendary. The record ends
  when Âu Lạc begins, rather than when the material culture abruptly disappeared.
