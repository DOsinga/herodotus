description: >-
  La Venta was an Olmec centre in the Gulf lowlands of present Tabasco, Mexico, flourishing approximately between 900 and 400 BCE. Built on raised ground amid wetlands, it became prominent after the decline of San Lorenzo. Earthen platforms, plazas, and a large pyramid organised a ceremonial landscape that required extensive collective labour. Colossal stone heads, sculpted thrones, rich offerings, and elaborate burials point to powerful leaders and a ranked society, although their names and governing institutions are unknown. Basalt and other valuable materials travelled from distant sources, revealing wide exchange networks. Farming communities supported specialists and ceremonial activity. The centre lost its prominence around 400 BCE and was largely abandoned; the reasons remain uncertain, rather than a securely documented conquest.
