description: >-
  The Únětice culture occupied parts of central Europe during the Early Bronze Age, approximately
  2300 to 1600 BCE. Named after a cemetery near Prague, it is known across modern Czechia,
  Germany, Poland, Slovakia, and neighbouring regions. Archaeologists identify it through
  pottery, burials, settlements, and an extensive tradition of bronze tools and ornaments.
  Villages contained wooden houses and storage pits, while some larger settlements had
  substantial defences. Rich burial mounds and metal hoards suggest differences in wealth and
  access to resources, but they do not establish a single kingdom or known rulers. Long distance
  exchange carried amber, metals, and finished objects between regions. The Nebra sky disc
  belongs to this cultural setting. Later Bronze Age traditions, including the Tumulus culture,
  followed.
