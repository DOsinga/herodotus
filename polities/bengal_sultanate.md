description: >-
  The Bengal Sultanate united much of the Bengal delta under an independent Muslim monarchy in 1352. Its rulers governed from centres including Pandua and Gaur, drawing on Persian court traditions while supporting a distinctly Bengali culture. Successive dynasties employed Muslim and Hindu officials, sponsored mosques and literature, and benefited from farmland and Indian Ocean trade. Bengal maintained diplomatic contacts as far as China and attracted merchants from many regions. Sher Shah's conquest brought the sultanate under the Sur Empire in 1539. A Bengal governor restored independent rule in 1554, followed by the Afghan Karrani dynasty. Its last sultan, Daud Khan, resisted Mughal expansion but was defeated at Rajmahal in 1576. Bengal then became a Mughal province, though resistance continued locally.
