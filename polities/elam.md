description: >-
  Elam occupied the lowlands and mountains of southwestern Iran, linking Susa with centers such as Anshan. This record spans its kingdoms from about 2700 to 539 BCE, rather than treating every ruling house separately. Kings and, in some periods, rulers bearing the title sukkalmah governed a region whose political unity often shifted. Elamite armies fought, traded, and negotiated with Mesopotamian neighbors; they helped bring down Ur and later carried Babylonian monuments to Susa. The monumental sanctuary at Chogha Zanbil reflects the wealth and religious ambitions of Middle Elamite rulers. Assyrian campaigns devastated Susa in the seventh century BCE, but local continuity outlasted the destruction. Persian incorporation ended the separate kingdom, while Elamite language and administrative practices survived under Achaemenid rule.
