description: >-
  The Gurjara Kingdom of Lata governed part of southern Gujarat in western India,
  conventionally dated here from about 580 to 738. Its rulers were based at
  Nandipuri, usually identified with Nandod, near the port of Bharuch. Copper plate
  land grants provide most surviving evidence for the dynasty and its
  administration. The family's rulers bore feudatory titles and acknowledged
  changing overlords, but issued grants and managed their own territory. Their lands
  lay between the Mahi and Narmada rivers, sometimes extending farther north or
  south. Early grants record sun worship, while later rulers supported Shaivism. The
  kingdom stood between competing regional powers, including the Maitrakas and
  Chalukyas. Its final loss occurred amid Arab campaigns and the Chalukya response,
  although circumstances remain uncertain.
