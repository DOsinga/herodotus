description: >-
  The Taifa of Mértola was a Muslim kingdom in the Guadiana valley of present southeastern
  Portugal, centred on the river port and castle of Mértola. Ibn Tayfur made it independent in
  1033, during the breakup of the Caliphate of Córdoba, but Seville took it in 1044. A century
  later Mértola became the base of Ibn Qasi, a Sufi leader whose followers, the Muridin, rose
  against Almoravid rule in 1144. He was driven out by the ruler of Badajoz and Évora in 1145,
  then returned with Almohad help in 1146 and governed again as an ally of the Almohads. His later
  turn towards the Portuguese king led to his murder at Silves in 1151, and the Almohads took his
  territory.
