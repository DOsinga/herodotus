description: >-
  The Vishnukundina Kingdom ruled parts of the eastern Deccan and coastal Andhra
  in southern India, conventionally dated from 420 to 624. Its kings expanded
  into the Godavari region and maintained royal centers, including Denduluru,
  Amaravati, and Indrapalanagara. The kingdom grew through warfare and marriage
  links with the Vakatakas, competing with the Pallavas and other neighboring
  powers. Royal relatives governed regional divisions, while inscriptions record
  land grants and officers responsible for military forces. Kings sponsored
  Vedic sacrifices and Hindu religious institutions, though Buddhist
  establishments also received support. Disputed inscriptions and genealogies
  make individual reigns difficult to date securely. Chalukya attacks under
  Pulakeshin II ended Vishnukundina government by 624, bringing its former lands
  under Chalukya administration before Vengi's later separate political
  development.
