description: >-
  Yan was the rival imperial state created during the An Lushan Rebellion in northern China,
  lasting from 756 to 763. An Lushan, formerly a powerful Tang frontier commander, declared
  himself emperor at Luoyang after rebelling in 755. His forces also captured Chang'an, but Tang
  government continued outside the conquered territories. Yan's court shifted between Luoyang,
  Yecheng and Fanyang as the war changed direction. Four emperors ruled it: An Lushan, his son An
  Qingxu, Shi Siming and Shi Chaoyi. Murders and disputes within these ruling families weakened
  its leadership. Tang armies and their allies gradually recovered the occupied lands. The last
  emperor, Shi Chaoyi, killed himself in 763. Yan's collapse ended its competing government,
  although the rebellion left Tang authority damaged.
