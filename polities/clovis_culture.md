description: >-
  Clovis culture names an archaeological tradition found across much of North America near the end
  of the last Ice Age. This record places it approximately between 11100 and 10800 BCE, with
  Blackwater Draw near Clovis, New Mexico, representing the type site. Its stone spear points have
  grooves removed from their bases, alongside blades, scrapers and tools made from bone or ivory.
  Finds associated with mammoths and other large animals show hunting, but food gathering and
  smaller prey also mattered. The evidence concerns mobile communities sharing technologies, not a
  single state or known government. Regional tool traditions followed as ways of life changed.
  Older sites demonstrate that Clovis people were not the first inhabitants of the Americas,
  contrary to earlier theories.
