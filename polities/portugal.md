  Portugal is a republic on the Atlantic edge of the Iberian Peninsula, including the
  Azores and Madeira. This record begins with the revolution of 1910, which ended the
  monarchy and established the First Republic in Lisbon. Political instability and the
  strain of participation in the First World War helped bring a military coup in 1926.
  Salazar's Estado Novo later imposed an authoritarian government that retained
  Portugal's overseas empire and fought colonial independence movements. The Carnation
  Revolution of 1974 opened the way to democracy and decolonization. A constitution
  adopted in 1976 established a system with an elected president, parliament, and
  government. Portugal joined the European Economic Community in 1986, while remaining
  the same state through these successive changes of political regime.
