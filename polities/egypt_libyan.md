description: >-
  Egypt under the Twenty-second Dynasty was ruled by kings of Libyan ancestry, beginning with Shoshenq I around 943 BCE. Their principal northern centers were Tanis and Bubastis, while royal relatives held powerful religious and military offices elsewhere. The rulers adopted Egyptian royal traditions and supported temples, rather than governing as an colonial administration. Shoshenq campaigned in the Levant, and early kings attempted to hold together authority across the Nile valley. Over time, competing royal branches and regional leaders reduced that unity. From about 837 BCE, the Twenty-third Dynasty governed separately, with additional local powers emerging afterward. Piye's Kushite conquest ended this record in 728 BCE. Osorkon IV nevertheless retained Tanis under Kushite overlordship for some years after that military defeat.
