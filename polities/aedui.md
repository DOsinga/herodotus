description: >-
  The Aedui were a Gaulish people whose state occupied much of modern Burgundy, between the Loire and Saône rivers. Its estimated beginning here around 200 BCE marks an independent political community, not the people's first appearance. Bibracte, a fortified hilltop settlement on Mont Beuvray, became their principal centre. An aristocratic senate and an annually elected magistrate, the vergobret, directed government and justice. Their position on river routes supported trade and influence over neighbouring client peoples. They cultivated a close alliance with Rome, which described them as brothers and kinsmen. Rivalry with the Sequani helped draw Caesar into Gaul, but the Aedui eventually joined the revolt of 52 BCE. Roman conquest ended their independence, while their community continued under Roman administration.
