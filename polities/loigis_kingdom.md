description: >-
  Loigis was a small Irish kingdom in western Leinster, in the region that gave modern
  County Laois its name. Its beginnings are traditionally placed around 300, but genealogies
  and accounts of migration do not establish a precise foundation date. Tradition connected
  its people with northern Ireland and described a grant of land for military service to
  Leinster's king. Local chiefs, later principally the O'More family, maintained their own
  government within the wider politics of Leinster. Dunamase represents the territory's
  fortified centre rather than an unchanged capital throughout its long history. English
  expansion and plantation ended its territorial government in 1556, when Queen's County
  administration was established. O'More leaders continued resistance afterward, but their
  survival did not preserve the former kingdom.
