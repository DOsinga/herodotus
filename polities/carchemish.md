description: >-
  Carchemish was a kingdom on the Euphrates, near the present Turkish and Syrian border,
  that survived the Hittite collapse around 1180 BCE. Earlier Hittite kings had placed
  relatives there as viceroys; their descendants preserved royal authority after Hattusa's
  government disappeared. The city commanded an river crossing and maintained Hittite and
  Luwian traditions through changing dynasties. Monumental reliefs and hieroglyphic
  inscriptions record rulers, gods and public building projects, making it one of the best
  documented kingdoms of Iron Age northern Syria. Relations with Assyria shifted between
  resistance and tribute as the larger empire advanced westward. In 717 BCE, Sargon II
  conquered Carchemish and removed its last king, Pisiri. The city continued under imperial
  rule, but its independent royal government had ended.
