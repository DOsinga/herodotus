description: >-
  The Lordship of Ruppin was a territory north of Berlin, between the Prignitz and the Uckermark,
  ruled by the counts of Lindow-Ruppin, a branch of the Saxon counts of Arnstein. They took the
  Ruppin land early in the thirteenth century, during the German settlement east of the Elbe, and
  founded the town of Neuruppin beside a Slavic stronghold, granting it a charter in 1256. The
  counts were neighbours and often vassals of the Brandenburg margraves but kept their lordship as
  a territory of its own, governed from Neuruppin and Alt Ruppin. They took part in the wars of
  the margraves and founded monasteries in their land. When the last count, Wichmann, died in 1524
  without heirs, Ruppin fell to Brandenburg.
