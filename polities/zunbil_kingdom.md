description: >-
  The Zunbil kingdom governed Zabulistan in southern Afghanistan from
  approximately 680 to 870. Its rulers emerged alongside the Turk Shahis of Kabul,
  with related ruling families sometimes cooperating and sometimes competing.
  Ghazni and Zamindawar were important centres, and the kingdom linked highland
  routes with the plains around Bost and Kandahar. The name Zunbil is associated
  with the deity Zun, whose shrine became prominent in accounts of early Arab
  campaigns. Religious life also reflected Buddhist and other regional traditions.
  Native rulers resisted repeated incursions from Sistan, negotiating tribute or
  temporary agreements without consistently surrendering their government. Their
  coins drew on several neighbouring cultural traditions. Saffarid expansion under
  Yaqub ibn al-Layth ultimately broke this resistance, ending the kingdom
  represented here in 870.
