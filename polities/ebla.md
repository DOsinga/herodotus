description: >-
  Ebla was a Bronze Age kingdom centered at Tell Mardikh in northern Syria, with its
  political history here estimated from 3000 to 1600 BCE. Kings, royal households, and
  officials managed a court whose surviving archives reveal trade, diplomacy, taxation,
  and relations with neighboring cities. Thousands of clay tablets made Ebla one of the
  best documented early Syrian kingdoms, preserving both Sumerian and the local Eblaite
  language. The city suffered major destructions around 2300 and 2000 BCE, but new
  Eblaite kingdoms followed each time. Later rulers rebuilt palaces, temples, and
  strong defenses, and maintained connections with Egypt and nearby Amorite courts. The
  third kingdom operated under Yamhad's overlordship before a Hittite attack destroyed
  it around 1600, ending its separate royal government.
