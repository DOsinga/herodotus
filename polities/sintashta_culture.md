description: >-
  The Sintashta culture developed around the southern Ural Mountains, in present Russia
  and northern Kazakhstan, roughly between 2100 and 1800 BCE. Archaeologists identify it
  through fortified settlements, metalworking remains, distinctive burials, and some of
  the earliest evidence for chariots with spoked wheels. Communities raised cattle, sheep,
  goats, and horses while producing copper and bronze on a remarkable scale. Settlements
  such as Sintashta and Arkaim contained closely arranged houses behind defensive walls.
  Rich graves with weapons, horses, and vehicles suggest social differences and
  competition, but they do not reveal a named kingdom or known system of government. Its
  connections with later Andronovo communities and early Indo-Iranian traditions remain
  important questions in understanding movement across the Eurasian steppe. Dates remain
  approximate throughout.
