description: >-
  Zichia was the land of the Zikhs, a people of the northwestern Caucasus known to Greek writers
  as the Zygii and regarded as ancestors of the Circassians. Strabo placed them on the coast
  north of Colchis, describing them as shepherds and pirates with boats built for raiding. Over
  the following centuries they spread from the coast between Gagra and Tuapse to the mouth of the
  Kuban and the Taman peninsula, taking over the lands of the old Bosporan towns such as
  Gorgippia, which their neighbours overran in the third century. They were led by princes and
  clan chiefs rather than a single king, sometimes under Khazar, Rus' or Byzantine overlords, and
  were partly Christian, with their own bishops. Genoese merchants traded on their coast from the
  late thirteenth century.
