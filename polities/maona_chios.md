description: >-
  The Maona of Chios was a Genoese company that ruled the Aegean island of Chios, and for a
  time the port of Phocaea, from 1346 to 1566. A Genoese fleet paid for by private investors
  took Chios from the Byzantines in 1346. The Commune could not repay them, so in 1347 it
  handed the investors the island's government and revenues as shares in a company. Within a
  few years the shares passed to settlers on Chios, who took the common name Giustiniani. The
  company appointed the island's officials, kept a garrison and paid tribute to Genoa and later
  to the Byzantines and the Ottomans. Chios grew rich on mastic and trade. In 1566 the Ottoman
  admiral Piyale Pasha occupied the island and ended the company's rule.
