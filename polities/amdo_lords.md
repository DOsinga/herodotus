description: >-
  After the Tibetan Empire broke apart in the civil wars that followed the death of Langdarma
  in 842, Amdo, the northeastern Tibetan region around Qinghai Lake and the upper Yellow River,
  was left to Tibetan tribes, clan chiefs and former imperial garrison families. Chinese
  sources describe them as many separate tribes, some of which recognised the Guiyi governors
  of Dunhuang for a few decades. From these tribes Gusiluo's Tsongkha state rose in the early
  eleventh century. When the Song annexed Tsongkha in 1104, the herding tribes of the lake
  pastures again governed themselves, while the Song, the Jin and the Western Xia fought over
  the farming valleys around Xining. Mongol armies imposed their authority on the region in the
  1240s.
