description: >-
  Brunswick-Lüneburg was a Welf duchy in what is now Lower Saxony, represented here as a
  governing state from 1250 to 1269. Emperor Frederick II had created it in 1235 for Otto
  the Child, grandson of Henry the Lion, as part of a settlement between rival ruling
  families. The duchy drew its name from Brunswick and Lüneburg and comprised Welf family
  estates and other lands. Otto's sons Albert and John governed jointly after his death in
  1252. In 1269 they divided their territories: Albert received the southern lands around
  Brunswick, while John took the northern lands around Lüneburg. Both branches kept the
  ducal title. Brunswick-Lüneburg survived as a legal name, but actual government passed
  to the separate principalities and their successors.
