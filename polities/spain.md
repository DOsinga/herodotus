  Spain occupies most of the Iberian Peninsula, with Madrid as its capital and territories in the
  Mediterranean, Atlantic and North Africa. This record begins in 1808, when resistance to
  Napoleon produced the Supreme Central Junta, followed by the Regency and Cortes of Cádiz. Their
  1812 constitution challenged absolute monarchy while war continued against Joseph Bonaparte. The
  following century brought colonial losses, civil conflicts, republics and restored monarchies.
  The Civil War of 1936 to 1939 led to Francisco Franco's dictatorship, which lasted until 1975.
  Democratic reform then established a parliamentary constitutional monarchy and wide powers for
  regions. Spain joined the European Communities in 1986. Its continuing state includes these
  changes of government rather than treating each regime as a separate polity.
