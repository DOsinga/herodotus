description: >-
  The Baro-Bhuyans of Assam were landholding chiefs of the middle Brahmaputra valley who
  governed their own districts after the Kamarupa kingdom collapsed in the twelfth century.
  Each Bhuyan ruled a group of villages with his own fighting men, and the chiefs joined in
  loose confederacies to drive off invaders, as against Iwaz Shah in the thirteenth century
  and the Bengal governors of Husain Shah around 1500. They held Darrang, Sonitpur and Nagaon,
  between the Kamata kingdom to the west, the Chutia kingdom to the east and the Kacharis to
  the south. One of them, Arimatta, seized the Kamata throne. In the sixteenth century the
  Koch kings pushed them out of Kamrup and the Ahom king Suhungmung took the north bank around
  Tezpur, settling the chiefs as his vassals.
