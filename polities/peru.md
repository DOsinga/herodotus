  Peru is a South American republic whose independent history begins with the
  declaration against Spanish rule in 1821. Lima, formerly the colonial capital,
  remained the national seat of government. Independence was secured through campaigns
  associated with José de San Martín and Simón Bolívar, culminating in decisive battles
  in 1824. The country includes a Pacific coastal strip, the Andes, and Amazonian lands,
  with diverse Indigenous and colonial inheritances. Republican government repeatedly
  alternated with military rule, while wealth from guano and minerals shaped economic
  life. Defeat in the War of the Pacific brought territorial losses to Chile. Twentieth
  century reforms, insurgency, and authoritarian government left divisions. Peru
  continues as one state despite changes of regime and recurrent struggles over
  representative political institutions.
