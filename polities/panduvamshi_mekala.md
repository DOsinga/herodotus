description: >-
  The Panduvamshi Kingdom of Mekala governed part of central India around the Maikal Hills, including areas of present Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh. Its estimated dates, 500 to 650, follow a reconstructed royal chronology rather than a recorded foundation or conquest. Inscriptions identify a hereditary line beginning with Jayabala and claim descent from the legendary Pandavas. Later kings used the title maharaja, suggesting increased authority after possible early dependence on larger neighbours. Nagabala, Bharatabala, and Shurabala are remembered through grants and royal praise that also show support for Shiva worship and Brahmins. The capital has not been firmly identified. Connections with the Panduvamshis of nearby Kosala remain uncertain, and surviving inscriptions leave the circumstances of this kingdom's decline and ending unclear.
