description: >-
  The Hassuna culture identifies farming communities in northern Mesopotamia, particularly present northern Iraq, during an estimated span of 6500 to 6000 BCE. It takes its name from Tell Hassuna, where excavations revealed village houses and distinctive pottery. This is an archaeological grouping, not a documented kingdom: its rulers, political boundaries, and forms of government are unknown. Settlements were small, built where rainfall supported agriculture without the irrigation systems of later southern cities. Adobe houses arranged around courtyards, storage bins, ovens, sickles, grinding stones, and animal bones show settled farming and herding. Potters decorated pale vessels with reddish geometric designs. Figurines and burials provide evidence of ritual practices, though their meanings remain uncertain. Related finds at other sites reveal wider connections.
