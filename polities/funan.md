description: >-
  Funan was an early kingdom or network of linked centres in the lower Mekong region,
  covering parts of present Cambodia and southern Vietnam. Its foundation around 50 CE is
  approximate; detailed knowledge begins with Chinese visitors in the third century. Chinese
  accounts describe rulers, embassies, and trade, but Funan's native name and exact
  political boundaries remain uncertain. Archaeology at Angkor Borei and Óc Eo reveals
  canals, dense settlement, craft production, and connections with distant markets. Its
  courts adopted elements of Indian religion and Sanskrit learning while maintaining local
  traditions. Historians debate whether one monarchy consistently controlled the whole
  region. Chenla gradually gained territory, with 627 used here for the conventional final
  conquest rather than the earlier loss of northern lands.
