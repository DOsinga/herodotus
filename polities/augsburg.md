description: >-
  Augsburg governed itself as a free imperial city in southern Germany from 1276 until its
  annexation by Bavaria in 1806. Located near the meeting of the Lech and Wertach rivers, it
  prospered through textiles, commerce and routes crossing the Alps toward Italy. Its city
  council operated independently of the neighbouring bishop, although disputes between civic
  and ecclesiastical authorities continued. The Fugger and Welser families made Augsburg an
  important centre of European finance during the sixteenth century. Imperial assemblies made
  it a prominent setting for religious negotiations, including the Augsburg Confession and
  Peace of Augsburg. Catholic and Protestant communities eventually shared municipal
  government. The city survived the first wave of imperial reorganisation but lost its
  independence during Napoleon's restructuring of Germany.
