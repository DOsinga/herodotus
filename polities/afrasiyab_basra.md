description: >-
  In 1596 the Ottoman governor of Basra sold his office to Afrasiyab, a local notable of
  uncertain Arab or Turkish origin, and the city became a hereditary principality under his
  family until 1668. Afrasiyab and his successors governed Basra and the marshes of southern Iraq
  with their own troops and revenues, giving the sultan little more than recognition. Basra
  prospered as a port where Portuguese, Dutch and English merchants traded. Shah Abbas the Great,
  who saw it as a rival to his own port of Bandar Abbas, attacked it in 1624, 1625 and from 1628
  to 1629 without success. When Husayn Pasha refused to acknowledge the sultan, an Ottoman
  expedition from Baghdad marched on Basra; Husayn fled, and in 1668 the governor of Baghdad
  restored direct Ottoman rule.
