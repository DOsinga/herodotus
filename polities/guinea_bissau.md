  Guinea-Bissau is a West African republic on the Atlantic coast, including the Bijagós Islands, with its capital at Bissau. This record begins with independence declared in 1973, when the PAIGC liberation movement already administered areas outside Portuguese control. Portugal recognised the new state in 1974 after its own revolution. The first government pursued single-party rule and close ties with Cape Verde, but a military coup in 1980 ended plans for political union. Multiparty elections began in 1994. Civil war, coups and repeated disputes between civilian leaders and the armed forces have marked subsequent politics. Agriculture, particularly cashew cultivation, supports many livelihoods, alongside fishing and trade. Despite changes in regime and instability, the country has continued as the same independent state.
