description: >-
  The Urnfield culture describes related Late Bronze Age communities across central
  Europe, approximately 1300 to 800 BCE, rather than one kingdom or people with a known
  government. Its name comes from cemeteries where cremated remains were placed in
  pottery urns, often buried together in extensive fields. The practice spread
  gradually from earlier burial traditions, with considerable variation between
  regions. Settlements, hillforts, bronze weapons, ornaments, and metalworking remains
  reveal farming communities linked by exchange and shared craft styles. Southern
  Germany provides a representative location, but the distribution reached from eastern
  France towards Hungary and beyond. Archaeology cannot establish a single ruler,
  language, or political structure for this whole area. Local developments eventually
  led into the Iron Age societies associated with Hallstatt.
