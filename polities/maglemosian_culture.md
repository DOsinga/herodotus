description: >-
  The Maglemosian culture describes early Mesolithic communities of northern
  Europe, approximately from 9000 to 6000 BCE. It takes its name from a Danish
  wetland site near Mullerup on Zealand, where preserved remains revealed life
  after the last Ice Age. People hunted forest animals, fished lakes and rivers,
  and gathered wild foods, using tools made from flint, bone, antler, and wood.
  Small flint blades were fitted into larger weapons, while fishing spears show
  the importance of aquatic resources. Dogs accompanied these communities, and
  seasonal huts suggest movement between useful locations. Lower sea levels
  connected areas now separated by water, including lands beneath the North Sea.
  These shared archaeological traits do not identify a unified government; in
  Scandinavia, Kongemose traditions succeeded them.
