  Azerbaijan is a republic in the South Caucasus on the western shore of the Caspian
  Sea, independent since 1991. Baku, its capital, has long been an important oil
  center. The country emerged from the dissolution of the Soviet Union, recalling an
  earlier republic that existed from 1918 to 1920. Its presidential government and
  parliament operate within a political system dominated by the Aliyev family.
  Petroleum and natural gas exports have shaped its economy and relations with
  neighboring states. Independence coincided with war over Nagorno-Karabakh, where
  Armenian forces established a separate government despite international
  recognition of Azerbaijani sovereignty. Azerbaijan regained extensive territory in
  2020 and took control of the remaining enclave in 2023, prompting the departure of
  its ethnic Armenian population.
