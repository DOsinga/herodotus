name: "Tichitt culture"
description: >-
  The Tichitt culture describes ancient settlement communities in eastern Mauritania and
  neighboring western Sahelian regions, approximately 2200 to 200 BCE. Dhar Tichitt is a
  representative center among related sites at Walata, Nema, and Tagant. Archaeology reveals
  stone walled compounds, houses, granaries, burial monuments, pottery, and rock art. People
  cultivated pearl millet and kept livestock while adapting to increasingly dry conditions.
  Settlements of different sizes suggest organized regional relationships and social
  ranking, but the evidence does not establish named rulers or one unified state. Shared
  walls and planned compounds show community cooperation. Researchers have connected the
  tradition with later West African societies, though direct political continuity remains
  uncertain. Its decline and regional transformations unfolded gradually rather than through
  a recorded conquest.
