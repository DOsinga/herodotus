  Kyrgyzstan is a mountainous Central Asian republic that became independent in 1991 as the
  Soviet Union dissolved. Bishkek is its capital, and its territory includes much of the
  Tian Shan and the lake Issyk Kul. The new state inherited Soviet institutions alongside
  older traditions of pastoral life and regional ties. Presidents and an elected legislature
  have governed through repeated constitutional changes. Revolutions in 2005 and 2010
  removed presidents, while unrest in 2020 brought another political change. Parliamentary
  arrangements introduced after 2010 later gave way to a stronger presidency under the 2021
  constitution. Economic life depends on agriculture, mining, services, and money sent home
  by migrant workers. Relations with neighboring states, Russia, and China shape its
  continuing political and economic development.
