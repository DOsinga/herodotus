  Trinidad and Tobago is a Caribbean country near the coast of Venezuela, independent from
  Britain since 1962. Its two islands had different colonial histories before being joined
  under British administration in 1889. Port of Spain is the capital. Independence initially
  preserved the British monarch as head of state; a republican constitution replaced that
  office with a president in 1976. Government follows a parliamentary system, with executive
  power centred on the prime minister and a legislature of two chambers. Tobago's assembly
  manages the island's local affairs. Oil and natural gas distinguish the economy from Caribbean
  countries more dependent on tourism. African and Indian heritage shapes its festivals and
  music, including Carnival, calypso and steelpan, alongside other influences within its
  diverse society.
