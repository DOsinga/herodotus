description: >-
  The Demak Sultanate emerged on the northern coast of Java in the late fifteenth century,
  amid Majapahit's decline, and became a major early centre of Islamic rule on the island.
  Its founding is traditionally associated with Raden Patah and dated around 1475.
  Hereditary sultans drew power from coastal trade, rice supplies, and alliances with
  other port rulers. Religious teachers remembered as the Wali Songo helped shape its
  identity, while the Great Mosque became its enduring landmark. Demak challenged
  Portuguese power at Malacca and expanded across Java under Sultan Trenggana. His death
  in 1546 opened a struggle among rival heirs and regional leaders. Jaka Tingkir
  eventually prevailed, shifting the political centre inland to Pajang. Demak's remaining
  political authority ended by 1568.
