description: >-
  The Scythian kingdom in Crimea was a late Scythian monarchy centred on Neapolis, near
  modern Simferopol. Its dates, roughly 200 BCE to 250 CE, are estimates for a kingdom known
  through ancient accounts and archaeology. Kings such as Skilurus and his son Palacus ruled
  from a fortified settlement that contained royal residences and burial monuments. Their
  domain included parts of Crimea and lands toward the lower Dnieper. Unlike the earlier
  image of wholly nomadic Scythians, this society combined settled communities with steppe
  traditions and contact with Greek cities. Its population also included Tauri and people
  influenced by Greek culture. Neapolis survived for several centuries before Gothic attacks
  destroyed it in the third century, ending this regional centre of royal power.
