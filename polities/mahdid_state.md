description: >-
  The Mahdid State was a short-lived regime in the Tihama of western Yemen, ruled from Zabid
  between 1159 and 1174. Its founder Ali ibn Mahdi, a preacher claiming Himyarite descent,
  built a following among the lowland people in the 1130s and 1140s, at first with the favour
  of the Najahid court. He later turned against the Najahids, and after the murder of their
  vizier Surur in 1156 he captured Zabid, dying soon after in 1159. His sons Mahdi and Abd al-
  Nabi ruled a strict, militant community that treated Muslims who opposed it as unbelievers.
  Abd al-Nabi annexed the Sulaymanid lands, took Taiz and Ibb and besieged Aden, but was
  defeated by the Zurayids and Hamdanids in 1173. The Ayyubids took Zabid in May 1174.
