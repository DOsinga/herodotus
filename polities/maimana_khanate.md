description: >-
  The Maimana Khanate was an Uzbek state in the northwestern foothills of Afghanistan, centred on
  the town of Maimana. Its rulers came from the Ming tribe, whose chiefs had governed Maimana for
  the Khanate of Bukhara since 1621. After the death of Nader Shah in 1747 Hajji Bi Ming made
  himself independent, and his successors ruled one of the Chahar Wilayat, the four Uzbek
  khanates of the region, together with Andkhui, Shibarghan and Sar-i Pul. They paid tribute to
  the Durrani kings when these were strong and fought their neighbours, including Bukhara and the
  Emirate of Herat, which tried to annex Maimana in 1847 and 1850. After a revolt against
  Afghanistan in 1875 the town was sacked, and Abdur Rahman Khan annexed the khanate in 1892.
