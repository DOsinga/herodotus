description: >-
  The Ajuran Sultanate was a Muslim state in the Horn of Africa, represented here from about 1200
  to 1700. Its influence extended through southern Somalia and adjoining river valleys, linking
  inland farming and pastoral communities with coastal trading towns such as Mogadishu. The
  ruling Garen house combined religious authority with a central administration and collected
  tribute. Control of wells, irrigation works and the Jubba and Shabelle rivers gave its rulers
  leverage over food production and access to water. Coastal commerce connected the region with
  Arabia, India and wider Indian Ocean routes. Ajuran forces confronted Portuguese attacks and
  pressures from expanding Oromo groups. Internal resistance and rival local powers eventually
  broke its authority apart, with successor states including Geledi and Hiraab.
