description: >-
  Tres Zapotes was an Epi-Olmec center in southern Veracruz, Mexico, represented here
  from approximately 400 BCE to 300 CE. It had older Olmec roots and remained occupied
  after La Venta declined. Located between the Los Tuxtlas uplands and the Papaloapan
  lowlands, it drew on farming lands and nearby sources of volcanic stone. Monumental
  sculpture and pottery show both continuity with Olmec traditions and local styles.
  Stela C preserves an early Long Count calendar date corresponding to 32 BCE, together
  with Epi-Olmec writing. Four spaced mound groups suggest authority shared among elite
  factions rather than one tightly centralized ruler. Its leaders are poorly
  documented. The approximate end marks declining prominence as Classic Veracruz
  centers grew, not immediate abandonment of the settlement.
