description: >-
  The Nordic Bronze Age describes related societies in southern Scandinavia and northern
  Germany, represented here approximately from 1700 to 600 BCE. It is an archaeological culture,
  not one known kingdom. Farmers lived in longhouses and raised livestock, while access to
  imported copper and tin supported skilled bronze working. Amber exports helped connect the
  region with distant European exchange networks. Rich graves, weapons, ornaments, and burial
  mounds suggest pronounced social differences, but they do not reveal a single political
  structure. Rock carvings show boats, armed figures, animals, and ritual scenes; surviving
  bronze objects also point to religious attention to the sun. Changes in burial practices and
  the growing use of iron gradually led toward the region's Iron Age and Jastorf culture.
