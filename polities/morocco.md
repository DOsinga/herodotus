  Modern independent Morocco began in 1956 when French and Spanish protectorate rule ended. The
  country lies in northwestern Africa, facing both the Atlantic Ocean and Mediterranean Sea, with
  Rabat as its capital. The Alaouite monarchy survived colonial rule and remained the centre of
  the restored state. Morocco has an elected parliament and government, but the king retains
  substantial authority over security, foreign relations and religious affairs. Its population
  combines Arab and Amazigh cultural traditions, and Arabic and Amazigh are official languages.
  Agriculture, phosphate production, manufacturing and tourism contribute to its economy. Since
  Spain withdrew from Western Sahara, Morocco has controlled most of that disputed territory.
  Constitutional changes have altered political institutions without ending the monarchy or the
  state's continuing identity.
