  Honduras is a Central American republic with Caribbean and Pacific coasts, independent
  of the Central American federation since 1838. Earlier it had belonged to Spain's
  colonial empire and briefly to independent Mexico. Its government has passed through
  civilian presidencies, military regimes, civil wars, and coups without ending the
  state's separate existence. Tegucigalpa replaced Comayagua as capital in 1880. Banana
  exports transformed the northern coast, where foreign companies gained extensive land,
  railways, and political influence, accompanied by repeated United States interventions.
  Mining, agriculture, manufacturing, and migration have also shaped its economy. The 1969
  war with El Salvador exposed regional tensions, while the 2009 coup disrupted
  constitutional politics. Honduras continues as a presidential republic with an elected
  congress and regional economic connections.
