description: >-
  The Calusa were a people of the southwestern coast of Florida whose chiefdom was one of
  the most complex societies in North America to live without farming. They lived on the
  rich estuary fisheries around Charlotte Harbor and Estero Bay and built shell mounds,
  canals and ceremonial centres. Their capital, Calos, was probably at Mound Key. A
  paramount chief collected tribute from towns along the coast, around Lake Okeechobee and
  on the Atlantic side of the peninsula. The Calusa drove off Juan Ponce de León in 1513 and
  wounded him mortally in 1521. Pedro Menéndez de Avilés allied with their chief in 1566,
  but the Spanish fort and mission there lasted only a few years. Slave raids by Carolina's
  allies destroyed them in the early eighteenth century.
