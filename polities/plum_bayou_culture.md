description: >-
  The Plum Bayou culture developed in east central Arkansas during the Late Woodland period,
  approximately 650 to 1050. Archaeologists identify it through settlements, pottery and
  ceremonial earthworks, especially the centre now called Plum Bayou Mounds. Communities
  generally lived in small villages near the Arkansas and White rivers, while sites
  contained platform mounds arranged around plazas. They cultivated native seed crops,
  squash and sunflowers, supplemented by hunting and gathering; maize appeared only in
  limited quantities at some later settlements. Imported materials show connections with the
  Ozarks, Ouachita Mountains and coastal plain. Their political organisation and named
  leaders are unknown. Ceremonial centres were eventually abandoned, although people
  remained in the region. The site's former name, Toltec Mounds, does not indicate Mexican
  origins.
