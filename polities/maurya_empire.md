description: >-
  The Maurya Empire ruled much of the Indian subcontinent from about 322 to 185 BCE, with
  its capital at Pataliputra in the Ganges valley. Chandragupta Maurya established the
  kingdom after overthrowing the Nandas and expanded into territories formerly held by
  Alexander's successors. Emperors governed through officials, provincial centres,
  taxation, and a substantial standing army, although their control varied across the
  realm. Bindusara extended Mauryan power, and Ashoka brought Kalinga under imperial rule.
  Ashoka's inscriptions expressed remorse over that conquest and promoted moral conduct,
  religious tolerance, and public welfare. His patronage helped Buddhism spread beyond
  India. Later rulers lost territory and influence. The dynasty ended when the general
  Pushyamitra Shunga killed the last emperor, Brihadratha, and founded his own ruling
  house.
