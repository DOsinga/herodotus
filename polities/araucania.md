description: >-
  Araucanía was the independent land of the Mapuche south of the Biobío River in present-day
  Chile, which resisted conquest for more than three centuries. The Mapuche had no central
  government: lineage groups under their lonkos joined into larger alliances, and in war
  they elected a toki to lead them. Their leaders Lautaro and Caupolicán fought the Spanish
  invasion, killing Pedro de Valdivia in 1553. After the victory at Curalaba in 1598, the
  Mapuche destroyed the seven Spanish towns south of the Biobío. The Parliament of Quilín in
  1641 recognised the river as a frontier, and later parliaments kept the peace with Spain
  and then with Chile. Mapuche groups also spread across the Andes into the pampas. Chile's
  occupation of Araucanía between 1861 and 1883 ended its independence.
