description: >-
  Harikela was an early medieval kingdom in eastern Bengal, represented here
  from approximately 650 to 900 and centered on the coastal region around
  Chittagong. Seventh-century Chinese travelers mention the kingdom, while later
  literary accounts and commercial references place it among Bengal's eastern
  territories. Its exact boundaries, connections with Samatata, and possible
  reach toward Sylhet remain disputed. Chittagong represents an important
  coastal center rather than a securely identified permanent royal capital. Arab
  traders knew a related regional name, Harkand, reflecting Bengal's links with
  Indian Ocean commerce. Evidence supports royal government, but does not
  provide a clear account of its institutions throughout this period. The record
  ends with the emergence of Chandra rule around 900, not the disappearance of
  Harikela's regional name.
