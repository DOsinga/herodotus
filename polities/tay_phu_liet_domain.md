description: >-
  Tây Phù Liệt was a domain controlled by Nguyễn Siêu during northern Vietnam's tenth
  century struggle among the Twelve Warlords. Its centre lay in what is now Thanh Trì,
  south of central Hanoi. The record places its separate government approximately between
  965 and 967, rather than claiming a documented founding. Siêu styled himself Nguyễn Hữu
  Công and exercised authority as a military leader, not as ruler of a Vietnamese kingdom.
  His brothers Nguyễn Khoan and Nguyễn Thủ Tiệp also held separate territories. These
  competing centres emerged as the Ngô order broke down. Đinh Bộ Lĩnh defeated Nguyễn Siêu
  during his campaign to reunify the region. Siêu's death in 967 ended this local
  government and brought its territory under Đinh control.
