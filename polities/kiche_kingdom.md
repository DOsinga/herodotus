description: >-
  The Kʼicheʼ kingdom developed in Guatemala's highlands during the thirteenth century and lasted
  until the Spanish conquest of 1524. Its ruling lineages traced their origins through traditions
  preserved in the Popol Vuh and other indigenous histories. Earlier centers preceded Qʼumarkaj, the
  fortified capital established around 1400 near modern Santa Cruz del Quiché. Kings governed
  alongside noble lineages and used warfare, tribute, and alliances to expand their authority. Under
  Kʼiqʼab the kingdom dominated extensive highland territory and neighboring peoples. Internal
  conflicts and the separation of the Kaqchikel weakened it before European arrival. Pedro de
  Alvarado's Spanish and indigenous forces defeated its armies, seized its rulers, and burned
  Qʼumarkaj. Kʼicheʼ communities survived, preserving language and traditions despite the destruction
  of the court.
