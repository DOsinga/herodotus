description: >-
  Later Zhao ruled much of northern China from 319 to 351 during the Sixteen Kingdoms period. Shi Le, a Jie
  military leader who had once been enslaved, founded the state after breaking with Former Zhao. His court combined Chinese officials
  and institutions with separate arrangements for non Chinese populations. Victory over Former Zhao in 329 made Later Zhao the leading
  northern power, while Eastern Jin remained beyond its southern frontier. Xiangguo was its first capital; Shi Hu later moved the
  court to Ye. His demanding building projects and harsh rule accompanied continued military expansion. After his death, violent succession struggles
  shattered the dynasty. Ran Min seized Ye in 350, and the remaining Zhao rulers at Xiangguo were eliminated in 351.
