description: >-
  The Principality of Lahijan stands for the local lords who governed Lahijan and much of Bia-
  pish, east of the Sefid-Rud river, between the end of Ilkhanid rule and the rise of the Kar-
  Kiya sayyids. The Ilkhan Öljeitü had conquered Gilan in 1307, the first time the Mongols
  subdued the province, but after the death of Abu Sa'id in 1335 its lords governed themselves
  again. Like their neighbours in Fuman and Rasht, the rulers of Lahijan lived from rice and silk
  and paid tribute to whichever power was strongest south of the Alborz. In the 1360s the Zaydi
  sayyids of the Kar-Kiya family, backed by the Zaydi population of eastern Gilan, began to take
  over its districts, and Sayyid Ali Kiya made Lahijan his capital in the 1370s.
