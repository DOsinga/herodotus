description: >-
  The County of Holstein lay between the Elbe and Eider rivers in what is now northern Germany.
  This record covers its separate government under the Schauenburg counts from 1250 to 1460,
  after the weakening of imperial control. Earlier counts had encouraged settlement and towns in
  a frontier region adjoining Danish Schleswig and Slavic Wagria. Holstein was repeatedly divided
  among branches of the ruling family, with Kiel among its centres. Its rulers became deeply
  involved in Schleswig and Danish affairs through marriages, warfare, and territorial claims.
  When Adolf VIII died without an heir, the estates chose Christian I of Denmark in 1460. The
  Danish kings thereafter governed through their own officials, ending the county's separate
  government while preserving its territorial identity.
