description: >-
  The Imamate of Futa Jallon was a Fula Islamic state in the Fouta Djallon highlands of
  present-day Guinea, founded by a jihad in 1725 and ended by French conquest in 1896. Its
  almamis came from two branches of the founding family, the Alfaya and the Soriya, which took
  turns in power at Timbo. Provincial chiefs governed the provinces with much independence,
  and Labé grew into the strongest of them. Muslim Fula held full rights, while much of the
  older farming population was reduced to servile status. The imamate became a leading centre
  of Islamic learning and traded cattle, captives and goods with the coast. Its army destroyed
  Kaabu at Kansala in 1867. French columns occupied Timbo in 1896 and made the territory part
  of French Guinea.
