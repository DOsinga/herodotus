description: >-
  The Western Satraps were Saka rulers of western and central India, here dated
  approximately 35–415 CE. Their domains included Gujarat and Malwa, with Ujjain among
  their centers. Rulers used the titles satrap and great satrap, while exercising
  hereditary royal power. Successive families, notably the Kshaharatas and Kardamakas,
  expanded and defended these lands against the Satavahanas and other neighbors.
  Nahapana supported trade and religious foundations before suffering defeat; Rudradaman
  later restored their strength. Ports such as Bharuch connected inland markets with
  Indian Ocean commerce. Their silver coins preserve names, portraits, and dates, while
  Rudradaman's Junagadh inscription is a landmark of Sanskrit royal writing. Gupta
  expansion eventually destroyed their rule, with Chandragupta II's conquest placed
  around 415 in this record's estimated chronology.
