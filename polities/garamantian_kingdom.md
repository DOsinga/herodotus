description: >-
  The Garamantian Kingdom developed around the oases of the Fezzan in southern Libya, with its
  urban formation estimated here around 500 BCE. Kings ruled from Garama, a centre surrounded by
  settlements and cultivated land in the Sahara. Underground water channels sustained farming
  away from rivers, supporting a society with towns, craft production, and long distance trade.
  Its people exchanged goods with Roman North Africa and regions farther south, and relations
  with Rome included both conflict and diplomacy. Enslaved labour contributed to its economy and
  waterworks. Declining groundwater and changing trade conditions weakened the kingdom over time.
  An Arab expedition in 669 and the king's submission conventionally mark its end, though the
  loss of local government and settlement continuity remain uncertain.
