description: >-
  The Cupisnique culture flourished along Peru's northern Pacific coast during approximately
  1500 to 500 BCE. Archaeologists recognise it through pottery, adobe architecture and
  distinctive religious imagery rather than records of kings or a unified state. Its ceramic
  vessels often have stirrup handles and depict animals, people or combinations of feline,
  bird and other forms. Temples such as Collud in the Lambayeque valley show elaborate
  ceremonial construction and images associated with a spider deity. Similar symbols occur
  in Chavin art, but the relationship between the two traditions remains debated. Coastal
  farming and fishing supported the communities that produced these objects. Cupisnique's
  dates and boundaries are estimates, and its artistic connections with later traditions do
  not demonstrate a continuous government or dynasty.
