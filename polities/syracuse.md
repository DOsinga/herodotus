description: >-
  Syracuse was a Greek city state on the southeastern coast of Sicily, founded by Corinthian
  settlers around 734 BCE. Its government changed repeatedly between oligarchy, democracy,
  tyranny, and monarchy without ending the city's political identity. A strong harbour,
  fertile hinterland, and fleet made it a power in the western Mediterranean. Rulers such as
  Gelon and Dionysius expanded its influence and fought Carthage for control of Sicily. In
  413 BCE, Syracuse defeated the invading Athenian expedition, one of the decisive events of
  the Peloponnesian War. Hiero II later allied with Rome while retaining his own army and
  government. His successors changed course during the Second Punic War. Roman forces
  captured the city in 212 BCE after a prolonged siege, ending independence.
