description: >-
  The Kingdom of Burundi was a monarchy in the hills east of Lake Tanganyika, in present-day
  Burundi. Its Ganwa dynasty dates from around 1680, the estimate used here, and the kingdom
  expanded during the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries to roughly the borders of the modern
  country. The mwami ruled together with princes of the royal family, the Ganwa, who governed
  provinces with great independence and often fought one another. Royal residences moved
  within the central highlands, around Muramvya and Gitega. Mwami Mwezi IV Gisabo resisted
  German forces for years after they arrived in the 1890s. He accepted German protection in
  the Treaty of Kiganda in 1903, which ends this record. The monarchy continued under German
  and Belgian rule, regained independence in 1962 and was abolished in 1966.
