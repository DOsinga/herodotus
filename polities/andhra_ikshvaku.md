description: >-
  The Andhra Ikshvaku kingdom governed part of the Krishna valley in present Andhra Pradesh
  after Satavahana power declined. Its estimated dates, 225 to 340 CE, rest on inscriptions
  whose regnal years cannot be tied precisely to a calendar. Vijayapuri, at modern
  Nagarjunakonda, was its centre. Four rulers are known from inscriptions and coins, beginning
  with Chamtamula. The kings performed Vedic sacrifices and supported Hindu shrines, while
  royal women and other donors funded important Buddhist monuments. Marriage links connected
  the court with the Western Kshatrapas and neighbouring ruling families. Excavated buildings,
  sculptures and records show a lively religious landscape rather than exclusive royal
  patronage of one faith. By the middle of the fourth century, Pallava expansion displaced or
  subordinated Ikshvaku authority.
