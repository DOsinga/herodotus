description: >-
  The Tokugawa shogunate governed Japan from Edo after Ieyasu's victory at Sekigahara in
  1600, lasting until the Meiji Restoration in 1868. Ieyasu formally became shogun in 1603,
  founding a hereditary military government while emperors remained at Kyoto. Regional lords
  administered their domains under rules imposed by the shogunate, including service and
  residence obligations that restrained independent action. Long peace encouraged growing
  towns, commerce, publishing and popular culture. Authorities restricted foreign relations
  and Christianity, but controlled trade continued through designated channels. Western
  naval pressure in the nineteenth century forced treaties that weakened the government's
  standing. Disputes over foreign policy and imperial authority drew domains into
  opposition. The restoration displaced the Tokugawa government and began the transformation
  toward a centralised imperial state.
