description: >-
  The Crown of Castile formed in 1230 when Ferdinand III permanently united Castile and León
  under one monarch. It covered much of central, northern, and western Iberia, including
  Galicia, and expanded south through conquests from Muslim rulers. Ferdinand captured Córdoba
  and Seville, while his son Alfonso X promoted law, learning, and royal administration. Kings
  governed alongside nobles, towns, and the Cortes, whose cooperation mattered for taxation
  and political support. Wool exports linked the interior to northern European markets.
  Succession conflicts brought the Trastámara dynasty to power and later challenged Isabella's
  accession. This record ends in 1479, when Ferdinand inherited Aragon and the crowns entered
  the Spanish monarchy together. Castilian laws and institutions continued within that larger
  monarchy for centuries afterwards.
