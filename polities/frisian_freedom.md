description: >-
  The Frisian freedom was the absence of feudal lordship in the Frisian lands along the North
  Sea coast, where farming communities governed themselves without counts or dukes. This
  record follows the Frisian lands west of the Lauwers, with Leeuwarden as its point, from
  1250 to 1498. Each district was run by elected judges and later by powerful chieftains
  called haedlingen, and the districts met in leagues such as the assembly at the Upstalsboom.
  The counts of Holland repeatedly tried to subdue Friesland, but defeats such as the Battle
  of Warns in 1345 kept them out. From the fourteenth century feuds between the Schieringers
  and Vetkopers divided the land, and in 1498 Maximilian I made Albert of Saxony its governor.
