description: >-
  After Tibetan power in the western Himalaya collapsed in the 840s, the valley of Gilgit, high
  in the Karakoram, was governed again by its own rulers, the ras of Gilgit. The best known line
  is the Trakhan dynasty, which local tradition traces to a prince who married into the old
  Buddhist ruling house and which took its name from a later ruler called Trakhan. Under the
  Trakhans, who became Muslim by the fourteenth century, the Shina and Burusho peoples of the
  region converted to Islam. The rulers of Gilgit fought and intermarried with the neighbouring
  houses of Hunza, Nagar, Yasin and Chitral. In the 1830s and 1840s civil war and invasions from
  Yasin weakened the kingdom, and the Sikh governors of Kashmir took Gilgit in 1842.
