description: >-
  The Kingdom of Kinda brought tribes of central and northern Arabia under Hujrid kings
  during roughly 450 to 550. Its rulers belonged to the South Arabian Kinda tribe and gained
  support from the Himyarite kingdom. They sought to mediate disputes within the Ma'add
  confederation rather than govern a settled territory through a uniform administration.
  Urban centres, represented here by Qaryat al-Faw, provided royal bases within this largely
  tribal realm. King al-Harith appears in contemporary Byzantine accounts, reflecting
  connections beyond Arabia. After his death, royal sons governed different tribal groupings
  and became increasingly caught in local feuds. Repeated rebellions and killings weakened
  their authority, and the ruling family eventually abandoned its northern kingdom. The
  chronology and relationships between branches remain uncertain.
