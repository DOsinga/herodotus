description: >-
  Katanga was a short lived breakaway state in the mineral rich southeast of the newly independent Congo, lasting from 1960 to 1963. Moïse Tshombe headed its government from Élisabethville, now Lubumbashi, after declaring separation during the Congo Crisis. Copper revenues and support from Belgian mining interests helped sustain the administration. Belgian advisers and foreign mercenaries played important roles in its armed forces and civil service. The secession faced resistance within Katanga as well as opposition from the central Congolese government, and it never gained broad diplomatic recognition. United Nations forces initially intervened as peacekeepers but eventually fought to end the breakaway government. Military operations forced Tshombe to abandon secession in January 1963, returning the territory to Congo after violent conflict.
