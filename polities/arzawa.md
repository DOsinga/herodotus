description: >-
  Arzawa was a Bronze Age kingdom in western Anatolia, recorded here from about 1650 to 1320 BCE.
  Its capital, Apasa, is generally identified with the later site of Ephesus in Turkey. Kings
  governed the main kingdom, sometimes leading a loose alignment of neighbouring states also
  called the Arzawa lands. Hittite texts reveal alternating conflict, submission and alliance,
  rather than uninterrupted regional unity. During Hittite weakness in the fourteenth century,
  Arzawa expanded and exchanged diplomatic letters with Egypt's Amenhotep III, who treated its
  ruler as a royal peer. Connections with the Aegean world also shaped its politics and material
  culture. Mursili II defeated its last ruling house, captured Apasa and redistributed its
  territory among kingdoms bound by restrictive Hittite vassal treaties.
