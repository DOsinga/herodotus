description: >-
  The Khvalynsk culture occupied parts of the middle and lower Volga region during the Copper Age,
  approximately 5000 to 3800 BCE in this record. Archaeologists identify it through cemeteries,
  pottery, stone tools and metal ornaments, with its name taken from a burial site near Khvalynsk in
  Russia. Communities kept cattle, sheep and goats; evidence concerning the early management of horses
  remains debated. Burials often included ochre and animal remains, while differences in grave
  offerings suggest unequal access to wealth. These differences do not establish a known monarchy or
  state, and the organization of leadership remains uncertain. Early and late phases connect Khvalynsk
  with neighboring steppe traditions. Its ending is not precisely fixed, with published estimates
  extending into the fourth millennium.
