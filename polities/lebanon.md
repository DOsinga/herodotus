  Lebanon is a republic on the eastern Mediterranean coast, between Syria and Israel, with its
  capital at Beirut. It became independent in 1943 after French mandate rule, although French
  troops departed later. Its parliamentary system distributes senior offices and representation
  among religious communities, reflecting the National Pact and later constitutional arrangements.
  Beirut developed into a commercial and cultural centre, while political tensions increased with
  regional conflicts and Palestinian displacement. Civil war from 1975 to 1990 devastated the
  country and drew in neighbouring powers. Syrian troops remained until 2005, and Israeli
  occupations affected the south. Lebanon continued as a state through these upheavals. Financial
  collapse and the 2020 Beirut port explosion further strained its institutions, economy and
  population in recent decades.
