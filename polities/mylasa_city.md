description: >-
  Mylasa was a Carian city in the hills of southwestern Anatolia, on the site of modern Milas.
  It was the first capital of the Hecatomnid rulers of Caria before Mausolus moved to
  Halicarnassus, and its territory took in the great sanctuary of Zeus at Labraunda. After
  Alexander it passed among his successors, and Rhodes extended its power over much of Caria
  after the Treaty of Apamea in 188 BCE. In 167 BCE Rome, angry at Rhodes, declared Caria free,
  and Mylasa governed itself again. It pushed against its neighbours Euromus and Alabanda in
  these years and fought the Rhodians. This record covers that free city until the Romans
  organised the province of Asia. Mylasa later prospered under Rome, and Labienus sacked it in
  40 BCE.
