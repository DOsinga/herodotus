description: >-
  The Qasimid State was a Yemeni imamate founded in 1597 and recorded here until 1849. Al-Mansur
  al-Qasim proclaimed himself imam in the northern highlands and organised resistance to Ottoman
  rule. His successors captured Sanaa in 1628 and expelled the remaining Ottoman forces by 1636.
  Zaydi religious leadership, family connections, and alliances with tribal groups supported
  government, although power depended on maintaining those relationships. Expansion under
  al-Mutawakkil Ismail brought much of Yemen under the imamate, while coffee exports through
  Mocha linked it with overseas trade. Later disputes within the ruling family and regional
  secessions reduced its reach, notably with Lahej's separation. The return of Ottoman forces in
  1849 marks the endpoint of this record, after a long period of political fragmentation.
