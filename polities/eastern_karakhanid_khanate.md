description: >-
  The Eastern Kara-Khanid Khanate governed Kashgar, Balasagun, and neighbouring Central Asian lands after the older khanate divided around 1041. Its Muslim Turkic rulers retained titles and traditions rooted in tribal kingship, while governing towns connected by caravan routes. The eastern branch held the dynasty's original homeland, distinct from the western court in Transoxiana. Yusuf Khass Hajib composed his advice book, Kutadgu Bilig, in this setting, combining Turkic writing with Islamic court culture. Kara-Khitai expansion in the twelfth century reduced the khans to tributary rulers, but their local government survived. Kashgar became their later centre after Balasagun was lost. The last khan died during a Kashgar rebellion in 1211; Kuchlug's subsequent suppression of the rebels did not restore this royal government.
