description: >-
  The Kingdom of Kerma developed along the Nile in present Sudan, approximately between
  2500 and 1500 BCE. Its capital near the Third Cataract became a substantial urban
  centre with monumental mudbrick buildings, workshops, and cemeteries. Large royal
  burial mounds and wealthy grave goods indicate powerful rulers and a sharply ranked
  society, although their names and institutions are only partly known. Farming, cattle
  keeping, and exchange along the Nile supported the kingdom, which connected Egypt
  with resources and communities farther south. Kerma sometimes traded with Egypt and
  sometimes challenged it militarily, including during the Hyksos period. Egyptian
  campaigns under Thutmose I destroyed the kingdom's independent power around 1500 BCE.
  Its cultural legacy survived conquest, though links to later Kush remain debated.
