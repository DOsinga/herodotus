description: >-
  Caral–Supe civilization developed in the dry river valleys of north central Peru,
  approximately between 3000 and 1800 BCE. Also called Norte Chico, it included
  numerous coastal and inland settlements, with Caral among its largest known
  centers. People built platform mounds, ceremonial spaces and sunken circular
  plazas before pottery became common in the region. Irrigated farming supported
  cotton production, while fish and shellfish linked inland communities with coastal
  settlements. Cotton supplied material for textiles and fishing nets. Large
  building projects suggest organized labor and unequal access to resources, but the
  nature of government remains unknown. Archaeologists debate how farming, maritime
  food and religious activity shaped this society. Its decline accompanied the
  emergence of other centers, rather than a documented political conquest.
