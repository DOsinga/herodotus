description: >-
  The Ahom kingdom governed much of the Brahmaputra valley in modern Assam from 1228
  until Burmese conquest ended its independent rule in 1822. Sukaphaa, a Tai prince,
  founded a small settlement based on wet rice farming that gradually expanded and
  incorporated neighbouring peoples. Kings ruled with councillors and officials, while
  the paik system organised labour and military service from ordinary households.
  Garhgaon was one of several royal centres. The court preserved historical records
  known as buranjis and increasingly adopted Assamese language and Hindu traditions. Its
  armies resisted repeated Mughal attempts to conquer the valley. The Moamoria rebellion
  weakened central authority before successive Burmese invasions. Chandrakanta Singha's
  final defeat in 1822 ended native control, although royal titles and claimants
  survived longer.
