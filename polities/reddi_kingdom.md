description: >-
  The Reddi Kingdom ruled coastal and central Andhra in southern India from 1325 to 1424. Prolaya
  Vema Reddi founded it after the Delhi Sultanate destroyed the Kakatiya kingdom, joining the
  wider recovery of local Telugu rule. Its hereditary kings governed from Addanki and then the
  hill fortress of Kondavidu, while a related court developed at Rajahmundry. Agriculture
  supported royal revenue, and the port of Motupalli connected merchants with overseas trade. The
  rulers endowed Hindu temples, supported Brahmin settlements, and encouraged Telugu and Sanskrit
  writing, including poets such as Errana and Srinatha. Rivalry with neighbouring powers and
  divisions within the ruling family weakened the realm. Vijayanagara took Kondavidu in 1424; the
  Rajahmundry branch, recorded separately, survived until 1448.
