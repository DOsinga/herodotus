description: >-
  The Crown of Aragon brought Aragon and Barcelona's territories under one ruler in 1162. It
  became a Mediterranean monarchy whose lands retained separate laws, assemblies, and
  political traditions. Zaragoza was an important royal centre, but the crown had no
  permanent capital. Kings expanded into Valencia and Majorca, then gained possessions
  including Sicily and Sardinia. Maritime trade connected its ports, while conquest and
  dynastic claims drew it into conflicts across the western Mediterranean. Royal government
  depended on negotiation with the estates of its constituent lands rather than treating
  them as one uniform kingdom. In 1479, Ferdinand inherited Aragon while already married to
  Isabella of Castile. This joined their crowns within the Spanish monarchy, although the
  Aragonese lands kept distinct institutions afterward.
