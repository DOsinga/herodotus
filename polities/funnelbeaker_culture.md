description: >-
  The Funnelbeaker culture comprised Neolithic farming communities across northern and
  central Europe, including Jutland and southern Scandinavia, roughly between 4300 and
  2800 BCE. Archaeologists named it after pottery with flaring, funnel-shaped rims. It was
  not one state, and no kings or common government are known. Settlements, cereal remains,
  livestock bones, polished stone axes, and burial monuments show the spread of farming
  into regions previously occupied mainly by hunter-gatherers. Its people built many of
  northern Europe's dolmens and passage graves, requiring shared labour and sustained
  local cooperation. Regional groups differed in their pottery and burial customs while
  exchanging materials and ideas. Later Globular Amphora and Corded Ware traditions
  replaced different groups, though older monuments continued to be reused by succeeding
  communities.
