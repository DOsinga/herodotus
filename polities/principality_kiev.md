description: >-
  The Principality of Kiev was the reduced domain around Kyiv after the wider Rus realm fragmented in 1132. Its grand princes belonged to the Rurikid dynasty, but possession of the throne no longer meant effective government over every Rus principality. Rival branches repeatedly competed for the city, while other centers pursued their own affairs. Kyiv retained important religious institutions and commercial connections along the Dnieper. In 1169 an army associated with Andrey Bogolyubsky of Vladimir sacked it, demonstrating the weakening of its former supremacy. This record ends in 1239, when Daniel of Galicia took Kyiv and installed his own governor, Dmytro. Mongol forces under Batu captured the city the following year, but its separately governing princely court had already ended.
