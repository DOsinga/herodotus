description: >-
  The County of Auxerre was a feudal county in northern Burgundy, centred on the city of
  Auxerre on the Yonne and recorded here from 1002 to 1370. Landry of Monceau took the county
  around 1002 during the struggle over the Duchy of Burgundy, and his descendants, the counts
  of Nevers, held Auxerre together with Nevers and Tonnerre for two centuries. Through
  heiresses it passed to Peter of Courtenay, later Latin emperor of Constantinople, and to the
  houses of Donzy, Châtillon, Bourbon and Burgundy, and in the later thirteenth century it
  became a separate county under a branch of the house of Chalon. The bishops of Auxerre held
  much of the city. Count John IV sold the county to Charles V of France in 1370.
