  Tunisia is a North African state on the Mediterranean, independent from French protection since
  1956. Its capital, Tunis, links the country's coastal population centres with an interior
  extending toward the Sahara. Independence initially preserved the monarchy, but a republic
  replaced it in 1957 under Habib Bourguiba. His government expanded education and introduced
  major changes to family law, while political power remained concentrated in the ruling party.
  Zine El Abidine Ben Ali took power in 1987 and governed until mass protests forced his
  departure in 2011. That revolution helped launch the wider Arab Spring and brought competitive
  elections and a new constitution. Presidential measures beginning in 2021 reversed much of that
  parliamentary opening. The country continues under a strongly presidential republic.
