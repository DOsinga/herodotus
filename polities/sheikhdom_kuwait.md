description: >-
  The Sheikhdom of Kuwait was the self-governing port town at the head of the Persian Gulf
  ruled by the Al Sabah family from about 1752 until the British protection agreement of
  1899. Clans of the Bani Utbah had settled the bay early in the eighteenth century, and
  around 1752 they chose Sabah I as their ruler, beginning the dynasty that still reigns.
  The town lived from pearl diving, shipping to India and East Africa, and the desert
  caravans to Aleppo and Baghdad. Its rulers
  kept good relations with the Ottoman governors of Basra, and in 1871 Abdullah II accepted
  an Ottoman title, but the sheikhs governed the town themselves. Mubarak the Great seized
  power in 1896 and in 1899 signed an agreement handing Kuwait's foreign relations to
  Britain.
