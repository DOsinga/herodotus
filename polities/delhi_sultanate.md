description: >-
  The Delhi Sultanate was a Muslim monarchy that ruled much of northern India between 1206
  and 1526, sometimes extending deep into the peninsula. It emerged from the Indian
  conquests of the Ghurids, when their former commander Qutb al-Din Aibak established his
  own independent rule. Five successive ruling houses, the Mamluks, Khaljis, Tughlaqs,
  Sayyids and Lodis, shared a political centre usually based at Delhi. Sultans relied on
  armies, revenue officials and provincial governors, using Persian in court and
  administration. They resisted Mongol attacks and encouraged forms of Indo Islamic
  architecture and cultural exchange. Expansion under the Khaljis and Tughlaqs was followed
  by regional breakaways and Timur's destructive raid. Babur defeated Ibrahim Lodi at
  Panipat in 1526, establishing Mughal rule in India.
