description: >-
  Chu was a powerful state in the Yangtze basin of ancient China, traditionally founded
  around 1030 BCE and conquered by Qin in 223 BCE. Its hereditary rulers began with a
  Zhou title but governed beyond the Zhou kings' effective reach, later claiming
  kingship themselves. From its heartland around present Hubei, Chu absorbed
  neighbouring states and spread south and east. Ying was its principal capital until
  Qin captured it in 278 BCE; the court eventually moved to Shouchun. Chu competed with
  Jin, Wu, and other major powers, combining military expansion with a distinctive
  regional culture visible in lacquerware, bronzes, and tombs. Administrative reforms
  briefly strengthened royal power, but successive Qin campaigns finally defeated its
  armies and captured its last ruling king.
