  Abkhazia is a disputed republic on the eastern Black Sea coast, with its government
  based in Sukhumi. This record begins in 1993, after separatist forces prevailed in the
  war against Georgia. The conflict caused destruction and the displacement of much of
  the Georgian population, leaving a deeply contested political settlement. Abkhazia has
  its own president, legislature, courts, and armed forces, but most countries continue
  to regard its territory as part of Georgia. Russian military, financial, and
  diplomatic support has been central to its survival. Russia recognised its
  independence after the 2008 war, while only a small number of other states followed.
  Tourism and agriculture remain important economic activities. Its institutions
  continue to govern amid unresolved disputes over recognition and security.
