description: >-
  Mycenae was a major Bronze Age palace kingdom in the northeastern Peloponnese of Greece,
  represented here from approximately 1600 to 1100 BCE. Its wealthy royal shaft graves, monumental
  tombs, and fortified citadel reveal a powerful ruling elite. The palace stood above the
  surrounding countryside, with the Lion Gate and massive stone walls advertising its authority.
  Linear B records place Mycenae within a Greek-speaking palatial world whose rulers organized
  labor, resources, religious offerings, and craft production. Its exact territorial reach and
  relationship with neighboring kingdoms remain debated. Trade connected the settlement with the
  wider eastern Mediterranean. Fires and destruction around the end of the thirteenth century BCE
  disrupted palace administration, though occupation continued afterward. Later Greek tradition
  associated Mycenae with Agamemnon.
