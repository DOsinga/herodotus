description: >-
  Takalik Abaj was a Maya political centre on Guatemala's Pacific slope,
  represented here from approximately 400 BCE to 1000 CE. Earlier settlement
  produced Olmec style sculpture, but these objects do not establish that
  outsiders governed the later kingdom. Early inscriptions and rich elite tombs
  indicate the emergence of royal authority as Maya traditions became prominent.
  Its position along trade routes linked the coast with the highlands and
  supplied goods such as obsidian. Builders arranged temples, plazas, and
  monuments across terraces, with channels managing water through the
  settlement. Political disruption and changing external connections affected
  the Early Classic city, followed by a Late Classic revival. The substantial
  governing centre eventually declined; the endpoint is an estimate rather than
  a documented conquest.
