description: >-
  The Helmand culture developed in the river valleys and desert margins of eastern Iran
  and southern Afghanistan. This record uses an estimated span from 3200 to 2100 BCE,
  while archaeological chronologies differ between sites. Shahr-e Sukhteh and Mundigak are
  its best known centres, linked by similar pottery, building methods, seals and craft
  production. Large settlements, monumental buildings and specialised workshops point to
  complex societies supported by agriculture and carefully managed water supplies. Painted
  vessels often carry geometric patterns alongside plants and animals. Finds also show
  contacts with regions farther west and with communities toward the Indus. These
  connections do not establish a single kingdom or known ruling dynasty. The record
  represents an archaeological culture whose political organisation remains uncertain
  today.
