description: >-
  The Ahmadnagar Sultanate ruled the western Deccan in India from 1490 to 1636 under the
  Nizam Shahi dynasty. Ahmad Nizam Shah established his own government as Bahmani
  authority weakened, initially ruling from Junnar before founding Ahmadnagar. The
  monarchy drew on Persian court culture, Deccani military networks, and a predominantly
  Marathi-speaking countryside. It competed and allied with neighbouring sultanates,
  joining the coalition that defeated Vijayanagara at Talikota in 1565 and later annexing
  Berar. Mughal expansion brought prolonged warfare. Chand Bibi defended Ahmadnagar, while
  Malik Ambar rebuilt resistance after the capital fell, using mobile armies and revised
  revenue arrangements. The state survived through shifting capitals and powerful
  ministers until Mughal conquest in 1636 ended its separate rule over the northern Deccan
  region.
