description: >-
  The Astrakhan Khanate ruled the lower Volga region from 1466 to 1556 as one of the successor
  states of the Golden Horde. Its capital, Xacitarxan, stood near the river's approach to the
  Caspian Sea, making the kingdom a valuable junction for trade between Russia, Central Asia,
  Persia and the Caucasus. Sunni Muslim khans governed alongside a hierarchy of nobles, with
  Tatar townspeople and Nogai pastoral communities among their subjects. The khanate was
  vulnerable to stronger neighbours, especially Crimea and the expanding Muscovite state, whose
  rival interventions shaped royal succession. Ivan IV's forces installed a compliant ruler in
  1554. When that arrangement failed, Russia annexed Astrakhan in 1556, securing the Volga's
  southern outlet and ending the khanate's separate political authority there.
