description: >-
  The Duchy of Burgundy was a principality in east-central France, west of the Saône, with Dijon
  as its main seat. Richard the Justiciar built it up in the late ninth century and was first
  called duke in 918. His successors and then brothers of Hugh Capet ruled it until 1004, when
  King Robert II took it after a war of succession. In 1032 Robert's son Robert received it,
  founding a Capetian line of dukes that governed the duchy as their own for more than three
  centuries. When that line died out in 1361 the duchy passed to King John II, who granted it in
  1363 to his son Philip the Bold. Philip's inheritance of Flanders in 1384 created the wider
  Burgundian state.
