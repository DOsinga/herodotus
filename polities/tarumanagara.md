description: >-
  Tarumanagara was an early kingdom in western Java, around modern Jakarta, Bekasi, and Bogor. Its traditional span, approximately 358 to 669, is less secure than the fifth century inscriptions of King Purnawarman. These Sanskrit texts provide direct evidence of royal authority and Hindu religious ideas, including comparisons between the king and Vishnu. The Tugu inscription describes a canal project and gifts to Brahmins, linking kingship with water management, cultivation, and ritual. Coastal connections placed the kingdom near routes joining Java, Sumatra, India, and China. Much of its broader dynastic story comes from later chronicles rather than contemporary records. Its supposed division into Sunda and Galuh in 669 should therefore be understood as a traditional reconstruction, not a firmly documented event.
