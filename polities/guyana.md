  Guyana is a state on South America's northeastern Atlantic coast, independent from Britain
  since 1966, with Georgetown as its capital. Formerly British Guiana, it inherited plantation
  communities shaped by African slavery, Indian indentured labour, and older Indigenous
  populations. It initially retained the British monarch, becoming a republic in 1970 while
  remaining in the Commonwealth. Its government combines an executive presidency with a National
  Assembly, and politics have long reflected competition between major parties with different
  ethnic support bases. Most people live along the coastal plain, while forests and rivers
  dominate the interior. Sugar, rice, bauxite, and gold were important exports before offshore
  oil transformed the economy. Guyana maintains strong Caribbean connections, and Venezuela's
  claim to Essequibo has complicated its relations.
