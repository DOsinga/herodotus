description: >-
  The Bavand Kingdom governed parts of Tabaristan, now Mazandaran in northern
  Iran, through several periods between an estimated foundation in 665 and its
  final overthrow in 1349. Its early traditions claimed Sasanian ancestry, but
  documentation begins with Sharwin in the eighth century. Rulers used the title
  ispahbadh and initially held mountain lands around Perim, resisting outside
  intervention while sometimes acknowledging stronger regional powers. The first
  government ended under Qabus in 1006. A restored kingdom based at Sari emerged
  in 1073 and survived until Khwarezmian conquest in 1210. Another court
  returned at Amol in 1238 under Mongol overlordship. Religious allegiance
  changed from Zoroastrianism to Islam across its history. The last Bavand ruler
  was killed in the takeover of 1349 by Afrasiyab.
