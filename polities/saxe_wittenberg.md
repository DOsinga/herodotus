description: >-
  Saxe-Wittenberg was a medieval German duchy centered on Wittenberg along the Elbe.
  This record follows the Ascanian Duchy of Saxony from 1250, including its
  Wittenberg branch after the final partition from Saxe-Lauenburg in 1296. The dukes
  ruled scattered estates rather than the whole territory of the earlier Saxon stem
  duchy. Their greatest political advantage was the disputed right to participate in
  choosing the German king. The Golden Bull of 1356 confirmed that electoral vote
  for the Wittenberg line, increasing its standing among imperial princes. Dynastic
  marriages and royal grants helped expand its lands. The Ascanian line died out in
  1422. In 1423 the emperor granted its territory and electoral dignity to the
  Wettin ruler of Meissen, creating the Saxon electorate.
