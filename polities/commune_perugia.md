description: >-
  The Commune of Perugia was the self-governing city state of Perugia in Umbria, from the
  twelfth century until Pope Paul III's victory in the Salt War of 1540. Its consuls are
  recorded from 1139, and the commune conquered most of Umbria's lesser towns, including
  Assisi, Gubbio and Città di Castello for a time. Perugia was Guelph and loyal to the
  popes in name while governing itself; it founded its university in 1308 and built the
  Palazzo dei Priori. After Gian Galeazzo Visconti and King Ladislaus of Naples held it,
  the condottiero Braccio da Montone ruled it from 1416 to 1424. The Baglioni then
  dominated the city under loose papal sovereignty, until Paul III crushed its revolt
  against the salt tax.
