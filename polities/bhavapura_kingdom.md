description: >-
  Bhavapura was a royal centre in early medieval Cambodia, represented here as a separately governed kingdom from about 700 to
  944. Its history belongs to the fragmented political landscape conventionally called Chenla, rather than a single continuously united Cambodian state.
  Kings, elite families and religious foundations exercised power through local centres whose boundaries are difficult to reconstruct. The name recalls
  Bhavavarman, an earlier ruler associated with Bhavapura, but the location of the city remains disputed. Sanskrit and Khmer inscriptions preserve
  much of the evidence for these courts and their patronage of Hindu temples. Bhavapura retained its own royal identity alongside
  the emerging Angkor kingdom. Rajendravarman's accession at Angkor eventually brought its lands into that kingdom, with incorporation estimated around 944.
