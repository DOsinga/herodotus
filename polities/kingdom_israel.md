description: >-
  The northern Kingdom of Israel occupied the northern highlands of the southern Levant,
  with Samaria becoming its royal capital. Its beginning around 930 BCE follows the
  conventional account of division after Solomon, although the earlier united monarchy's
  extent remains disputed. Kings ruled through changing dynasties, including the houses of
  Omri and Jehu. Archaeological remains and Assyrian inscriptions complement biblical
  accounts of its politics and religion. Agriculture, fortified towns, and regional trade
  supported a kingdom that competed with Judah and Aram Damascus. Omri's dynasty established
  a durable royal center at Samaria. Assyrian expansion gradually reduced its territory and
  independence. Conquest and deportations associated with Shalmaneser V and Sargon II ended
  the kingdom by 720 BCE, bringing its lands under Assyrian administration.
