description: >-
  Later Liang was a kingdom in the Hexi Corridor of northwestern China, ruled from Guzang in
  present day Gansu between 386 and 403. Lü Guang established its government after returning
  from a Former Qin expedition to Central Asia and learning of that empire's collapse. The
  ruling family was associated with the Di people, and its kings eventually claimed the
  title Heavenly King. Control of the corridor connected the realm with routes between China
  and western regions. The captured Buddhist scholar Kumārajīva remained there during this
  period. Succession conflicts and breakaway movements weakened Lü Guang's descendants.
  Northern Liang and Southern Liang reduced their territory, leaving the capital
  increasingly isolated. In 403 the last ruler surrendered to Later Qin, ending the kingdom.
