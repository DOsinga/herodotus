description: >-
  The Imamate of Oman was the elected Ibadi state of the Omani interior in the twentieth
  century, with its capital at Nizwa. It began with the revolt of 1913, when Salim ibn Rashid
  al-Kharusi was chosen imam and the interior tribes rose against the British-backed Sultan
  of Muscat. The Treaty of Seeb in 1920 ended the fighting and in practice left the interior
  to the imam while the sultan kept the coast. The imam governed through walis in the towns
  and with the backing of tribal sheikhs. Oil prospecting in the interior revived the
  dispute, and after Ghalib al-Hinai became imam in 1954 the sultan's forces, with British
  support, took Nizwa in December 1955. The imam's supporters fought on in the Jebel Akhdar
  War until 1959.
