description: >-
  The Zaydi Imamate was the state of the Zaydi Shia imams in the northern highlands of Yemen.
  It began when al-Hadi ila'l-Haqq Yahya, a descendant of the Prophet born in Medina, came to
  Saada in 897 at the invitation of the local tribes. The office was not hereditary: any
  learned descendant of Hasan or Husayn could proclaim a call and had to prove himself as
  scholar and leader, so rival claimants were common. Al-Hadi and his successors never built a
  lasting administration, and from 1066 to 1138 there was no imam while the Sulayhids dominated
  Yemen. The imamate revived under al-Mutawakkil Ahmad, held its own against the Rasulids and
  Tahirids, and was often split between contenders after 1436. The Ottomans took Sanaa in 1547.
