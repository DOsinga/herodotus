description: >-
  Gutian rule in Sumer belongs to the unsettled decades after the Akkadian Empire weakened, represented
  here by approximately 2154 to 2119 BCE. Kings identified as Gutian claimed authority over parts
  of Mesopotamia, now Iraq, while local cities also regained room to govern themselves. Their territory,
  royal seat, and succession remain poorly understood; the marker at Akkad is only approximate. Contemporary
  inscriptions preserve a few rulers, but most names come from later versions of the Sumerian
  King List, whose accounts conflict. Later literature blamed the Gutians for disorder, a picture that
  cannot simply be read as fact. Their last ruler, Tirigan, was defeated by Utu-hengal of
  Uruk, who claimed to restore Sumerian kingship before the rise of Ur's powerful third dynasty.
