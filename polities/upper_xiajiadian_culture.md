description: >-
  The Upper Xiajiadian culture was a Bronze Age archaeological tradition in
  northeastern China, approximately 1000 to 600 BCE. Finds cluster around southeastern
  Inner Mongolia and western Liaoning, with Chifeng representing its type site region.
  Communities combined farming with increasingly important livestock raising,
  especially sheep and goats, and horses became prominent. Compared with the earlier
  Lower Xiajiadian settlements, occupation was more dispersed and permanent building
  received less emphasis. Bronze daggers, knives, weapons, helmets, and animal
  ornaments show connections with steppe traditions, while imported or imitated vessels
  reveal contact with Zhou China. Rich graves suggest social differences, but the
  evidence does not identify one kingdom or named rulers. Its association with the
  Donghu remains an interpretation linking archaeology with later historical accounts.
