  Nigeria became independent from Britain in 1960, uniting a large, diverse population
  around a federal government. It initially retained the monarch as ceremonial head of
  state, then became a republic in 1963. Regional rivalry and military coups led to civil
  war when Biafra attempted to secede in 1967. Federal victory in 1970 preserved the
  country, but military governments dominated much of its subsequent history. Civilian
  rule returned in 1999 under a presidential constitution. Abuja replaced Lagos as the
  capital in 1991, while Lagos remained a major commercial centre. Oil production became
  central to exports and public revenue, bringing wealth alongside conflict and
  environmental damage in the Niger Delta. Nigeria continues as a federal republic and a
  major West African power.
