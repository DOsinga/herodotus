description: >-
  Ura-Tyube, today Istaravshan in northern Tajikistan, lies in the foothills of the Turkestan
  range between the Ferghana valley and the Zarafshan, on the road from Khujand to Samarkand. In
  the late eighteenth and nineteenth centuries the town and its district were governed by their
  own begs, Uzbek chiefs who exploited their position between the rival khanates of Bukhara and
  Kokand. Both khanates claimed the town and besieged it repeatedly, and Kokand held it for some
  years at a time, but the begs regained control whenever the larger powers turned elsewhere. The
  town was a centre of crafts and of Persian and Chagatai learning, home to the poet and
  historian Dilshad Barna. Russian troops stormed Ura-Tyube in 1866 during their conquest of
  Central Asia.
