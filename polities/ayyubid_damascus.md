description: >-
  The Ayyubid Emirate of Damascus was a separately governed Syrian monarchy within the wider
  family of states founded by Saladin. This record covers its autonomous periods after his
  death in 1193, interrupted when rulers in Egypt took direct control. Damascus was an urban
  and agricultural centre whose possession shaped Ayyubid rivalry across Syria and Palestine.
  Its rulers included al-Afdal, al-Muazzam, al-Ashraf and as-Salih Ismail, who governed with
  military households and officials. Family alliances repeatedly changed as princes competed
  for lands and seniority. Their diplomacy with Crusader rulers reflected these struggles
  rather than a united policy. Ismail allied with the Crusaders against Egypt, but his
  coalition lost at La Forbie in 1244. Egyptian forces captured Damascus in 1245, ending this
  emirate.
