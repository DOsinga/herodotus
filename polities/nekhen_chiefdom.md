description: >-
  Nekhen, later called Hierakonpolis, was a predynastic centre in Upper Egypt. This record
  represents its chiefdom from approximately 3500 to 3150 BCE, before Egyptian unification.
  Archaeology reveals a large settlement, specialised production, ceremonial buildings, rich
  graves, and animal burials that point to powerful local elites. The painted tomb and
  decorated objects show images that later became familiar in Egyptian royal art. Brewing
  installations demonstrate organised production, while exotic animals and valuable
  materials suggest elite access to distant resources. Its leaders cannot be reconstructed
  as a secure sequence of named kings. Later tradition associated Nekhen with Upper Egyptian
  kingship and the god Horus. A reconstruction places its absorption by Thinis around 3150
  BCE, but the order and timing remain highly uncertain.
