description: >-
  The Almohad Caliphate ruled much of North Africa and Muslim Iberia between 1130 and 1269.
  It grew from Ibn Tumart's religious movement among the Masmuda Berbers of the Atlas
  Mountains. His successor Abd al-Mumin transformed the movement into an expanding monarchy,
  claimed the caliphate and captured Marrakesh from the Almoravids in 1147. Almohad rulers
  united much of the Maghreb and crossed into al-Andalus, governing through royal officials
  and armies. Their court supported major monumental buildings and thinkers, including Ibn
  Rushd, while its religious policies could be harsh toward dissenters and minorities. Defeat
  at Las Navas de Tolosa in 1212 weakened their Iberian position. Provincial breakaways and
  succession struggles followed, and the Marinids captured Marrakesh in 1269, ending the
  remaining caliphate.
