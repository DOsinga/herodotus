  Burkina Faso is a landlocked West African state that became independent from France as Upper
  Volta in 1960. Its capital, Ouagadougou, lies in the former heartland of the Mossi kingdoms.
  The republic has alternated between civilian presidents and military governments rather than
  following a steady constitutional path. Thomas Sankara took power in 1983, promoted public
  health, literacy and land reform, and renamed the country in 1984. His assassination brought
  Blaise Compaoré to power until protests forced him out in 2014. Agriculture, especially
  cotton, and gold mining are important to its economy. Armed insurgency across the wider
  Sahel region and further coups have shaped its recent history, while the same independent
  country has continued through these changes of leadership and government.
