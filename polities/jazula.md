description: >-
  The Jazula were a confederation of Berber tribes in the Sous valley and the Anti-Atlas of
  southern Morocco, recorded here as a chiefdom from 1465 to 1510. After the Marinid state
  collapsed in 1465, the Wattasids of Fez did not reach the Sous, and its tribes, towns and
  religious lodges governed themselves. The Sufi teaching of al-Jazuli, who died in 1465,
  spread among them and gave the lodges great influence. The Portuguese built a fort at Agadir
  in 1505, and the tribes called for a leader in the holy war against them. In 1510 they
  recognised Muhammad al-Qa'im, a sharif settled at Tidsi near Taroudant, whose family founded
  the Saadi dynasty with the Jazula and Arab tribes of the Sous as its first base.
