description: >-
  The Saadi Sultanate governed Morocco between 1510 and 1659, growing from a movement in the southern Sous valley against Portuguese
  coastal occupation. Its rulers claimed descent from Muhammad, drawing religious authority from their sharifian ancestry. They established their court at
  Marrakesh, displaced the Wattasids, and resisted Ottoman attempts to dominate Morocco. Their victory over a Portuguese invasion at Ksar el
  Kebir in 1578 brought Ahmad al Mansur to power. He sponsored impressive buildings, developed overseas diplomacy, and sent an army
  across the Sahara that defeated Songhai in 1591. His death triggered rival claims and prolonged civil war. Although the realm
  was reunited, competing regional political powers undermined it. The assassination of the last Saadi sultan in 1659 ended the dynasty.
