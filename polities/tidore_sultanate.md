description: >-
  Tidore was a sultanate on a small volcanic island in the northern Moluccas, facing its rival Ternate
  across a narrow strait. Local genealogies place its first king in 1081, but the court is only
  clearly attested from the fifteenth century, and Islam became the state religion under Sultan
  Jamaluddin late in that century. Tidore grew rich on cloves and claimed lordship over Halmahera, the
  Raja Ampat islands and parts of western New Guinea. Its sultans balanced the European powers against
  Ternate, siding first with the Portuguese and then with Spain, which kept a fort on the island until
  1662. After the Spanish left, Tidore was drawn into the Dutch East India Company's system of
  treaties and lost control of its foreign relations.
