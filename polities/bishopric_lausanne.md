description: >-
  The Prince-Bishopric of Lausanne was the territory of the bishops of Lausanne on the northern
  shore of Lake Geneva. King Rudolph III of Burgundy made the bishops counts of Vaud in 1011, but
  the house of Savoy took most of Vaud in the thirteenth century, leaving the bishops the city and
  district of Lausanne, the vineyards of Lavaux and some towns in the Vaud and Fribourg
  countryside. They were recognised as imperial princes in 1270. Savoy held the advocacy of the
  see and often filled it with its own men, and the burghers of the upper and lower town
  quarrelled with the bishops over their rights. When Bern conquered Vaud in 1536, Bishop
  Sébastien de Montfalcon fled, and Bern introduced the Reformation.
