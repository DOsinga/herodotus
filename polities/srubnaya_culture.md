description: >-
  The Srubnaya culture occupied the broad Pontic Caspian steppe during the Late Bronze
  Age, approximately from 1900 to 1200 BCE. Its spread reached from the Dnieper region
  toward the Volga and the southern Urals, with the Middle Volga used here as a
  representative point. Archaeologists named it for timber frameworks built inside
  burial pits, often covered by earthen mounds. Settlements included partly sunken
  houses, while tools and animal remains show farming alongside cattle, sheep, and pig
  keeping. Horse equipment and vehicle images suggest participation in wider traditions
  of chariot use. These shared remains do not identify a single state or known rulers.
  Its origins and relationship to neighbouring cultures remain debated, and later steppe
  societies developed across its former area.
