description: >-
  The Hephthalite Empire dominated parts of Central Asia from approximately 440 to
  560. Its strongest base lay in Bactria and Tokharistan, around northern
  Afghanistan and southern Uzbekistan, with Kunduz a proposed capital. Imperial
  rulers governed a confederation containing mobile pastoral groups and settled
  urban communities. They used Bactrian in official documents and coin legends,
  while controlling important routes through Sogdiana and toward the Tarim Basin.
  Their victories over the Sasanians included the defeat and death of King Peroz in
  484, after which Persian rulers paid tribute. Their origins and early leadership
  remain uncertain. A coalition of Sasanian and Turkic forces broke the empire
  around 560. Separate Hephthalite principalities survived afterward, but no longer
  formed the imperial state of earlier decades.
