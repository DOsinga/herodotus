description: >-
  The Przeworsk culture describes Iron Age communities chiefly in central and
  southern Poland, represented here from approximately 300 BCE to 400 CE. Named
  after finds near Przeworsk, it later extended toward the Carpathians and
  neighbouring river basins. Small villages contained timber houses, sometimes
  partly sunk into the ground, while shared cemeteries linked nearby settlements.
  Farming, livestock keeping, ironworking, and trade supported these communities.
  Cremation burials often included weapons and deliberately damaged objects,
  giving archaeologists evidence of social distinctions and ritual practices. Its
  material culture combined influences from Celtic La Tène traditions,
  neighbouring Germanic communities, and contacts with the Roman world. It is
  often associated with Vandals, but shared artefacts cannot establish one ethnic
  population, unified government, or fixed political borders.
