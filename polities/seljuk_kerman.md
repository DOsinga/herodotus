description: >-
  The Kerman Seljuk Sultanate governed southeastern Iran from 1041 to 1187, with its main seat at
  Bardasir, the historical name of Kerman city. Qavurt founded this hereditary branch after
  taking territory from the Buyids. Although related to the Great Seljuk rulers, its sultans ran
  a separate regional government. Their realm included Kerman and Makran, linking inland routes
  with ports on the Indian Ocean and Persian Gulf. Long distance trade, farming and royal
  patronage helped make Kerman a prosperous regional commercial centre. Persian administrative
  culture operated alongside the dynasty's Turkic origins and Sunni Muslim identity. Later
  succession struggles weakened the state and opened it to Oghuz incursions. Malik Dinar's forces
  displaced the last Seljuk government in 1187, ending this branch's rule.
