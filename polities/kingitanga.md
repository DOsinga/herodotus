description: >-
  The Kīngitanga, or Māori King movement, arose among the iwi of the central North Island of New
  Zealand in the 1850s to stop the sale of Māori land to settlers. In 1858 the Waikato chief
  Pōtatau Te Wherowhero was crowned king at Ngāruawāhia. The movement built a council of state,
  laws, magistrates, police and a newspaper, and for some years worked as a government of its
  own alongside the colonial one. The colonial government saw it as a challenge to the Crown and
  invaded the Waikato in 1863. After defeat at Ōrākau in 1864, King Tāwhiao and his followers
  withdrew behind a boundary into the Ngāti Maniapoto lands, which became known as the King
  Country and stayed closed to settlers until Tāwhiao made peace in 1881.
