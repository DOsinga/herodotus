description: >-
  Magonsaete was a small kingdom on the western edge of Mercia, around present day Herefordshire and neighbouring parts of Shropshire. Its beginning is conventionally placed around 656, when Merewalh ruled the Western Hecani; its absorption into Mercia is estimated here at 750. Neither boundary marks a precisely documented founding or final conquest. The population included Anglian settlers and existing British communities. Kenchester, the former Roman town of Magnis, represents a proposed centre rather than a securely identified royal capital. Merewalh, Mildfrith, and Merchelm are among its known rulers, who exercised local authority under Mercian overlordship. Christianity probably spread through British missionaries, and early church organisation retained British features. By the later eighth century, separate royal government had disappeared within Mercia.
