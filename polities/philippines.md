  The Philippines is an island republic in Southeast Asia, independent from the United States since
  1946. Manila is its capital, and its territory spans the major island groups of Luzon, the Visayas,
  and Mindanao. Independence followed Spanish and American colonial rule, a Commonwealth government,
  and destructive Japanese occupation during the Second World War. The republic has a presidential
  system with a Congress and courts, although Ferdinand Marcos's martial law regime sharply curtailed
  democratic institutions. The People Power movement removed Marcos in 1986, and a new constitution
  followed in 1987. Regional inequalities, communist insurgency, and conflicts involving Muslim
  communities have shaped its politics. Maritime trade, overseas workers, and strong cultural links
  across the Pacific connect the country with the wider world.
