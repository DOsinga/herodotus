description: >-
  The Zagwe Kingdom ruled Christian highland communities in northern Ethiopia from a disputed beginning, represented here by the traditional date 1137, until 1270. Its rulers came from the Agaw population and governed from the Lasta region around Adefa, later called Lalibela. Royal authority rested on military power, dynastic connections, and support for the Ethiopian church. The kingdom is especially remembered for churches carved from living rock, associated with King Lalibela and conceived as sacred spaces for worship and pilgrimage. Written evidence is limited, and traditions disagree about the number of kings and length of their rule. Yekuno Amlak overthrew the last Zagwe ruler in 1270 and established the Solomonic monarchy. Zagwe descendants nevertheless retained local standing after losing the throne.
