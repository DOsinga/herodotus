description: >-
  Wu was a kingdom near the lower Yangtze River in eastern China during the Spring and
  Autumn period. This record begins in 585 BCE with Shoumeng, its first historically
  attested king, rather than the earlier legendary royal genealogy. Its court
  eventually settled at Gusu, associated with modern Suzhou. Initially outside the
  central Zhou cultural world, Wu became a powerful military rival of Chu through
  alliances with northern states. King Helu's forces captured Chu's capital in 506 BCE,
  and his successor Fuchai pursued wider influence. Their campaigns also brought
  repeated conflict with neighboring Yue. Fuchai defeated Yue's king Goujian but
  allowed his return home. Yue rebuilt its strength, attacked Wu during northern
  campaigns, and conquered the exhausted kingdom in 473 BCE.
