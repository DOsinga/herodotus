description: >-
  Marién was a Taíno chiefdom in the northwest of Hispaniola, covering much of present-day
  northern Haiti. At the time of contact it was ruled by the cacique Guacanagarí, whose main
  town lay near the coast east of present Cap-Haïtien. When Columbus's flagship the Santa
  María ran aground there on Christmas Eve of 1492, Guacanagarí's people helped unload it,
  and he allowed Columbus to leave thirty-nine men in a fort called La Navidad. The garrison
  was wiped out in 1493 by the forces of Caonabo of Maguana. Guacanagarí stayed loyal to the
  Spanish and fought beside them against the other caciques in 1495, which earned him the
  hostility of his neighbours. As Spanish rule spread he fled into the mountains, where he
  died.
