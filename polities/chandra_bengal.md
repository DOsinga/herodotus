description: >-
  The Chandra kingdom governed southeastern Bengal, in present Bangladesh, from
  approximately 900 to 1050. It emerged under Trailokyachandra from the political world
  of Harikela and expanded into Vanga and Samatata. These Bengali kings should not be
  confused with earlier Chandra rulers in Arakan. Srichandra established an
  administrative centre at Bikrampur and campaigned towards Kamarupa in the Brahmaputra
  valley. Royal copperplate grants provide much of the evidence for their government,
  landholding, and religious patronage. Although Buddhist, the kings also supported
  Brahmin institutions. Coastal trade connected their realm with merchants and
  communities across the Bay of Bengal. Govindachandra faced attacks from neighbouring
  powers, including a Chola expedition; the kingdom survived that raid before losing
  its separate government around the middle eleventh century.
