description: >-
  The Northern Xiongnu were a steppe kingdom centered in Mongolia from the division of the
  Xiongnu confederation in 48 CE until 91. Punu remained chanyu, or supreme ruler, after
  southern tribes chose a rival leader and later aligned with Han China. His court near
  the Orkhon River governed through tribal rulers and mobile armies. The northern kingdom
  contested influence over the Tarim Basin and raided Chinese frontiers, while facing
  attacks from the Southern Xiongnu, Wuhuan, and Xianbei. Famine and internal disorder
  weakened its position. A Xianbei victory killed its ruler in 87, and Han campaigns under
  Dou Xian delivered decisive defeats in 89 and 91. The northern chanyu fled northwest;
  remaining groups surrendered or were absorbed into the rising Xianbei.
