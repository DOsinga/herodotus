  Ireland is a parliamentary republic occupying most of the island of Ireland, with its
  capital at Dublin. This record begins with the Irish Free State in 1922, created
  after the independence war and the Anglo-Irish Treaty. Northern Ireland remained
  within the United Kingdom, while disagreement over the treaty produced a civil war in
  the new state. The constitution of 1937 established the name Ireland and an elected
  presidency; legislation taking effect in 1949 declared the republic and ended
  Commonwealth membership. Government rests on a parliament and a cabinet led by the
  Taoiseach. Neutrality, emigration, and agriculture shaped much of its earlier
  history. European integration from 1973 and later economic growth changed the
  country, which continues under the same state identity.
