description: >-
  The Tuʻi Tonga Empire describes a Tongan monarchy and sphere of influence
  that emerged around 950 and spread through parts of the Pacific. Its
  rulers held sacred authority, with Tongatapu and Muʻa providing the royal centre.
  Voyaging canoes carried warriors, goods, and tribute between islands, while marriages linked
  families across Tonga, Samoa, Fiji, and neighbouring archipelagos. Monumental gateways and royal
  tombs show the resources concentrated at the court. The extent of direct
  rule is debated: cultural exchange and political influence do not always mean
  conquest or permanent control. Internal conflict and assassinations weakened the ruling line.
  Following Takalaua's death in 1535, government passed to the Tuʻi Haʻatakalaua, while
  the Tuʻi Tonga retained a religious position within Tonga's changing chiefly order.
