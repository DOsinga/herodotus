description: >-
  The Hawaiian Kingdom united most of the Hawaiian Islands under Kamehameha I in 1795, with Kauai joining in 1810. Its monarchy initially rested on chiefly authority, then developed a constitution, legislature and written laws during the nineteenth century. Honolulu became the capital and a centre of Pacific shipping and diplomacy. Hawaiian rulers sought international recognition while managing foreign missionaries, merchants and plantation interests. Sugar production brought economic ties to the United States and recruited workers from abroad. Land reforms and disease changed Hawaiian society. A constitution imposed in 1887 restricted royal power and political participation. In 1893 opponents overthrew Queen Liliuokalani with support from American diplomatic and military representatives, replacing the monarchy with a provisional government that preceded the republic.
