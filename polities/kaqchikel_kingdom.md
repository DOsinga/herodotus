description: >-
  The Kaqchikel kingdom governed part of Guatemala's western highlands from Iximche, founded
  around 1470 after its leaders broke with their Kʼicheʼ allies. Deep ravines protected the new
  capital, whose plazas, temples, palaces and ballcourts expressed the power of its ruling clans.
  Four principal lords shared authority, with the Sotzʼil and Xahil leaders holding the strongest
  positions. Warfare with the Kʼicheʼ and internal clan struggles marked the kingdom's short
  independent history. Smallpox reached the population before Spanish troops arrived. In 1524, the
  Kaqchikel initially welcomed Pedro de Alvarado and supplied allies against neighbouring
  kingdoms. Spanish tribute demands soon provoked a break and the abandonment of Iximche.
  Organised Kaqchikel resistance continued until the lords surrendered in 1530, where this record
  ends.
