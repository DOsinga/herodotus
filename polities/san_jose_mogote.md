description: >-
  San José Mogote was a Zapotec centre in the Etla branch of Mexico's Oaxaca Valley, prominent approximately between 1500
  and 500 BCE. It grew from a farming village into a settlement with public buildings, craft specialists, and influence over
  smaller surrounding communities. Differences among houses, burials, and ceremonial spaces suggest a ranked society, represented here as a chiefdom rather
  than a fully documented state. Its inhabitants worked fertile valley land, developed irrigation, and made pottery and other goods. Monumental
  architecture and a carved captive bearing an early glyph show emerging links between warfare, ritual, and writing. Around the end
  of this period, Monte Albán became the dominant Zapotec centre. San José Mogote lost its leading position but remained inhabited
  afterwards.
