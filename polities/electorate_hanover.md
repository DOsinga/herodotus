description: >-
  The Electorate of Hanover was a northern German state created in 1692 when the ruler of
  Brunswick-Lüneburg received electoral rank. Its Welf dynasty governed a collection of
  territories whose principal seat was Hanover. From 1714 its electors also ruled Great
  Britain, beginning with George I, but the German lands retained their own government and
  institutions. A privy council in Hanover and a German chancery in London managed affairs
  across this personal union. Territorial acquisitions expanded the electorate, including
  Bremen and Verden. Its position made it vulnerable during Britain's continental wars,
  particularly against France. French forces occupied Hanover in 1803, ending the
  independent period recorded here. After occupations and changing rulers, the territory
  reappeared as the Kingdom of Hanover in 1814.
