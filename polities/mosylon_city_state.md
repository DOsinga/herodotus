description: >-
  Mosylon was a trading port on the northern Somali coast, known from Greco-Roman accounts of
  commerce around the Gulf of Aden. This record estimates its activity from about 50 to 650 CE,
  although its exact location and lifetime remain uncertain. The marker follows an identification near
  Bosaso; other sites have also been proposed. Classical writers associated Mosylon with valuable
  aromatic products, particularly goods described as cinnamon and cassia, alongside incense and other
  exports. Ships connected its market with Arabia, Egypt, the Mediterranean and Indian Ocean routes.
  Leadership presumably organized the port and its trade, but evidence does not reveal a constitution
  or sequence of rulers. Its classification as a chiefdom avoids treating those commercial references
  as proof of a kingdom.
