description: >-
  The Sulayhid State was an Ismaili Shia kingdom that ruled most of Yemen from 1047 to
  1138. Ali ibn Muhammad al-Sulayhi, a missionary of the Fatimid caliphs of Cairo, rose in
  the mountains of Haraz in 1047 and within twenty years had taken Sanaa, Zabid and Aden and
  extended his authority to Mecca. The dynasty acknowledged the Fatimids, and its reign brought
  Yemen a peace not seen since Himyarite times. After Ali's murder in 1067 his son al-Mukarram and then al-Mukarram's widow Arwa
  governed, and Arwa moved the capital to Jibla, where she ruled for half a century as
  queen. The Zaydi imams of the north were constant enemies. On Arwa's death in 1138 the
  kingdom broke up among the Zurayids, Hamdanids and other local dynasties.
