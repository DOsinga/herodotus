description: >-
  The Kingdom of Gowa developed around the southwestern coast of Sulawesi, in present Indonesia,
  from approximately 1300. Its Makassarese rulers built a strong monarchy and linked their court
  with neighboring Tallo. Somba Opu became a fortified royal and commercial center, attracting
  traders from across the archipelago and farther abroad. Conversion to Islam in the early
  seventeenth century gave the rulers the title of sultan and accompanied expansion against
  neighboring kingdoms. Gowa's open trading policy challenged Dutch attempts to monopolize
  commerce. Sultan Hasanuddin fought the Dutch East India Company and its Bugis allies in the
  Makassar War. The fall of Somba Opu in 1669 ended its independent power. A reduced royal court
  continued afterward within an increasingly Dutch dominated political order.
