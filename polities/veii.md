description: >-
  Veii was an Etruscan city state north of Rome, flourishing from the eighth century BCE until its Roman conquest in 396 BCE. It occupied a defensible plateau near routes connecting the Tiber valley with inland Etruria. Agricultural land, trade, and skilled crafts supported its wealth, while drainage channels improved the surrounding countryside. Its sanctuary at Portonaccio produced painted terracotta sculpture, including the famous figure of Apollo. Veii belonged to the wider Etruscan world but pursued its own alliances and wars. Kings appear in Roman accounts, though those narratives were written much later. Competition with Rome over territory and communications led to prolonged fighting. Camillus captured the city after a long siege, ending its independence; occupation later continued under Roman rule.
