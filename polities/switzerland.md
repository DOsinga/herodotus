  Switzerland is a country in central Europe whose modern continuity is recorded here
  from Napoleon's Act of Mediation in 1803. That settlement restored much cantonal
  autonomy after the centralized Helvetic Republic. In 1815 European powers recognized
  Swiss independence and permanent neutrality. Political conflict between conservative
  and liberal cantons culminated in the short Sonderbund War, followed by the federal
  constitution of 1848. Bern became the seat of federal institutions. Switzerland
  developed a federal republic in which cantons retained extensive powers and citizens
  increasingly exercised direct voting rights over laws and constitutional changes. Its
  multilingual population includes German, French, Italian, and Romansh communities.
  Industrialization, finance, and international diplomacy transformed the economy, while
  the country continued to balance local government with national institutions.
