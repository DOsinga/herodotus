description: >-
  The Kalabhra kingdom represents a poorly documented period of rule in the Tamil country of southern India, estimated here from about 250 to 560. Later Pallava and Pandya accounts describe the Kalabhras as displacing the older Tamil ruling houses, but these hostile sources leave their origins, territory and administration uncertain. They were rulers rather than a reconstructed unified empire, and even their seat of government is unknown. Literature connects the period with Jain and Buddhist learning, although individual rulers and patronage remain debated. The familiar label of a dark age reflects gaps in surviving evidence, not an absence of cultural activity. The restoration of Pandya power under Kadungon ended their rule, alongside Pallava expansion farther north during the sixth century.
