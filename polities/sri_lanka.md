  Sri Lanka is an island state south of India, independent from Britain since 1948. It
  initially kept the British monarch as head of state under the name Ceylon, then became a
  republic and adopted its present name in 1972. Parliamentary politics continued, while
  the 1978 constitution introduced a powerful elected presidency. Colombo remains its
  commercial centre, alongside the legislative capital at Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte. Tea
  plantations, ports, tourism and garment production have connected its economy to
  overseas markets. Disputes over language, citizenship and political representation
  deepened divisions between Sinhalese and Tamil communities. A civil war against Tamil
  separatists lasted from 1983 to 2009. Subsequent economic crises and protests have
  challenged governments without ending the country's independence or its elected
  institutions.
