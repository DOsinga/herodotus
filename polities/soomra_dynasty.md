description: >-
  The Soomra dynasty ruled Sindh, the lower Indus valley in present day Pakistan, from the eleventh
  to the fourteenth century. The Soomro tribe rose to power as the Habbari emirate of Mansura
  collapsed under Ghaznavid attack, and its chiefs governed lower Sindh from Mansura and later from
  Thari. They paid tribute in turn to the Ghaznavids, the Ghurids and the Delhi sultans, whose
  governors often held the forts of upper Sindh such as Bhakkar and Sehwan, but the Soomras ran
  their own lands and fought their own wars. Many of them followed Ismaili Islam, which set them
  apart from their Sunni overlords. Their rule is remembered in Sindhi folk poetry. In 1351 the
  Samma tribe overthrew the last Soomra ruler, Hamir, and took over lower Sindh.
