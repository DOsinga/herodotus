description: >-
  Copán was a Maya kingdom in a fertile valley of western Honduras, close to modern Guatemala. This record begins with the documented royal dynasty founded by K'inich Yax K'uk' Mo' in 426, rather than the settlement's much earlier occupation. Successive kings built temples, carved elaborate stelae and recorded their ancestry and victories in inscriptions. These monuments make Copán unusually valuable for reconstructing Maya political history. In 738, its king Uaxaclajuun Ub'aah K'awiil was captured and executed by the ruler of Quiriguá, previously a subordinate centre. Royal construction resumed, but political authority weakened during the wider Classic Maya collapse. The last royal monument dates to 822. People remained in the valley after the dynasty's end, without demonstrating continued royal government there.
