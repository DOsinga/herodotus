description: >-
  The Pallava kingdom ruled parts of southern India from approximately 275 to 897, with
  Kanchipuram as its capital. Its early origins and chronology remain uncertain, but
  inscriptions reveal rulers connected with the Tamil and Telugu regions. The monarchy
  became a major southern power under Mahendravarman I and Narasimhavarman I in the
  seventh century. Wars with the Chalukyas, Pandyas, and Cholas repeatedly altered its
  reach, including a Pallava conquest of the Chalukya capital Vatapi. Royal patronage
  supported temples, sculpture, and learning. Rock cut monuments and the Shore Temple at
  Mamallapuram show its lasting architectural influence. Pallava writing traditions also
  helped shape scripts across Southeast Asia. The kingdom ended when the Chola ruler
  Aditya I defeated its last king, Aparajita, in 897.
