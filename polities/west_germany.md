  The Federal Republic of Germany began in 1949 in the western occupation zones of a
  country deeply devastated by the Second World War. Bonn became its provisional capital.
  The Basic Law established a federal parliamentary republic, with elected
  representatives, a chancellor heading government, and powers shared with the states.
  West Germany combined reconstruction with a social market economy and joined NATO and
  European integration. It remained separate from communist East Germany throughout the
  Cold War, while later diplomacy eased relations across the divide. In 1990, East Germany
  joined the Federal Republic, extending the existing state rather than replacing it.
  Berlin became the capital after reunification. Germany continues as a constitutional
  parliamentary democracy and a central member of the European Union.
