description: >-
  The Commune of Arles was the self-governing city of Arles in Provence, recorded here from
  1131 to 1251. Consuls are first recorded in 1131, and the citizens, the archbishop and the
  local nobility shared government while the counts of Provence held only distant authority.
  Frederick Barbarossa was crowned king of Burgundy in the city in 1178, and Arles remained
  the nominal capital of the Kingdom of Arles. During the thirteenth century the city was
  governed for periods by elected podestàs, chief magistrates on the Italian model, and allied
  with Avignon and Marseille against the count. Charles of Anjou, the new count of Provence,
  besieged the city and forced it to submit in 1251.
