description: >-
  The Alaouite Sultanate ruled Morocco from 1666 until the establishment of European
  protectorates in 1912. Its rulers came from a family based in the Tafilalt oasis region
  and claimed descent from the Prophet Muhammad. Moulay al-Rashid reunited much of the
  country after the decline of the Saadian state. His brother Ismail strengthened central
  power, built at Meknes, and relied on a large army to control rivals. Later sultans faced
  succession struggles, regional resistance, and increasingly powerful European states.
  Muhammad III encouraged maritime trade and diplomacy during the eighteenth century.
  Military defeats, debt, and foreign intervention weakened sovereignty during the
  nineteenth century. The Treaty of Fez placed Morocco under French protection in 1912,
  while the dynasty continued under restricted colonial authority.
