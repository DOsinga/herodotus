description: >-
  The Nagas of Padmavati ruled a kingdom in north central India during the third and fourth
  centuries, approximately 210 to 350. Their capital is identified with Pawaya near Gwalior
  in Madhya Pradesh. They rose as Kushan authority weakened, but the circumstances of their
  emergence remain unclear. Coins bearing royal names provide the main evidence for their
  succession and geographical reach, supported by brief literary and inscriptional
  references. Historians often connect them with the Bharashiva family mentioned in Vakataka
  records, including King Bhava-naga. Relationships with other Naga ruling houses at Mathura
  and Vidisha cannot be established securely. Samudragupta's Allahabad pillar inscription
  names Ganapati-naga among defeated rulers. His conquest brought this kingdom into the
  expanding Gupta Empire around the middle fourth century.
