description: >-
  The Duchy of Courland and Semigallia governed western and southern parts of present Latvia
  from 1561 to 1795. Gotthard Kettler, the last master of the Livonian Order, became its
  first duke after the order dissolved during the Livonian War. Dukes ruled from Mitau
  alongside a powerful assembly of Baltic German landowners, while acknowledging Lithuanian
  and later Polish Lithuanian overlordship. Most peasants were Latvian and increasingly
  bound to estates. Under Jacob Kettler, shipbuilding, metalworking and overseas trade
  flourished, and the small Baltic state established colonies in Gambia and Tobago. Wars and
  foreign intervention weakened these ventures. Russian influence grew during the eighteenth
  century, and the last reigning duke surrendered his rights when Russia annexed Courland in
  the third partition of Poland.
