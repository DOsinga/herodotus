description: >-
  Baden-Durlach was a hereditary margraviate in the upper Rhine region of southwestern Germany
  from 1535 to 1771. It emerged when Baden was divided between branches of its ruling family,
  leaving its lands separated by the Catholic territory of Baden-Baden. Its rulers adopted
  Lutheranism in 1556, giving the division a lasting religious character. Government moved from
  Pforzheim to Durlach, then to the newly founded Karlsruhe in the eighteenth century. The
  margraves briefly occupied Baden-Baden, but defeat at Wimpfen in 1622 ended that expansion.
  Later wars brought further devastation, including French destruction during the Nine Years'
  War. Charles Frederick inherited Baden-Baden when its ruling line ended in 1771, reuniting the
  territories as Baden and closing the separate history of this Protestant branch.
