description: >-
  The Wattasid Sultanate governed northern Morocco from Fez between 1472 and 1554. Its Sunni
  Muslim rulers came from a Zenata Berber family related to the Marinids, whom they had served as
  powerful viziers. After the last Marinid sultan's overthrow, Muhammad al Shaykh established the
  royal line. Wattasid authority remained uneven, with southern Morocco divided among other rulers
  and Portuguese expansion threatening coastal towns. The sultans negotiated with European powers
  but struggled to contain the Saadians, whose leadership of resistance to Portugal strengthened
  their position in the south. Saadian forces captured Fez in 1549. Abu Hassun briefly recovered
  it with Ottoman support in 1554, before defeat ended Wattasid rule and allowed the Saadian
  Sultanate to consolidate control across much of Morocco.
