description: >-
  The Najahid State was ruled from Zabid, in the Tihama coastal plain of western Yemen, by a
  Sunni dynasty of Abyssinian origin. Its founder Najah had been a slave officer of the
  Ziyadids. After the last Ziyadid boy-king was murdered, Najah defeated the killers, took
  Zabid in 1022 and won recognition from the Abbasid caliph. The Najahids were the main rivals
  of the Ismaili Sulayhids, who had Najah poisoned about 1060 and held Zabid for long stretches
  while his sons waited in exile on the Dahlak islands, until Jayyash recovered the city. In
  the twelfth century the kings were overshadowed by viziers, often freedmen, such as Surur al-
  Fatiki. After Surur's murder in 1156 the city appealed to the Zaydi imam, but Ali ibn Mahdi
  took Zabid in 1159.
