description: >-
  The Akkadian Empire united much of Mesopotamia under a royal government from 2334 to 2154 BCE
  in the middle chronology. Sargon founded it by defeating rival rulers and bringing southern
  city-states under the authority of Akkad, whose exact location remains unknown. Kings appointed
  governors and drew resources from conquered communities, while campaigns extended their
  influence into Syria and western Iran. Akkadian and Sumerian speakers lived within the empire,
  and both languages appear in its written record. Under Naram-Sin, royal claims became
  especially ambitious, including divine status for the king. Rebellions and later military
  pressures undermined central control. Gutian incursions accompanied its breakup, though the
  causes remain debated. Later Mesopotamian rulers remembered Sargon and Naram-Sin as models of
  impressive imperial power.
