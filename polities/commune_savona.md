description: >-
  The Commune of Savona was the self-governing city state of Savona on the Ligurian coast,
  from the end of the eleventh century until Genoa took it for good in 1528. Savona's
  consuls governed a busy port and its hinterland, and the city spent four centuries
  resisting its much larger neighbour Genoa, which forced several treaties of submission
  on it without ending its self-government. Savona looked for protection to the emperors,
  the marquises of Montferrat, the Visconti and the French kings, and it shared in Genoa's
  French and Milanese occupations. It gave the Church two popes from its della Rovere
  family, Sixtus IV and Julius II. In 1528 Genoa, newly independent under Andrea Doria,
  conquered Savona, pulled down its towers and filled in its harbour.
