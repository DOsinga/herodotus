description: >-
  Ban Chiang culture represents prehistoric communities in northeastern Thailand, named
  after the archaeological site in Udon Thani province. This record uses an estimated span
  from 2100 BCE to 200 CE, though scholars disagree about the chronology, especially the
  beginnings of bronze working. Excavations reveal settlement layers, human burials, rice
  remains, stone implements and local metal objects. Bronze ornaments and tools occur
  alongside pottery, with iron appearing later. The site's vessels painted with red patterns
  belong to its later phases, not its earliest occupation. People combined farming with
  hunting and gathering, burying the dead near or beneath dwellings. These remains do not
  identify a kingdom, named rulers or a unified government. The culture's dates describe
  archaeological development rather than political transitions.
