  Timor-Leste became independent in 2002 after Indonesian occupation and a transitional United Nations administration. It occupies eastern Timor, the Oecusse enclave in the island's west, and nearby islands, with its capital at Dili. The state is a semi-presidential republic: an elected president shares executive authority with a prime minister responsible to parliament. Portuguese colonial rule left a lasting influence on language and institutions, while Tetum connects communities with many local languages. A 1999 vote for independence brought severe violence and international intervention before sovereignty was restored. Building public institutions, overcoming political conflict, and managing petroleum income have shaped the country's subsequent history. It remains a distinct state, linking Southeast Asian geography with strong Portuguese-speaking and predominantly Roman Catholic cultural traditions.
