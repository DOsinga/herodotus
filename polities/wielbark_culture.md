description: >-
  The Wielbark culture developed around the lower Vistula and Baltic coast of present day
  Poland during the first centuries CE, conventionally represented here from about 1 to
  400. Its name comes from a cemetery near Malbork, formerly called Willenberg.
  Archaeologists recognise it through burial customs, jewellery, pottery and settlement
  remains rather than records of a government. Communities used both cremation and
  burial, generally avoiding weapons as grave goods. Stone circles and barrows
  distinguish some cemeteries. Farming and exchange along the Amber Road connected these
  communities with neighbouring regions and the Roman world. The culture is associated
  with Goths and related peoples, but material remains cannot assign every individual an
  identity. Its southward expansion contributed to the later Chernyakhov cultural
  horizon.
