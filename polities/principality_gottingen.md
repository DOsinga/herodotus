description: >-
  The Principality of Göttingen was a Guelph principality in southern Lower Saxony, around
  Göttingen, Northeim, Münden and Uslar. It was created in 1345, when the sons of Albert II of
  Brunswick divided his lands and Ernest received the southern part. Its dukes were poor and weak
  beside their wealthy towns. Göttingen itself, a Hanseatic member, governed its own affairs, and
  in 1387 its citizens stormed the ducal castle in the town. Duke Otto the One-eyed, nearly blind,
  handed the government to his estates in 1435 and lived on as a private man. When he died in 1463
  the principality passed to his cousin William the Victorious of Calenberg, and it was merged
  into the Calenberg lands.
