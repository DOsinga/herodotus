description: >-
  The Bosporan Kingdom linked Greek cities and neighboring peoples around the Kerch
  Strait, between eastern Crimea and the Taman Peninsula. From 438 BCE, the Spartocid
  dynasty ruled from Panticapaeum, combining Greek civic traditions with kingship over a
  mixed population. Grain exports tied the kingdom closely to Mediterranean markets.
  Mithridates VI of Pontus took control around 107 BCE, interrupting its separate
  government until Pharnaces II restored a kingdom in 63 BCE. Later kings maintained their
  own armies while acknowledging Roman supremacy. Coins and inscriptions document this
  long relationship, but evidence becomes thinner after royal coinage stopped in the
  fourth century. Gothic and Hunnic pressure complicated its later history. Justinian's
  intervention in 527 brought the remaining kingdom under direct Byzantine imperial
  control.
