description: >-
  Li Gui's Liang state governed parts of modern Gansu west of the Yellow River from 617 to 619.
  Its capital was Wuwei, where Li Gui, a wealthy local militia officer, joined prominent families
  and regional leaders in rebelling against the collapsing Sui government. Initially claiming
  princely rank, he built a court modelled on Sui institutions and adopted the imperial title in
  618. His territory lay along the Hexi corridor, a route between China and Central Asia.
  Although he considered cooperation with Li Yuan's emerging Tang government, negotiations failed
  to settle his status. In 619 An Xinggui entered his service, organised an uprising, and
  delivered the captured ruler to Tang. Li Gui was executed, and Tang absorbed his short-lived
  territorial government.
