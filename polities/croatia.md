  Croatia is a republic in Central and Southeastern Europe, stretching from inland plains
  around Zagreb to the Adriatic coast and its many islands. The modern independent state
  began in 1991, when it declared separation from socialist Yugoslavia. War followed against
  Yugoslav forces and rebel Serb authorities, and the conflict continued until 1995, leaving
  destruction, displacement and lasting political disputes. Croatia developed from an
  initially strong presidential system into a parliamentary republic, with an elected
  president, a prime minister leading the government and the Sabor as its legislature. It
  joined NATO in 2009 and the European Union in 2013, later entering the euro area and
  Schengen zone. Zagreb remains the capital, while coastal tourism and industry contribute
  to its continuing economy.
