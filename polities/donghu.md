description: >-
  The Donghu were a grouping of pastoral peoples in eastern Inner Mongolia and neighbouring northeastern Asia, represented here as a confederation from roughly 400 to 209 BCE. Chinese texts describe communities beyond the northeastern frontier, but the extent of their unity and their internal government are uncertain. Their name means eastern Hu, distinguishing them from other northern peoples rather than defining a nationality. They raised livestock, moved across steppe landscapes and fought neighbouring powers, including Yan and the rising Xiongnu. Accounts of their defeat describe Modu Chanyu destroying their leadership and taking people and herds. This ended the confederation's independent power. Surviving groups are associated with the later Wuhuan and Xianbei, whose histories continued beyond the fall of Donghu rule.
