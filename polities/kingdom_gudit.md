description: >-
  The Kingdom of Gudit stands for the rulers of the northern Ethiopian highlands between the fall
  of Aksum and the rise of the Zagwe, a period known almost only from later tradition. Around 960
  a queen remembered as Gudit, or Yodit, laid waste to what remained of Aksum, burning churches
  and attacking the royal family; an Arabic geographer, Ibn Hawqal, mentions a queen ruling the
  country at that time. Traditions differ on whether she was an Aksumite princess, a Beta Israel
  queen or a foreign invader. According to one account she ruled for forty years and passed the
  throne to her descendants, the last of whom was overthrown by Mara Takla Haymanot, the founder
  of the Zagwe dynasty, in 1137.
