description: >-
  The Archbishopric of Riga was the church principality of the bishops of Livonia, who ruled
  Riga and a large part of present Latvia. Bishop Albert moved the Livonian see from Üxküll to
  the new town of Riga in 1201 and led the crusade that conquered the region, and the
  king made him a prince of the Empire in 1207. The see became an archbishopric in 1255, with
  authority over the other Livonian bishops. The archbishops governed their lands through
  castles such as Kokenhusen, Treyden and Ronneburg and through vassal families, while the
  citizens of Riga ran much of their own trade. Rivalry with the Livonian Order led to long
  wars, and the order took Riga in 1330. From 1435 the archbishop was one of the members
  of the Livonian Confederation.
