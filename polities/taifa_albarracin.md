description: >-
  The Taifa of Albarracín was a small Muslim kingdom in the mountains of eastern al-Andalus, in
  present Aragon, ruled by the Berber Banu Razin family from their fortress town of Albarracín. It
  emerged from the civil war that broke up the Caliphate of Córdoba after 1009, when local
  commanders and families took power in their own districts. The Banu Razin held a rugged frontier
  district between the larger taifas of Zaragoza, Toledo and Valencia, surviving by shifting
  alliances and by paying tribute, the parias, to the Christian kings of Castile and Aragon.
  Albarracín outlasted many richer neighbours because of its remote position and defensible site.
  The Almoravids, who had crossed from Morocco to unite al-Andalus, took the town in 1104 and
  ended the dynasty.
