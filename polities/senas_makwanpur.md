description: >-
  The Senas of Makwanpur ruled a kingdom in the hills and plains south of the Kathmandu
  valley, in the northern part of the Mithila region of Nepal, from the sixteenth century until
  the Gorkha conquest of 1762. The family claimed descent from the Sena kings of Bengal, and
  their kingdom formed when the lands of Mukunda Sen of Palpa were divided after his death in
  1553. Its seat was the hill fort of Makwanpur Gadhi, and it held the Terai as far as Janakpur,
  where a Makwanpur prince signed a document for the Janaki monastery in 1732. Its court used
  Maithili and an administration modelled on the Mughal one, and it paid tribute to the Rajas
  of Darbhanga until 1675. Prithvi Narayan Shah took Makwanpur Gadhi in 1762.
