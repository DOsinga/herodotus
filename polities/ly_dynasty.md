description: >-
  The Lý dynasty governed northern Vietnam from 1009 to 1225, building the monarchy usually
  known as Đại Việt. Its founder, Lý Công Uẩn, moved the capital from Hoa Lư to Thăng Long,
  the site of modern Hanoi, in 1010. Emperors ruled through an expanding administration of
  civil and military officials, while Buddhist institutions enjoyed strong royal support. The
  court also developed Confucian education and examinations, and issued a written law code.
  Relations with Song China combined tribute, diplomacy and war, including a major conflict in
  the 1070s. Campaigns against Champa extended Vietnamese control southward. Weakened by court
  struggles and regional unrest, the royal house lost power when the young empress Lý Chiêu
  Hoàng transferred the throne to her Trần husband.
