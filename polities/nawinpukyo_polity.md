description: >-
  Nawinpukyo was a local Huarpa chiefly centre near present Ayacucho in the Peruvian
  highlands, represented here approximately between 200 and 600 CE. Archaeology, rather than
  written royal histories, supplies the evidence for its community and leadership.
  Excavations have uncovered domestic buildings, distinctive pottery and ceremonial
  structures, including a circular area on the hilltop. These remains suggest a centre where
  communal ritual helped organise local society, but they do not establish named chiefs or a
  detailed constitution. Earlier interpretations made Nawinpukyo the capital of a much
  larger Huarpa state; later research treats it more cautiously as one among several
  regional centres. Around the beginning of the Wari period, its community continued while
  losing autonomy within a new political and administrative order.
