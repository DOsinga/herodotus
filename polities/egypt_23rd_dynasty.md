description: >-
  This record represents rival Egyptian royal governments associated with the Twenty-third
  Dynasty, approximately from 837 to 728 BCE. Their centres included Thebes and later Leontopolis
  during the fragmented Third Intermediate Period. Kings of Libyan ancestry claimed pharaonic
  authority alongside other rulers rather than governing a united Egypt. Takelot II, Pedubast I,
  and their successors fought for power in Upper Egypt, where royal ambitions were closely
  connected with the priesthood of Amun. Inscriptions and temple records help reconstruct their
  reigns, but chronology and the grouping of these overlapping royal lines remain disputed. Later
  local rulers also held parts of the country. Piye's campaign from Kush brought these powers
  under his supremacy, marking the endpoint used here for this rival Egyptian polity.
