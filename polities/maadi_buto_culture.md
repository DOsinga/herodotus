description: >-
  The Maadi–Buto culture is an archaeological tradition of Lower Egypt, represented here between
  approximately 3900 and 3300 BCE. Its settlements and cemeteries include Maadi near modern Cairo and
  sites farther north in the Nile delta. Houses used wooden posts and screens of woven plants covered
  with mud, while distinctive pottery had rounded bodies, flat bases and flared rims. Similarities in
  pottery and stone tools show connections with communities in Palestine. Graves generally contained
  few offerings, unlike the increasingly elaborate burials found in contemporary Upper Egypt. No named
  rulers or securely identified government are known, so this record describes shared material
  evidence rather than a kingdom. Expansion of the Naqada tradition from Upper Egypt eventually
  replaced these traditions across the region.
