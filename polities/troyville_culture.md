description: >-
  Troyville culture developed in the lower Mississippi valley, especially present Louisiana, between about 400 and 700 CE. Archaeologists identify it through pottery, settlements, and large earthwork centres rather than written accounts of rulers or a single state. It followed the Marksville tradition and existed alongside Baytown and Coastal Troyville communities. Unlike the more dispersed Baytown settlements, Troyville sites continued the construction of substantial mound complexes. The Troyville Earthworks at modern Jonesville give the culture its name. People gathered wild plants, cultivated local crops such as squash and maygrass, and hunted deer and smaller animals. Maize remained relatively unimportant. The material traditions gradually gave way to Coles Creek culture, a major regional development rather than a documented conquest or dynastic change.
