description: >-
  After the Hittite empire fell around 1180 BCE, the plateau south of the Halys, around Tuwana
  near modern Niğde, passed to local Luwian-speaking lords who kept parts of the Hittite royal
  tradition alive. Almost nothing is written about them. The hieroglyphic Luwian monuments of
  a king Hartapu on the Konya plain, whose script looks thirteenth century, are sometimes taken
  as evidence that a successor kingdom of Tarhuntassa survived in the region, though others
  date them to the eighth century. Settlement continued at Tuwana and the neighbouring towns,
  and when Assyrian records first name the land of Tabal in 837 BCE they find several kings
  already ruling there. This record stands for those unnamed lords of the plateau in the dark
  centuries before Tabal appears in writing.
