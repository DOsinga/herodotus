description: >-
  The Luba Empire developed in the lake and river country of southeastern Congo, with its
  traditional foundation placed around 1585. The royal centre moved between settlements,
  including Mwibele near modern Kabongo. A sacred king, the mulopwe, ruled through nobles
  and subordinate chiefs, while the Mbudye association preserved political history and royal
  traditions. Oral accounts connect its beginnings with Kalala Ilunga, but these stories
  cannot securely fix the founding date. Farming, fishing, ironworking and trade in copper
  and salt supported expansion. Luba institutions influenced neighbouring kingdoms,
  especially Lunda. During the nineteenth century, slave raiding and competing trade
  networks weakened royal authority. A succession struggle divided the unified empire in
  1889, and its territories subsequently fell under the Congo Free State's control.
