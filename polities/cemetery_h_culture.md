description: >-
  Cemetery H culture describes late Harappan communities in the Punjab, approximately
  from 1900 to 1300 BCE. It takes its name from a burial ground excavated at Harappa,
  now in Pakistan. Distinctive red pottery painted with black animals and other designs
  appears alongside changes in burial practice, including cremated remains placed in
  urns. Settlement patterns shifted, long distance exchange contracted, and mud brick
  construction continued. These remains show both change and continuity after the mature
  Indus cities, rather than proving that invading outsiders destroyed them. Scholars
  debate possible connections with Indo Aryan migration and later Vedic traditions. No
  evidence identifies a state, ruling dynasty, or uniform government. The record
  therefore represents an archaeological culture, with estimated boundaries in time and
  space.
