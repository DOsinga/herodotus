description: >-
  Mamluk Iraq was the self-governing regime that ruled Baghdad, Basra and the surrounding
  provinces from 1704 to 1831 under nominal Ottoman sovereignty. Hasan Pasha, appointed
  governor of Baghdad in 1704, and his son Ahmad Pasha built up a household of slave
  soldiers, mostly from Georgia, and from 1747 these mamluks supplied the pashas themselves.
  The sultans confirmed each new ruler but could rarely choose him. The mamluk pashas
  collected their own taxes, kept their own army, held back the tribes and Kurdish princes,
  and dealt with Persia, the Wahhabis and British traders largely on their own account.
  Sulayman the Great brought a long period of order after 1780. When Dawud Pasha resisted
  the reforming sultan Mahmud II, an Ottoman army took Baghdad in 1831 and restored direct
  rule.
