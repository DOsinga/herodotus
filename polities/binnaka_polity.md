description: >-
  Binnaka was an early city-state in Myanmar's dry zone, near the Kyaukse rice growing plain
  and southeast of Bagan. This record estimates its separate political life from 100 BCE
  until about 1050 CE, although its governing chronology is unknown. Archaeological finds
  connect it with the Pyu cities: burial urns, pottery, coins, terracotta tablets, and
  fragments bearing writing similar to Pyu script. Excavations also uncovered masonry that
  might belong to a temple foundation or city walls. Later chronicles describe a ruler of
  Binnaka attacking Tagaung, but they do not provide a secure contemporary history. Its
  estimated endpoint marks incorporation into Pagan's kingdom. Occupation continued long
  afterward, with artifacts and written records extending into the nineteenth century,
  without establishing continued independent government.
