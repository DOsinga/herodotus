description: >-
  The Khanate of Bukhara was an Uzbek monarchy in Central Asia from 1501 to 1785. Muhammad Shaybani established it by conquering cities previously held by the Timurids. Its lands included much of Transoxiana, with Bukhara eventually becoming the main capital. Government depended on negotiations between the khan, ruling relatives, tribal nobles, and holders of regional territories. Persian remained important in administration and literature alongside Chagatai Turkic. Abdullah Khan strengthened central authority during the sixteenth century, while the succeeding Janid dynasty faced growing regional independence. Nader Shah imposed his power in 1740. After his death, Manghit leaders increasingly controlled the government behind nominal khans. Shah Murad formalized their rule in 1785, adopting the title emir and establishing the Emirate of Bukhara.
