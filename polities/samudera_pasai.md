description: >-
  Samudera Pasai was a Muslim sultanate on the north coast of Sumatra, near modern Lhokseumawe, from
  the late thirteenth century to the early sixteenth. Tradition names its founder as Merah Silu, who
  converted to Islam and ruled as Malik ul Salih from about 1267; his gravestone survives. Pasai was
  among the first Muslim states in the archipelago, and Marco Polo and Ibn Battuta both described it.
  Its wealth came from pepper and from its place on the route between the Indian Ocean and China.
  Sultans struck gold coins and kept up diplomatic ties with Ming China and with Malacca, which took
  much of its learning from Pasai. Majapahit raided it in the fourteenth century. The Portuguese
  seized the port in 1521, and Aceh annexed it in 1524.
