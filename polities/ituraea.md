description: >-
  Ituraea was a principality of the Itureans, a Semitic-speaking people of the Lebanon and
  Anti-Lebanon mountains, who rose as Seleucid power declined in the second century BCE.
  Whether they were Arabs or Arameans is still debated. From the Beqaa valley they spread
  into the Hermon, the northern Golan and the Hauran. Their state is best known under
  Ptolemy son of Mennaeus, who ruled from about 85 BCE from Chalcis, a capital whose site has
  not been found, and kept his throne under Pompey and the Romans. His son Lysanias was
  called king of the Itureans but was put to death in 36 BCE. A chief named Zenodorus later
  leased the lands, and after his death in 20 BCE Augustus gave his territory to Herod
  the Great.
