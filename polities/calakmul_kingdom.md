description: >-
  Calakmul was a Maya kingdom in the forests of present Campeche, Mexico, represented here
  from about 500 to 910. Its monumental city contained large pyramids, plazas, reservoirs,
  and inscribed royal stones. During the seventh century, the Kaan, or Snake, court moved
  there from Dzibanché, greatly strengthening Calakmul's place in regional politics. Its
  kings built a network of allies and subordinate rulers that challenged Tikal across the
  Maya lowlands. Yuknoom Che'en II directed this expansion through warfare, diplomacy, and
  royal relationships. Defeat by Tikal in 695 weakened the kingdom's supremacy, though
  kings continued to commission monuments afterward. Late inscriptions around 899 or 909
  mark the final known royal activity. Political collapse soon followed, without requiring
  the city's immediate or complete abandonment.
