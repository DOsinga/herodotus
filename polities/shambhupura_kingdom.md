description: >-
  Shambhupura was a regional royal court on the Mekong near modern Kratie in Cambodia, represented here from approximately 700 to 802. It belonged to the fragmented political landscape conventionally described as Chenla, rather than a securely documented unified kingdom. Inscriptions identify kings and queens associated with this center, including Indraloka and successive queens Nrpatendradevi, Jayendrabhā, and Jyeshtharya. Their names provide evidence for a local ruling lineage, although the court's territory and sequence remain uncertain. Religious foundations and inscriptions are more informative than later narratives about a division into Land and Water Chenla. The site should not be confused with Isanapura at Sambor Prei Kuk. Its absorption is estimated within Jayavarman II's consolidation of the Khmer Empire around the year 802.
