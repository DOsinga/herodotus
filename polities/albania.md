  Albania lies on the Adriatic and Ionian coasts of the western Balkans. It declared
  independence from the Ottoman Empire in 1912, although its borders and government
  remained contested during the following years. After a brief principality and republican
  period, Ahmet Zogu became King Zog in 1928. Italy invaded in 1939, followed by German
  occupation during the Second World War. Communist partisans took power in 1944,
  restoring an Albanian government under Enver Hoxha. His regime imposed central planning,
  restricted religion and travel, and eventually isolated the country even from former
  communist allies. Multiparty elections followed the collapse of communist rule in the
  early 1990s. Albania has since continued as a parliamentary republic, with Tirana as its
  political centre and national capital.
