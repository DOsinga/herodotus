description: >-
  The Pazyryk culture describes Iron Age horse nomads of the Altai Mountains and
  neighboring regions, approximately between 600 and 300 BCE. It is identified through
  burial mounds rather than written accounts of a kingdom or named rulers. Ice
  preserved wooden chambers, sacrificed horses, clothing, felt hangings, and tattooed
  human bodies that would normally have decayed. These finds reveal skilled
  craftsmanship, elaborate funerals, and strong differences in wealth among the buried
  people. Animal imagery connects Pazyryk art with the wider Scythian and Saka world.
  Imported textiles and other objects indicate links reaching toward China and western
  Asia. Herding and mobility shaped everyday life, but the burials cannot establish a
  single government. Their preservation offers an intimate view of ancient steppe
  societies.
