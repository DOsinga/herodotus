description: >-
  The Ziyadid emirate governed western Yemen from Zabid between 819 and 1018. Muhammad ibn
  Ziyad arrived with an Abbasid commission to suppress unrest, then founded a capital and a
  hereditary ruling house in the Tihama lowlands. Its emirs acknowledged the caliph while
  increasingly retaining revenues and directing their own affairs. Coastal commerce
  connected the realm with India and eastern Africa, bringing customs income from luxury
  goods and other trade. Control of the highlands was contested by rival dynasties,
  religious movements, and tribes. After a revival under Abu'l Jaysh Ishaq, military
  households exercised real authority behind nominal rulers. The killing of the last Ziyadid
  ruler in 1018 ended this government, amid struggles that eventually produced the Najahid
  state based in Zabid.
