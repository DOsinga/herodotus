description: >-
  The Prince-Bishopric of Liège was an ecclesiastical state centred on the Meuse valley
  in present Belgium, with scattered territories farther afield. Its bishop exercised
  secular authority as an imperial prince as well as religious leadership, though the
  diocese itself covered a larger area. This record follows its separately governing
  period from 1250 until French occupation in 1794. Cathedral clergy, nobles, and towns
  shared in political life, and disputes over civic freedoms repeatedly challenged
  episcopal power. The acquisition of Loon and Horne enlarged its lands. Liège remained
  distinct from the neighbouring Netherlands under Burgundian and Habsburg rulers.
  Revolutionary upheaval in 1789 briefly displaced the bishop, followed by restorations
  and renewed fighting. French occupation and subsequent annexation ended the old
  principality's rule.
