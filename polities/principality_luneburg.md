description: >-
  The Principality of Lüneburg governed territory in what is now Lower Saxony from 1269 to
  1705. It arose when the Welf rulers divided Brunswick-Lüneburg, and its princes retained
  the wider family's ducal title. Initially based in Lüneburg, the government moved to Celle
  in 1378 following conflict over succession and relations with the city. Its lands expanded
  through acquired counties and rights, covering much of the Lüneburg Heath and Wendland.
  Princes ruled alongside estates representing nobles, clergy, and towns, whose financial
  support mattered to government. Ernest the Confessor introduced the Lutheran Reformation
  during the sixteenth century. Later dynastic arrangements tied the principality
  increasingly to Hanover. When George William died in 1705, his nephew inherited the
  territory and governed it from Hanover.
