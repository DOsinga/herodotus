description: >-
  Wei was a hereditary state of ancient China that emerged from Jin's partition
  in 453 BCE and survived until Qin conquered it in 225 BCE. Its ruling family
  governed independently before the Zhou king recognized Wei, Han, and Zhao as
  marquessates in 403 BCE. Initially based at Anyi, the court moved to Daliang,
  present-day Kaifeng, in 361 BCE; the state was consequently also called Liang.
  Early rulers supported administrative reforms, irrigation, and military
  development, making Wei a leading Warring States power. Defeats against Qi and
  repeated pressure from Qin reduced its position and territory. Its rulers
  adopted royal titles as Zhou authority weakened. Qin general Wang Ben finally
  forced surrender by diverting river water into Daliang and flooding the
  capital.
