description: >-
  Northern Qi ruled northeastern China from 550 to 577 during the division between Northern and Southern dynasties. Gao Yang replaced Eastern Wei's emperor and founded his own imperial house, governing from Ye. The state inherited agricultural lands, an army and institutions shaped by both Chinese and Xianbei traditions. It competed with Northern Zhou in the northwest and southern Chinese regimes, while maintaining relations with the Turks. Buddhist patronage encouraged cave temples and sculpture, and excavated tombs reveal artistic exchanges with Central and Western Asia. Court violence, unstable succession and ineffective later rulers weakened its advantages. Northern Zhou invaded in 577, captured the capital and seized Gao Wei and Gao Heng. Gao Shaoyi's subsequent exile claim did not restore the kingdom.
