description: >-
  Chinchawas was a highland community in Ancash, Peru, represented here as a chiefdom from
  approximately 400 to 1000 CE. Its position above a route between the coast and mountains made it a
  useful regional center of exchange. Archaeological study places its early development within
  Recuay traditions, rather than identifying a named royal dynasty. Public enclosures, stone
  sculptures, tombs, pottery, and animal remains show how leaders and families used ancestor worship
  and communal feasts in local politics. Underground tombs and large gatherings later gave way to
  aboveground mausolea and changed ritual practices. Wari-related goods indicate wider contacts and
  cultural change, not certain direct conquest. The community's governing arrangements, territorial
  reach, and endpoint remain uncertain; the dates summarize the local archaeological sequence.
