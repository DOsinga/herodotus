description: >-
  Jōmon culture describes a varied archaeological horizon in Japan, conventionally extending from about 14000 to 300 BCE. Its name refers to cord impressions decorating pottery, among the earliest ceramic traditions known. Communities hunted, fished, gathered nuts and plants, and in some places cultivated useful species. Many lived in settled villages with pit houses, storage facilities, and communal spaces. Sannai-Maruyama in northern Honshu is a prominent example of this settled life. Clay figurines, ornaments, lacquered objects, and stone circles reveal rich craft and ritual traditions. No written evidence identifies rulers, and the label does not imply a single government across the islands. Regional chronologies differ, but expanding Yayoi farming communities gradually transformed much of Japan while related traditions persisted farther north.
