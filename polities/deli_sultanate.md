description: >-
  The Sultanate of Deli was a Malay state on the east coast of North Sumatra, centred on the Deli
  river near modern Medan. Aceh conquered the coast in 1612, and in 1632 Sultan Iskandar Muda
  granted the former lands of Aru to his commander Gocah Pahlawan as viceroy. Gocah's son Tuanku
  Panglima Perunggit used Aceh's weakness after 1641 to declare independence in 1669, with his
  capital at Labuhan Deli. A succession war after 1720 split off the rival Sultanate of Serdang, and
  Siak, Johor and Aceh in turn claimed overlordship of Deli. In 1862 the sultan signed a contract
  with the Dutch, whose tobacco plantations soon transformed the region and turned Medan into a
  colonial city.
