description: >-
  The Sultanate of Sulu was a Muslim maritime state centered on Jolo and the Sulu archipelago, established around 1457. A sultan governed alongside influential datus and a council, linking island communities through kinship, tribute, trade, and religious authority. Its reach extended into nearby Philippine coastal areas and parts of northern Borneo, though control varied considerably. Commercial ties connected Sulu with China and other Asian markets, while slave raiding became important to its economy and conflicts with neighboring powers. Spanish campaigns repeatedly challenged the sultanate without immediately destroying its government. The 1878 agreement placed it under Spanish protection and marks the end of independent external affairs here. The royal institution survived later American oversight, but did not regain its sovereign position.
