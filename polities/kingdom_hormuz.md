description: >-
  The Kingdom of Ormus controlled a trading gateway between the Persian Gulf and the Indian
  Ocean from the eleventh century to 1622. Its rulers initially governed from a mainland
  port near modern Minab in southern Iran. Around 1300, the court and trading settlement
  moved to Jarun, later called Hormuz Island, where merchants built a wealthy international
  port. Kings of southern Arab origin ruled a realm reaching across Gulf shores, including
  parts of Oman and, at its height, Bahrain. They paid tribute to larger powers while
  retaining local government. Portuguese intervention brought a fortress and increasing
  influence after 1515. In 1622, Safavid forces, supported by English ships, captured the
  island and ended the kingdom, redirecting its trade toward the Iranian mainland.
