description: >-
  Shanga was a Swahili trading town on Pate Island, off the coast of present Kenya.
  Archaeological layers trace its occupation from the eighth century to the early
  fifteenth; the political dates here are inferred from that settlement history. Imported
  pottery, glass, beads and coins connect its inhabitants with Indian Ocean trade.
  Excavations show a town that developed coral stone buildings and a succession of mosques
  from earlier structures. Its local government is less securely documented than its
  commerce and religious life. Oral traditions remember Shanga as independent before
  pressure from Pate's rulers. The town was abandoned around 1400 to 1425, though the
  cause remains uncertain. Political conflict and declining water supplies have both been
  suggested; its community survived in settlements.
