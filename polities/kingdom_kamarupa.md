description: >-
  Kamarupa was an early kingdom of the Brahmaputra Valley in present northeastern India, conventionally dated from about 350 to 1140.
  The Varman, Mlechchha and Pala dynasties successively governed it, with capitals at Pragjyotishpura, Tezpur and Durjaya. Its territory varied, reaching
  beyond Assam into neighbouring regions at different times. The Allahabad inscription of Samudragupta names Kamarupa among frontier kingdoms, providing a
  written reference. Inscriptions and land grants reveal kings, officials and religious patronage, including support for Brahmanical institutions. Bhaskaravarman's alliance with
  Harsha made the kingdom prominent in seventh century politics, and the Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang visited his court. The united kingdom
  fragmented by the twelfth century. Its name and claimed legacy persisted among later Assamese rulers after its own government disappeared.
