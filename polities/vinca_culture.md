description: >-
  The Vinča culture describes Neolithic farming communities of the central Balkans, centred on present Serbia and extending into neighbouring countries.
  This record uses an estimated span of 5700 to 4500 BCE; archaeological chronologies vary. Its name comes from Vinča near
  Belgrade, where repeated rebuilding created a settlement mound. Villages could be unusually extensive for prehistoric Europe, supported by cereal cultivation
  and domestic animals. Dark polished pottery, human and animal figurines, and signs scratched on objects are characteristic finds. Those signs
  are not established evidence of a written language. Communities exchanged materials and shared practices across considerable distances, while some sites
  show early copper smelting. No known kings or unified government can be assigned to this archaeological culture from surviving evidence.
