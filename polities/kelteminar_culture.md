description: >-
  Kelteminar culture describes Neolithic communities of Central Asia, approximately between
  5500 and 3500 BCE. Sites occur around the Aral Sea and the river deltas and desert margins
  of present day Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, and Kazakhstan. The map point represents this
  wider region rather than a capital. People relied on fishing, hunting, and gathering,
  using wetlands and river channels within otherwise dry landscapes. Some settlements
  contained large communal houses, while stone axes, small flint arrowheads, shell beads,
  and handmade pottery reveal technology and decoration. Subsistence and settlement patterns
  changed over time, with stockbreeding appearing alongside older practices. Archaeological
  similarities identify a cultural tradition, not a known state or unified ethnic group. No
  rulers, governmental institutions, or single political ending are securely documented.
