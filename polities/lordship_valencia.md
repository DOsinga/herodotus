description: >-
  The Lordship of Valencia was the short-lived state governed by Rodrigo Díaz de Vivar, known as
  El Cid, on Spain's Mediterranean coast from 1094 to 1102. After a long siege, he took Valencia
  from its Muslim rulers and established his own court. Although he acknowledged Alfonso VI, he
  exercised practical independence. His rule combined a Christian military elite with a largely
  Muslim population and retained elements of the city's existing administration. Victories
  against Almoravid forces secured the lordship for several years, while marriage alliances
  connected his family with neighboring rulers. After his death in 1099, his widow Jimena Díaz
  continued governing. Almoravid pressure made the position untenable. In 1102 Jimena and her
  supporters evacuated Valencia, which passed to Almoravid rule.
