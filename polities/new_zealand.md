  New Zealand is an island country in the southwestern Pacific, centred on the North and
  South Islands, with government based in Wellington. This record begins in 1939, when its
  government declared war independently, rather than with earlier internal political
  autonomy or its 1907 dominion status. Adoption of the Statute of Westminster in 1947
  confirmed its legislative independence. The country remains a parliamentary democracy and
  constitutional monarchy, with an elected parliament, a prime minister and an appointed
  governor-general representing the monarch. Its history joins Māori settlement and
  institutions with British colonisation, the Treaty of Waitangi and later disputes over
  land and sovereignty. Twentieth century governments developed extensive welfare services,
  then liberalised the economy in the 1980s while maintaining independent national
  government.
