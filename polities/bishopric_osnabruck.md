description: >-
  The Prince-Bishopric of Osnabrück was an ecclesiastical principality in northwestern Germany,
  around the town whose see Charlemagne founded in about 780 during the conquest of Saxony. Its
  bishops gained imperial immunity early and ruled a territory between the Ems and the Hunte as
  imperial princes from the thirteenth century. The cathedral chapter elected the bishops and the
  estates of the land shared in government. The Reformation divided the principality, and the
  Peace of Westphalia, negotiated partly in the town itself, gave it a unique constitution: Catholic
  bishops alternated with Lutheran princes of the house of Brunswick-Lüneburg. The last of these,
  Frederick, Duke of York, was a son of George III. In 1803 the bishopric was secularised and given
  to Hanover.
