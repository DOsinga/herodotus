description: >-
  Chaghaniyan was a principality north of the Oxus in present southern Uzbekistan, with its
  centre represented near modern Denov. Its separately governing court is conventionally dated
  from the Hephthalite empire's fragmentation around 560 to direct Abbasid control around 780.
  Local rulers, later known as Chaghan Khudah, managed their own affairs while acknowledging
  Turkic and Arab powers. Diplomatic contact with Samarkand is preserved in the Afrasiyab murals,
  which depict an envoy from Chaghaniyan. The principality stood among competing courts of
  Bactria and Transoxiana, where alliances shifted as Arab armies advanced. Some rulers sought
  Arab support against neighbouring enemies; later warfare drew the region deeper into caliphal
  politics. Evidence for its beginnings and final absorption is incomplete, making both boundary
  dates estimates.
