description: >-
  The Taifa of Agrigento was one of the Muslim lordships that divided Sicily after the
  Kalbid emirate collapsed, from about 1053 until the Normans took Agrigento in 1087. Its
  first lord, Ali ibn Nima, known as Ibn al-Hawwas, held Agrigento, Castrogiovanni and the
  interior of the island. His war with Ibn al-Thumna of Syracuse led his rival to invite
  the Normans into Sicily in 1061, and Ibn al-Hawwas held Castrogiovanni against them
  before he died around 1065. His successor Hammud ruled the shrinking state from
  Agrigento and Castrogiovanni. Count Roger I captured Agrigento in 1087, and Hammud then
  surrendered Castrogiovanni and converted to Christianity, leaving only a few Muslim
  towns in the southeast still holding out.
