description: >-
  The Faiyum A culture represents early farming communities around Lake Qarun in Egypt's
  Faiyum depression. Its dates here, approximately 5200 to 4200 BCE, are estimates within
  the broader development of Egyptian Neolithic life. Archaeological finds include pottery
  and carefully shaped stone tools, while evidence of cultivated crops and domestic
  animals shows farming alongside older hunting, fishing and gathering practices. This
  combination mattered in a landscape shaped by changing water levels and expanding
  desert. The communities form part of the story of agriculture entering northeastern
  Africa and being adapted to local conditions. They are not a documented kingdom: no
  named rulers or clear state government can be reconstructed. Faiyum A is distinguished
  from the earlier Qarunian culture, whose remains lack pottery.
