description: >-
  The Eastern Indo-Greek kingdom represents the Greek ruled territories of the Punjab from about 130
  BCE to 10 CE. Centred on Sagala, near modern Sialkot, its monarchy continued eastern Indo-Greek
  rule through rulers including Strato I and Strato II. Coins provide much of the evidence
  for succession, showing portraits, Greek inscriptions and local scripts that reflect interaction with Indian society.
  Political boundaries and reign dates remain uncertain because written accounts are sparse and coin sequences
  require interpretation. This record separates the eastern kingdom from Hippostratus' western Punjab realm. As Indo-Scythian
  power spread, Greek rulers lost territory and survived farther east. Around 10 CE, the Saka
  ruler Rajuvula conquered the last eastern kingdom, although Greek cultural influences continued in the region.
