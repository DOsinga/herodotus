description: >-
  Wey was a small hereditary state in northeastern Henan, China, governed by rulers of the
  Zhou royal Ji lineage. Its earlier origins lay in a Western Zhou grant, but this record
  follows its separate government from 770 BCE. During the Spring and Autumn period,
  succession disputes and attacks repeatedly weakened it. After enemies destroyed its capital
  at Zhaoge, assistance from Qi helped restore the court, which eventually settled at Diqiu,
  near modern Puyang. Wey survived beside much stronger neighbours, including Jin and the
  larger state of Wei, with which its name is easily confused. Qin captured Puyang in 241 BCE
  and removed the ruler to Yewang. His lineage survived longer, but without control of an
  independent state of its own.
