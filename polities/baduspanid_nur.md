description: >-
  The Baduspanids of Nur were one of the two branches into which the old Baduspanid kingdom of
  Rustamdar, in the mountains of Mazandaran south of the Caspian, divided after the death of
  Kayumarth I in 1453. One line ruled from Kojur and the other from Nur, holding the upper
  valleys around Nur and Baladeh. Both branches traced their descent to the princes who had ruled
  Ruyan since the seventh century and had outlasted Arab, Seljuk, Mongol and Timurid overlords by
  keeping to their mountain strongholds. Kayumarth himself had been reduced to the castle of Nur
  by Timur's troops before recovering Rustamdar in 1405. Under the Safavids the rulers of Nur
  acknowledged the shah while governing their own valleys, until Shah Abbas I ended both branches
  in the 1590s.
