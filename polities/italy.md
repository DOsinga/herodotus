  Italy has been a parliamentary republic since the referendum of 1946 ended the
  monarchy after Fascism and the Second World War. Its territory includes the Italian
  peninsula, Sicily, Sardinia, and islands, with Rome as capital. A constitution taking
  effect in 1948 established elected parliamentary chambers, a president, and
  governments responsible to Parliament. Postwar reconstruction brought industrial
  growth, migration from the countryside, and a persistent economic divide between north
  and south. Italy helped found the European integration project and joined NATO.
  Coalition politics, political violence, and corruption scandals tested its
  institutions, especially during the later twentieth century. Despite changes of
  government, the republic has continued. Manufacturing, services, tourism, and a
  cultural inheritance connect it closely with Europe and the wider world.
