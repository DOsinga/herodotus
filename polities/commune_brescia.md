description: >-
  The Commune of Brescia was the self-governing city state of Brescia in eastern Lombardy,
  from the late eleventh century until Mastino II della Scala took the city in 1332. Its
  consuls and councils governed a large territory between Lake Garda and Lake Iseo. In the
  twelfth century the preacher Arnold of Brescia attacked the wealth of the clergy, and
  the city joined the Lombard League against Frederick Barbarossa. Brescia withstood
  Frederick II's siege of 1238, but surrendered to Henry VII in 1311 after months of
  resistance. Guelph and Ghibelline exiles kept coming back with outside help, and in 1330
  the city gave itself to John of Bohemia. Two years later Verona's Scaliger lords took
  it, and the Visconti followed in 1337.
