name: Fourth Imamate of Oman

description: >-
  The Fourth Imamate of Oman was an Ibadi state in the mountains and oases of the Omani
  interior, established in 1016 when the scholars elected a new imam after decades without
  one. The coast and its ports remained under outside rule, first by the Buyids and later by
  the Seljuks of Kerman, so the imams governed the inland towns such as Nizwa while foreign
  governors held the coast and the sea trade. As in the earlier imamates, the imam was
  elected by the scholars and tribal leaders and shared power with local governors and
  tribes. The imamate came to an end when the Nabhani family rose in 1154 and ruled as
  hereditary kings, setting aside the elected imamate for about two and a half centuries.
