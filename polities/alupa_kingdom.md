description: >-
  The Alupa Kingdom governed the coastal belt of southwestern India known as Alvakheda or Tulu Nadu,
  including parts of modern Karnataka and northern Kerala. Its beginnings around 200 CE are
  conventional estimates; inscriptions provide firmer evidence for later centuries. Native kings ruled
  from Mangalore, Udyavara, and Barkur, balancing local authority with the overlordship of larger
  powers such as the Chalukyas, Rashtrakutas, Hoysalas, and Vijayanagara. Marriage alliances helped
  maintain those relationships. Kannada inscriptions, coins bearing paired fish, and grants to
  religious institutions record the court's activities. Fertile river valleys and Arabian Sea ports
  supported agriculture and maritime exchange. The last known royal inscription dates to 1444, marking
  an estimated end rather than a clearly documented conquest or a certain dynastic extinction.
