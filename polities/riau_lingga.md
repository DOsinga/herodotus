description: >-
  The Riau-Lingga Sultanate was a Malay kingdom in the Riau and Lingga islands south of Singapore,
  formed when the Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824 divided the old Johor-Riau realm. Abdul Rahman was
  installed as its first sultan, while his elder brother Hussein was recognised as sultan of Johor
  in the British sphere. The court sat at Daik on Lingga, and Bugis viceroys based on Penyengat
  island held much practical power and promoted Malay scholarship, including the writing of Raja
  Ali Haji. Tin, gambier and maritime trade supported the islands. A treaty of 1857 placed the
  sultanate under Dutch sovereignty and ended its own foreign relations, and when Sultan Abdul
  Rahman II resisted Dutch demands they deposed him in 1911.
