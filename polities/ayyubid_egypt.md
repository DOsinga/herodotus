description: >-
  The Ayyubid Sultanate of Egypt began in 1171 when Saladin ended the Fatimid caliphate
  and established Sunni rule. Cairo anchored a government supported by substantial
  agricultural revenues, powerful military households, officials, and religious
  institutions. Egypt supplied the resources behind Saladin's campaigns in Syria and
  Palestine, including his recovery of Jerusalem in 1187. After his death, relatives
  divided the wider Ayyubid territories and struggled for precedence. Egyptian sultans
  remained major players in this network, defending the Nile delta against crusader
  invasions while negotiating with other courts. The growing importance of enslaved
  military recruits strengthened the Mamluk commanders. During the Seventh Crusade,
  Egyptian forces defeated and captured Louis IX. In 1250 those commanders killed Sultan
  Turanshah and established the succeeding Mamluk government.
