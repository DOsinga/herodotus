description: >-
  The Republic of Lucca governed a small territory in northwestern Tuscany, governing itself as a
  commune from 1160 until 1314 and again from 1369, when its citizens bought release from Pisan
  rule from Emperor Charles IV, until French occupation in 1799. Lucca prospered through banking
  and the manufacture and export of costly silk fabrics. Councils and magistrates governed the
  republic, although the Guinigi family briefly concentrated power and later institutions became
  dominated by leading families. Surrounded by stronger neighbours, it relied on careful diplomacy
  to preserve its independence. Its imposing Renaissance walls expressed both wealth and concern
  for defence. French revolutionary forces occupied the city in 1799, ending this independent
  republic before subsequent Napoleonic and Bourbon regimes.
