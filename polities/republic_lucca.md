description: >-
  The Republic of Lucca governed a small territory in northwestern Tuscany, represented here
  from its recovery of freedom in 1369 until French occupation in 1799. Its citizens secured
  release from Pisan rule through Emperor Charles IV, restoring local government after decades
  of outside domination. Lucca prospered through banking and the manufacture and export of
  costly silk fabrics. Councils and magistrates governed the republic, although the Guinigi
  family briefly concentrated power and later institutions became dominated by leading families.
  Surrounded by stronger neighbours, it relied on careful diplomacy to preserve its
  independence. Its imposing Renaissance walls expressed both wealth and concern for defence.
  French revolutionary forces occupied the city in 1799, ending this independent republic before
  subsequent Napoleonic and Bourbon regimes.
