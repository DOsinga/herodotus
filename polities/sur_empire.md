description: >-
  The Sur Empire ruled much of northern India between 1538 and 1555, interrupting the early expansion of the Mughal Empire. Its founder, Sher Shah Suri, rose from an Afghan noble family based in Bihar and conquered Bengal before defeating the Mughal emperor Humayun. Sasaram and Delhi served as centres of his monarchy. Sher Shah strengthened roads, built roadside inns, organised postal relays, and issued a standard silver rupee. His conquests extended across the Indo-Gangetic plain, although resistance persisted in frontier regions. Islam Shah maintained the empire after his father's death in 1545, but later succession struggles divided its leaders. Humayun returned from exile and recovered Delhi in 1555, ending Sur control of the imperial centre and restoring Mughal rule there.
