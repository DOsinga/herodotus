description: >-
  The Kingdom of Loango was a state on the Atlantic coast north of the Congo River, in what is
  now the Republic of the Congo, southern Gabon and Cabinda. It formed in the sixteenth
  century, around 1550 by the estimate used here, and the English traveller Andrew Battel
  described its court around 1610. The king, the maloango, ruled from Buali, also called
  Mbanza Loango, through provincial nobles and a council, and the succession passed by a
  rotation among princely lines. Loango exported copper, ivory and palm cloth, and from the
  seventeenth century became a major port for the Atlantic trade in captives with Dutch,
  French and English ships. Royal authority weakened after the death of King Buatu in 1787,
  and France took over the coast in 1883.
