description: >-
  The Wild Jurchens were the Jurchen tribes the Ming counted as furthest from China, in the
  forests and river valleys of eastern and northern Manchuria from the Tumen and the Ussuri
  to the lower Amur. Unlike the Jianzhou and Haixi groups, few of their chiefs kept up
  regular tribute or trade with the Ming, though many held nominal guard titles under
  the Nurgan command of the early fifteenth century. They lived by hunting, fishing and some
  farming in villages led by hereditary chiefs, without any common ruler. The southern
  tribes, among them the Warka of the Tumen basin and the coast, raided and traded with
  Korean garrisons. Between 1607 and 1611 Nurhaci's campaigns brought the Warka
  and their neighbours into his state and moved many of their people west.
