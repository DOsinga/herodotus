description: >-
  The County of Toulouse was a hereditary principality in southern France, centered on Toulouse and extending toward the Mediterranean. This record begins in 852 with Raymond's locally rooted government, rather than the county's earlier creation for royal officials. Its counts built power as Carolingian authority weakened, ruling through aristocratic alliances and managing rival claims over neighboring lands. Occitan culture and growing towns flourished within their domains. The spread of Cathar beliefs drew papal hostility and the Albigensian Crusade, which devastated the region and reduced the counts' strength. The settlement of 1229 imposed submission to France but left local government intact. Alphonse of Poitiers later inherited the county; his death without an heir in 1271 brought incorporation into the royal domain.
