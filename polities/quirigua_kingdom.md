description: >-
  Quiriguá was a Maya kingdom in Guatemala's lower Motagua valley, with an attested royal line
  beginning in 426 and political collapse estimated around 850. Its kings maintained their own
  court while paying tribute to nearby Copán. The city's position linked highland trade with
  Caribbean routes, including traffic in jade and cacao. In 738 K'ak' Tiliw Chan Yopaat captured
  and executed Copán's king, overturning the relationship between the two courts and securing
  greater control over trade. Monument building then expanded dramatically. Quiriguá is
  particularly known for exceptionally tall sandstone stelae and carved animal forms, whose
  inscriptions record royal events. Later kings continued construction, but resources and
  population declined during the ninth century. Subsequent reoccupation did not restore the
  earlier royal kingdom.
