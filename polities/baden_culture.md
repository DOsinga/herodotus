description: >-
  The Baden culture describes related Copper Age communities across the Carpathian Basin
  and neighbouring central Europe, approximately 3600 to 2800 BCE. Named after Baden near
  Vienna, it is identified through pottery, settlements and burial practices rather than
  written accounts of rulers. Its remains occur across modern Hungary, Austria, Slovakia
  and surrounding countries. Farming and livestock keeping supported both shifting
  settlements and more permanent centres, including fortified hilltop sites. Ceramic wagon
  models and paired cattle burials suggest knowledge of wheeled transport, although actual
  wagons have not survived. People buried their dead and also cremated them, sometimes
  placing remains in human-shaped urns. These shared practices do not establish a single
  state or government. Baden overlapped with other cultures, including early Corded Ware.
