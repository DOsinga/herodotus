description: >-
  Naqada culture describes archaeological communities of predynastic Upper Egypt rather than a unified
  state. This record covers its early phase, approximately 4000 to 3500 BCE, before the larger centers
  are represented separately as chiefdoms. Named after Naqada near the Nile, it is recognized through
  pottery, burials, stone tools, and other shared material traditions. Farming along the floodplain
  supported growing settlements and specialized crafts, while connections with desert resources and
  distant communities widened exchange. Differences between graves suggest increasing social
  distinctions, but they do not supply a list of rulers or a precise government. Later Naqada phases
  continued beyond this record and contributed to the emergence of Egyptian kingship. Both boundaries
  are estimates, reflecting gradual cultural change rather than political events.
