description: >-
  The County of Blois was a feudal principality on the middle Loire, centred on Blois and
  recorded here from 940 to 1397. Theobald the Trickster, son of a viscount of Tours, received
  the county around 940 and also held Tours and Chartres. His grandson Odo II added Meaux and
  Troyes, the core of the later County of Champagne, and fought the counts of Anjou for the
  Loire valley, but his son lost Tours to Geoffrey Martel in 1044. Count Stephen led crusaders
  to Antioch in 1097, and his son Stephen became king of England. From 1152 Blois was held by
  a separate branch of the family and later by the house of Châtillon, until Guy II sold it to
  Louis of Orléans.
