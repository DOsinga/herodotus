description: >-
  Philip's tetrarchy occupied the northeastern part of Herod the Great's former kingdom, including
  Batanea, Trachonitis and Gaulanitis, from 4 BCE to 34 CE. Rome confirmed Philip as a client
  ruler after his father's death and the division of the Herodian realm. His territory lay around
  the northern Jordan valley and the lands east of the Sea of Galilee. Philip rebuilt Paneas as
  Caesarea Philippi, making it his capital and distinguishing it from the coastal Caesarea. He
  issued dated coins honouring Roman emperors, evidence that helps reconstruct his reign. The
  tetrarchy was a personal monarchy within Rome's political order, rather than a Roman province
  during his lifetime. After Philip died, Rome attached his lands to Syria until Agrippa received
  them later.
