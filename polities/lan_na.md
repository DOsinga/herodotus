description: >-
  Lan Na was a kingdom of river valleys and towns in what is now northern Thailand. This record begins in
  1292, during Mangrai's consolidation of northern territories. Mangrai founded Chiang Mai in 1296,
  making it the main royal centre. Kings ruled through local lords whose loyalties could shift through war, marriage, and succession
  disputes. Rice cultivation and trade supported towns, while royal patronage helped Theravada Buddhism, temples, and distinctive regional writing flourish. Under
  Tilokaraj in the fifteenth century, Lan Na expanded and fought Ayutthaya as a major rival. Later dynastic conflict weakened its
  rulers. Bayinnaung's Toungoo armies took Chiang Mai in 1558. A tax revolt in 1727 drove out the Burmese, and Chiang Mai governed itself under Ong Kham until Burma retook it in 1763.
