description: >-
  The Northern Yuan continued Mongol imperial rule on the Mongolian Plateau after Ming forces drove the Yuan court from China in 1368. Its khans belonged mainly to the Borjigin family, but their authority depended on powerful nobles and competing tribal groups. Karakorum served as an early centre before warfare and shifting alliances made government more mobile. Conflict with the Oirats repeatedly weakened the khans. In the late fifteenth century, Mandukhai and Dayan Khan reunited many Mongol groups, although grants to royal relatives later dispersed power again. Tibetan Buddhism spread strongly during the sixteenth century. Ligdan Khan failed to restore central authority against growing Manchu influence. His successor Ejei submitted to the Later Jin in 1635, ending the surviving imperial regime.
