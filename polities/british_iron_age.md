description: >-
  The British Iron Age describes archaeological societies in Britain rather than a single kingdom or government. This record covers the conventional period from about 800 to 50 BCE, before named tribal rulers become clearer in coins and Roman accounts. Iron tools and weapons spread alongside changing farming practices, settlement patterns, and regional styles of pottery and metalwork. Roundhouses, enclosed farms, and hillforts reveal varied communities; Maiden Castle in Dorset represents one large fortified centre. Burials and rich objects suggest differences in status, but cannot identify one political structure shared across the island. Trade connected Britain with continental Europe, carrying goods and ideas in both directions. Later Iron Age kingdoms grew from these regional societies, which continued beyond this record's endpoint.
