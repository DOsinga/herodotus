description: >-
  The Grand Duchy of Finland existed from 1809 to 1917 after Sweden ceded Finland
  to Russia. The Russian emperor held the title of grand duke, but Finland
  retained its own laws, Lutheran institutions, and a Senate that administered
  domestic affairs. Helsinki replaced Turku as the capital in 1812. During the
  nineteenth century, separate institutions, economic development, and growing
  Finnish national culture strengthened its distinct identity within the empire.
  Finnish gained official standing alongside Swedish, and the reformed parliament
  introduced universal suffrage in 1906, including women's eligibility for
  election. Imperial attempts to restrict autonomy caused conflict in the final
  decades. The Russian monarchy's collapse in 1917 removed the old basis of the
  union, and Finland declared independence as a separate state.
