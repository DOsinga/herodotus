description: >-
  Bihwa Gaya was a kingdom around modern Changnyeong in southeastern Korea, associated with the
  Gaya confederacy during the Three Kingdoms period. Its formation is estimated here around 300
  CE from the archaeological sequence, not a recorded founding ceremony. It may have developed
  from the earlier Jinhan community called Bulsaguk. Written references are sparse, including
  Korean historical accounts and the Japanese Nihon Shoki, which uses a related name. Royal tombs
  at Gyo-dong reveal a local ruling elite, rich burial customs, and connections with neighbouring
  Silla. Those connections did not prevent conflict or eventual conquest. Silla absorbed the
  kingdom sometime before 555; the endpoint is an upper bound rather than an exact surrender
  date. Its individual kings and administrative arrangements remain unclear.
