  Serbia is a landlocked republic in southeastern Europe, with its capital at Belgrade where the
  Sava meets the Danube. The present independent state dates from 2006, when Montenegro left
  their joint state union after a referendum. Serbia continued the union's international legal
  position while establishing its own constitutional framework. It is a parliamentary republic
  with an elected president, a prime minister leading the government, and a unicameral National
  Assembly. Its territory joins the agricultural plains of Vojvodina with the river valleys and
  mountains farther south. Kosovo declared independence in 2008; Serbia rejects that declaration,
  and recognition remains divided internationally. Relations with neighboring countries, the
  legacy of Yugoslavia's breakup, and negotiations over European Union membership have shaped the
  republic's subsequent history.
