description: >-
  The Peru–Bolivian Confederation was a union of Bolivia with two Peruvian states, North
  Peru and South Peru, created by the Bolivian president Andrés de Santa Cruz. Santa Cruz
  intervened in Peru's civil wars in 1835, defeated the rival leaders Felipe Salaverry and
  Agustín Gamarra, and in 1836 took the title of Supreme Protector. The Pact of Tacna of
  1837 set out a loose federal constitution, but real power lay with Santa Cruz, who
  directed the army, foreign policy and much of the administration from Lima. Chile and
  Argentina saw the new state as a threat and declared war, joined by Peruvian exiles. A
  Chilean and Peruvian army defeated Santa Cruz at Yungay in January 1839, and the
  confederation was dissolved later that year.
