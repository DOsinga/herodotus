description: >-
  Ashdod was one of the five principal Philistine cities on the southern Levantine coastal plain, near the Mediterranean in present day Israel. Philistine settlement is estimated here from about 1175 BCE. The city had its own ruler and participated in the political and trading networks connecting Philistia, Egypt and inland kingdoms. Its archaeological remains show rebuilding, imported pottery and changing cultural influences across the Iron Age. Assyrian expansion repeatedly altered its position. After rebellion, Sargon II captured Ashdod in 711 BCE and imposed provincial rule. A local king, Mitinti, is again attested by 701 BCE, marking renewed government under Assyrian supremacy. Babylonian campaigns under Nebuchadnezzar ended this later kingdom around 604 BCE, although settlement and the city's name continued afterward.
