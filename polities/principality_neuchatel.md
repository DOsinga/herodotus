description: >-
  The Principality of Neuchâtel was a small state on the northern shore of Lake
  Neuchâtel and in the Jura mountains, in western Switzerland. It grew out of a county
  held by local counts from the twelfth century, later passing to the houses of
  Fribourg, Hochberg and Orléans-Longueville. Its estates and town councils kept wide
  rights, and it allied with Swiss cantons from the fifteenth century as an associate of
  the Confederacy. When the Longueville line died out in 1707, the estates chose the
  king of Prussia as prince, but Neuchâtel kept its own laws and government. In 1806
  Napoleon gave it to Marshal Berthier, who ruled through French officials. Prussian
  rule returned in 1814, and Neuchâtel joined Switzerland as a canton in 1815.
