description: >-
  The Seuna Kingdom, also called the Yadava kingdom, ruled much of the western Deccan in India
  from 1187 to 1313. Its kings governed from the hill fortress of Devagiri, now Daulatabad in
  Maharashtra. Bhillama V established independent rule as Western Chalukya authority collapsed,
  although struggles with the Hoysalas constrained early expansion. Under Simhana the kingdom
  became a major regional power, extending influence north toward the Narmada and south into
  former Hoysala lands. Sanskrit scholarship flourished at its court, while Marathi literature,
  including influential religious writing, also developed during this period. Delhi's armies
  first attacked Devagiri in 1296 and subsequently imposed tribute. Repeated intervention
  followed, and Malik Kafur's campaign in 1313 ended the kingdom's separate government under the
  expanding Delhi Sultanate.
