description: >-
  The Republic of Metz was the self-governing city of Metz in Lorraine, recorded here from
  1250 until France took the city in 1552. Metz became a free imperial city in 1189, and its
  bishops withdrew to Vic-sur-Seille, leaving government to the citizens. A council of
  aldermen and a master alderman, chosen from a few patrician family associations called
  paraiges, ran the city and its surrounding countryside. Metz grew rich on trade, banking and
  wine, and defended its independence against the dukes of Lorraine, Luxembourg and Bar and
  the archbishop of Trier, notably in the War of Metz of 1324. In 1552 Henry II of France
  occupied the city under the Treaty of Chambord and held it against the siege of Charles V.
