description: >-
  The Achaemenid Empire grew from Persia into a vast monarchy spanning the eastern
  Mediterranean, Egypt, Mesopotamia, Iran, and parts of Central Asia. Cyrus established
  its imperial position by overthrowing the Median king in 550 BCE and conquering Lydia
  and Babylon. Later rulers extended its territory and organised provinces governed by
  satraps under the king. Roads, royal messengers, taxation, and administrative
  languages connected its diverse peoples. Persepolis was a ceremonial royal centre,
  while Susa, Babylon, and other cities also served the court. Conflicts with Greek
  states formed only one part of its wider history. Alexander of Macedonia invaded in
  the fourth century BCE and captured the capitals. Darius III's death in 330 BCE marks
  the end of the empire recorded here.
