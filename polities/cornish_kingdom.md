description: >-
  The Cornish Kingdom represents the reduced British government in Britain's
  southwestern peninsula after Dumnonia lost its eastern lands. Its history here runs
  from 710 to the conventional final incorporation under Athelstan in 936, although
  the survival of native kingship is disputed. Cornish rulers faced repeated West
  Saxon attacks while maintaining connections with other Brittonic communities across
  the sea. A Cornish victory at Hehil in 722 helped sustain resistance; defeat
  alongside Viking allies at Hingston Down in 838 marked increasing pressure. The
  drowning of King Donyarth in 875 provides another possible boundary for royal
  independence. English authority spread unevenly, and Cornish language and culture
  continued afterward. Bodmin represents a later regional centre, not a documented
  capital of this poorly recorded monarchy.
