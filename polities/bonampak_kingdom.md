description: >-
  Bonampak was a small Maya kingdom in the Usumacinta region of present day Chiapas,
  Mexico, with a court estimated here from 400 to 800. Its kings maintained their own
  local government despite the growing influence of nearby Yaxchilan. Inscriptions
  record early conflicts and the capture of rulers and nobles, while later kings were
  installed with Yaxchilan's support. Bonampak is best known for the richly coloured
  murals commissioned under Chan Muwan II near the end of the eighth century. Their
  scenes show court ceremony, warfare, captives, music, and ritual bloodletting, giving
  an unusually detailed view of Maya royal life. Temples and carved monuments reinforce
  that picture. The court disappeared around the beginning of the ninth century as
  neighbouring kingdoms also declined.
