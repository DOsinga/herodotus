description: >-
  The Ghassulian culture flourished in the southern Levant approximately between 4400 and 3500 BCE. It
  is named after Teleilat el-Ghassul, a settlement near the northern Dead Sea in present Jordan.
  Archaeology identifies farming villages, mud brick houses, underground dwellings in some areas, and distinctive pottery
  rather than a known kingdom or royal dynasty. Communities cultivated crops, kept livestock, and developed
  copper working while continuing to use stone tools. Objects, painted walls, and ritual sites reveal
  skilled crafts and shared beliefs, but do not establish a single government across the region.
  Secondary burial and decorated ossuaries are important parts of the surviving evidence. The culture ended
  before the urban societies of the Early Bronze Age, through changes whose causes remain debated.
