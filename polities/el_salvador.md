  El Salvador is a republic on Central America's Pacific coast, governed from San Salvador. After
  independence from Spain, a brief Mexican union, and membership in the Central American federation,
  it established separate sovereignty in 1841. Coffee exports reshaped landholding and enriched an
  elite, while rural communities faced dispossession and repression. Military governments dominated
  much of the twentieth century, including the violent suppression of the 1932 uprising. A civil war
  between the government and leftist guerrillas lasted from 1980 until peace agreements in 1992
  brought political reforms and turned the insurgents into a party. Subsequent governments have faced
  poverty, emigration, and organized violence. The state continues under a presidential system, with
  its history here spanning successive governments rather than separating regimes.
