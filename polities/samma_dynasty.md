description: >-
  The Samma dynasty ruled Sindh, in present day Pakistan, from 1351 until the early sixteenth
  century. The Samma tribe overthrew the Soomras in 1351, and its rulers took the title of jam,
  which served as their equivalent of sultan. Their capital was Samanagar, the later Thatta, near
  the Indus delta. For a few decades the jams paid tribute to the Delhi sultans, who sent armies
  against them in the 1360s, but from the end of the fourteenth century they governed as an
  independent regional sultanate. Trade passed through the delta ports, and the dynasty left its
  mark in the tombs of the Makli necropolis. Shah Beg Arghun, coming from Kandahar, defeated Jam
  Feroz near Thatta in 1520 and took over the country, ending Samma rule.
